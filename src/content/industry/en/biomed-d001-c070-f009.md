# Citation and Traceability for Clinical Trial Pre-screening in Regulatory Affairs

## Data Characteristics

Data for clinical trial pre-screening in regulatory affairs primarily comes from regulatory documents, guidelines, clinical trial reports of approved drugs, pharmacological and toxicological research data, and relevant medical literature. This data has a relatively stable update frequency, typically occurring when regulations are revised, new guidelines are issued, or significant research findings are published. Document structures are mainly PDF, Word, and RTF formats, containing large amounts of unstructured text such as trial protocol descriptions, subject inclusion/exclusion criteria, adverse event reports, and statistical analysis results. Data fields cover compound names, indications, trial phases, research centers, sample sizes, primary endpoints, secondary endpoints, and various biomarker data. Diverse units of measurement are involved, including drug dosages (mg/kg), time (days, weeks, months), and biological indicators (mol/L, U/L).

## Constraints on Citation and Traceability

Regulatory affairs data has a relatively low update frequency, but each update involves a large volume of information and demands extremely high accuracy. This requires citation source indexing to balance depth and breadth, ensuring precise capture of subtle regulatory changes or clinical data updates. Documents are often unstructured text, which means traditional keyword-based retrieval might miss important context, affecting traceability completeness. Complex document structures and diverse fields and units necessitate precise referencing to specific paragraphs, table rows, or chart areas within the original text. Furthermore, the highly specialized biomedical terminology involved in the data requires advanced model capabilities in understanding context and identifying semantic relationships to avoid ambiguity or misinterpretation during traceability.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item                 | Recommended Value              | Rationale                                                                        |
| :--------------------------------- | :----------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Chunk size` (Segment Length)        | `500–800 characters`           | Balances contextual completeness with retrieval granularity; avoids diluting key information in overly long segments. |
| `Recall count` (Recall Count)          | `Top 8–12 entries`             | Covers regulations, guidelines, and multiple trial reports, ensuring comprehensive information. |
| `Similarity threshold` (Similarity Threshold) | `0.75–0.85`                    | Improves relevance of retrieval results, reduces interference from irrelevant or low-quality information. |
| `Rerank result count` (Rerank Return Count) | `Top 5 entries`                | Refines the final presented citations, highlighting the most relevant and authoritative sources. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`       | `600 seconds`                  | Addresses long parsing times for large PDF or Word documents, prevents timeout failures. |
| `UPLOAD_FILE_MAX_SIZE`             | `500 MB`                       | Accommodates the upload requirements for large files like clinical trial reports, supports uploading complete documents. |

## Common Pitfalls

*   Empty or incomplete citation sources: Typically due to insufficient knowledge base indexing or a `Similarity threshold` (Similarity Threshold) set too high, preventing relevant documents from being recalled.
*   Low relevance of returned citations to the question: This might occur if `Chunk size` (Segment Length) is too large, causing a single segment to contain too much irrelevant information, or if `Rerank result count` (Rerank Return Count) is insufficient to display diverse relevant results.
*   File upload failure or parsing timeout: Common when processing large clinical trial reports or regulatory compilation files, if `UPLOAD_FILE_MAX_SIZE` or `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` parameters are set too low.

## Validation Steps

*   Select a typical regulatory affairs question. Observe whether the returned citation sources cover the key regulations, guidelines, and experimental data related to the question. Check the specific content and relevance of each citation to the question.
*   Upload a clinical trial report PDF containing complex tables and charts. Verify that the file is successfully parsed and indexed, and that specific data points or conclusions can be accurately recalled through questioning.
*   Ask about the scope of application or interpretation of a specific regulatory clause. Observe whether the returned citations precisely point to the specific chapter or paragraph of the original regulation, verifying traceability accuracy.
*   Try queries of different lengths and complexities. Observe citation return speed and quality. Evaluate the impact of the `maxContext` parameter on response efficiency.
