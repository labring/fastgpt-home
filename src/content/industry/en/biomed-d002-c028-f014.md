# Forms and Interactions for Molecular Diagnostics Products

## Data Characteristics for This Category

Molecular diagnostics product data centers on biological information, such as specific genes, nucleic acid sequences, and protein markers. Data sources include clinical trial reports, research literature, product manuals, regulatory documents, and internal experimental data. Update frequency typically aligns with new reagent releases, detection method improvements, or epidemiological changes, occurring quarterly or semi-annually. Document structures are often a mix of structured and semi-structured formats. For example, product manuals contain explicit fields for detection principles, applicable sample types, detection ranges, performance indicators (e.g., sensitivity, specificity), lot numbers, and expiration dates. Field units are precise, such as `copies/mL` or `IU/mL` for viral load, `ng/µL` for nucleic acid concentration, `Cycle Threshold (Ct)` for amplification curves, and `%` for accuracy or variation frequency. Documents frequently include complex graphs, tables, and specialized terminology.

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on "Forms and Interactions"

The specialized and complex nature of molecular diagnostics data places specific demands on form design and interaction logic. Frequent professional terminology requires forms to provide accurate contextual hints or term explanations to prevent user misunderstandings. Diverse measurement units necessitate input fields that support unit selection or automatic conversion, such as `ng/µL` to `µg/mL`. Structured performance indicator fields (e.g., sensitivity, specificity) require clear input type validation to ensure data accuracy. Semi-structured clinical reports or research literature demand forms capable of attachment uploads and internal parsing to extract key information. Periodic data updates mean that form submissions may need to trigger backend tasks to compare or update against the latest knowledge base. Complex logical relationships (e.g., a specific reagent kit only applies to certain sample types) require linked validation at the form level to reduce invalid submissions.

## Configuration Guidelines

| Configuration Item | Suggested Value | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `UPLOAD_FILE_MAX_SIZE` | `200 MB` | Supports uploading large product manuals or clinical reports, which include graphs and extensive text. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `300 seconds` | Parsing complex PDF documents is time-consuming and requires sufficient processing time to avoid timeouts. |
| `maxContext` | `4000 characters` | Ensures AI conversations can cover core information from product manuals, such as detection principles and performance indicators. |
| `Chunk size` | `800 characters` | Molecular diagnostics documents often contain long descriptive paragraphs; this length helps maintain semantic integrity. |
| `Recall count` | `Top 8 entries` | Ensures recall of enough relevant performance parameters, applicable ranges, or precautions to improve answer accuracy. |
| `Similarity threshold` | `0.75` | Higher than general thresholds, ensuring recalled knowledge snippets are highly relevant to the query and exclude interference. |

## Three Common Mistakes

*   The system becomes unresponsive or errors after form submission because the uploaded PDF file is too large or complex, and `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` is set too short, leading to a parsing timeout.
*   AI conversation responses about specific reagent kit performance indicators are incomplete. This occurs when `Chunk size` is set too small, causing critical data to be split across different knowledge blocks, affecting completeness.
*   When users input detection concentration units, the system reports a format error or inability to recognize. This happens when the form does not offer unit selection and does not effectively validate and convert special units like `ng/µL` or `copies/mL`.

## How to Confirm Proper Configuration

*   Upload a molecular diagnostics product manual PDF containing multiple pages of charts and detailed technical parameters. Check if it successfully parses and extracts key fields.
*   Simulate user queries about the sensitivity and specificity of a particular reagent kit. Observe if the AI response is complete and cites correct values and units.
*   Input numerical values in different formats and units into the form (e.g., `1000 copies/mL` and `1 IU/mL`). Verify if the system correctly identifies, stores, or converts them to a unified format.
*   Test submitting form data with mutually exclusive conditions (e.g., selecting incompatible sample types and reagent kits). Check if the form validation logic activates and provides clear prompts.
