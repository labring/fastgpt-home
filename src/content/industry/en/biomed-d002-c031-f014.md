# Forms and Interaction for Medical Insurance Access Products

## Data Characteristics

Medical insurance access data originates from official sources. These include policy documents, drug and device catalogs, payment standards, and negotiation announcements from national and local medical insurance bureaus. Data updates frequently. For example, the national medical insurance catalog typically adjusts annually, while local policies may release updates irregularly.

Document formats vary. Official documents are often PDFs. Catalog lists typically use Excel. Public information appears on web pages.

Catalog lists include fields such as drug name, generic name, dosage form, specification, medical insurance payment scope, reimbursement ratio, and restricted payment conditions. Policy documents are largely unstructured text. Natural language processing extracts key information like applicable diseases, indications, and special approval processes.

Field names may lack uniformity, for example, "medical insurance code" or "payment code." Units like "yuan/box" or "mg/tablet" require standardization.

## Constraints from Data Characteristics on Forms and Interaction

The time-sensitive nature of medical insurance access data requires forms to support rapid updates and version management. When a new medical insurance catalog releases, the system must quickly import and update reimbursement information for relevant drugs or devices. This ensures users query the latest data.

Diverse data formats mean form input must support structured data entry. It also requires non-structured text upload and parsing, for example, parsing restricted payment conditions from PDF policy documents.

Inconsistent field names require flexible field mapping mechanisms in forms. This adapts to naming conventions from different data sources.

Complex restricted payment conditions and reimbursement ratios mean simple dropdowns or text inputs are insufficient. This requires multi-level, condition-triggered dynamic form controls.

Compliance considerations necessitate clear error messages and data validation in form interactions. This ensures user input conforms to medical insurance policy regulations.

## Configuration Guidelines

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
| :----------------- | :---------------- | :-------- |
| `Chunk size` (Segment Length) | `500-800 characters` | Medical insurance policy texts often contain long descriptive clauses. This length helps maintain contextual integrity. |
| `Recall count` (Recall Count) | `Top 10-15 entries` | Medical insurance access queries typically require synthesizing multiple policy pieces. Increasing recall count improves relevance coverage. |
| `Similarity threshold` (Similarity Threshold) | `0.75-0.85` | Medical insurance policy terminology is highly specialized. A high threshold ensures precise matching of recall results to avoid misinformation. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `600 seconds` | Parsing large medical insurance catalogs or policy files can be time-consuming. Sufficient processing time is necessary. |
| `maxContext` | `32k tokens` | Medical insurance policy interpretation often involves complex logic and multi-condition judgments. A large context window helps the model understand. |
| `Allowed Upload File Types` | `PDF, XLSX, DOCX` | Covers common official release formats for medical insurance policy documents and catalog tables. |

## Common Pitfalls

*   Query results lack the latest policy entries. This occurs when the data synchronization mechanism does not trigger promptly or the parser fails to correctly process new file formats.
*   The system fails to return an accurate medical insurance payment scope after a user inputs a specific drug name. This occurs due to incorrect field mapping configuration, leading to a mismatch between the drug name and the standard name in the medical insurance catalog.
*   After form submission, the system prompts "reimbursement ratio calculation error." This occurs when dynamic form logic incorrectly handles tiered reimbursement rules under restricted payment conditions.

## Validation Steps

*   Upload the latest medical insurance policy documents. Verify the system correctly parses and extracts key field information.
*   Input different drug or device names and specifications. Cross-check that the returned medical insurance payment scope, reimbursement ratio, and restricted conditions align with official documents.
*   Simulate various complex reimbursement scenarios, including different medical insurance types and special medication situations. Check the accuracy of the form's dynamic interaction logic and calculation results.
