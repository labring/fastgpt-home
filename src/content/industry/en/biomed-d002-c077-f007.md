# Workflow Orchestration for Dermatology Products

## Data Characteristics for This Category

Dermatology product data primarily originates from pharmaceutical companies' official product inserts, clinical trial reports, academic journal articles, industry regulatory documents, and market research reports. This data typically exists as PDF documents, Word documents, structured databases (e.g., product ingredient lists, efficacy claims), and semi-structured text (e.g., user feedback, expert interpretations). Data update frequency is relatively stable. Updates occur in concentrated bursts when new products launch or regulations change, but core product information remains largely consistent, usually maintained quarterly or annually. Document structures often include fixed fields like "Product Name," "Ingredients," "Indications," "Dosage and Administration," "Adverse Reactions," and "Contraindications" in product inserts. Clinical reports focus on trial design, statistical results, and conclusions. Market reports might include dimensions such as "Market Share" and "Competing Products." Regarding fields and units, ingredient content often uses percentages (%) or milligrams (mg). Efficacy validation might involve specialized units like skin moisture values (%) and elasticity indices (AU).

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on Workflow Orchestration

The coexistence of highly structured and semi-structured dermatology product data requires robust multi-format parsing capabilities during the data processing stage of the workflow. Fixed fields in product inserts need precise extraction to build structured knowledge. Unstructured text in clinical and market research reports requires semantic understanding and key information extraction. A relatively low data update frequency means that the workflow's index rebuilding and knowledge base synchronization frequency can be less aggressive, not requiring real-time updates, but new product information must be incorporated promptly. Furthermore, the presence of specialized medical terminology and measurement units necessitates that the workflow preserves contextual integrity during text segmentation. This prevents the loss of meaning for technical terms or critical values due to improper splitting. Handling sensitive information like "Adverse Reactions" and "Contraindications" requires the workflow design to prioritize retrieval accuracy and response rigor to avoid misleading information.

## Configuration Guidelines

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `maxContext` | `3000 Tokens` | Accommodates longer professional descriptions in dermatology product inserts and clinical reports, ensuring contextual completeness. |
| `Chunk size` | `500 characters` | Balances segmentation granularity with semantic integrity, suitable for paragraphs containing multiple pieces of information. |
| `Recall count` | `Top 8 entries` | Considers that queries might involve multiple aspects of product information, increasing recall to improve coverage. |
| `Similarity threshold` | `0.75` | Ensures precise matching of retrieved knowledge segments for dermatology product inquiries, reducing irrelevant information. |
| `Rerank result count` | `Top 3 entries` | Focuses on the top few most relevant pieces of information while maintaining accuracy, improving response efficiency. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `600 seconds` | Allows ample time to process large PDF documents and complex clinical trial report parsing. |

## Common Pitfalls

*   Links in workflow responses are automatically converted to interactive "Click here to ask now" prompts. This occurs because the response content is not explicitly designated as plain text or a specific link format, leading the system to interpret it as an interactive command.
*   The workflow fails to maintain global variables across multi-turn conversations, resulting in loss of contextual information. This happens when global variable lifecycle settings are incorrect, not configured for cross-session persistence.
*   Improper workflow input instruction settings prevent specific functions from being triggered correctly. This manifests as instructions not responding or triggering incorrect processes, because the instruction format does not match the predefined trigger rules.

## Validation Steps

*   Simulate multiple typical dermatology product inquiry scenarios. Check the accuracy and completeness of key product parameters (e.g., ingredients, indications, dosage, and administration) recalled in workflow responses. Ensure consistency between responses and original source material.
*   Upload large product inserts or clinical reports. Observe if file parsing completes within the `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` limit. Check the knowledge base segmentation effect, ensuring that specialized terminology and critical data are not incorrectly split.
*   Conduct multi-turn dialogue tests. Verify if the workflow maintains contextual understanding of product information during continuous questioning, especially for follow-up questions regarding adverse reactions and contraindications, ensuring response coherence and rigor.
