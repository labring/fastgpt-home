# HTTP Interface and External Systems for CAR-T Cell Therapy Regulations

## Data Characteristics

CAR-T cell therapy regulations and Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) documents typically exist as PDFs, Word files, or internal knowledge base pages. These documents cover the entire process, from patient screening, cell collection, preparation, quality control, reinfusion, to post-treatment follow-up, along with related ethical and regulatory requirements. Data sources primarily include guidelines from national drug administration agencies, operational standards from industry associations, detailed SOPs from hospitals or research institutions, and product manuals from pharmaceutical companies. Document updates are infrequent, usually occurring every six months to several years, coinciding with regulatory revisions or technological advancements. Document structures are rigorous, containing numerous technical terms, flowcharts, tables, and references. Fields include, but are not limited to: cell type, treatment plan, dosage, quality control indicators, adverse event grades and management measures, and follow-up cycles. Units are predominantly international standard units or commonly used clinical units.

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on HTTP Interfaces and External Systems

The low update frequency of CAR-T cell therapy regulation documents means external systems do not require frequent polling when retrieving data via HTTP interfaces. A less frequent synchronization strategy, such as weekly or monthly full or incremental synchronization, can be adopted. The highly specialized nature of the document content, involving many obscure medical terms and complex processes, demands more robust text parsing and semantic understanding modules. This requires HTTP interfaces to reliably transmit large files and the backend to effectively process multi-format documents. Non-textual information like tables and flowcharts necessitates HTTP interfaces supporting binary file transfer and external systems having corresponding parsing capabilities. Furthermore, the strictness of the documents requires maintaining content integrity and accuracy during data transmission and parsing. Missing fields or misinterpreting units could lead to severe consequences.

## Common Pitfalls

*   HTTP request failure or a 500 status code: This usually occurs due to excessively large files or an unstable network, leading to upload or download timeouts, and the external system failing to handle large file transfers correctly.
*   Lack of critical dosage or quality control standard information in knowledge base answers: This may happen if key data in tables or flowcharts are not effectively extracted during document parsing, or if field identification is inaccurate.
*   System prompts "No relevant content found" or returns generic answers: This could be due to incorrect segmentation of specialized terms during document chunking, or a similarity matching threshold set too high, preventing the retrieval of relevant knowledge chunks.

## How to Verify Configuration

*   Manually upload a typical CAR-T treatment SOP document (e.g., a 50MB PDF file) and check if it uploads successfully and is parsed by the system.
*   Trigger a data synchronization via the HTTP interface and check the logs. Confirm that all expected documents are successfully pulled and processed, with no file download failures or parsing errors.
*   Query the knowledge base, for example, "What are the quality control indicators before CAR-T cell reinfusion?". Verify that the system accurately returns specific values and units from the document and check the completeness of the returned content.

The values provided are common starting points and should be measured against the reader's own samples.
