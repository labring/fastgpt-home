# Tool Calling and Plugins for CRO Regulations

## Data Characteristics in This Category

CRO (Contract Research Organization) regulatory and SOP (Standard Operating Procedure) documents in the biopharmaceutical field originate from internal quality management systems, project management processes, and regulatory compliance files. These documents are typically in PDF, Word, or structured XML/JSON formats. Content covers all stages of drug development, including preclinical research, clinical trials, and regulatory submissions. Update frequency depends on regulatory changes, new project launches, and internal process optimizations, usually quarterly or annually. Some critical SOPs may be revised in real-time as projects progress. Document structures are rigorous, containing numerous clauses, definitions, operating procedures, responsibility assignments, attachments, and reference documents. Fields include "version number," "effective date," "revision history," "scope," "responsibilities," "operating steps," and "recording requirements." Units often involve time (e.g., "hours," "days"), quantity (e.g., "copies," "times"), and temperature (e.g., "℃").

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on Tool Calling and Plugins

The rigor and specificity of CRO regulatory documents demand high semantic understanding and precise information extraction capabilities from tool calling and plugins. The periodic nature of document updates means the knowledge base requires regular synchronization with the latest versions to ensure timely responses. Diverse document formats and complex structures limit the effectiveness of single text processing methods, necessitating pre-processing with technologies like OCR and NLP. Strong correlations between clauses and definitions require tools to trace reference sources and handle cross-document knowledge associations when answering. The standardization of fields and units enables structured data extraction by plugins, but also requires plugins to accurately identify and convert these specialized terms to avoid misunderstandings.

## Three Common Mistakes

*   **Tool calls return empty or incomplete values:** This occurs when tool configuration parameters do not match field names or expected units in the document.
*   **Disordered tool call sequence in the workflow leads to abnormal results:** This happens when the execution order of tools is not logically arranged according to the hierarchical structure of regulatory Q&A, for example, failing to classify clauses before extracting details.
*   **Expected aggregated results are not obtained after tool calls:** This is due to the outputs of multiple AI models or tools in the workflow not being effectively integrated or filtered, leading to the final response containing excessive intermediate process information.

## How to Verify Configuration

*   Ask questions about typical regulatory clauses. Check if the response accurately cites the original document and provides corresponding version numbers and effective dates.
*   Simulate specific operational procedures from SOPs. Verify if the tool can correctly identify and extract key information step-by-step, such as "executor" and "recording method."
*   Upload a newly revised regulatory document. After the knowledge base updates, check if the Q&A content reflects the latest revised clauses.
*   Use queries containing specialized terms and abbreviations. Observe if the tool can correctly parse and link them to definitions and explanations in the regulatory documents.
