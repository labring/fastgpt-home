# Model Integration and Configuration for mRNA Vaccine Regulations

## Data Characteristics

mRNA vaccine regulatory and SOP documents originate from pharmaceutical regulatory bodies (regulations, guidelines, technical review requirements) and internal vaccine manufacturer documents (management specifications, standard operating procedures, batch production records). Data updates are relatively stable, with new regulations or guidelines typically released quarterly or annually. Internal SOPs are revised based on production process optimization and quality management system updates. Document structures are often hierarchical PDFs, Word documents, or internal knowledge base pages. They contain extensive technical terminology, specific operating procedures, charts, flowcharts, and attachments. Fields and units are highly standardized, including drug batch numbers, production dates, expiration dates, storage conditions (temperature in `℃`), dosage (in `μg`, `ml`), purity (in `%`), and detection limits (in `ng`).

## Constraints on Model Integration and Configuration

The hierarchical structure and dense technical terminology of mRNA vaccine regulatory documents require models to effectively identify section boundaries during text segmentation to avoid semantic fragmentation. Stable update frequencies necessitate periodic synchronization of model training data to ensure regulatory compliance. Documents containing charts and flowcharts imply that purely text-based models may not capture all information, requiring consideration of multimodal or image processing assistance. The presence of standardized fields and units demands precision in entity recognition and information extraction; models must distinguish numerical values under different units and correctly associate them with corresponding fields. Furthermore, the rigor of regulatory documents requires high accuracy and traceability in model recall to prevent misleading answers, especially concerning quality control and production compliance.

## Configuration Strategy

| Configuration Item             | Recommended Value    | Rationale                                          |
| :----------------------------- | :------------------- | :------------------------------------------------- |
| `Chunk Size`                   | `800–1200 characters` | Accommodates the length of regulatory chapters and SOP steps, maintaining semantic completeness. |
| `Recall Count`                 | `Top 5–8`            | Covers relevant regulatory clauses, improves accuracy, and reduces interference from irrelevant information. |
| `Similarity Threshold`         | `0.75–0.85`          | Ensures strong relevance of recalled content, filtering out fuzzy matches. |
| `maxContext`                   | `4000 Tokens`        | Addresses the context requirements of professional documents, preventing information loss. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`   | `600 seconds`        | Handles parsing time for large PDF and Word documents, preventing timeouts. |
| `Rerank Return Count`          | `Top 3`              | Further refines recall results, focusing on the most critical regulatory basis. |

## Common Pitfalls

- Document parsing failure or content omission, leading to some regulatory clauses being unretrievable. This occurs due to complex document formats, including numerous embedded images, tables, or non-standard fonts, which parsers cannot effectively extract.
- Insufficient relevance in recall results, where answers do not directly point to specific regulations or SOP clauses. This happens when text segmentation granularity is too coarse or too fine, resulting in incomplete semantics or excessive irrelevant information within a single text block, affecting vector matching accuracy.
- Model confusion when processing specific technical terms or units, such as misinterpreting `µg/ml` as `mg/ml`. This is due to a lack of sufficient diversity in training data or insufficient reinforcement learning for specialized vocabulary.

## Verification Steps

- Upload an mRNA vaccine SOP document containing various formats (e.g., tables, images, multi-level headings) and verify that the knowledge base has fully parsed and indexed all text content.
- For critical steps in the vaccine production process, such as "formulation filling" or "lyophilization process," query relevant regulatory requirements and check if the recall results accurately cite the corresponding regulatory clauses and SOP numbers.
- Randomly select technical terms and numerical values with units from the document. Conduct question-and-answer tests to verify whether the model can correctly identify and explain this information. For example, ask about "storage temperature range" or "purity requirements" and check if the returned values and units match the original text.
