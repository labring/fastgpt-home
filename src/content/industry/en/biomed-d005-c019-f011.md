# Document Parsing and Chunking for Metabolic and Endocrine Regulations

## Data Characteristics in this Domain
Regulatory and SOP documents in the metabolic and endocrine field primarily originate from national health commissions, drug administration agencies (e.g., regulations), and professional organizations (e.g., clinical guidelines from medical associations). Internal implementation rules from healthcare institutions also contribute. These documents have a relatively low update frequency, typically a few times per year, with slightly higher frequency during major policy changes or clinical research breakthroughs.

Document structures vary:
- Regulatory files often use multi-level nesting (chapters, articles, detailed rules).
- Guidelines frequently present structured evidence-based medical recommendations with grading.
- SOPs focus on operational steps, precautions, and risk assessments.

Common fields in document content include disease names, drug names, dosage units (e.g., `mg/kg`, `IU`), diagnostic criteria, treatment pathways, and laboratory indicators (e.g., `HbA1c`, `TG`) with their normal ranges.

## Constraints on Document Parsing and Chunking
The clear hierarchical structure, specialized terminology, and presence of numerical values with units in metabolic and endocrine documents impose specific parsing requirements.

The rigor of regulations and guidelines means that the integrity of individual articles or steps is crucial. Arbitrary truncation can lead to semantic distortion. Specialized fields like drug dosages and lab indicator ranges often appear as numbers with units; the parser must recognize and maintain these as a single entity.

Although document updates are infrequent, each update may involve critical revisions. Chunking must consider comparing old and new versions to accurately identify and index revised content. This requires fine-grained identification of logical text units and enhanced processing for specific patterns during chunking.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale for this Value |
| :----- | :------- | :----------- |
| `Chunk Length` | `300–500 characters` | Ensures each knowledge chunk contains complete regulatory articles or SOP steps, preventing semantic fragmentation. |
| `Overlap Length` | `50–80 characters` | Retains contextual information, helping the model understand content across chunks, especially at the junctions of multi-level articles or operational steps. |
| `Separator` | `\n\n`, `。`, `；` | Prioritizes natural paragraphs, periods, and semicolons for splitting, maintaining logical integrity. |
| `Parsing Mode` | `Split by Title` | Effectively identifies logical units for regulations and guidelines with multi-chapter, multi-article structures. |
| `Max File Size` | `50 MB` | Accommodates the size of large clinical guidelines and regulatory compilations. |
| `Parsing Timeout` | `600 seconds` | Provides sufficient time to parse complex PDF or Word documents, preventing failures due to excessively long parsing times. |

## Common Pitfalls
- Critical drug dosages or lab indicator ranges are incorrectly split after document parsing. For example, `HbA1c < 7.0%` is divided into two parts. This typically results from a chunk length that is too short or improperly set separators, failing to treat the number and unit as a single entity.
- The system becomes unresponsive for an extended period or reports a parsing failure after uploading a large clinical guideline PDF. This may occur if the `Parsing Timeout` is set too short, insufficient to process complex charts and formats within the file.
- The same article appears multiple times in the knowledge base, or old articles are not correctly replaced after a version update. This indicates that the document parsing and chunking process did not effectively identify and handle duplicate content, or the version management mechanism was not fully utilized.

## How to Verify Configuration
- Randomly select different types of metabolic and endocrine documents. After uploading and parsing, examine the generated knowledge chunks in the knowledge base. Confirm that each chunk contains complete regulatory articles, SOP steps, or clinical recommendations.
- Check the content of the knowledge chunks, particularly expressions involving numerical values and units (e.g., `insulin 0.5 U/kg`, `blood glucose < 7.8 mmol/L`). Confirm they are retained as a single entity and not incorrectly truncated.
- For documents with version updates, upload both old and new versions. Use the knowledge base query function to confirm that core revisions in the new version are accurately recalled, and that obsolete articles from the old version no longer appear or are clearly marked.
