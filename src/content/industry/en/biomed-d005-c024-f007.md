# Workflow Orchestration for Health Management Regulations

## Data Characteristics for this Category

Health management regulations and SOP documents typically originate from internal institutional rules, operational guidelines, and job descriptions within medical organizations. These documents are usually stored as PDFs, Word files, or plain text. They have a relatively low update frequency, generally revised quarterly or annually, with ad-hoc updates for policy changes or major events. Document structures commonly include a table of contents, chapter headings, main body, and appendices, with clear hierarchies. Common fields include regulation numbers, publication dates, revision version numbers, scope of application, responsible departments, operational steps, and risk advisories. Specific operational steps often involve medical terminology, drug names, examination item codes, units of measurement (e.g., `mg`, `ml`, `mmol/L`), and specific process node identifiers.

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on "Workflow Orchestration"

The low update frequency of health management regulation documents means knowledge base index rebuilding does not need to be frequent; a longer cache period can be set. The structured nature of documents necessitates prioritizing the identification and extraction of titles and chapters during content parsing to support more precise recall. The presence of specialized medical terminology and units of measurement requires selecting embedding models capable of effectively processing such vocabulary to avoid inaccurate answers due due to semantic misunderstandings. Furthermore, common procedural descriptions, such as "first perform A, then proceed with B," require the workflow to have sequential processing capabilities to correctly parse and respond to user inquiries about operational steps. Metadata like regulation numbers and publication dates should be retained as additional information during knowledge chunking to provide context or enable filtering in Q&A.

## Configuration Guidelines

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
| :----------------- | :---------------- | :----------------------------------------- |
| `Chunk size` (Chunk Length) | `500-800 characters` | Balances semantic completeness and recall efficiency, preventing chunks from being too long or too short. |
| `Recall count` (Recall Count) | `Top 8` | Covers more potentially relevant content, reducing the risk of missed recall. |
| `Similarity threshold` (Similarity Threshold) | `0.75-0.85` | Improves result relevance, reducing interference from irrelevant information. |
| `maxContext` | `4096` | Ensures context information is not truncated during Q&A involving large regulations. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `300 seconds` | Accommodates parsing time for large PDF or Word documents. |
| `Rerank result count` (Reranked Return Count) | `Top 3` | Further refines results, prioritizing the most relevant content. |

## Three Common Mistakes

*   **Symptom:** The system prompts "required variable missing" or "global variable undefined." **Reason:** When switching between different APPs in the workflow, global variable configurations from the previous APP were not correctly passed or cleared, leading to validation failures in subsequent APPs.
*   **Symptom:** When users ask about specific operational procedures, the model's answers are generic or omit critical steps. **Reason:** Knowledge chunking failed to effectively identify process nodes and sequential relationships in the document, resulting in retrieved text segments lacking a complete operational chain.
*   **Symptom:** After saving workflow configurations, refreshing the page shows changes are not applied. **Reason:** This may be due to front-end caching issues or the back-end service failing to persist configurations in time, leading to data loss.

## How to Confirm Correct Configuration

*   Select core clauses from health management regulations. Simulate user questions and check if answers are accurate, complete, and include key metadata such as regulation numbers and responsible departments.
*   For complex operational procedures, construct multi-turn conversations. Verify if the workflow correctly understands and outputs operational steps in sequence. Check if the answers mention units of measurement and medical terminology.
*   After modifying workflow configurations, immediately refresh the page and test to confirm that changes have been persisted and applied.
