# Workflow Orchestration for Cold Chain Logistics Regulations

## Data Characteristics

Biopharmaceutical cold chain logistics regulations and SOP documents typically exist as PDFs, Word files, or scanned images. Content covers temperature control, humidity monitoring, emergency plans, equipment maintenance, and qualification certifications. Data sources include internal quality management systems, compliance departments, and technical manuals from equipment suppliers. Document updates are relatively stable, usually annual revisions or updates due to significant changes in regulations or technical standards. Document structures are rigorous, containing specialized terminology, charts, and flowcharts. Common fields include drug batch numbers, serial numbers, temperature and humidity records, alarm thresholds, and calibration dates. Units strictly follow international standards, such as Celsius (°C), relative humidity (%RH), and milliliters (mL).

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on Workflow Orchestration

The rigor and specialization of cold chain logistics documents require high accuracy and strong semantic understanding in information extraction and Q&A workflows. Flowcharts and tables in documents mean traditional text segmentation may not capture complete information, necessitating more refined document parsing strategies. Stable update frequency provides clear periodic basis for knowledge base synchronization and incremental updates, reducing the complexity of real-time synchronization. Extensive specialized terminology and standard units demand comprehensive vocabulary coverage and unit recognition from the model to avoid misinterpretations. Furthermore, compliance requirements mean workflows must ensure answer accuracy and traceability when processing user queries, avoiding vague or non-compliant responses.

## Configuration Guidelines

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
| :----- | :------- | :----------- |
| `Chunk Length` | `500–800 characters` | Regulatory document paragraphs are long and contain complete logic; this prevents semantic fragmentation. |
| `Recall Count` | `Top 8` | Ensures coverage of multi-dimensional relevant clauses in regulations, improving answer comprehensiveness. |
| `Similarity Threshold` | `0.78–0.85` | Cold chain regulations are highly specialized, requiring a higher threshold for precise matching of recalled content. |
| `Rerank Return Count` | `Top 3` | After high recall, reranking focuses on the most relevant content. |
| `maxContext` | `3000 Tokens` | Provides sufficient context for the model to reason when handling complex regulatory queries. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `600 seconds` | Regulatory documents may contain many charts and complex formats, requiring longer parsing times. |

## Common Pitfalls

*   Workflow execution stops midway; the AI directly provides a response, but subsequent processes do not trigger. This may be due to incorrect conditional branch logic or the model generating intermediate results in an unexpected format, preventing subsequent nodes from recognizing them.
*   The workflow fails to operate correctly after deployment to a Feishu bot. This could be due to insufficient API permissions configured for the Feishu bot or network access restrictions in the Feishu environment for external service interfaces called by the workflow.
*   The workflow fails to correctly identify temperature and humidity units in regulatory documents, leading to inaccurate Q&A results. This occurs if the knowledge base did not standardize units during embedding or if the model was not sufficiently trained in the specialized domain, leading to inaccurate understanding of unit terminology.

## Verification Steps

*   Submit queries containing core cold chain logistics terminology. Check if the returned results accurately cite relevant clauses from regulatory documents and verify citation locations.
*   Query parts of regulatory documents containing tables or flowcharts. Verify if the workflow can correctly parse and extract key information.
*   Simulate abnormal situations (e.g., temperature excursions). Verify if the workflow can provide correct handling procedures and responsible departments based on emergency plan clauses in the regulations.
*   Test with different versions of regulatory documents. Confirm that after knowledge base updates, the workflow correctly responds to the latest content and no longer references outdated content.
