# Tool Calling and Plugins for Molecular Diagnostics Regulations

## Data Characteristics in This Category
Molecular diagnostics regulations and Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) documents typically exist as PDFs, Word files, or scanned images. Data sources include guidelines from regulatory bodies (e.g., NMPA, FDA), industry association standards, internal corporate quality management system documents, and clinical laboratory operating manuals. Document update frequencies vary; regulatory documents might be revised annually, while internal SOPs are updated periodically based on technological advancements or process optimizations. Documents feature a rigorous structure, including numerous chapter headings, numbering, figures, appendices, and cross-references. The text is highly specialized, covering medical terminology, biochemical reactions, instrument parameters, reagent lot numbers, quality control indicators, calculation formulas, and data recording formats. Fields and units are precisely standardized, for example, reagent concentration in `mol/L`, detection time in `min`, temperature in `℃`, and various biological indicator units.

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on Tool Calling and Plugins
The rigorous structure and specialized terminology of molecular diagnostics documents require precise contextual understanding during tool calls to avoid errors from ambiguity. The presence of cross-references and appendices means simple text matching is insufficient; more complex semantic understanding and knowledge graph construction capabilities are necessary. High update frequencies for regulatory documents and internal SOPs challenge knowledge base update mechanisms, requiring rapid ingestion of new versions and automatic identification of changes. The abundance of specialized fields and units mandates accurate parameter passing and unit consistency when executing calculation or data query tools. Failure to do so can lead to invalid or incorrect results. For instance, a query about reagent preparation steps requires accurate extraction of reagent name, concentration, and volume, then passing this information to a calculation plugin. For scanned documents, OCR accuracy directly impacts subsequent text processing and tool calling effectiveness.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
| :----- | :------- | :----------- |
| `Chunk Length` | `500–800 characters` | Balances semantic completeness with single retrieval efficiency, preventing context loss or fragmentation from chunks that are too long or too short. |
| `Similarity Threshold` | `0.78–0.85` | Ensures precision of recalled content, filtering irrelevant regulatory clauses. This range effectively identifies highly relevant specialized text. |
| `Rerank Top N` | `Top 5` | Reduces the number of tokens processed by the model while maintaining retrieval quality, thereby improving response speed. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `600 seconds` | Accommodates molecular diagnostics documents that may contain numerous figures or complex layouts, leading to longer file parsing times. |
| `UPLOAD_FILE_MAX_SIZE` | `500 MB` | Accounts for large SOPs or compliance manuals that may contain extensive content and attachments, ensuring unhindered file uploads. |
| `deepseek-r1` `max_tokens` | `1024` | Adapts to the need for detailed explanations and citations in molecular diagnostics answers, ensuring completeness of responses. |

## Three Common Pitfalls
*   Tool call results in a workflow are empty. The model responds with "I cannot perform this action" or "No relevant information found." This typically indicates incorrect plugin parameter binding, such as binding the expected `reagent name` field to `product number`, preventing the tool from recognizing valid input.
*   After uploading a large regulatory document, logs show `slow operation xxxxms` and file parsing fails. This occurs when `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` is set too short. For complex documents, parsing time exceeds the preset threshold and is forcibly interrupted.
*   AI answers involving calculation results are inaccurate, for example, incorrect units or numerical deviations. This often happens when the tool plugin does not normalize molecular diagnostics-specific units (e.g., `nM`, `μL`) during input parameter processing, leading to errors in the internal calculation logic.

## How to Confirm Correct Configuration
*   Upload and parse a molecular diagnostics SOP document containing complex tables and cross-references. Verify that the knowledge base correctly extracts all sections and key information.
*   Ask a regulatory question involving calculation or querying, such as "How to calculate the final concentration of primers in a PCR reaction?" Observe whether the AI successfully calls the pre-configured calculation plugin and returns a numerical result with correct units.
*   Simulate a regulation update by uploading a new version of an SOP. Ask about a modified clause to verify that the AI can cite the latest content after the knowledge base update.
*   Check logs to confirm no `deepseek-r1` `max_tokens` overflow or `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` timeout errors occur during peak periods or when processing complex queries.
