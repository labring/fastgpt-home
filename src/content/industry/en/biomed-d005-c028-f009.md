# Referencing and Tracing for Molecular Diagnostics Regulations

## Data Characteristics

Molecular diagnostics regulations and SOP documents primarily originate from quality management system files of medical device manufacturers, technical guidance principles from national and international regulatory bodies, industry standards, and internal clinical laboratory procedures. These documents are typically in PDF, Word, or internal knowledge management system page formats. Update frequency varies: national regulations and industry standards may update annually or every few years. Internal SOPs are revised irregularly based on production process changes, technology upgrades, or quality audit requirements, usually with minor quarterly or semi-annual revisions and major annual updates. Document structure for normative files generally includes chapter numbering, detailed clauses, and appendices, containing numerous definitions, operating procedures, charts, parameter ranges, and quality control standards. Fields include test item names, reagent lot numbers, instrument models, operators, test result units (e.g., `copies/mL`, `CT value`), and judgment criteria.

## Constraints on Referencing and Tracing

The normative and structured nature of molecular diagnostics documents demands high precision in reference sources and reliability in tracing. Chapter numbering and detailed clauses mean retrieval results must match semantics and pinpoint specific clause locations to meet compliance review requirements. High update frequency for internal SOPs requires the knowledge base to quickly synchronize with the latest versions and effectively manage differences between old and new versions. Professional terms, units, and parameter ranges in documents mean text segmentation must avoid breaking critical information, such as separating `PCR reaction temperature` from `95℃`. Additionally, extensive charts and tables, if not effectively extracted and indexed, lead to missing references. Strict requirements for test result units mean the system must accurately identify and associate units with values.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item | Suggested Value | Rationale |
| :----------------- | :-------------- | :-------- |
| `Chunk size` (Segment Length) | `500–800 characters` | Balances paragraph integrity and retrieval efficiency, preventing the splitting of key operating steps or judgment criteria. |
| `Recall count` (Recall Count) | `8–12 entries` | Increases coverage to ensure recall of context related to various regulatory clauses, such as `GB/T 29791.1`. |
| `Similarity threshold` (Similarity Threshold) | Calibrate based on actual measurements | Ensures relevance while avoiding excessive filtering of potentially related regulatory clauses. |
| `Rerank result count` (Rerank Return Count) | `3–5 entries` | Selects the most relevant original regulatory snippets, improving answer precision and reducing redundant information. |
| `maxContext` | `3000–4000 tokens` | Provides sufficient contextual information for the model to understand complex regulatory logic and related clauses. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `600 seconds` | Handles parsing time for large SOPs and technical guidance documents in molecular diagnostics, ensuring complete file processing. |

## Common Pitfalls

*   Answers lack specific clause numbers or chapter paths, making tracing difficult and failing to meet compliance requirements. This occurs when knowledge segmentation does not retain original document structure or the retrieval model fails to link answers to precise document locations.
*   After a knowledge base update, the model still references outdated regulatory content, leading to obsolete or incorrect answers. This happens when new and old document versions are not effectively managed, old version indexes are not removed promptly, or new version indexes are not fully activated.
*   When querying specific test parameters or units, answers show values detached from units or incorrect units. This occurs when text segmentation strategies fail to identify and protect the semantic integrity of professional terms, values, and units.

## Verification Steps

*   Ask a series of typical compliance questions. Check if answers include specific regulatory clauses, chapter numbers, or paragraph citations, and compare them with original documents.
*   Simulate a regulation update scenario. After uploading a new SOP version, verify if the model prioritizes the new version and accurately identifies key differences between old and new versions.
*   Construct queries containing professional terms, values, and units, such as `linear range of HBV DNA quantitative detection`. Verify if the association between values and units in the answer is accurate.
*   Check system logs for `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` timeout errors during file parsing to ensure all regulatory documents are processed completely.

The values given are common starting points and should be measured against the reader's own samples.
