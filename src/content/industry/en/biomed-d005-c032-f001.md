# HTTP Interface and External Systems for Medical Insurance Settlement Regulations

## Data Characteristics for this Category
Medical insurance settlement regulation data typically originates from official documents published by national, provincial, or municipal medical insurance administrations. These documents include policy regulations, operational guidelines, coding standards (e.g., ICD-10 for disease diagnosis, medical service item codes), and reimbursement catalogs. Data update frequencies vary. Policy regulations may change annually or quarterly, while coding standards and reimbursement catalogs update more frequently, possibly monthly or bi-monthly. Documents are often in PDF, Word, or Excel formats. Some institutions also provide XML or JSON interfaces. Fields and units require high standardization. For example, monetary amounts must be precise to the cent, drug dosage units must conform to national drug standards, and diagnosis and surgical codes must align with medical insurance information systems.

## Constraints Imposed by these Characteristics on "HTTP Interface and External Systems"
The authoritative nature of medical insurance settlement data sources means interfaces must prioritize official publication channels to ensure data accuracy. The uncertain update frequency requires interfaces to support scheduled retrieval and incremental updates, preventing outdated Q&A results due to policy changes. Diverse document structures mean the data parsing module must support multiple file formats, especially structured table extraction from PDFs and Excel files. Strict field and unit specifications, particularly for coding systems, demand rigorous format validation during data transmission and verification to ensure data interoperability with medical insurance information systems. For instance, when medical insurance settlement Q&A involves cost calculations, precise handling of monetary fields is critical; any decimal error can lead to settlement inaccuracies.

## Configuration Guidelines

| Configuration Item | Suggested Value | Rationale |
| :----- | :------- | :----------- |
| `HTTP_REQUEST_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `60 seconds` | Many medical insurance interfaces have longer response times; this provides ample time to avoid timeouts. |
| `MAX_FILE_SIZE_MB` | `100 MB` | Medical insurance policy documents, especially PDFs with extensive tables, can be large. |
| `TEXT_SPLIT_CHUNK_SIZE` | `800–1200 characters` | Ensures each text chunk contains sufficient context without becoming semantically dispersed, suitable for legal text analysis. |
| `RECALL_TOP_K` | `5` | Medical insurance policy articles are highly interconnected; recalling more relevant regulations improves accuracy. |
| `SIMILARITY_THRESHOLD` | `0.75` | Medical insurance policy Q&A demands high precision; increasing the similarity threshold filters irrelevant content. |
| `HTTP_AUTH_HEADER` | `Bearer Token` or `Basic Auth` | Ensures interface call security, complying with medical insurance data access regulations. |

## Three Common Pitfalls
* Symptom: Calling an external medical insurance interface returns status code 401 or 403, indicating "authentication failed" or "unauthorized access." Cause: The authentication credential configured in `HTTP_AUTH_HEADER` is expired, incorrect, or not transmitted properly.
* Symptom: The Q&A system cannot provide correct answers for the latest medical insurance policy changes, still referencing old regulations. Cause: The data source interface lacks a scheduled retrieval task, or the incremental update mechanism is ineffective, preventing the local knowledge base from synchronizing with official latest data.
* Symptom: In Q&A involving cost calculations, the returned monetary amounts show decimal discrepancies or unit errors. Cause: The cost field returned by the external system did not undergo strict unit conversion or precision handling, for example, misinterpreting "yuan" as "fen," or improper numeric type conversion.

## How to Verify Correct Configuration
* Select a recently published medical insurance policy document, retrieve its content via the HTTP interface, and check for completeness and correct encoding.
* For a specific disease or drug in the medical insurance reimbursement catalog, query the relevant reimbursement ratio and verify the returned result against official regulations.
* Simulate a complex medical insurance settlement scenario involving multiple medical services and drug costs, then check if the Q&A system accurately calculates individual and medical insurance payment amounts.
