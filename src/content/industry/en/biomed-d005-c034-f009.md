# Reference Source and Traceability for Pharmaceutical E-commerce Regulations

## Data Characteristics in this Category
Regulatory and SOP data in pharmaceutical e-commerce primarily originates from official documents issued by the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA), local drug regulatory departments, and internal corporate guidelines. These guidelines cover drug procurement, sales, warehousing, distribution, returns, and exchanges. Documents are typically in PDF, Word, or scanned image formats. Their content is rigorously structured, containing extensive legal provisions, operational procedures, quality standards, and responsibility assignments. Updates occur quarterly or annually, driven by policy adjustments and internal management optimizations. Urgent revisions may happen during significant policy changes. Common fields include regulation numbers, issue dates, effective dates, scope, specific clauses, operational steps, risk control points, and responsible departments. Some documents include specific business data like drug batch numbers, expiration dates, and storage conditions, with units often being dates, batches, temperatures, and humidity.

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on "Reference Source and Traceability"
The authoritative and rigorous nature of pharmaceutical e-commerce regulatory documents demands precise reference sourcing, allowing direct traceability to original clauses. Documents contain numerous legal provisions and specific operational steps, requiring knowledge base segmentation to maintain semantic integrity and prevent misinterpretation due to truncated content. Regulatory document update frequencies are relatively fixed, but their broad impact necessitates version management capabilities in the knowledge base to ensure the latest valid version is referenced. Furthermore, fields like drug batch numbers and expiration dates have strong time sensitivity. The system must identify and prompt the timeliness of such information when referencing to avoid providing outdated or inapplicable guidance. Precise referencing of specific clauses also requires recall results to clearly display the original context and provide links or page numbers to the original documents, meeting compliance review requirements.

## Configuration Guidelines

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale for Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| `Chunk Length` | `500–800 characters` | Ensures semantic integrity of regulatory clauses and operational steps, preventing critical information from being cut off. |
| `Recall Count` | `Top 5–8` | Pharmaceutical e-commerce regulatory Q&A demands high accuracy, requiring more relevant context for judgment. |
| `Similarity Threshold` | `0.78–0.85` | Improves recall precision, reduces interference from irrelevant clauses, and ensures high relevance of referenced content. |
| `Rerank Return Count` | `Top 3` | Prioritizes the most relevant content through reranking while maintaining a sufficient recall quantity. |
| `maxContext` | `3000–4000 characters` | Provides sufficient context for complex regulatory provisions and detailed operational procedure Q&A. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `600 seconds` | Pharmaceutical e-commerce documents may contain numerous charts and complex layouts, leading to longer parsing times. |

## Three Common Pitfalls
*   Missing or incorrect reference sources: Document parsing fails to correctly extract source file paths or page numbers, breaking the traceability chain.
*   Quoted clauses in the answer do not fully match the user's question: Knowledge base segmentation is too short or recall count is insufficient, preventing the model from obtaining adequate context and leading to the citation of related but imprecise clauses.
*   Q&A results still reference old content after knowledge base document updates: Lack of an effective version management mechanism or update trigger mechanism prevents the system from loading the latest regulatory documents in a timely manner.

## How to Verify Correct Configuration
*   For typical regulatory Q&A, check if the referenced sources in the answer accurately link to specific sections or page numbers in the original document.
*   After uploading a new version of a regulatory document, verify that the system prioritizes referencing the new version and can distinguish differences between versions.
*   Test queries containing key numbers, dates, or specific drug names to ensure these fields in the referenced content match the original text.
