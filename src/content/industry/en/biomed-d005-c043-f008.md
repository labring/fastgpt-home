# Tool Calling and Plugins for Gene Therapy AAV Regulations

## Data Characteristics

Gene therapy AAV (adeno-associated virus) regulations and SOP documents typically exist as PDFs, Word files, or internal knowledge base pages. Data sources include guidelines from regulatory bodies like the NMPA, FDA, and EMA, technical standards from industry associations, and internal quality management system documents. Document update frequencies vary. Regulatory guidelines may be revised annually or supplemented with new discoveries. Internal SOPs may be updated quarterly due to technological iterations or production process optimizations.

Document structures are rigorous, containing chapter titles, numbers, main text, appendices, figures, and tables. Common fields include `Document Number`, `Version Number`, `Release Date`, `Effective Date`, `Revision History`, `Approver`, `Scope`, `Operating Procedures`, `Risk Assessment`, `Quality Standards`, and `Bill of Materials`. Units involve time (e.g., `hours`, `days`), temperature (e.g., `℃`), concentration (e.g., `g/L`, `IU/mL`), and pressure (e.g., `psi`). Precision requirements are high.

## Constraints on Tool Calling and Plugins

The data characteristics of gene therapy AAV regulatory documents impose specific requirements on tool calling and plugin capabilities.

First, diverse document sources and complex formats (PDF, Word, internal systems) demand robust multi-format file parsing from plugins. Accurate extraction of structured information, such as tables and numbered steps, is crucial.

Second, strict version control and effective date fields require the ability to retrieve specific document versions based on timestamps during Q&A. This ensures answer timeliness and compliance.

Documents contain extensive specialized terminology, abbreviations, and specific units. Tool calling must integrate with specialized dictionaries or unit conversion plugins to prevent semantic misunderstandings.

Furthermore, operating procedures and risk assessments often appear as flowcharts or nested lists. This challenges text block segmentation and semantic relevance recall, necessitating more refined text processing strategies and graph-based plugin support. Queries for specific parameters, such as `AAV titer` or `vector purity`, may require calling external databases or calculator plugins for real-time computation or comparison.

## Common Pitfalls

*   External database plugin calls return garbled or empty Chinese content. The database connection string's `characterEncoding` parameter is not set to `utf8mb4`.
*   HTTP module calls to internal LIMS or ELN systems for AAV batch information return `HTTP 401 Unauthorized`. The `Bearer Token` in the `Authorization` request header has expired or is not passed correctly.
*   Table content in specific sections of SOP documents is not correctly extracted as structured data during parsing. The file parsing plugin's `table_extraction_mode` parameter is not set to `accurate`, or OCR enhancement is not integrated.

## Validation Steps

*   For key regulatory documents, simulate questions about specific operating procedures, quality control standards, or bills of materials. Verify that the returned answers exactly match the original content and include correct parameters and units.
*   Test whether tool calling successfully connects to and queries external regulatory databases. Validate the ability to accurately retrieve the latest AAV-related regulations or guidelines via `Document Number` or `Release Date`.
*   Execute queries involving complex calculations or logical judgments, such as "calculate consumable requirements for a batch yield of X." Check if the `USE_CODE_INTERPRETER` results meet expectations and if the dependent internal calculator plugin is correctly invoked.
*   Verify that when questions involve specific regulation versions, such as "FDA 2022 AAV manufacturing guidelines on purity," the system accurately identifies and references the content of the corresponding document version.
