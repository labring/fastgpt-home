# Imaging Equipment Regulations: HTTP Interface and External Systems

## Data Characteristics

Imaging equipment, a critical component of medical devices, relies on highly specialized and rigorous regulatory and Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) documents. These documents typically exist as PDFs, Word files, or scanned images. Content covers the entire lifecycle, including procurement, installation, calibration, operation, maintenance, troubleshooting, and disposal. Data sources primarily include manufacturer manuals, internal hospital quality management system files, and regulatory guidelines. Update frequency is relatively low, usually occurring with new equipment models, regulatory changes, or internal process optimizations. Document structure is strict, often using chapters, clauses, and appendices. Fields and units include numerous professional terms, technical parameters (e.g., kVp, mA, exposure time, Dose Area Product (DAP)), equipment serial numbers, calibration dates, and maintenance cycles. Units are precise, down to milliseconds, millimeters, and Sieverts.

## Constraints on HTTP Interfaces and External Systems

The specialized and rigorous nature of imaging equipment documents imposes specific requirements on HTTP interfaces and external system integration. Documents are largely unstructured, necessitating efficient document parsing capabilities, especially for text extraction and layout reconstruction from PDFs and scanned images. Low update frequency means initial data synchronization can be a full import. Subsequent updates require incremental updates and version management to avoid reprocessing unchanged data. Strict document structure demands that the RAG system identify and preserve logical associations during chunking. For example, an operating step and its corresponding safety precautions should remain together. High density of professional terms and technical parameters requires the semantic understanding model to accurately identify these entities and establish contextual relationships, preventing inaccuracies due to misidentified jargon. Accurate unit identification and conversion are also crucial. For instance, when querying dosage information, the system must distinguish between different dosage units and provide precise values.

## Common Pitfalls

*   HTTP request returns `Failed to fetch`. This may indicate an incorrect external system API address or network connectivity issues.
*   JSON fields are empty or missing after calling an external system API. This usually means API request parameters do not meet the external system's requirements.
*   RAG Q&A results show errors in professional term recognition or unit confusion. This may be due to a knowledge base chunking strategy that fails to effectively preserve the context of specialized vocabulary.
