# Multiturn Conversation and Prompts for Imaging Equipment Regulations

## Data Characteristics for This Category

Imaging equipment, such as CT, MRI, and X-ray machines, typically has regulations and Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) documents in PDF, Word, or internal web page formats. These documents are highly structured, containing extensive technical terminology, diagrams, flowcharts, and operational steps. Data update frequency is relatively low, primarily occurring during equipment upgrades, regulatory changes, or clinical practice optimizations. Document content usually covers equipment parameters, operating procedures, troubleshooting, maintenance, and safety specifications. Fields include equipment model, serial number, operating mode, radiation dose, and examination area. Units involve physical quantities (e.g., kV, mA, ms, mm), time, and frequency.

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on "Multiturn Conversation and Prompts"

The specialized and structured nature of imaging equipment regulation documents requires multiturn conversation systems to accurately identify technical terms and contextual relationships when understanding user intent. Due to the low document update frequency, the knowledge base update cycle can be relatively flexible, but each update must ensure content completeness and accuracy. Complex flowcharts and operational steps in the documents necessitate prompt design that guides the model to decompose steps and perform logical reasoning, avoiding simple enumeration of the original text. Additionally, fields involving physical quantities and units require the model to correctly cite and perform necessary unit conversions or explanations in its responses, for example, by emphasizing sensitivity to radiation dosage in prompts. In multiturn conversations, users may frequently inquire about operational details or specific parameters, requiring the system to retain and associate historical conversation context.

## Three Common Mistakes

*   Conversation flow breaks at critical points, manifesting as the system returning generic error messages or no response. This occurs because workflow design lacks exception handling branches for specific equipment parameters or operational steps.
*   Model responses contain parameter unit confusion or numerical errors. This happens because prompts do not explicitly require the model to verify and cite exact values and units from documents, or because knowledge base segmentation fails to effectively distinguish between values and units.
*   When users ask for details of specific operational steps, the model repeatedly provides general overviews of regulations. This is due to prompts not sufficiently guiding the model to perform deep reasoning and step decomposition, or multiturn conversation context management failing to effectively link previous questions.

## How to Confirm Proper Configuration

*   Select imaging equipment SOP documents containing complex operational procedures and multiple parameters. Conduct multiturn questioning to check if the model accurately understands and provides step-by-step guidance.
*   For fields in documents containing specific values and units (e.g., radiation dose, exposure time), ask questions and verify the accuracy of values and units in the model's responses, ensuring consistency with the original document.
*   Simulate a user repeatedly asking for details of an equipment troubleshooting scenario within a single conversation. Observe if the model maintains contextual coherence throughout the conversation history and progressively provides more in-depth solutions.
