# Workflow Orchestration for Imaging Equipment Procedures

## Data Characteristics for This Category

Imaging equipment, specifically medical imaging devices like CT, MRI, and ultrasound scanners, generates specific data. Related procedures and SOP documents are typically in PDF, Word, or scanned image formats. These documents cover equipment operation protocols, maintenance manuals, troubleshooting guides, quality control standards, and safety management regulations. Data sources include official manuals from equipment manufacturers, internal hospital guidelines, and compliance requirements from regulatory bodies.

Update frequency is relatively low, usually annually or biennially, tied to equipment model upgrades, regulatory changes, or internal hospital management process adjustments. Document structure is rigorous, often using chapters and numbered clauses, and includes extensive technical terminology, diagrams, and tables. Fields include equipment model, serial number, operating steps, parameter ranges (e.g., kVp, mA, exposure time), calibration cycles, and safety precautions. Units cover physical quantities (e.g., kV, mA, ms, Hz) and time periods.

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on Workflow Orchestration

The low update frequency of imaging equipment procedure data means less pressure for incremental updates after initial knowledge base construction. The focus is on the accuracy and completeness of the first import.

The rigorous chapter structure and specialized terminology in the documents require text segmentation in the workflow to maintain semantic integrity, preventing the splitting of key operational steps or parameter descriptions.

The presence of PDF and scanned image formats demands high accuracy from Optical Character Recognition (OCR). OCR quality directly impacts the effectiveness of subsequent text embedding.

Extensive diagrams and tables may require multimodal processing capabilities in the workflow, or structured information extraction after text retrieval.

The inclusion of parameter ranges and physical units makes understanding and comparing numerical information a challenge in question-answering scenarios. The workflow needs to identify and process these specific data types to provide precise procedural compliance advice or troubleshooting guidance.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
| :----- | :------- | :----------- |
| `Chunk size` (Chunk Length) | `500–800 characters` | Ensures each knowledge chunk contains complete operational steps or clauses, maintaining semantic coherence. |
| `Recall count` (Recall Count) | `Top 5–8 entries` | Covers multiple relevant procedural regulations or operational guidelines, improving answer comprehensiveness. |
| `Similarity threshold` (Similarity Threshold) | `0.78–0.85` | Balances recall accuracy and relevance, avoiding interference from irrelevant procedures. |
| `Rerank result count` (Reranked Return Count) | `Top 3 entries` | Selects the most relevant procedural entries, reducing user effort in filtering. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `600 seconds` | Accommodates the time required for OCR processing of large PDFs or complex scanned documents, preventing timeouts. |
| `CHUNK_OVERLAP_SIZE` | `100–150 characters` | Ensures contextual continuity between adjacent paragraphs, handling technical terms explained across segments. |

## Common Pitfalls

*   Workflow execution timeouts or processing failures often occur when importing scanned equipment manuals. This is typically due to a `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` parameter set too low, which does not allocate enough time for OCR to process complex images.
*   After a user query, the answer may omit critical operating parameters, such as `kVp` or `mA` values. This often happens when text segmentation splits sentences containing numerical information, or low OCR recognition rates lead to the loss of these field values.
*   When multi-turn conversations are needed to clarify fault phenomena, the workflow may fail to guide the user to provide more information. This manifests as conversation interruptions or generic answers, caused by an insufficient `maxContext` parameter to retain complete user query history and system follow-up logic.

## Verification Steps

*   Import different types of imaging equipment procedure documents (PDF, Word, scanned) into the knowledge base. Check logs for `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` related errors and confirm that all page content is correctly extracted.
*   Query with typical questions about equipment operation, troubleshooting, and maintenance. Verify if the answers include key operational steps, parameter values, and precautions from the documents. Evaluate the combined effect of `Similarity threshold` (Similarity Threshold) and `Recall count` (Recall Count).
*   Simulate multi-turn conversations. For example, after asking about the equipment startup process, follow up with a question about detailed parameters for a specific step. Verify if the workflow maintains contextual coherence as expected. This relates to `CHUNK_OVERLAP_SIZE` and `maxContext`.
