# Imaging Equipment Regulations: Citation and Traceability

## Data Characteristics

Imaging equipment, such as CT scanners, MRIs, and ultrasound diagnostic devices, relies on regulatory documents. These documents originate from medical device registration regulations, national standards, industry norms, and manufacturer-provided operation manuals and maintenance guides. Documents are typically in PDF, Word, or scanned image formats. They feature a rigorous structure, specialized terminology, technical parameters, charts, and flowcharts. Update frequency is relatively low, primarily occurring during regulatory revisions, new equipment releases, or technology upgrades. Content covers equipment functions, performance indicators, operating procedures, maintenance, troubleshooting, and safety specifications. This includes strict regulations for specific parameters (e.g., radiation dose, magnetic field strength, probe frequency) and precise unit usage (e.g., Gy, Tesla, MHz).

## Constraints on Citation and Traceability

The specialized and rigorous nature of imaging equipment regulatory documents demands high accuracy and reliability from citation and traceability features. Complex charts and flowcharts in documents mean that plain text chunking can lose critical information, impacting semantic integrity. Citing sensitive parameters like radiation dose and magnetic field strength requires accurate sources to avoid misleading information. Lengthy operating procedures and maintenance guides mean a single chunk may not cover complete steps, requiring consideration of contextual relevance. Low update frequency reduces the need for frequent knowledge base retraining. However, each update requires strict version management and verification to prevent confusion with citations from older regulations.

## Common Pitfalls

*   Model responses lack any citation information, preventing users from verifying answer accuracy. This usually occurs when `Similarity Threshold` is set too high, causing recalled knowledge chunks to fail citation standards.
*   Cited chunk content in responses has low relevance to the user's query or includes irrelevant information. This may be due to improper `Chunk Size` settings or an excessive `Recall Count`, introducing low-relevance content.
*   When users query specific equipment parameters, the model's response provides inaccurate parameter values or units. This might be due to parsing errors in the original knowledge base documents or critical numerical information being truncated during chunking.
