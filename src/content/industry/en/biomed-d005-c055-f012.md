# Model Integration and Configuration for Imaging Equipment Regulations

## Data Characteristics

Imaging equipment regulation data primarily originates from healthcare institutions' quality management system documents, equipment operation manuals, maintenance records, regulatory compliance files, and internal training materials. These documents are typically in PDF, Word, or scanned image formats. Some new regulations may be published as web pages or database records. Data update frequencies vary; regulatory files might update annually, operation manuals with equipment model iterations, and internal SOPs based on actual operational adjustments. Document structures for SOPs usually include fixed fields like flowcharts, step descriptions, responsible parties, risk points, and emergency measures. Maintenance records involve equipment serial numbers, fault codes, repair dates, replaced component models, and engineer signatures. Common units include dose units (e.g., mGy·cm), time units (seconds, minutes), physical quantity units (kV, mA), and equipment counts (e.g., exposure times).

## Constraints on Model Integration and Configuration

The diversity and complexity of imaging equipment regulation data impose specific requirements on model integration and configuration. First, a large volume of unstructured documents (PDFs, scanned images) necessitates robust document parsing capabilities. This ensures accurate extraction of text content, especially key information within tables and diagrams. Second, irregular update frequencies require the knowledge base to support incremental updates and version management mechanisms. This prevents the model from misusing outdated regulatory information. The extensive use of specialized terminology and acronyms in documents, such as "DICOM," "PACS," and "CTDIvol," requires the model to accurately understand their meaning during vectorization. This may necessitate specific word embedding models or domain-specific dictionaries. Furthermore, regulation Q&A scenarios demand high accuracy and authority in answers. The model must precisely locate original sources and avoid generative hallucinations. This influences the choice of retrieval strategies and answer generation methods.

## Configuration Guidelines

| Configuration Item             | Recommended Value | Rationale                                                                                                 |
| :----------------------------- | :---------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `UPLOAD_FILE_MAX_SIZE`         | `500 MB`          | Large PDF documents can contain numerous images and charts.                                               |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`   | `600 seconds`          | Complex document parsing can be time-consuming; this prevents parsing timeouts.                           |
| `Chunk size` (Segment Length)    | `800-1200 characters`   | Ensures each segment contains a complete SOP step or clause.                                              |
| `Recall count` (Retrieval Count)   | `8-12 entries`         | Increases the retrieval scope, covering multiple relevant clauses potentially involved in regulation Q&A. |
| `Similarity threshold` (Similarity Threshold) | `Calibrate by actual measurement`      | Balances retrieval accuracy and quantity, avoiding the omission of critical information.                   |
| `Rerank result count` (Reranked Return Count) | `3-5 entries`          | Selects the most relevant few items for model inference, reducing model load.                             |

## Common Pitfalls

*   Model returns regulation clauses that do not match the query content. This often occurs when segment length is too short, splitting a single regulation clause and making it difficult for the model to understand its complete context.
*   Uploaded scanned PDF files are not recognized or parsed by the model, appearing as empty content or garbled text. This typically indicates that the document parser failed to correctly handle Optical Character Recognition (OCR) or encoding issues.
*   When users ask about the latest operating procedures for a specific equipment model, the model provides outdated information. This happens when the knowledge base is not updated promptly or version management mechanisms fail, leading the model to index obsolete documents.

## Verification of Configuration

*   Upload a batch of imaging equipment regulation files in various formats (PDF, Word, scanned images). Verify that the content extraction in the knowledge base is complete and free of garbled text for each document.
*   Query specific steps, responsible parties, or risk points within complex SOPs. Cross-reference whether the model's answers accurately correspond to the original text and if the cited original passages are correct.
*   Design queries containing specialized terminology and acronyms, such as asking for the meaning of "CTDIvol" or maintenance requirements for a "PACS" system. Evaluate the model's understanding of domain vocabulary and the professionalism of its responses.
*   Upload regulation files with both old and new versions. Test whether the model prioritizes answering with the latest version's content or can provide older version information when explicitly requested. This verifies the effectiveness of the version management mechanism.
