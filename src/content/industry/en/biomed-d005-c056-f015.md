# Cardiovascular Policy Deployment and Upgrade

## Data Characteristics in the Cardiovascular Domain

Cardiovascular policies and Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) documents originate from hospitals, pharmaceutical companies, medical device manufacturers, regulatory bodies, and professional associations. These documents are typically in PDF, Word, Excel, or structured database formats. Update frequency for core policies like clinical guidelines, drug instructions, and device operation procedures is usually 1–3 years. However, policy regulations or emergency SOPs might update within months. Document structures often include sections such as introduction, purpose, scope, definitions, responsibilities, operating procedures, precautions, references, and appendices. Fields and units involve dosage (mg, g, IU), time (min, h, d), biomarkers (mmol/L, ng/mL), blood pressure (mmHg), and heart rate (bpm), all requiring high precision and consistency.

## Constraints on Deployment and Upgrade from These Characteristics

The update frequency of cardiovascular policy documents, especially the regular revisions of core clinical guidelines and drug instructions, requires the deployment system to have efficient version management and incremental update capabilities. Extensive medical terminology, abbreviations, and numerical data in documents challenge the semantic understanding accuracy of text vectorization models. Models need to differentiate similar concepts and accurately capture numerical relationships. The complex structure and multi-format nature of documents necessitate support for various document types during file parsing. The system must effectively extract key information, such as step numbers in operating procedures or critical phrases in precautions. Strict compliance requirements, such as accuracy of drug dosages and contraindications, mean the deployed Q&A system must provide highly reliable and traceable answers, avoiding misleading information. These factors directly influence vector model selection, data preprocessing workflows, and knowledge base retrieval and reranking strategies.

## Configuration Strategy

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
| :----------------- | :---------------- | :--------------------------------------------- |
| `Chunk size` (Chunk Size) | `800–1200 characters` | Balances context completeness with vector model processing efficiency, preventing information fragmentation. |
| `Overlap Length` | `100 characters` | Ensures context continuity and reduces information loss due to chunking. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `600 seconds` | Handles parsing of large PDF or Word documents, preventing timeout errors. |
| `Recall count` (Recall Count) | `Top 8` | Covers more potentially relevant passages, improving answer comprehensiveness. |
| `Similarity threshold` (Similarity Threshold) | Calibrate based on actual measurements | Requires fine-tuning based on the specific vector model and corpus to balance recall and precision. |
| `maxContext` | `4000 tokens` | Supports complex queries by providing longer context. |

## Common Pitfalls

*   System errors occur when uploading large PDF documents, displaying "file parsing timeout." This usually happens when the `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` parameter is set too low, not allowing enough time for complex documents to parse.
*   Q&A results show incorrect information related to cardiovascular drug dosages or omit critical contraindication warnings. This often stems from the vector model's insufficient understanding of specialized terminology and numerical units, or knowledge base chunking strategies that fragment critical information.
*   After a system upgrade, some historical questions do not receive expected answers, or answer quality significantly declines. This might result from incompatibility between new vector models or knowledge base indexes and old data during the upgrade process, requiring data reprocessing and index rebuilding.
