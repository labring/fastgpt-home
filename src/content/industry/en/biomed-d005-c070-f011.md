# Document Parsing and Chunking for Regulatory Affairs

## Data Characteristics in This Category

Regulatory affairs documents in the biopharmaceutical sector originate primarily from official bodies like the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA), the European Medicines Agency (EMA), and the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA). These documents include regulations, guidelines, technical requirements, and review standards. They update frequently, especially with new drug development, technological advancements, or international regulatory harmonization. Documents are typically in PDF format, highly structured, and contain extensive terminology, tables, figures, and references. They feature clear hierarchies, such as "Measures for the Administration of Drug Registration," "Ethical Review Approval for Drug Clinical Trials," and "ICH E6(R2) Good Clinical Practice Guideline." Precision in fields and units is a core characteristic, including dosage units (mg/kg), time units (days, months, years), concentration units (g/L), and identifiers like batch numbers, expiration dates, and registration numbers.

## Constraints from These Characteristics on "Document Parsing and Chunking"

Regulatory affairs document characteristics impose specific requirements on document parsing and chunking. High update frequency necessitates efficient document update detection and incremental parsing mechanisms to maintain knowledge base timeliness. PDF complexity, especially with nested tables, multi-column layouts, and scanned images, challenges text extraction accuracy, potentially leading to information loss or misalignment. The strict structure and extensive specialized terminology require chunking to effectively identify logical units like chapters, paragraphs, and lists, avoiding semantic fragmentation. Field and unit precision determines the granularity of information after chunking; key data must remain intact within a single chunk to support accurate subsequent question answering. Timeout settings must account for the computational load of parsing large regulatory files to prevent parsing failures due to default timeouts.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `Chunk size` (Chunk Length) | `800–1200 characters` | Regulatory affairs documents have high semantic density. This length helps retain complete concepts and context while preventing individual chunks from becoming too large. |
| `Chunk Overlap Length` (Chunk Overlap Length) | `100–200 characters` | Ensures sufficient contextual overlap between adjacent chunks, improving recall, especially for cross-paragraph queries. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `600 seconds` | Parsing large regulatory files is time-consuming. Increase the timeout to prevent service interruptions due to complex PDF parsing. |
| `UPLOAD_FILE_MAX_SIZE` | `200 MB` | Regulatory affairs files may contain numerous charts and attachments. Appropriately increase the file upload limit to support large files. |
| `Text Chunking Strategy` | `By title, paragraph separation` | Follows the logical structure of regulatory documents, ensuring chunk integrity and avoiding disruption of tables or critical clauses. |
| `Recall count` (Recall Count) | `Top 5–8 items` | Ensures retrieval covers enough relevant regulatory clauses or guideline content, improving answer comprehensiveness. |

## Three Common Mistakes

*   Some data in uploaded Excel files cannot be retrieved or returns incomplete results. This occurs because the default parser inaccurately parses complex tables or Excel files with merged cells, leading to data loss in some rows or columns.
*   The system becomes unresponsive for an extended period or returns a `504 Gateway Timeout` error after uploading a large PDF document. This usually happens when the `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` parameter is set too low, failing to accommodate the time required to parse complex PDFs.
*   Question-answering results lack contextual information for key regulatory clauses. Answers appear fragmented because the `Chunk size` (Chunk Length) is set too small, splitting an originally complete regulatory clause into multiple discontinuous chunks.

## How to Confirm Correct Configuration

*   Upload a regulatory affairs PDF document containing complex tables and multi-level headings. Check if the parsed text content is complete and structurally clear, paying special attention to the retention of table data and hierarchical headings.
*   Upload a very large regulatory file (e.g., over 100MB). Observe if the parsing process completes within the `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` setting without timeout errors.
*   Perform a search for specific regulatory clauses or specialized terminology within the document. Check if the returned chunks completely present the semantic meaning of the clause and include sufficient contextual information.
*   Use an Excel file containing specific fields (e.g., registration number, batch number) for question answering. Verify if the system can accurately extract and utilize these fields for retrieval and information integration.
