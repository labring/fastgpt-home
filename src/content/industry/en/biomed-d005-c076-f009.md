# Medical Record Quality Control: Citation and Traceability

## Data Characteristics

Medical record quality control policies and SOP documents originate from internal regulations, operational guidelines, industry standards, and national health authority directives. These documents are typically in PDF, Word, or internal knowledge base formats. Updates are infrequent, usually quarterly or annually, with ad-hoc updates for major policy changes. Document structures are rigorous, containing numerous section titles, clause numbers, definitions, scopes, procedures, responsible parties, quality control standards, and corrective actions. Quality control standards may include specific medical indicators, timelines, and responsibility assignments. Fields are often structured or semi-structured, with units such as percentages, days, and numerical ranges.

## Constraints on Citation and Traceability

The rigorous nature and low update frequency of medical record quality control documents demand precise citations to specific clauses or paragraphs in the original text, ensuring accurate traceability. The presence of chapter titles and numbers in documents means that knowledge base chunking should prioritize semantic completeness, avoiding the splitting of critical clauses. Since documents include specific quality control standards and medical indicators, recall and citation must retain sufficient contextual information to support correct interpretation of values, units, and logical relationships. The authoritative and serious nature of the content requires clear identification of the original source in citations, such as file name, version number, and publication date, to enhance answer credibility.

## Configuration Parameters

| Configuration Item          | Recommended Value         | Rationale                                          |
| :-------------------------- | :------------------------ | :------------------------------------------------- |
| `Chunk size` (Chunk Size)     | `800–1200 characters`     | Ensures semantic completeness of individual QC clauses or steps |
| `Chunk Overlap Length` (Overlap Size) | `100 characters`          | Links adjacent clauses, preventing loss of critical information |
| `Recall count` (Recall Count)   | `Top 5–8`                 | Covers relevant policy clauses, improving recall accuracy |
| `Similarity threshold` (Similarity Threshold) | `0.75–0.85`               | Balances recall breadth and precision, avoiding irrelevant content |
| `Rerank result count` (Rerank Count) | `Top 3`                   | Focuses on the most relevant policy clauses, reducing model processing load |
| `Citation Display Format` | `File Name (Version Number)` (File Name (Version Number)) | Clearly identifies policy document source for manual verification and traceability |

## Common Pitfalls

*   Missing context in cited content, leading to unclear quality control standards or responsibility definitions. This happens due to excessively small knowledge base chunks or insufficient recall count.
*   The model cites outdated or superseded policy clauses. This occurs when the knowledge base is not updated promptly or version management mechanisms are ineffective.
*   Citation sources display vague file names or lack specific location information, increasing the difficulty of manual traceability. This happens when the `Citation Display Format` configuration does not include file version numbers or page/chapter information.
