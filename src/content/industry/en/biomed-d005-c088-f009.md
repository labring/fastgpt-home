# Autoimmune Disease Policy: Citation and Traceability

## Data Characteristics

Policy and SOP documents in the field of autoimmune diseases primarily originate from pharmaceutical companies' internal R&D, clinical trial, and Quality Management System (QMS) files, as well as regulatory guidelines issued by drug regulatory agencies worldwide. These documents typically exist as PDFs, Word documents, or rich text formats within internal knowledge management systems. They contain extensive medical terminology, abbreviations, and complex flowcharts.

Update frequency varies: R&D SOPs may be revised frequently as projects progress, while post-market quality management policies are relatively stable, usually reviewed and updated annually or biennially. Document structures commonly include numbered chapters and sections. Appendices often contain glossaries, reference lists, and historical revision records.

When clinical data is involved, fields and units may include dosage (mg/kg), time points (hours, days, weeks), and biomarker concentrations (ng/mL). This information is often embedded within tables or paragraph descriptions.

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on Citation and Traceability

The complexity of autoimmune disease documents requires citations to precisely point to specific locations within the original text, enabling engineers to quickly locate information. Diverse document formats and nested data mean that traditional text-segment-based retrieval may lose context or critical numerical values. For example, a specific reagent batch number and expiration date for a cytokine detection step in an SOP might be hidden within a table cell or presented in an image.

The high frequency of R&D SOP updates demands a robust index update mechanism for the RAG system to ensure citations always refer to the latest version. Furthermore, due to the large number of specialized terms and abbreviations, the system needs to identify these terms and link them to their first definition in the document or to explanations in an appendix, ensuring complete traceability. Precise citations enhance engineer trust and meet the high standards for information rigor in the biomedical field, preventing operational deviations or compliance risks due to inaccurate citations.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item     | Recommended Value | Rationale                                                                                             |
| :--------------------- | :---------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Chunk Size`           | `600–800 characters` | Balances complex process descriptions with contextual completeness, avoiding overly long or short chunks. |
| `Chunk Overlap Length` | `100 characters`  | Ensures contextual continuity between chunks, reducing information loss.                               |
| `Recall Count`         | `Top 5`           | Balances recall precision with computational resource consumption, covering core information.         |
| `Similarity Threshold` | Calibrate by measurement | Ensures highly relevant retrieved segments to the query, filtering noise.                             |
| `Rerank Return Count`  | `Top 3`           | Focuses on the most relevant citations, optimizing final presentation.                                |
| `File Parse Timeout`   | `600 seconds`     | Handles parsing large PDF files, preventing timeouts due to excessive file size.                      |

## Common Pitfalls

*   Knowledge base citations in answers are empty or incomplete. This may be due to a `Similarity Threshold` set too high, filtering out valid but slightly less relevant segments.
*   The system cannot provide precise batch numbers and expiration dates when answering questions about specific reagent batches. This usually occurs because the document parser fails to correctly identify and extract key fields from tables or nested text.
*   When a user queries the latest revision status of an SOP, the system provides citations pointing to an older version. This indicates that the knowledge base's index update mechanism did not synchronize with the latest uploaded document in a timely manner.

## How to Verify Correct Configuration

*   Select an autoimmune SOP document containing complex tables and specialized terminology. Ask questions about key parameters or processes within it, and verify that citations accurately point to specific table cells or paragraphs in the original text.
*   Upload different versions of the same SOP document. Ask questions about the document's revision history or latest content, and confirm that the system cites the corresponding content from the most recent version.
*   For a document containing many abbreviations, ask for the meaning of a specific abbreviation. Check if the citation links to the abbreviation's definition or glossary section within the document.
