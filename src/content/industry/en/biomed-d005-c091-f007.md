# Workflow Orchestration for Pharmacovigilance Regulations

## Data Characteristics

Pharmacovigilance (PV) regulation data originates from regulatory documents, guidelines, Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), Risk Management Plans (RMPs), and Adverse Drug Event (ADE) reporting process documents. These documents are typically in PDF format, but may also include Word or rich text files. Update frequency is relatively low, occurring mainly during regulatory revisions, new drug approvals, or the emergence of serious safety signals. Document structures usually include chapters, sub-sections, tables, and appendices. Content is precise and legally binding. Key fields include regulatory article numbers, SOP version numbers, revision dates, effective dates, specific operating procedures, responsible persons, review cycles, risk assessment criteria, and adverse event grading standards. Field content is mostly textual descriptions, with some dates, numbers, and enumerated values. Units in risk assessment may involve dosage and frequency.

## Constraints on Workflow Orchestration

The low update frequency of pharmacovigilance regulation documents means knowledge base index rebuilding or incremental updates do not need to be frequent, which reduces system resource consumption. The rigorous document structure, containing numerous regulatory articles and SOP steps, requires workflows to precisely match specific clauses or operational steps during knowledge retrieval, avoiding vague recalls. Fields like `SOP version number` and `effective date` are crucial for version control and timeliness. Workflows must identify and use this metadata for filtering and sorting to ensure the provision of the latest and valid regulatory information. The legal enforceability of regulatory documents demands highly accurate and traceable answers from the workflow. This requires clear citation of original sources in generated answers and strict semantic matching and fact-checking of retrieval results. Queries regarding adverse event grading standards may require the workflow to possess conditional judgment capabilities to match appropriate grading rules based on user-provided adverse event descriptions.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
| :----------------- | :---------------- | :-------- |
| `maxContext` | `1024` | Pharmacovigilance regulatory texts are often long, requiring a larger context window to understand complete semantics. |
| `Chunk size` | `500 characters` | Ensures each knowledge chunk contains sufficient information while avoiding excessive length that leads to imprecise recall. |
| `Recall count` | `8` | Increases coverage of relevant regulatory provisions, reducing the risk of missing important information. |
| `Similarity threshold` | `0.75` | Pharmacovigilance content is highly specialized, requiring high similarity to ensure retrieval accuracy. |
| `Rerank result count` | `3` | With a high number of recalled items, re-ranking selects the most relevant entries to improve answer quality. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `300 seconds` | Regulatory documents are often large, requiring longer parsing times to avoid timeouts. |

## Common Pitfalls

*   Knowledge base search results are empty or inaccurate. This may occur if document segmentation is too fine or the segmentation strategy is unreasonable, preventing individual segments from expressing complete semantics independently.
*   Workflows respond slowly or encounter connection errors during external calls, manifesting as `HTTP 504 Gateway Timeout`. This often indicates insufficient backend service concurrency to respond to requests in a timely manner.
*   Cited regulatory provisions in answers are outdated or inapplicable. This occurs when the knowledge base is not updated promptly, or when metadata such as `effective date` is not effectively used for filtering during retrieval.

*   Simulate user queries for multiple typical regulatory Q&A scenarios. Verify that the workflow's output accurately cites the latest regulatory provisions and check the cited `SOP Version Number`.
*   For inquiries involving specific operational steps, validate that the workflow's answer provides a clear, actionable list of steps and confirm consistency with the original text.
*   Simulate concurrent calls during peak hours. Monitor the workflow's response time and ensure `average response time` remains within acceptable limits, with no significant number of `HTTP 50x` errors.
