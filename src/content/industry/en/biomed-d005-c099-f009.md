# Remote Healthcare Policy Citation and Traceability

## Data Characteristics

Remote healthcare policy data primarily originates from regulations, guidelines, and technical specifications published by the National Health Commission and provincial health administrative departments. These documents are typically in PDF or DOCX format, highly structured with clear chapter headings, clause numbers, and appendices. Update frequency is relatively low, usually annual revisions or irregular updates based on policy adjustments. Document fields include, but are not limited to, issuing authority, publication date, effective date, document number, title, and main content. The main content may contain detailed provisions for specific medical practices, technical standards, service processes, and specific descriptions of medical personnel qualifications and equipment requirements.

## Constraints on Citation and Traceability

Remote healthcare policy documents are highly authoritative, requiring extreme accuracy in citations. Due to their legal and regulatory nature, any citation error can lead to severe compliance risks. The low update frequency means the knowledge base must pay close attention to version iterations during updates, ensuring the latest valid version is cited while retaining historical records of older versions. The structured nature of the documents facilitates precise matching through parsing chapters and clause numbers, improving recall accuracy. Specific behaviors and technical standards mentioned in the main text require citations to point precisely to relevant paragraphs, avoiding vague references. Furthermore, as these documents may contain extensive professional terminology and normative expressions, segment granularity must be appropriate for the model to understand the context and cite accurately.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
| :----- | :------- | :----------- |
| `Segment Length` | `500 characters` | Ensures each segment can contain a complete regulatory clause or sub-clause, aiding model context understanding while avoiding redundancy from excessive length. |
| `Segment Overlap Length` | `50 characters` | Maintains contextual continuity, reducing semantic loss due to segment truncation, especially relevant for logical connections between regulatory clauses. |
| `Recall Count` | `Top 5` | Considering the rigor of regulatory documents, recalling more highly relevant snippets increases the model's chance of finding precise citation sources. |
| `Similarity Threshold` | `0.75` | Improves the relevance of recall results, filtering out irrelevant regulatory content, and ensuring the authority and accuracy of citations. |
| `Rerank Return Count` | `Top 3` | After optimization by the reranking model, further filters for the most relevant few snippets, enhancing the precision of final citations. |
| `Citation ID Field` | `Document Number` | Uses the unique identifier of the regulatory document as the citation ID, allowing users to trace back to the specific original file. |

## Common Pitfalls

*   The model provides citations in its answer, but the cited content is irrelevant to the question. This often occurs when the `Similarity Threshold` is set too low, leading to the recall of many low-relevance text snippets.
*   The conversation interface's returned citation information lacks a `cite` ID. This might be because the knowledge base failed to correctly parse or store the `Document Number` field during import, or the correct citation ID field was not specified in the configuration.
*   When asked about policies not present in the knowledge base, a citation card is still returned. This indicates that the `Recall Count` might be set too high, and there is a lack of strict judgment on the relevance of recall results, leading the model to "force" citations.

## Validation Steps

*   Query core regulatory clauses and verify that the returned citation sources precisely point to the corresponding clause numbers in the original text.
*   Query recently updated policy documents and check that the citation source's version and publication date are the latest valid versions.
*   Input a remote healthcare question explicitly not present in the knowledge base. Verify that the model provides a "no relevant information found" prompt and does not return any citations.
