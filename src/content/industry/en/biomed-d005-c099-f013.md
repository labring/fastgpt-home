# Knowledge Base Retrieval and Recall for Telemedicine Regulations

## Data Characteristics

Telemedicine relies heavily on regulations and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). Data primarily originates from regulatory documents issued by national and local health authorities, alongside internal telemedicine guidelines, consultation procedures, and data security protocols from various medical institutions. These documents are typically updated infrequently, but revisions often involve extensive clause changes. Document formats are mainly PDF and Word, with rigorous content structures including numerous terminology definitions, operational steps, responsibility assignments, and legal provisions. Fields often cover medical institution qualifications, practitioner scope, patient informed consent, data transmission encryption standards, and emergency plans. Units may include time (minutes, hours), distance (kilometers), and cost (Yuan).

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on "Knowledge Base Retrieval and Recall"

The authoritative and rigorous nature of telemedicine regulatory documents demands highly accurate knowledge base retrieval results with an extremely low tolerance for error. Infrequent but impactful document updates necessitate robust incremental updates and version management for the knowledge base to ensure the latest effective version is retrieved. The highly structured content, with nested clauses and references, means simple keyword matching struggles to capture deep semantic relationships, requiring more refined text segmentation strategies. Furthermore, the specialized terminology and legal provisions challenge the semantic understanding capabilities of embedding models, potentially leading to skewed similarity calculations. The precision required for fields and units suggests that additional data extraction and validation steps might be needed after retrieval.

## Configuration Guidelines

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
| :----------------- | :---------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Chunk size` (Segment Length) | `300–500 characters` | Balances clause completeness with semantic density, preventing information overload in a single segment. |
| `Chunk Overlap Length` (Segment Overlap Length) | `50 characters` | Ensures contextual continuity across segments, capturing connecting words and phrases. |
| `Recall count` (Number of Retrieved Items) | `top 8–12 items` | Covers multiple interpretations of regulatory documents, providing more comprehensive references. |
| `Similarity threshold` (Similarity Threshold) | `0.75–0.85` | Prioritizes strongly relevant results, reducing interference from irrelevant clauses. |
| `Rerank result count` (Number of Reranked Items) | `top 3 items` | Refines the final output, ensuring the most core and relevant regulatory clauses are prioritized. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `600 seconds` | Accommodates parsing time for large PDF or Word documents, preventing timeout interruptions. |

## Three Common Pitfalls

*   Retrieval results include outdated or superseded regulatory clauses because the knowledge base was not updated in time or document effective dates were not correctly marked.
*   A user asks "approval time limit for remote consultation process," but the results fail to provide specific days. This might be due to overly coarse document segmentation, mixing time limit information with much irrelevant content, leading to insufficient semantic matching during retrieval.
*   Knowledge base training orders remain pending for extended periods, preventing newly uploaded regulatory documents from being retrieved. This is caused by insufficient system resources or the `CREATE_TRAIN_ORDER_BATCH_SIZE` parameter being set too high, leading to a backlog in the batch processing queue.

## How to Verify Configuration

*   Select multiple typical telemedicine regulation Q&A scenarios. Verify that retrieval results include all key clauses and that they are the currently effective versions.
*   For questions involving specific numerical values (e.g., "consultation duration limit," "data retention period"), check if the retrieved results accurately point to the original document segments containing these values and compare the correctness of the values.
*   Simulate a regulatory document update, upload the new version, and perform training. Then, verify if the knowledge base retrieval results prioritize the new version and if old version content no longer appears or is correctly marked.
*   Conduct stress tests during peak hours. Monitor the response time and success rate of the knowledge base interface to ensure that configurations like `maxContext` and `Recall count` (Number of Retrieved Items) remain stable under high concurrency.

The values provided are common starting points and should be measured against the reader's own samples.
