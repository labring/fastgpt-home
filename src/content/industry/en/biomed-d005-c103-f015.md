# Deployment and Upgrade for Retail Chain Regulations

## Data Characteristics for This Category
Retail chain regulations and SOP data primarily originate from internal company policy documents, operational manuals, and training materials. These are typically stored in formats like PDF, Word, and Excel. Updates to these documents follow a relatively fixed schedule, usually revised quarterly or annually, or immediately updated during major business adjustments or changes in policies and regulations. Document structures often use a chapter-based format for regulations, including numerous clauses, detailed rules, charts, and flowcharts. SOP documents focus more on step-by-step descriptions, often accompanied by illustrative images. Data fields commonly include regulation number, publication date, effective date, revision version number, responsible department, and applicable store types. Key metrics and units within processes, such as "Inventory Cycle: Monthly" or "Return Processing Time Limit: 24 hours," are also common.

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on Deployment and Upgrade
The characteristics of retail chain regulation data impose specific constraints on deployment and upgrade. First, the large number of documents and diverse formats require the knowledge base to efficiently parse various file types and identify incremental changes during content updates. Second, the time-sensitive nature of regulations and SOPs dictates that knowledge base updates must be timely and accurate to prevent store employees from operating based on outdated information. Third, the high inter-relatedness of regulatory clauses requires that knowledge block splitting preserves context during deployment and that the integrity of the knowledge network is maintained after upgrades. Specific fields and units within documents, such as version numbers and effective dates, need structured identification during the data preprocessing stage for subsequent version comparison and validity checks. Finally, the stability of batch deployment and regular update operations is crucial for ensuring consistent regulations across all stores nationwide.

## Configuration Guidelines

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `UPLOAD_FILE_MAX_SIZE` | `100 MB` | Regulation documents are often large; this ensures coverage for most single uploads. |
| `Chunk size` (Chunk Length) | `800–1200 characters` (characters) | Balances context completeness and retrieval efficiency, adapting to the length of regulatory clauses. |
| `Recall count` (Recall Count) | `Top 5 entries` (top 5) | Initially retrieves more potentially relevant information, improving recall accuracy. |
| `Similarity threshold` (Similarity Threshold) | `0.75` | Avoids interference from irrelevant information, enhancing the precision of regulation Q&A. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `600 seconds` (seconds) | Addresses potentially long parsing times for large PDF or Word files. |
| `maxContext` | `3500 characters` (characters) | Provides sufficient context for the model to understand complex regulatory clauses. |

## Three Common Pitfalls
*   After an upgrade, some existing workflows fail with an error message "text processing function does not exist." This occurs because version upgrades may involve core component refactoring, where some old functions are replaced or removed by new ones, and old workflows are not adapted to the new API or function interfaces.
*   When deploying to an offline environment, the `aiproxy_pg` image fails to start, reporting "port conflict" or "database connection failed." This usually happens because required ports are already in use in the offline environment, or the database connection parameters configured for `aiproxy_pg` do not match the actual environment.
*   After a knowledge base update, some store employees report that Q&A results still show old versions of regulations. This may be due to the knowledge base cache not being refreshed in time, or the update operation failing to cover all index partitions completely.

## How to Verify Successful Configuration
*   Upload a typical regulation document (e.g., a 50MB PDF) and observe the upload and parsing status to ensure the file is successfully imported and knowledge blocks are generated.
*   For regulatory clauses present in both old and new versions, ask questions to verify that the Q&A results are based on the latest version.
*   Randomly select 10 complex SOP processes, simulate questions from store employees, check the accuracy and completeness of the Q&A results, and compare them with the original documents to confirm that key steps and parameters are correct.
