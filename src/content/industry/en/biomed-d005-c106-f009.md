# Reference Sourcing and Traceability for High-Value Consumable Policies

## Data Characteristics for This Category
Policy and SOP data for high-value consumables primarily originates from formal documents issued by internal quality management, medical affairs, and procurement departments within healthcare institutions. These documents are typically in PDF, Word, or internal knowledge base page formats. Content covers consumable procurement processes, usage specifications, inventory management details, adverse event reporting guidelines, and clinical operation SOPs for specific consumables. Data update frequency is relatively low. Updates usually occur quarterly or annually, driven by policy and regulatory adjustments, new consumable introductions, or internal process optimizations. Document structures are rigorous, including clear chapter titles, clause numbers, and appendices. Fields often involve consumable names, specifications, manufacturers, applicable scopes, operating procedures, responsible departments, and approval processes. Units may include procurement quantity (e.g., "pieces," "boxes"), usage dosage (e.g., "ml," "mg"), and storage temperature (e.g., "℃").

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on "Reference Sourcing and Traceability"
The rigorous nature and low update frequency of high-value consumable policy documents require reference sources to precisely point to specific paragraphs or clauses in the original text. This ensures the authority and traceability of responses. Documents are often long and structured. Traditional full-text matching may result in overly broad references, preventing precise location. The existence of multiple SOP versions and slightly different supplementary regulations for the same consumable across departments necessitates the ability to identify and prioritize the latest or most relevant version during retrieval. Furthermore, due to medical safety concerns, any reference must quickly trace back to the original document's page number or chapter. This avoids misinterpretation or misuse. Precise identification of fields, such as distinguishing between different specifications or batches of high-value consumables, also demands more stringent reference mechanisms to prevent confusion.

## Configuration Guidelines

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
| :----- | :------- | :----------- |
| `Chunk size` | `300–500 characters` | Ensures recalled passages are sufficiently complete while preventing overly long segments that could affect matching accuracy and context comprehension. |
| `Recall count` | `Top 5–8 entries` | Balances accuracy and coverage, addressing complex queries that may involve multiple relevant clauses. |
| `Similarity threshold` | `0.75–0.85` | Strictly controls similarity to ensure recalled content is highly relevant to the query, avoiding the introduction of irrelevant information. |
| `Rerank result count` | `Top 3 entries` | Further refines results, prioritizing the most critical references that best align with user intent. |
| `maxContext` | `3000–4000 token` | Provides the model with sufficient context to understand complex policy clauses and generate accurate references. |

## Three Common Pitfalls
*   After knowledge base association, even with `Similarity threshold` set to `1`, many irrelevant references appear. This occurs because `Chunk size` is too large when segmenting documents, causing individual segments to contain excessive irrelevant information. The model struggles to accurately identify core content.
*   When a user asks about an SOP for a specific high-value consumable, an outdated version of the policy is returned. This happens because different document versions are not effectively managed and tagged. The model fails to prioritize the latest or most authoritative version.
*   A policy clause is cited in the response, but clicking it does not navigate to the specific page number in the original PDF. This is due to not extracting and storing original document page numbers or chapter anchor information during the document processing stage, or not correctly mapping this information to reference links.

## How to Confirm Correct Configuration
*   For typical high-value consumable usage process questions, check if the policy clauses cited in the response are precise. Verify that these references point to specific page numbers or chapters in the original document.
*   Upload different versions of the same policy document. Ask questions involving policy updates. Verify that the system prioritizes the latest version of the policy content.
*   Simulate questions about specific fields of a high-value consumable (e.g., "storage temperature," "batch number"). Check if the system accurately extracts and cites relevant information from the policy document.
