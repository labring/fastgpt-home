# Workflow Orchestration for CSO Registration and Declaration Document Preparation

## Data Characteristics in This Category

Contract Sales Organizations (CSOs) preparing registration and declaration documents handle data primarily from regulatory bodies. This includes regulations, guidelines, technical review requirements, public information on approved drugs, and pharmaceutical company data like R&D reports, clinical trial results, manufacturing process documents, and quality standards. These documents are often PDFs or Word files, containing extensive unstructured text, tables, and charts. Data updates are infrequent, usually tied to regulatory changes or new drug approval announcements. Document structures are complex; for instance, drug inserts typically have multiple sections, each subdivided into fields like dosage, indications, and adverse reactions. Field names and units must strictly adhere to regulatory specifications, such as dosage units (mg, g), concentration (%), and time (h, day).

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on "Workflow Orchestration"

The data characteristics of CSO registration and declaration document preparation impose specific requirements on workflow orchestration. The update frequency of regulatory documents dictates the knowledge base refresh cycle, ensuring workflows use the latest versions. Complex document structures require knowledge base segmentation strategies that effectively handle multi-level headings and table content, preventing semantic loss. For example, a paragraph on drug interactions might lose context if segmented too finely. The strictness of fields and units means workflows must accurately identify and validate these specific formats during information extraction and verification, preventing review failures due to unit errors. Furthermore, the ability to understand large volumes of unstructured text directly impacts whether workflows can accurately identify the match between regulatory requirements and product data, thereby affecting the compliance of declaration documents.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
| :----- | :------- | :----------- |
| `Chunk size` (Segment Length) | `500–800 characters` | Balances document structural integrity with model processing efficiency, preventing information loss from long paragraphs or lack of context from overly short segments. |
| `Recall count` (Recall Count) | `8–12 items` | Ensures coverage of relevant information while controlling model input length and reducing unnecessary computational resource consumption. |
| `Similarity threshold` (Similarity Threshold) | `0.75–0.85` | Guarantees the precision of recalled content, filtering out irrelevant or weakly related information to improve matching quality. |
| `Rerank result count` (Rerank Return Count) | `3–5 items` | Further refines recall results, providing the model with the most relevant, limited information to enhance answer accuracy. |
| `maxContext` | `3000–4000 tokens` | Ensures the model has sufficient context to understand complex regulatory requirements and product data, preventing information omission due to insufficient context. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `120 seconds` | Allows ample file parsing time when processing large PDF or Word documents, preventing processing failures due to timeouts. |

## Three Common Pitfalls

*   Workflows often generate declaration documents with mismatches between cited regulations and product data. This occurs because the knowledge base segmentation granularity is too fine, preventing the model from acquiring complete regulatory context.
*   When processing numerous attached documents, workflows frequently interrupt due to file parsing timeouts. This is typically caused by setting the `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` parameter too low, failing to account for the parsing time of large documents.
*   Users report inaccuracies in key fields, such as dosage units or test method descriptions, in the declaration documents produced by workflows. This happens because the workflow fails to effectively identify and validate these specific formats, leading to information extraction or generation errors.

## How to Confirm Proper Configuration

*   Select typical regulatory documents and product documents, run the workflow, and verify the accuracy of regulatory citations and completeness of product data in the generated content.
*   Check workflow logs to confirm no `Timeout` errors or `Parsing Failed` status codes occurred during file parsing.
*   Randomly sample multiple generated declaration documents and manually compare the accuracy and compliance of key fields (e.g., drug dosage, trial period) against original documents and regulatory requirements.
*   Simulate common user queries to observe whether the workflow can provide precise and contextually coherent responses based on knowledge base content, and evaluate if the `token` consumption in responses is within a reasonable range.
