# Reference and Traceability for GMP Compliance Registration Dossier Preparation

## Data Characteristics for this Category

GMP (Good Manufacturing Practice) compliance registration dossiers primarily include regulatory documents, quality management system documents, manufacturing process documents, testing methods and validation reports, and stability study reports. Data sources are extensive, covering guidelines issued by national and international drug regulatory agencies, internal SOPs (Standard Operating Procedures), batch production records, original inspection records, and change control documents. Document formats vary, including PDFs, Word documents, Excel spreadsheets, structured database records, and scanned images. Regulatory documents update less frequently, typically annually or with policy adjustments. Internal company documents update according to actual production and quality management needs, potentially monthly or quarterly. Fields and units are highly specialized, such as "batch number," "expiration date," "production date," "test results (e.g., content %, pH value)," and "deviation number." Strict requirements apply to numerical precision and unit consistency.

## Constraints on "Reference and Traceability"

The reference and traceability mechanism for GMP compliance documents requires strict assurance of accuracy, real-time availability, and traceability of citations. The authority and stability of regulatory documents demand that the knowledge base precisely identify version differences during updates, avoiding citations of outdated or superseded clauses. Frequent updates to internal SOPs and batch records necessitate that the knowledge base possesses efficient incremental update capabilities and clearly marks the latest revision date and version number of documents, ensuring answers are based on the latest valid files. The diversity of document formats, especially scanned images and complex tables, poses challenges for text extraction and structured processing, impacting knowledge block segmentation and metadata extraction. Strict requirements for specialized fields and units mean that when generating answers and citations, this information must be accurately identified and retained to avoid compliance risks due to misinterpretation or loss. For critical numerical values, the system must directly link to original records to support auditing and verification.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale for Recommendation |
| :----------------- | :---------------- | :--------------------------- |
| `Chunk size`         | `500–800 characters`    | Ensures completeness of regulatory clauses or SOP steps, preventing key information from being truncated |
| `Recall count`         | `8–12 entries`         | Covers multiple potentially relevant regulatory clauses or internal guidance documents, improving recall accuracy |
| `Similarity threshold`       | `0.78–0.85`       | Balances recall and precision, avoids irrelevant content interference, and captures subtle regulatory differences |
| `Rerank result count`     | `4–6 entries`          | Filters the most relevant knowledge blocks for in-depth analysis, reducing the model's burden of processing irrelevant information |
| `maxContext`       | `3000–4000 token` | Accommodates sufficient context information to support comprehensive analysis of complex regulatory clauses and multiple reference sources |
| `ENABLE_CHUNK_OVERLAP` | `true`        | Ensures information at segment edges is not lost, especially applicable for continuous interpretation of regulatory clauses |

## Common Pitfalls

*   The returned answer does not match the content of the reference source. System logs show low relevance between knowledge base recall and the query. This occurs when `Similarity threshold` is set too high, failing to recall relevant but not perfectly matching knowledge blocks.
*   Certain key numerical values or batch numbers are missing from the answer, or the referenced link does not lead to corresponding information in the original text. This occurs when the knowledge base file processing fails to effectively identify and extract specific fields from tables or unstructured text.
*   The system returns an outdated version of the reference document, leading to compliance risks. This occurs when the knowledge base update mechanism is not effectively synchronized with the internal document management system, and metadata like `PARSE_FILE_TIMESTAMP` is not correctly updated.

## Verification of Configuration

*   Ask typical compliance questions. Check that all reference sources in the returned answers point to the latest valid document versions and verify document version numbers.
*   Randomly select answers containing key numerical values or specialized terms. Verify that the values and terms in the answer are consistent with the original text of the reference source and that units are correct.
*   Test with GMP compliance documents in various formats (PDF, Word, Excel, scanned images) to confirm the system can accurately extract and cite information, especially text content within tables and images.
