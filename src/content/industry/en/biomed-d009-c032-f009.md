# Reference and Traceability for Health Insurance Claim Registration Documents

## Data Characteristics for This Category

Health insurance claim registration documents draw from diverse sources and have varying update frequencies. These sources include policy documents from the National Healthcare Security Administration, local implementation rules from regional healthcare security bureaus, internal settlement process documents from medical institutions, drug and treatment item catalogs, and relevant fee coding standards. Policy documents typically update annually or quarterly, with local rules adjusting accordingly. Document structures vary: policy documents are primarily normative texts with numerous clauses, definitions, and appendices; settlement process documents are often operational manuals, flowcharts, and case studies. Fields involve health insurance payment scope, reimbursement ratios, special disease identification standards, and fee codes (e.g., `ICD-10`, `CPT`). Units are often percentages, monetary amounts (Yuan), and time periods (days, months).

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on "Reference and Traceability"

The data characteristics of health insurance claim documents impose specific constraints on reference and traceability. Frequent policy updates require the knowledge base to quickly synchronize with the latest versions to ensure reference accuracy. References must specify the policy document's publication date and document number to differentiate versions. Regional variations in local rules mean references must precisely target specific provinces, cities, or regions to avoid confusion. Documents with numerous clauses and definitions necessitate references that pinpoint specific paragraphs or clause numbers. Referencing fee codes and reimbursement ratios requires the system to identify and link to the latest coding standards and rate tables, ensuring that numerical values in generated responses align with policies. The heterogeneous nature of data sources complicates identifying and associating similar or related information across different document types.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale for This Value |
|---|---|---|
| `Chunk Length` | `500-800 characters` | Health insurance policy clauses and process descriptions often use medium-to-long sentences. An appropriate chunk length helps maintain semantic completeness and prevents key information from being truncated. |
| `Recall Count` | `Top 8` | Health insurance settlements involve highly interconnected policies. Increasing the recall count covers more relevant clauses and background information, improving the comprehensiveness of references. |
| `Similarity Threshold` | `0.75-0.85` | Health insurance texts are highly specialized. A high similarity threshold more accurately matches relevant clauses, reduces interference from irrelevant information, and ensures reference accuracy. |
| `Rerank Return Count` | `Top 5` | In conjunction with the recall count, reranking filters the few most relevant items, ensuring the final cited content focuses on the core issue. |
| `Reference Link Display Format` | `File Name (Page or Clause Number)` | Health insurance documents often have page numbers or explicit clause numbers. Displaying this information helps users quickly locate the original source, improving traceability efficiency. |
| `Knowledge Base Update Frequency` | `Once a week` | Health insurance policies update relatively frequently. Weekly updates ensure the knowledge base content stays synchronized with the latest policies, preventing the citation of outdated information. |

## Three Common Mistakes

*   Responses include outdated or incorrect reimbursement ratios because the knowledge base failed to timely synchronize the latest health insurance policy documents.
*   The AI response cites an irrelevant internal process document because of incorrect variable reference configuration in the knowledge base search module, leading to the retrieval of non-policy-related materials.
*   Generating a response results in "No relevant reference source found." This might be due to a `Similarity Threshold` set too high, preventing even relevant information from being recalled.

## How to Verify Configuration

*   Select a health insurance claim document with recent policy updates. Ask questions about differences between new and old policies. Check if the response accurately cites the latest policy document and relevant clauses.
*   Ask questions about specific operational steps in the health insurance settlement process. Verify if the cited internal process documents in the response are correct and if the cited page or chapter numbers match the original text.
*   Input a question containing a specific fee code. Check if the response correctly links to the latest fee coding standard and provides the corresponding reimbursement scope or ratio, while also verifying the accuracy of the reference source.
