# Model Integration and Configuration for Hospital Operations Registration and Declaration Document Preparation

## Data Characteristics for This Category

Hospital operations registration and declaration documents primarily include application materials for medical institution practice licenses, diagnostic and treatment subject registration, large medical equipment configuration permits, and radiation safety permits. Data sources are mainly official regulations, guidelines, and templates published by the National Health Commission, the National Medical Products Administration, and local medical insurance bureaus. These are combined with internal hospital regulations, personnel qualification certificates, equipment procurement contracts, and quality management system documents. These documents have a relatively low update frequency, typically updated with policy adjustments or annual review cycles. Document structures primarily consist of standardized application forms, appendix lists, and supporting materials, mostly in PDF and Word formats. Fields include institution name, license number, address, legal representative, approved beds, and scope of diagnosis and treatment. Units involve quantity, date, validity period, and capacity, exhibiting high standardization and structural characteristics.

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on "Model Integration and Configuration"

The high standardization and structural nature of hospital operations registration and declaration documents require the model to accurately identify and extract key field information during knowledge base construction, ensuring the accuracy of declaration content. The low document update frequency means that the knowledge base needs a comprehensive initial data import and processing. Subsequent maintenance will primarily involve incremental updates and local corrections, so the model's ability to process new information in real-time is relatively low. Common document formats like PDF and Word demand robust document parsing capabilities from the model, especially for recognizing text within tables and images. The standardization of fields and units necessitates the model's entity recognition capabilities to precisely match and reference information during Q&A or assisted form filling. Furthermore, due to the strictness of declaration materials, the model must avoid hallucinations when generating responses, ensuring information traceability.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
| :------------------------------ | :--------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Chunk size` (Segment Length) | `800–1200 characters` (characters) | Declaration document paragraphs are often long and contain multiple key pieces of information. This length helps maintain contextual completeness and improves recall accuracy. |
| `Chunk Overlap Length` (Segment Overlap Length) | `100 characters` (characters) | Ensures sufficient contextual connection between adjacent segments, reducing information loss, especially when extracting information across paragraphs. |
| `Recall count` (Recall Count) | `5–8 entries` (items) | Declaration document content is highly interconnected. Increasing the recall count helps cover more potentially relevant information, improving the comprehensiveness of answers. |
| `Similarity threshold` (Similarity Threshold) | `0.78–0.85` | Declaration documents require high rigor. A higher threshold filters out more precisely matched knowledge snippets, reducing interference from irrelevant information. |
| `Rerank result count` (Rerank Return Count) | `3 entries` (items) | After reranking, the top few items typically contain the most relevant and high-quality information, meeting the accuracy requirements for declaration documents. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `600 seconds` (seconds) | Some declaration files are large and contain charts or complex formats. Appropriately extending the parsing timeout ensures that files can be processed completely. |

## Three Common Pitfalls

*   Model responses containing statements inconsistent with regulatory requirements often stem from outdated or incorrect regulatory versions in the knowledge base, or from model "hallucinations" during generation.
*   After uploading a large number of PDF files, some files remain in processing for extended periods or report parsing failures. This occurs due to complex file content, exceeding size limits, or the parser timing out.
*   When users inquire about the process for a specific license, the model may fail to provide complete or continuous steps. This is often due to an improper knowledge base segmentation strategy, leading to fragmented process information and loss of context.

## How to Confirm Configuration Effectiveness

*   Upload a batch of typical declaration documents. Use the knowledge base management interface to check if each document is successfully parsed and if the segmentation effect meets expectations.
*   Pose questions related to core regulatory terms and declaration procedures. Verify if the model can accurately and completely cite original texts and provide correct answers, paying close attention to whether key fields and units are consistent.
*   Simulate complex problems that may arise in actual declarations, such as licensing matters involving multi-departmental collaboration. Check if the model can integrate information from different sources and provide logical solutions. Compare the answers against official guidelines to determine accuracy.
