# Reference and Traceability for Nursing Management Registration Documents

## Data Characteristics in this Category

Nursing management registration documents primarily come from national and local health commissions. These include nursing standards, operational guidelines, quality evaluation standards, hospital internal nursing policies, and SOP documents. Data updates are relatively stable, typically occurring periodically (e.g., annually or biennially) as policies and regulations change or clinical practices evolve. Document structures are often hierarchical Word, PDF, or occasionally Excel files. They contain extensive specialized terminology, flowcharts, and tables. Key fields include nursing levels, operational steps, risk assessment indicators, quality control standards, and personnel qualification requirements. Units involve time (minutes, hours), quantity (person-times, items), and ratios (percentages). Precision and standardization for numerical values are critical.

## Constraints from these Characteristics on "Reference and Traceability"

The specialized and standardized nature of nursing management documents requires precise source citations, down to the exact location in the original text, to support the rigor of the declared content. Documents often have hierarchical structures. Traditional paragraph-based chunking can lead to incomplete semantics, requiring a more refined text block division strategy. The update frequency means the knowledge base needs regular full or incremental updates to ensure the timeliness of cited content. Flowcharts and tables, when converted to text, may lose their original structural information, affecting the accuracy of reference traceability. The demand for numerical precision requires careful attention to the context of values during citation to avoid misinterpretation due to truncation. The specificity of fields and units also means improving the recognition and matching capabilities for these specialized terms during retrieval.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item | Suggested Value | Rationale |
| :----- | :------- | :----------- |
| `Chunk size` | `500–800 characters` | Nursing standard documents often have high logical completeness within a single segment. This length balances information density and retrieval accuracy. |
| `Recall count` | `Top 8–12 entries` | Ensures coverage of enough potentially relevant sections to handle complex queries. |
| `Similarity threshold` | `0.75–0.85` | High repetition of domain-specific terms. Increasing the threshold reduces citations with low relevance. |
| `Rerank result count` | `Top 3 entries` | Selects the most relevant citations, reducing redundant information and improving user experience. |
| `maxContext` | `4096 characters` | Provides the model with sufficient context to understand query intent and cited content, especially for process details. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `600 seconds` | Prevents failures when processing large PDF standard files due to excessive parsing time. |

## Three Common Mistakes

*   Generated responses contain citations outside the knowledge base or incorrect citations. This happens when `Similarity threshold` is set too low, leading to the recall of irrelevant text blocks.
*   After a user asks a question, no citation sources appear in the conversation, but the answer contains knowledge base information. This usually occurs when `Recall count` is too small or `Similarity threshold` is too high. The knowledge base is hit, but the conditions for displaying citations are not met.
*   Uploading a nursing operation manual PDF file fails with a `500` status code. This might be because `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` is insufficient to process the complex table and chart structures within the file.

## How to Confirm Correct Configuration

*   Upload typical nursing management standard documents. Ask multiple rounds of questions. Check if citation sources are accurately displayed below each answer. Click links to verify they navigate to the corresponding location in the original text.
*   For questions involving specific numerical values or specialized terms, check if the returned citations include this key information, and if the values and units are correct.
*   Test that after a knowledge base update, citations from old version documents are replaced with content from the new version, ensuring the timeliness of citation sources.
*   Simulate a user asking a question not directly related to the knowledge base content. Observe if knowledge base citations are effectively suppressed to avoid "hallucinated" citations.
