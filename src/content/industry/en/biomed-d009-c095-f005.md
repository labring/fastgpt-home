# Multi-turn Conversation and Prompts for Quality Document Management in Regulatory Submission Preparation

## Data Characteristics

Quality documents in the biomedical field include Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), batch production records, batch inspection records, deviation investigation reports, change control documents, and validation reports. These documents are typically in PDF, Word, or scanned image formats. They are highly structured and contain extensive technical terminology, regulatory citations, and detailed operating procedures. Data sources primarily include internal Quality Management Systems (QMS), Laboratory Information Management Systems (LIMS), and external regulatory databases. Document update frequency is relatively low, usually following annual reviews or updates triggered by significant changes. Fields and units are highly standardized, such as batch number, production date, expiration date, inspection results (percentage content, impurity limits), temperature (°C), and pressure (Pa). These are core elements for regulatory submissions.

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on "Multi-turn Conversation and Prompts"

The specialized and standardized nature of quality documents necessitates precise understanding of terminology in multi-turn conversations. Low document update frequency means the knowledge base retrieval strategy must prioritize tracing and comparing historical versions to ensure that cited regulations and guidelines are the latest effective versions. The presence of extensive structured data and specific fields requires prompt design to guide the model in accurately extracting specific information, such as key process parameters from batch production records or analysis results for specific batches from inspection reports. For scanned documents, OCR accuracy is critical to avoid semantic deviations caused by text recognition errors. Multi-turn conversations must also support users in tracing information sources, for example, by identifying the specific SOP version number or regulatory clause number cited, which is crucial for the rigor of regulatory submissions.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale                                                    |
| :----------------- | :---------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| `maxContext`       | `1600 tokens`     | Quality documents have high content density, requiring a larger context window to understand complex logical relationships and regulatory provisions. |
| `Chunk size`         | `800–1000 characters`   | Ensures each text block contains sufficient semantic information for the model to understand complete concepts. |
| `Recall count`         | `Top 10 entries`        | Covers more potentially relevant document snippets, increasing recall accuracy and handling fuzzy queries for specialized terminology. |
| `Similarity threshold`       | `0.78–0.85`       | Filters out irrelevant recall results, improving matching precision and reducing misleading information. |
| `Rerank result count`     | `Top 3 entries`         | After re-ranking, presents the most relevant few results to the user, improving information retrieval efficiency. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `600 seconds`          | File parsing for large PDFs or scanned documents can be time-consuming; this prevents timeout interruptions. |

## Three Common Pitfalls

*   Incorrect explanation or confusion of technical terms in conversation results. This occurs when definitions of relevant terms in the knowledge base are not precise enough, or prompts fail to effectively guide the model in distinguishing terms.
*   The model fails to provide specific values or provides incorrect data when users ask for key data for a specific batch. This happens when key fields are not correctly identified or extracted during document parsing, or prompts do not explicitly specify the required fields.
*   The model cites outdated regulatory versions or incorrect clauses when answering regulatory compliance questions. This is due to an inadequate knowledge base content update mechanism, or prompts failing to force the model to prioritize retrieving the latest versions of regulatory documents.

## How to Verify Configuration

*   For typical and complex quality document queries, such as "What is the approval process for deviation handling in the latest version of SOP-QA-001?", check if the model can accurately extract and present the complete approval steps and identify the SOP version number.
*   Select specific batch production records and inspection reports, then ask "Do the finished product release standards and actual inspection results for batch XYZ comply?". Verify if the release standards and inspection results provided by the model match the original documents, and determine if it can correctly provide compliance conclusions.
*   Input queries containing ambiguous technical terms, such as "validation requirements for sterile manufacturing". Observe if the model can recall relevant validation SOPs, validation protocols, and regulatory guidelines, and verify if the relevance of the recalled results meets the expected threshold.
