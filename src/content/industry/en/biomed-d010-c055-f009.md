# Reference and Traceability for Structured Parsing of Medical Imaging Device R&D Documents

## Data Characteristics

R&D documents for medical imaging devices (CT, MRI, ultrasound) are highly specialized and standardized. Data sources include internal technical specifications, design documents, test reports, clinical trial data, and regulatory submission materials. Document updates are relatively stable, typically aligning with R&D milestones or regulatory cycles, with intensive updates during design verification and clinical trial phases. Documents have a strict structure, often using section numbering, paragraph headings, and embedded figures. They contain technical parameter tables, calibration procedures, fault codes, and safety warnings. Fields include physical quantities, engineering units, medical terminology, and abbreviations, such as `kVp` (kilovoltage peak), `mA` (milliamperes), `FOV` (field of view), and `SNR` (signal-to-noise ratio). Units are standardized, e.g., `mm`, `Hz`, `dB`.

## Constraints on Reference and Traceability

The specialized, structured nature and specific field units of medical imaging device R&D documents impose clear requirements on reference and traceability. First, the accuracy of numerous technical parameters and specifications is critical. Traceability must point to specific values, figures, or paragraphs in the original text, avoiding vague references. Second, stable update frequencies allow for indexing strategies that balance depth and efficiency. Full updates are not required frequently; focus on incremental updates and version management. The strict document structure requires accurate identification of chapters and sections during parsing, preserving hierarchical relationships in references, such as `《Technical Specification V2.0》Section 3.2.1`. The presence of many specialized terms and abbreviations means the underlying vector retrieval model needs strong domain understanding to effectively match queries with document content. For potential compliance requirements, ensuring each reference can be traced to its original source is key to accurate and credible answers.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `Chunk size` (Segment Length) | `500–800 characters` | Paragraphs in imaging device documents often contain complete technical information. Shorter segments can break context; longer segments reduce retrieval precision. |
| `Recall count` (Retrieval Count) | `Top 8–12 entries` | Ensures coverage of multiple potentially relevant technical details or specifications, improving answer comprehensiveness. |
| `Similarity threshold` (Similarity Threshold) | `0.78–0.85` | Ensures retrieved segments are highly relevant to the query, filtering out low-quality or vague matches, especially critical for high parameter precision. |
| `Rerank result count` (Reranked Return Count) | `Top 3–5 entries` | After optimization by the reranking model, focuses on the most relevant and core segments, improving answer efficiency. |
| `maxContext` | `8000–12000 tokens` | Imaging device documents have a large amount of contextual information. This ensures the model can process a sufficiently long context to understand complex technical descriptions. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `300–600 seconds` | For large technical documents, this provides ample parsing time, preventing structuring failures due to timeouts. |

## Common Pitfalls

*   **Symptom:** AI responses show "unknown" or "no reference" for citation sources.
    **Reason:** Document parsing failed to effectively identify and extract original document metadata (e.g., document title, section numbers), leading to loss of traceability information.
*   **Symptom:** Technical parameters provided by the AI slightly deviate from values in the original document.
    **Reason:** Improper segmentation strategy led to truncation of sentences containing key values, or the vector model lacked sufficient sensitivity to numbers and units, resulting in imprecise matching during retrieval.
*   **Symptom:** For queries about specific fault codes, the AI cannot provide detailed solutions or only offers generic explanations.
    **Reason:** Fault troubleshooting sections in the knowledge base were not sufficiently structured, or the association between fault codes and solutions was not established during indexing.

*   Randomly select a professional abbreviation from a document (e.g., `DICOM`). Ask for its meaning and related applications. Verify the AI's answer accuracy and check that the cited source comes from relevant standards or specification sections.
*   Simulate a complex fault diagnosis scenario. Ask the AI for possible solutions. Verify that the AI cites the corresponding troubleshooting procedures or code descriptions from the device manual.
