# Reference and Traceability for Lead Compound Screening R&D Document Analysis

## Data Characteristics for Lead Compound Screening

Lead compound screening data originates from high-throughput screening reports, activity validation data, structure-activity relationship (SAR) analysis documents, patent applications and publications, and internal experimental records. These documents typically exist in formats such as PDF, DOCX, and XLSX. Some data may reside in specialized cheminformatics databases. Document update frequencies vary; high-throughput screening reports may generate weekly, while SAR analysis documents update at critical project milestones. Document structures are diverse, containing structural diagrams, tabular data (e.g., IC50, Ki values), experimental procedure descriptions, compound numbers, batch information, supplier details, and pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) data. Fields and units are highly specialized, for example, "micromolar/liter" for concentration, "nanomolar" for dosage, and "% inhibition rate" for activity.

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on "Reference and Traceability"

The batch nature and periodic updates of high-throughput screening reports require FastGPT to have efficient batch processing capabilities and an incremental update mechanism for references, ensuring information timeliness. Complex structural formulas and associated data in SAR analysis documents mean that text-based similarity recall alone is insufficient for accurate traceability. This requires embedding models to understand images and chemical structure information. The legal rigor of patent texts demands that references be precise down to paragraphs or even sentences to avoid misinterpretation. Furthermore, varying compound numbering systems and mixed units increase the difficulty of data cleaning and standardization, directly affecting the accuracy and traceability of references. For PK/PD data, due to its dynamic and multi-dimensional nature, a single text block reference may not fully convey its meaning, requiring consideration of finer-grained citation methods.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item | Suggested Value | Rationale for this Value |
| :----- | :------- | :----------- |
| `maxContext` | `1500 characters` | A paragraph in SAR analysis documents often contains multiple related data points, requiring a longer context for complete expression. |
| `Chunk size` | `800 characters` | Balances the conciseness of high-throughput screening reports with the detail of patent documents, avoiding overly long or short text blocks. |
| `Similarity threshold` | `0.78` | Ensures recalled results are highly relevant to lead compound activity data and structural information, reducing noise. |
| `Recall count` | `8 entries` | Covers enough potential related documents, especially when a compound is involved in multiple experimental reports. |
| `Rerank result count` | `3 entries` | Prioritizes the most relevant and information-dense references, reducing the engineer's screening workload. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `600 seconds` | Handles large high-throughput screening reports or patent documents containing numerous images and tables, preventing parsing timeouts. |

## Three Common Mistakes

*   Irrelevant reference links appear in the answer. The symptom is that reference links point to documents with no clear connection to the answer content. This occurs when the `Similarity threshold` is set too low, leading to the recall of many low-relevance document segments.
*   Some critical data (e.g., IC50 values) are cited in the answer but cannot be traced back to their specific location in the original document. This happens when the `Chunk size` is set too long, causing a reference block to contain too much information, making precise localization difficult.
*   After document updates, the system answer still cites old data. The symptom is that the answer content does not match the latest experimental results. This occurs when the knowledge base is not incrementally updated or re-indexed in a timely manner, resulting in the retrieval of outdated information.

## How to Confirm Proper Configuration

*   Ask multiple rounds of questions for typical lead compounds. Check if the references in the answers accurately point to the corresponding data points or descriptive paragraphs in the original documents.
*   Upload a screening report containing the latest activity data. Immediately ask a question about the compound's activity information. Verify if the answer cites this latest document.
*   Randomly select 10 reference links. Manually check the relevance between the content of the linked documents and the answer. Evaluate the effectiveness of the `Similarity threshold`.
*   Simulate parsing large patent documents and high-throughput screening reports. Observe for any parsing failures or timeout errors to confirm the reasonableness of the `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` setting.

The values provided are common starting points. Measure them against your own samples to determine the optimal configuration for your specific use case.
