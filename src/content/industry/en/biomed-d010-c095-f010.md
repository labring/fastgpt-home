# Database and Operations for Structured Analysis of R&D Quality Documents

## Data Characteristics

Quality document management in the biopharmaceutical sector involves diverse data types. These primarily include Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), batch production records, inspection reports, deviation reports, change control documents, and supplier qualification files. Documents are typically stored in formats such as PDF, Word, and Excel. Their content is highly structured, containing specialized terminology, regulatory requirements, experimental data, and batch information. Data update frequency is relatively stable; new drug development phases see frequent updates, while post-market product documents have longer update cycles. Document fields include production batch numbers, expiration dates, inspection results, equipment parameters, and operator signatures. Units are precise, down to milligrams, microliters, and degrees Celsius, demanding high accuracy and traceability. Documents have complex reference and association relationships; for example, an SOP may reference multiple inspection methods, and batch production records link to specific SOPs and material batches.

## Constraints on Database and Operations

The highly structured and specialized nature of quality documents requires databases with robust text parsing capabilities and efficient vector storage. Precise fields and units in documents mean that data extraction and indexing must pay special attention to identifying and normalizing numerical values and units of measurement to avoid parsing errors due to format discrepancies. Complex relationships between documents demand advanced retrieval strategies from vector databases, requiring support for multi-dimensional, multi-conditional queries and the ability to quickly locate related document fragments. The stable update frequency allows for batch processing updates during off-peak hours, minimizing impact on real-time queries. Furthermore, regulatory compliance mandates auditable operations and traceable data. Therefore, the database and operations must ensure complete logging, strict access control, data security, and version management. For critical data like production batches and inspection results, data consistency and integrity are operational priorities; any data loss or corruption could lead to severe consequences.

## Configuration Guidelines

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `UPLOAD_FILE_MAX_SIZE` | `500 MB` | Quality documents, such as batch production records, can contain numerous images and charts, leading to large file sizes. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `600 seconds` | Parsing large files requires considerable time; this avoids parsing failures due to timeouts. |
| `maxContext` | `8192 token` | Ensures the capture of longer paragraphs and contextual information in quality documents, preventing loss of critical information. |
| `Chunk size` | `512 characters` | Balances semantic integrity and vectorization efficiency, accommodating the longer sentence structures common in quality documents. |
| `Recall count` | `Top 10 entries` | Increases the recall rate of relevant document fragments, covering more potential associated information. |
| `Similarity threshold` | `Calibrate by actual measurement` | Calibrate based on the actual similarity distribution of document content to avoid false positives or negatives in retrieval. |

## Common Pitfalls

*   Database connection failures, indicated by `MongoNetworkError` or `connection refused` in logs, typically stem from the database service not running, a port being occupied, or a firewall blocking the necessary port.
*   After document parsing, some critical fields are empty or incorrectly formatted. This manifests as inaccurate retrieval results or missing important information. The cause is often improper parser configuration, failing to correctly identify unique field patterns or unit expressions specific to quality documents.
*   After logging in, the system reports insufficient permissions or inability to access certain functions, even with an administrator account. This might be due to an incorrect `init root user` process or misconfigured user roles in MongoDB.

## Verification Steps

*   Upload a typical quality document containing complex tables and specialized terminology. Verify that all important information is present in the parsed document fragments and that field extraction is accurate.
*   Execute a complex query involving multiple dimensions, such as batch numbers and inspection results. Confirm that the system accurately retrieves relevant document fragments and that response times are acceptable.
*   Simulate a database service interruption. Observe whether the system correctly logs errors and if data automatically recovers after service restoration, confirming the effectiveness of backup and recovery mechanisms.
