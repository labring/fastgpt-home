# Document Parsing and Chunking for Cold Chain Logistics Quality Documents

## Data Characteristics
Cold chain logistics quality documents include temperature and humidity records, equipment calibration certificates, validation reports, Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), and deviation records. Data sources are diverse, encompassing automated sensor readings, manually completed forms, and third-party inspection reports. Update frequencies vary by document type: temperature and humidity data may update every minute or hour, while SOPs and validation reports are typically updated quarterly, annually, or upon change. Document structures often feature mixed tables, charts, and text in PDF reports. SOPs are usually chapter-based, with clear steps and defined responsibilities. Fields and units include Celsius (℃) or Fahrenheit (℉) for temperature, percentage relative humidity (%RH) for humidity, second-level timestamps, and identifiers like equipment models and batch numbers.

## Constraints on Document Parsing and Chunking
The characteristics of cold chain logistics quality documents impose specific requirements on document parsing and chunking. First, temperature and humidity records contain dense time-series and numerical data. The parser must accurately identify and extract numbers and units, avoiding incorrect segmentation of continuous data into separate blocks. Second, SOP documents have hierarchical structures and step descriptions. Chunking logic must maintain the integrity of instructions, avoiding truncation in the middle of critical steps. Third, the prevalence of tables and charts means enhanced PDF parsing capabilities are essential to preserve table structures and associate chart descriptions with relevant text. Finally, varying document update frequencies require effective document version identification after chunking and support for incremental updates. This avoids reprocessing large amounts of unchanged content, especially for log-type data, which needs efficient segmentation by time range or event.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
| :----- | :------- | :----------- |
| `Chunk size` (Chunk Length) | `800–1200 characters` | Accommodates longer step descriptions in SOPs and paragraphs in validation reports, ensuring contextual completeness. |
| `Maximum Paragraph Depth` (Max Paragraph Depth) | `3` | Matches the common three-level heading structure in SOP documents, such as "Chapter-Subchapter-Specific Step." |
| `Enable Enhanced PDF Parsing` | `Yes` | Ensures text content and structure within tables and charts are correctly recognized and extracted. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `600 seconds` | Handles reports containing numerous charts or scanned images, preventing parsing timeouts. |
| `Index Size` | `128` | Balances index granularity and recall efficiency, suitable for quality documents containing various information types. |
| `API_FILE_CHUNK_SIZE` | `2000000 bytes` | Accommodates potentially large individual temperature and humidity record files, avoiding API upload limits. |

## Common Pitfalls
*   Table data is garbled or missing in parsing results. This occurs when enhanced PDF parsing is not enabled or configured incorrectly, preventing the parser from correctly identifying table row and column structures.
*   SOP document step descriptions are truncated, leading to incomplete semantics. This happens when `Chunk size` (Chunk Length) is set too small, or the chunking strategy does not consider paragraph integrity, splitting in the middle of critical instructions.
*   `413 Payload Too Large` errors occur when uploading documents via API. This is due to `API_FILE_CHUNK_SIZE` or the server-side `client_max_body_size` being configured smaller than the actual file size being uploaded.

## Verification Steps
*   Select a validation report containing complex tables and charts. Upload it and examine the chunking results to confirm table content is complete and structurally clear.
*   Choose a typical SOP document. Review the parsed text blocks to ensure each operational step description is preserved as a complete semantic unit, without unnatural truncation.
*   Upload a temperature and humidity record file close to the `UPLOAD_FILE_MAX_SIZE` limit. Observe if parsing completes normally and indexing occurs, checking logs for file size-related warnings or errors.
*   For documents with different update frequencies, such as frequently updated temperature and humidity logs and less frequently updated SOPs, upload and parse them separately. Verify the system's performance regarding incremental updates and version management.

Note: The values provided are common starting points. Measure performance against your own samples to determine optimal settings.
