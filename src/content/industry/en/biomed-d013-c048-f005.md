# Multi-Turn Conversations and Prompts for Home Healthcare Quality Documentation

## Data Characteristics

Home healthcare quality documentation data primarily originates from regulations, standards, product manuals, registration certificates, manufacturing process documents, inspection reports, and user feedback. These documents are typically stored in formats like PDF, Word, and Excel. Some data may reside in internal management systems. Document update frequency depends on regulatory changes, product iterations, and quality system audits, usually occurring quarterly or annually. However, abnormal event reports can be generated at any time. Documents have complex structures, containing extensive specialized terminology, charts, and tables. Fields and units have strong industry-specific characteristics, such as device classification codes, batch numbers, production dates, expiration dates, sterilization methods, adverse event report codes, and measurement units (e.g., mmHg, mg/dL). Data accuracy and traceability requirements are extremely high.

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on Multi-Turn Conversations and Prompts

The complex structure and specialized terminology of home healthcare documents require multi-turn dialogue systems to possess high semantic understanding capabilities. The system must accurately identify and associate information across different documents. Low update frequency means knowledge base construction needs to focus on version management and incremental updates, ensuring retrieved information is always current and valid. Strict field and unit requirements in documents challenge prompt engineering. Prompts must guide the model to precisely cite data in responses, avoiding vague statements. Multi-turn conversations may involve tracing critical information like batch and serial numbers, requiring the system to handle entity recognition and chained queries with specific formats. Additionally, the high compliance requirements in inspection scenarios necessitate that the dialogue system clearly and accurately cites regulatory provisions and supports quick verification of cited sources by users.

## Configuration Guidelines

| Configuration Item | Suggested Value | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `maxContext` | `2000 characters` | Ensures multi-turn conversations cover multiple product batches or regulatory clauses involved in user queries, preventing information loss. |
| `chunk_size` | `500 characters` | Balances recall granularity and context completeness, adapting to the paragraph length of regulatory clauses and operational procedures. |
| `recall_count` | `top 8` | Increases the coverage of relevant document snippets in complex queries, such as adverse event analysis and compliance audits. |
| `similarity_threshold` | `0.78` | Improves retrieval accuracy, filtering out irrelevant content with low association to home healthcare specialized terminology. |
| `rerank_count` | `5` | Further refines retrieval results, prioritizing core information most directly related to inspections or quality management. |
| `system_prompt_template` | `Calibrated by actual measurement` | Refines model roles and output format requirements for different document types, such as home healthcare regulations and product manuals. |

## Common Pitfalls

*   During conversations, the system fails to recognize or incorrectly cites critical information like product batch numbers or registration certificate numbers. This occurs because these specific entities were not annotated or preprocessed during knowledge base chunking.
*   When users inquire about regulatory clauses, the AI's response content does not match the top-level title. This indicates that the system prompt's constraints are insufficient to cover specific elements of embedded pages, leading the model to not strictly follow interface requirements when generating responses.
*   The AI's response includes unofficial units of measurement or abbreviations. This happens because the prompt did not explicitly require the model to use standard terminology and units defined in the documents.

## Verification Methods

*   Select a typical set of questions covering different product types, regulatory clauses, and abnormal reports. Conduct multi-turn conversation tests to verify the accuracy of key information in responses and the validity of cited sources.
*   Simulate inspection scenarios by asking questions about product compliance and quality standards. Check if the AI's response accurately cites the corresponding regulatory provisions and document numbers, and assess its alignment with expected compliance requirements.
*   Verify fields such as batch numbers, models, and production dates in the AI's responses to ensure they exactly match the data in the original knowledge base. Check if measurement units comply with industry standards.
