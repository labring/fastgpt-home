# Multi-Turn Conversations and Prompts for Regulatory Submission Quality Documents

## Data Characteristics

Regulatory submission quality document data originates from the entire lifecycle of pharmaceutical and medical device products, including research, development, production, and testing. This data includes clinical trial reports, manufacturing process specifications, quality standards, inspection reports, stability study data, and risk management plans. Documents are typically in PDF, Word, or Excel formats, containing both highly structured and semi-structured content. Core quality standards and regulatory requirements remain relatively stable. However, some documents, such as clinical data and batch production records, update dynamically due to R&D progress, manufacturing process optimization, or regulatory policy changes. Documents contain extensive specialized terminology, abbreviations, units of measurement (e.g., mg/mL, IU, % w/w), and complex tables and graphs. Field naming conventions are generally standardized but vary across enterprises.

## Constraints on Multi-Turn Conversations and Prompts

The characteristics of regulatory submission quality document data impose specific requirements on multi-turn conversations and prompt construction. First, the specialized and terminology-dense nature of the documents requires the model to accurately identify entities and avoid misinterpreting professional vocabulary. Second, dynamic document updates necessitate an efficient incremental update mechanism for the knowledge base to ensure conversations use the latest data. If the system fails to update promptly, conversations may cite outdated information, leading to compliance risks in submission materials. The multi-format and structured nature of documents requires effective extraction of key information from tables and graphs during data preprocessing. This information must be organized appropriately in prompts to support complex queries. Accurate recognition and conversion of units of measurement are crucial for questions involving critical parameters like dosage and concentration. During multi-turn conversations, users may ask follow-up questions about inspection results for a specific batch number or detailed steps of a particular experiment. This requires the system to efficiently retrieve and integrate information from numerous related documents.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item         | Recommended Value | Rationale                                                  |
| :------------------------- | :---------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------- |
| `maxContext`               | `6`               | Regulatory queries often require reviewing previous turns to track complex issues. |
| `Chunk size` (Segment Length) | `800–1200 characters`   | Balances the completeness of professional terminology context with retrieval efficiency. |
| `Recall count` (Recall Count)  | `Top 10 entries`        | Ensures coverage of multiple relevant document segments, increasing information recall. |
| `Similarity threshold` (Similarity Threshold) | `0.75`            | Filters out low-relevance results, reducing noise interference. |
| `Rerank result count` (Rerank Return Count) | `Top 5 entries`         | Focuses on the most critical relevant information, improving model response quality. |
| `UPLOAD_FILE_MAX_SIZE`     | `500 MB`          | Accommodates the upload requirements for large clinical trial reports or manufacturing process files. |

## Common Pitfalls

*   **Symptom**: Model responses include outdated or superseded regulatory clauses or standards.
    **Cause**: The knowledge base update mechanism failed to synchronize the latest regulatory submission regulations or internal enterprise quality standards in time.
*   **Symptom**: When a user asks for inspection results for a specific batch number, the model cannot provide information or provides incorrect information.
    **Cause**: Document parsing failed to effectively identify and extract batch numbers and corresponding inspection data from tables, or knowledge base permission settings prevented access to relevant documents.
*   **Symptom**: During a conversation, the model misunderstands professional abbreviations or units of measurement, leading to inaccurate responses.
    **Cause**: Prompts did not sufficiently emphasize the requirements for identifying and parsing professional terminology and units, or the knowledge base lacks corresponding professional glossaries.

## Configuration Validation

*   Select 5–10 complex questions covering different regulatory submission stages (e.g., clinical, production, quality control). Verify if the model can consistently provide accurate and coherent answers in multi-turn conversations and assess its understanding of professional terminology.
*   Upload a key quality document containing the latest revisions. Then, ask questions related to the revised content. Confirm the model accurately cites the latest information to evaluate the timeliness of knowledge base updates.
*   Select documents containing complex tables and graphs. Test if the model can accurately extract and integrate data from them. For example, ask about product yield under specific experimental conditions or stability data for a particular batch of medicine, and compare the model's output with the original document for consistency.

The values provided are common starting points and should be measured against the reader's own samples.
