# Deployment and Upgrades for Regulatory Submission Quality Documents

## Data Characteristics for This Category

Regulatory submission quality documents in the biopharmaceutical field include clinical trial reports, non-clinical study reports, manufacturing process specifications, quality standards, and stability study data. These documents are typically in PDF, Word, or structured data table formats (e.g., Excel, CSV). They are voluminous, with individual files potentially containing hundreds of pages. Data primarily originates from internal R&D departments, manufacturing departments, and external collaborating institutions. The update frequency is relatively low, mainly concentrated during the submission of R&D phase results and revisions to submission materials. Document structures strictly adhere to regulatory agency guidelines, including fixed chapters, sub-chapters, and a large number of specialized terms, abbreviations, and units of measurement (e.g., `mg/kg`, `nM`, `℃`). Field content is highly standardized, such as subject information, adverse events, and efficacy evaluation indicators in clinical trial reports.

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on "Deployment and Upgrades"

Regulatory submission documents are large, contain many specialized terms, and have a low update frequency. These characteristics impose specific requirements on deployment and upgrades. First, parsing and vectorizing large-scale documents require sufficient computing resources and stable file processing capabilities to prevent data import failures due to oversized files or parsing timeouts. Second, the numerous specialized abbreviations and specific units of measurement in the documents require the model to maintain accurate understanding and recall of this information after upgrades. This depends on high-quality text segmentation and vector embedding models. The low update frequency means that after a model upgrade, compatibility and consistency with existing documents are particularly important to avoid recall deviations of historical data due to model iterations. Additionally, the regulatory submission process has extremely high data security and compliance requirements. Deployment and upgrade processes must ensure data is not leaked or lost, and that service continuity is not affected.

## Configuration Settings

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `UPLOAD_FILE_MAX_SIZE` | `1000 MB` | Regulatory submission documents often contain many charts, tables, and detailed descriptions, leading to large individual file sizes. |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `600 seconds` | Processing large PDF or Word documents requires longer parsing times to prevent import failures due to timeouts. |
| `maxContext` | `800–1200 characters` | Key information in regulatory submission documents often spans longer paragraphs, ensuring contextual completeness. |
| `Chunk size` | `400 characters` | Balances semantic completeness with indexing efficiency, suitable for highly specialized text content. |
| `Recall count` | `Top 5 entries` | Ensures that under complex queries, enough relevant high-quality passages can be recalled from a large number of documents. |
| `Similarity threshold` | Calibrate by actual measurement | Determine the balance between recall precision and recall rate through actual testing, based on the specialized nature of regulatory submission documents. |

## Three Common Mistakes

*   After an upgrade, a chat window error `Failed to parse URL from /model/getProviders` usually indicates that new model interface or configuration paths have changed, and old configurations were not correctly synchronized.
*   Uploading files and text datasets fails, but template import works. This may be due to the file parsing service `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` being set too low, causing large documents to time out during parsing.
*   After establishing an indexing model channel, query results show poor relevance. This may be related to inappropriate `Chunk size` or `Similarity threshold` configurations, failing to effectively capture the semantic characteristics of regulatory submission documents.

## How to Confirm Correct Configuration

*   Upload a 500MB PDF document containing complex charts and specialized terms. Observe whether parsing and indexing complete successfully.
*   Ask multiple questions about a clinical trial report containing specific abbreviations and units of measurement. Evaluate the model's understanding and recall accuracy of this specialized information.
*   Simulate uploading 10 documents of 200MB each concurrently. Check the system's stability and response time under high-concurrency file processing.
*   After an upgrade, randomly select historical submission documents for querying. Compare the relevance of query results before and after the upgrade to ensure no significant degradation.

The values given are common starting points and should be measured against the reader's own samples.
