# Vector Models and Indexing for High-Value Consumable Quality Documentation

## Data Characteristics for This Category

Quality documentation for high-value consumables primarily originates from manufacturer technical specifications, registration application materials, clinical trial reports, and post-market adverse event monitoring reports. These documents have a relatively low update frequency, typically changing with product iterations or regulatory requirements. Document structures are mainly structured and semi-structured, including product manuals, registration certificates, inspection reports, and risk management reports. They often contain numerous charts, specialized terminology, and abbreviations. Fields and units are highly specialized, such as material composition (%), dimensional accuracy (mm), failure modes (FMEA level), and sterilization parameters (Gy). The precision of numerical values and their context dependence are extremely high.

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on "Vector Models and Indexing"

The low update frequency of high-value consumable quality documentation means significant resources are needed for initial index construction, but subsequent maintenance costs are relatively manageable. The charts and specialized terminology within documents demand higher requirements for text segmentation and vectorization model selection. Models must effectively understand contextual semantics and process non-textual information (e.g., converting tabular data into a vectorizable format). The precision requirements for fields and units dictate that similarity recall cannot rely solely on keyword matching. Instead, it should focus on semantic relevance to avoid incorrect recalls due to unit or numerical range differences. Furthermore, due to compliance and traceability needs, index accuracy and interpretability are crucial. Recall results must precisely point to the original document's source.

## Configuration Guidelines

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `chunk_size` | `512 characters` | High-value consumable documents often have strong semantic integrity within paragraphs. This length balances content completeness with vector precision. |
| `overlap_size` | `64 characters` | Appropriate overlap helps capture cross-paragraph semantic connections, reducing information loss from segmentation. |
| `embedding_model` | `text-embedding-ada-002` or compatible with `bge-large-zh` | This balances understanding of specialized Chinese terminology with recall effectiveness, performing well in the biomedical field. |
| `recall_top_k` | `top 8 results` | This ensures coverage of sufficient potentially relevant document segments for complex queries, while avoiding excessive irrelevant information. |
| `similarity_threshold` | `0.78` | This ensures recall results are highly relevant to the query content, reducing the false positive rate. Fine-tuning based on actual performance is necessary. |
| `rerank_top_n` | `top 3 results` | This refines results through a secondary sort after initial recall, improving the accuracy and relevance of the final answer. |

## Three Common Mistakes

*   The query results contain many irrelevant or low-relevance document segments. This occurs when `similarity_threshold` is set too low, leading to an overly broad recall range that fails to effectively filter noise.
*   Some critical information is missing or the answer is incomplete. This can happen if `chunk_size` is set too small, causing semantics to be fragmented and individual segments unable to convey a complete meaning.
*   Newly uploaded document content cannot be retrieved. This may be due to file parsing failure or the index rebuild not being triggered. The `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` parameter might be too short, causing large document parsing to time out.

## How to Verify Configuration

*   Select representative high-value consumable quality queries. Observe the `recall_top_k` document segments recalled, verifying their content is highly relevant to the query and free of obvious semantic breaks.
*   For queries containing specialized terminology, abbreviations, or numerical ranges, check the `rerank_top_n` sorted results. Confirm that the most relevant segments are ranked highest.
*   Upload and index a new high-value consumable document. Immediately perform a query test to confirm that key information from the new document can be accurately recalled, and assess the timeliness of the index update.

The values provided are common starting points and should be measured against the reader's own samples.
