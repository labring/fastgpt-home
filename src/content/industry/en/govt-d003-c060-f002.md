# Ledger Summary and Report Generation for Warehousing and Logistics: Vector Models and Indexes

## What the Data for This Category Looks Like
Warehousing and logistics ledger data comes from warehouse management systems (WMS), electronic or paper inbound and outbound bills, monthly inventory reports, and transportation trajectory logs. Data update rhythms vary significantly. Inbound and outbound operation data is synchronized in real time. Daily settlement ledgers are generated each day. Monthly summary reports are created each month. Most documents use structured table formats, with fields including material code, material name, specification model, inbound quantity, outbound quantity, current inventory, operation time, warehouse area number, unit of measurement, and more. Units of measurement include pieces, boxes, tons, cubic meters, kilograms, and others.

## What Constraints Do These Characteristics Impose on the Vector Models and Indexes Stage
Warehousing logistics ledgers are mostly structured tabular data, with a small number of text-based operation records. Data update rhythms vary significantly, including real-time synchronized inbound and outbound operation data, as well as batch reports for daily settlement and monthly summaries. This type of data has many fields and clear units of measurement. Individual ledger entries are moderately sized, but the overall dataset scale is large.
The vector models and indexes stage must retain field associations for structured data, support incremental indexing to match real-time and batch update rhythms, unify field units of measurement to avoid vector matching deviations, and avoid breaking the structured logic of tables during chunking.

## How to Configure the Settings
This configuration applies to FastGPT v4.17.0.

| Configuration Item | Recommended Setting | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `maxToken` | 8000 | Adapt to the context length requirements of warehousing ledger summary reports, to accommodate multiple retrieved ledger entry contents |
| `defaultToken` | 2000 | Used for vector extraction of single ledger data, balancing feature retention and model computing overhead |
| Chunk Length | 800–1200 characters | Adapt to the structured entry length of individual warehousing ledgers, avoid splitting that breaks the association of core fields such as material codes and inventory values |
| Overlap Length | 50–80 characters | Retain field association information for adjacent chunks, prevent key data from breaking across chunks |
| Similarity Threshold | 0.72–0.85 | Match the structured characteristics of ledgers, filter low-correlation results, and adapt to precise material and inventory data retrieval |
| Single Search Limit | Top 10 entries | Cover the core ledger entries required for a single summary report, avoid redundant data occupying context space |

## Three Common Misconfigurations
- Phenomenon: A large number of irrelevant material entries appear in retrieval results, and the scope cannot be narrowed by adjusting parameters. Cause: The similarity threshold is set too low, failing to match the structured field characteristics of warehousing ledgers, resulting in low-correlation data being recalled.
- Phenomenon: Index parameters cannot be adjusted directly in the retrieval debugging page, and must be operated in the knowledge base content management page. Cause: After version updates, the retrieval debugging entry was migrated to the knowledge base content editing page, and operational path awareness was not updated synchronously.
- Phenomenon: After uploading monthly ledger reports, vector extraction for some structured fields is empty. Cause: The chunk length is set too large, which destroys the structured format of the table during splitting, leading to field parsing failure.

## How to Confirm the Configuration Is Properly Set
- Upload a typical single warehousing ledger file, check whether the vector retrieval results include complete core fields such as material codes and inventory values.
- Trigger an incremental index update, wait for the preset duration, then retrieve real-time inbound and outbound data to confirm whether data synchronization is completed.
- Adjust the similarity threshold to verify whether the correlation of retrieval results meets the business requirements of ledger summary.
- View the index logs to confirm there are no error messages such as field parsing failures or chunking exceptions.
