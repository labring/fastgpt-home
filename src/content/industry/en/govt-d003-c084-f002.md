# Ledger Summary and Report Generation for Commodity Trading: Vector Models and Indexes

## What the data for this category looks like
The ledger data for commodity trading mainly comes from internal business systems, port delivery documents, warehouse receipts, and trade contract files. Data updates follow the rhythm of single-batch transactions or natural days. A single ledger document includes fields such as product category, specification model, transaction quantity, settlement unit price, delivery date, storage location, and trading counterparty. Units are mostly physical and pricing units such as tons, cubic meters, and yuan per ton. Some files are in structured CSV format, while others are scanned copies or PDF versions of electronic paper ledgers.

## Constraints on Vector Models and Indexing
Commodity trading ledger data has many fields tied to multiple business scenarios. Long text contracts and documents increase chunking difficulty. Reasonable splitting is required to preserve semantic integrity. Structured CSV data has a large volume and standardized fields, with a high risk of duplicate or redundant entries. Basic deduplication must be completed before indexing. Data updated per batch or natural day creates frequency pressure for incremental indexing. The system must adapt to batch processing efficiency. Different commodity categories have large field differences. Field-level vector association must be retained to avoid semantic confusion across categories.

## Configuration Settings
| Configuration Item | Recommended Value Range | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Chunk Length | 800–1200 characters | Adapts to the field combinations of commodity trading ledgers and the semantic integrity of long-text documents, avoiding information breakage in single chunks |
| Overlap Length | 100–150 characters | Retains business-related information across chunks, such as trading counterparties and delivery dates that span multiple chunks |
| `defaultToken` | 4096 | Adapts to the default input length of most vector models, covering the token requirements of conventional ledger chunks |
| `maxToken` | 8192 | Processes long-text trade contracts and warehouse receipts, avoiding truncation of critical business information |
| Similarity Threshold | 0.75–0.85 | Distinguishes similar entries of the same commodity category from confusing entries of different categories, meeting the precise matching requirements of trading scenarios |
| Single Search Limit | Top 10 entries | Balances recall efficiency and full information coverage for ledger summaries |

## Three Common Misconfigurations
- Phenomenon: Vector calculation scores are abnormally high, and multiple results have identical scores. Cause: Deduplication preprocessing is not performed for structured fields of commodity trading ledgers. Duplicate transaction entries lead to highly overlapping vector features.
- Phenomenon: After uploading 100,000 CSV entries to the knowledge base, the total data volume after vectorization is only more than 90,000 entries. Cause: The indexing process automatically filters entries with missing fields or abnormal formats. Entries with empty required fields such as delivery date and unit price in some ledgers are skipped.
- Phenomenon: Vectorization processing of large warehouse receipt PDF files takes too long. Cause: Chunk length and batch processing parameters are not adjusted. Too many chunks processed in a single batch leads to excessive computing resource usage.

## How to Verify Proper Configuration
- Import a single standardized commodity trading ledger CSV file, check that the number of entries after indexing matches the number of entries in the source data, and confirm that the filtering rules are suitable for the current business data.
- Submit a query request for ledger summary, check that the returned vector recall results match the fields of the source data as expected, and confirm that the similarity threshold setting meets the business matching accuracy requirements.
- Test the incremental indexing function, import new daily transaction ledger data, and check that the index update time meets the business rhythm requirements.
- View the vector model call logs in FastGPT v4.17.0, and confirm that the `defaultToken` and `maxToken` configurations do not trigger text truncation errors.
