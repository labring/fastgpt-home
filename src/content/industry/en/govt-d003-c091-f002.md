# General Contracting of Building Construction Projects: Ledger Summary and Report Generation - Vector Models and Indexes

## What the data for this category looks like
Ledger data for general contracting of building construction projects originates primarily from project management systems, material delivery receipts, weekly progress reports, acceptance documents, and similar sources. Data update frequency aligns with business milestones: updated in real time when materials are delivered, updated weekly for weekly progress reports, and summarized monthly for monthly cost ledgers. Documents fall into two categories: structured tables and unstructured reports. Structured fields include project number, project name, construction team, material usage (tons), individual building floor area (square meters), acceptance status, and more. Some documents include associated on-site photos.

## What constraints these characteristics impose on vector models and indexing
Dispersed, multi-source data requires indexing to support cross-system aggregation, to avoid missing cross-departmental associated ledgers. Uneven update frequencies demand an incremental indexing strategy: only regenerate vectors for changed content to reduce computing costs. The presence of specialized fields and units requires vector models to retain term semantics, without damaging the integrity of professional expressions. A single project can have hundreds of ledger entries. Generating reports requires integrating multiple data types, which imposes clear requirements on the context window.

## How to set configurations
| Configuration Item | Recommended Setting | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chunk Length | 800–1200 characters | Individual entries in building construction ledgers are usually hundreds of characters long. Excessively long chunks will lose contextual connections, while excessively short chunks will damage the integrity of professional terms |
| Overlap Length | 100–150 characters | Ledger entries contain consecutive specialized fields. Overlap preserves semantic connections between fields such as material model and usage |
| Similarity Threshold | 0.72–0.80 | Building construction professional terms have high recognition rates. A threshold that is too low will introduce irrelevant ledger entries, while a threshold that is too high will miss relevant content |
| Single Search Limit | Top 8 entries | Ledger entries for a single project are concentrated in hundreds of entries. The top 8 entries cover core associated content and avoid redundant data interfering with report generation |
| `maxToken` | 8000 token | Generating building construction reports requires integrating multiple types of ledger data. 8000 tokens can accommodate the core content of 3 to 4 complete ledgers |
| `defaultToken` | 2000 token | Token requirement for vector generation of a single ledger entry, which fits the input limits of most open-source vector models |

## Three common misconfigurations
- Issue: Professional terms are split during index generation, or fields such as material model and area are incomplete in retrieval results. Cause: Chunk length was not adjusted for building construction ledger specialized fields, leading to key terms being split across different chunks.
- Issue: The number of retrieval results returned is far lower than expected, or a context overflow prompt appears during report generation. Cause: `maxToken` was not set reasonably, leading to integrated ledger data exceeding the model's context limits, and the system automatically truncates key content.
- Issue: Retrieval results vary significantly across different vector models, making it impossible to unify retrieval logic. Cause: Vector models from different semantic spaces were mixed, leading to mismatched vector dimensions between the index and queries, with no unified retrieval standard possible.

## How to confirm proper configuration
- Upload a single building construction material delivery ledger, and check that chunked text retains complete specialized fields such as material model and usage, with no forced truncation.
- Enter a query such as "View the March steel material delivery ledger for XX project", and verify that the similarity scores of returned results fall within the 0.72–0.80 range.
- Generate a single project progress report, and check that the generated content integrates all associated ledger entries, with no context missing prompts.
- Test with different vector models in FastGPT v4.17.0, and confirm semantic consistency of retrieval results, with no significant result deviations.
