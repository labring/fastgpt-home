# Ledger Consolidation and Report Generation for Oil and Gas Exploration and Development: Vector Models and Indexing

## What This Category of Data Looks Like
Ledger data for oil and gas exploration and development originates from first-hand materials including drilling site logs, well logging scans, oil test analysis reports, and reserve calculation documents. Data update cadence adjusts based on the exploration phase: updated by work shift during the drilling phase, and updated by single well or block batch during the oil test and reserve assessment phases. Individual documents contain fixed fields such as well ID, operating depth, lithology description, porosity, and permeability. Units cover professional measurement standards including meters, millidarcies, and cubic meters. Document length varies widely, from short on-site records of a few hundred characters to comprehensive assessment reports of tens of thousands of characters.

## What Constraints Do These Characteristics Impose on Vector Models and Indexing
The wide range of document lengths requires vector encoding and chunking strategies to adapt to different text volumes, from short records to long reports, to avoid redundant encoding of short texts or semantic fragmentation of long texts. Fixed units and terms in professional fields require vector models to have semantic alignment capabilities for the oil and gas exploration domain, to ensure that vector mappings of professional expressions such as well ID, permeability, and porosity align with domain consensus. Differentiated update cadences require indexes to support incremental refreshes by work shift or block batch, to reduce resource consumption from full index rebuilds. Data sources that contain both structured fields and unstructured text require indexes to support hybrid query logic that combines structured attribute matching and unstructured vector retrieval.

## Configuration Settings
| Configuration Item | Recommended Range | Rationale |
| ---- | ---- | ---- |
| Chunk Length | 800–1200 characters | Adapts to the full range of ledger text lengths for oil and gas exploration, from short records to long reports, and ensures semantic integrity |
| Overlap Length | 100–150 characters | Prevents chunk truncation of professional terms, and maintains contextual continuity across chunks |
| Similarity Threshold | 0.72–0.80 | Matches the similarity range for domain-specific semantics, and filters irrelevant exploration data |
| Single Search Limit | Top 8 results | Balances retrieval accuracy and response speed, and adapts to ledger queries that associate multiple fields |
| `maxToken` | 8000 | Adapts to vector encoding and context concatenation for long documents, and aligns with the parameter limit for FastGPT v4.17.0 |
| `defaultToken` | 1024 | Controls the content length of single-round report generation, and aligns with the output boundaries for ledger consolidation |

## Three Common Misconfigurations
- Retrieval results do not return traceability information such as well ID or report name of associated documents. This occurs when the binding configuration between vector indexes and structured metadata is not enabled, and only text vectors are stored without associating original document fields.
- Vector retrieval results do not match the semantics of professional terms. This occurs when a general-purpose vector model is used without adaptation for oil and gas exploration professional terms, leading to deviations in vector mappings for professional expressions.
- Vector encoding of long assessment reports is truncated, and the generated summary report lacks content from the latter half. This occurs when chunk length is set too small or the `maxToken` parameter value is insufficient, leading to incomplete encoding of semantic information for long texts.

## How to Verify Proper Configuration
- Upload a single short ledger record and a long assessment report, check that the number of chunked text blocks matches expectations, and confirm that the chunk length and overlap length configurations are active.
- Enter a professional query such as "Porosity data for a specific well", check that the similarity scores of retrieval results fall within the preset range, and confirm that the similarity threshold configuration is reasonable.
- Initiate a batch incremental index task, check that index refresh speed and resource usage align with work cadence, and confirm that the incremental refresh strategy is configured correctly.
- View the metadata fields of retrieval results, confirm that traceability information such as well ID and report name is returned along with vector results, and confirm that the structured metadata binding configuration is active.

The values provided here are common starting points, and should be validated against the deploying organization’s own samples.
