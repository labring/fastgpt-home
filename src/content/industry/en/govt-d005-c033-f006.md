# Policy and Regulatory Basis Q&A for Tendering and Procurement Compliance Management: Citation Sources and Traceability

## What the data for this category looks like
This category of data covers documents such as procurement plans, requirement specifications, bidding documents, bid evaluation records, contracts, acceptance documents, and supplier complaint materials generated by central state-owned enterprise procurement centers, bidding offices, business requirement departments, audit and discipline inspection departments, and bidding agencies. Data sources include enterprise procurement platforms, public resource trading platforms, supplier databases, contract management systems, and audit issue databases, with a daily update frequency. Documents include fields such as procurement method, budget basis, scoring method, supplier qualification conditions, and contract performance clauses, with no fixed unified format, adjusted according to business scenarios.

## What constraints these characteristics impose on the "citation sources and traceability" link
Decentralized data sources and daily updates require the traceability link to support integration with multiple types of business systems and real-time pulling of the latest content. Documents have no fixed unified format, requiring adaptation to fragment positioning and citation for multiple types of text. Fields cover core procurement compliance elements, requiring precise association of traceability results with the original content of corresponding fields. Audit and discipline inspection scenarios require traceability results to be directly linked to original documents, rather than relying solely on abstract summaries.

## Configuration Settings
Reference only; verify against the target FastGPT release and deployment documentation.

| Configuration Item | Recommended Setting | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `quoteMaxToken` | 800–1200 token | Covers the complete content of a single segment of tendering and procurement compliance basis, avoiding truncation of key clauses |
| `responseData` | Enabled | Synchronizes original citation fragments to meet traceability requirements of audit and discipline inspection departments |
| `detail` | Display as original document fragments | Restores the context of cited content, facilitating verification of the accuracy of compliance basis |
| Single search limit | Top 5 results | Matches the number of core basis documents for daily compliance checks, avoiding information overload |
| Recall matching threshold | Calibrated based on actual testing | Adapts compliance basis matching accuracy for different business scenarios |

## Three Common Configuration Mistakes
- Phenomenon: The answer does not display original database fragments, only presents summarized content. Cause: The `responseData` configuration is not enabled, and original data fragments are not synchronized.
- Phenomenon: Cited policy clauses are truncated, making it impossible to fully display compliance basis. Cause: The `quoteMaxToken` setting value is too small, failing to cover the full length of a single segment of compliance basis.
- Phenomenon: Too many citation sources are recalled, leading to redundant answers that do not meet the streamlined requirements of audit traceability. Cause: The single search limit is set too high, failing to match the reasonable quantity for daily compliance checks.

## How to Confirm Proper Configuration
- Upload a test document containing a bidding document and its corresponding policy basis, initiate a compliance basis Q&A, and check whether the answer includes original citation fragments.
- Adjust the value of `quoteMaxToken`, initiate a Q&A containing long clauses, and verify that the clause content can be fully displayed.
- Check system logs to confirm that each Q&A can pull the latest data from the corresponding business system with no update delay.
- Verify the number of recalled citation sources, confirm that it falls within the reasonable range for daily compliance checks, and can be adjusted according to business needs.
