# Policy and Regulatory Basis Q&A for Food Safety Supervision and Sampling: Citation Sources and Traceability

## What the data for this category looks like
The core data for this category includes five document types: sampling plans, sampling slips, inspection reports, unqualified food verification and disposal reports, and risk control notices. Data sources are the food safety sampling and monitoring system, the State Administration for Market Regulation’s sampling result inquiry system, the laboratory information management system (LIMS) of inspection institutions, and account books of food production and business enterprises. Data is updated monthly. Each individual document has dedicated fields such as sampling number, inspection items, unqualified judgment basis, and disposal progress. Documents from different sources have inconsistent fields, and no unified standardized format template exists.

## What constraints do these characteristics impose on the "citation sources and traceability" link?
The data for this category is multi-source and scattered, has a fixed update cycle, and inconsistent document fields. These factors impose multiple constraints on citation traceability.
First, data comes from multiple independent systems. Dedicated source identifiers for each document must be accurately associated to avoid confusion between sampling data from different systems.
Second, the monthly update frequency requires the knowledge base synchronization cycle to align with business rhythms. This prevents outdated or redundant data.
Third, fields differ across documents. Unique identifiers such as sampling numbers and inspection report numbers must be extracted as traceability basis. General fields cannot be relied on for matching.
Finally, core documents involve unqualified food disposal. Traceability accuracy directly affects regulatory compliance, and mismatches or omissions are not permitted.

## Three Common Misconfigurations
- The number of citations displayed on the page does not match the number passed to the model. The symptom is that the backend log shows 310 citations, but only 30 are displayed on the frontend interface. The cause is that the frontend display citation limit was not configured correctly, resulting in truncation of the complete traceability data actually passed to the model.
- Citation formatting cannot be removed correctly. The symptom is that citation markers always appear in the generated response. The cause is that the `detail` configuration was set to disabled, or the interface switch to hide citations was not checked, resulting in mandatory retention of traceability content.
- Citation content does not match the dedicated documents for this category. The symptom is that the response cites policy documents unrelated to sampling. The cause is that no data source filtering rules were set, and the search was not limited to dedicated data sources such as the food safety sampling and monitoring system and the LIMS of inspection institutions.

## How to Confirm Proper Configuration
- View the knowledge base synchronization log to confirm that dedicated documents such as sampling plans and inspection reports are automatically synchronized monthly, and the synchronization cycle aligns with the business update frequency.
- Submit a test query, verify that the source fields cited in the response include dedicated identifiers such as sampling numbers and inspection report numbers, and confirm that the `responseData` configuration is effective.
- Check the backend log to confirm that the number of citations passed to the model matches the number displayed on the frontend, and verify that the truncation logic of configurations such as `quoteMaxToken` is correct.
- Attempt the operation to hide citations, confirm that citation formatting can be removed as needed in the response, and verify that the relevant settings are effective.
