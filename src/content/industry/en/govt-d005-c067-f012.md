# Policy and Regulatory Basis Q&A for Fertilizers and Pesticides: Deployment and Upgrade

## What the data for this category looks like
Policy and regulatory basis data for the fertilizer and pesticide sector is primarily sourced from official public normative documents, industry standard announcements, and agricultural materials supervision bulletins issued by the Institute for the Control of Agrochemicals of the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs, as well as provincial and municipal agricultural and rural bureaus.
The update rhythm adjusts dynamically with the release of national and local agricultural materials regulatory policies, with no fixed cycle.
Documents are primarily structured official papers, including fields such as policy document number, issuing body, effective time limit, applicable categories (e.g., herbicides, compound fertilizers), core clauses, and violation disposal requirements. Some supporting documents include quantitative indicators such as pesticide residue limits and application concentration standards.

## What constraints do these characteristics impose on the deployment and upgrade process
The structured characteristics and dynamic update requirements of fertilizer and pesticide policy data impose clear constraints on the deployment and upgrade process.
Metadata with multiple fields must have extraction rules specified in knowledge base configuration to ensure accurate filtering by policy timeliness and applicable region during retrieval.
Long-text policy clauses must adapt to long-context processing logic to avoid loss of core clauses due to information truncation.
Cross-regional policy documents require a multi-path synchronization mechanism to ensure complete collection of regulatory requirements for different regions.
Policies with no fixed update cycle must be bound to automatic synchronization trigger rules to prevent knowledge base content from lagging behind the latest regulatory requirements. This setup must also adapt to the FastGPT v4.17.0 metadata management interface.

## Three Common Configuration Mistakes
- Phenomenon: After deployment on a Windows system and restart, all policy data sources and model parameters configured in the knowledge base are lost. Cause: The FastGPT data directory is not mounted to the system's persistent storage, and configuration files in the temporary directory are cleared after restart.
- Phenomenon: Workflow debugging returns the `workflow error {"message":"Dangerous behavior detected"}` error. Cause: The `ROOT_KEY` permission verification is not configured, or the permission configuration does not comply with system security rules, triggering FastGPT's abnormal operation interception mechanism.
- Phenomenon: Geographic-specific clauses cannot be matched when retrieving policy documents. Cause: The metadata filtering function is not enabled in the knowledge base configuration, and the geographic application scope of the policy is not used as a retrieval dimension, resulting in failure to accurately recall cross-regional policies.

## How to Verify That the Configuration Is Correct
- Execute the FastGPT local startup script, check the console logs to confirm that configuration items such as `MONGODB_URI` and `REDIS_URL` are loaded normally, with no connection failure errors.
- Upload a fertilizer and pesticide policy original document to the knowledge base, trigger automatic parsing and indexing, and verify that the parsed metadata includes fields such as policy document number and effective date, with field integrity meeting configuration requirements.
- Initiate a policy retrieval test, enter a query term that includes region and effective time, confirm that retrieval results can be sorted and filtered according to the configured filtering dimensions.
- Restart the deployment server, access the FastGPT management backend again, confirm that saved knowledge base configurations and model parameters are not lost, and verify that the persistent storage configuration is effective.
