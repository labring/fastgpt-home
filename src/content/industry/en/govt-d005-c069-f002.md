# Hazardous Chemicals Storage and Transportation Policy and Regulatory Basis Q&A: Vector Models and Indexing

## What This Type of Data Looks Like
Data on policy and regulatory basis for hazardous chemicals storage and transportation comes primarily from official specifications and supervision rules published by the Ministry of Emergency Management, Ministry of Transport, and Ministry of Ecology and Environment. Updates align with the release of new policies and adjustments to regulatory requirements. Individual documents typically include fields such as document number, issuing authority, effective date, clause classification, applicable storage and transportation scenarios, and violation judgment standards. Some documents include quantitative indicators such as storage volume thresholds and transportation distance limits. Document structures have clear hierarchical levels, and professional terminology density is high.

## What Constraints Do These Characteristics Impose on Vector Models and Indexing
The effective date field in hazardous chemicals storage and transportation policy documents requires indexing to support time-based filtering of retrieval scope, preventing expired policies from being retrieved. Individual documents have high character counts, so the system must adapt to long-text chunking logic to preserve complete semantics. Quantitative indicators present in documents require vector models to accurately encode numerical semantics to ensure retrieval accuracy. Policy updates do not follow a fixed cycle, so the system must support incremental index construction to reduce update costs. The high density of professional terminology requires vector models to meet minimum standards for encoding specialized domain semantics.

## How to Configure Settings
| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Chunk Length | 800–1200 characters | Hazardous chemicals storage and transportation policy documents have high individual character counts. This length balances semantic completeness and retrieval accuracy |
| Overlap Length | 100–150 characters | Prevents cross-chunk policy clauses from being split, ensuring complete regulatory requirements are covered during retrieval |
| Similarity Threshold | 0.72–0.78 | Policy texts contain large volumes of professional terminology and fixed phrasing. This range filters low-relevance results while retaining compliant content |
| Single Search Limit | Top 8 results | Hazardous chemicals storage and transportation policies are linked to many clauses. This quantity covers major regulatory scenarios |
| maxToken | 8000 token | Policy document chunks require integration of multiple clause contents. This value supports sufficient retrieval results |
| defaultToken | Calibrated via actual testing | Token consumption and model load vary across different deployment environments. Adjust based on actual conditions |

## Three Common Misconfigurations
- Symptom: Retrieval returns matching policy documents, but the large language model outputs "no relevant content found". Cause: The maxToken parameter is set too low, so retrieved document fragments are not fully included in the model input range.
- Symptom: An out-of-memory error occurs during index construction. Cause: Chunk length is set too large, with the character count per chunk exceeding the vector model's encoding limit.
- Symptom: Expired policies with low relevance make up an excessive share of retrieval results. Cause: No effective date filtering retrieval rule is configured, leading to older policies being prioritized for retrieval.
