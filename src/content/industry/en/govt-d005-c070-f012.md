# Policy and Regulatory Basis Q&A for Power Generation Equipment Manufacturing: Deployment and Upgrade

## What the data for this category looks like
Policy and regulatory basis data for power generation equipment manufacturing mainly comes from special policies, industry standards and supporting rules for the equipment manufacturing industry released by the National Energy Administration and the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, as well as localized industrial support documents issued by local governments. Data updates occur in real time alongside policy releases. Individual document lengths vary from hundreds-of-word notices to full policy interpretations spanning tens of thousands of words. Document structures include fields such as policy document number, issuing authority, effective date, applicable equipment categories (such as wind power, nuclear power, thermal power equipment), and subsidy standards. Field units include document number formats, date formats, currency units, and other relevant units.

## What constraints do these characteristics impose on deployment and upgrade
Power generation equipment manufacturing policy data has scattered sources, wide variations in document lengths, and frequent updates. Deployment processes must support multi-source data access and incremental update logic. Policies for specific subcategories have clear equipment type boundaries, so knowledge base index configuration must use layered classification by equipment category to avoid cross-category retrieval interference. Wide variations in individual policy document lengths require vector storage sharding configurations to adapt to different text embedding lengths, while reserving sufficient storage quota to handle ongoing growth in policy documents.

## Three Common Misconfigurations
- Restarting the deployment server causes configured policy knowledge base and model association relationships to disappear. Cause: Core configurations such as `MONGODB_URI` are only loaded in temporary sessions and not written to persistent configuration files, resulting in loss of temporary data after restart.
- A `Dangerous behavior` error appears during workflow debugging. Cause: The value of `DB_MAX_LINK` is not restricted, leading to concurrent connections exceeding the threshold configured by the database, triggering the security intercept mechanism.
- Timeout occurs when calling the embedding model to generate vectors for policy documents. Cause: `REDIS_URL` is not configured as a cache middleware, so high-frequency retrieval requests directly access the database, exceeding the database's response threshold and causing a timeout.
