# Policy and Regulatory Basis Q&A for State-owned Asset Management Companies: Deployment and Upgrade

## What this category of data looks like
Policy and regulatory basis data for state-owned asset management companies mainly comes from normative documents issued by state-owned asset supervision and administration institutions at all levels, internal compliance system compilations of the company, and annual state-owned asset operation and management documents. Data updates have no fixed cycle, and are synchronized with regulatory policy adjustments or internal system revisions. Most documents use the standard official red-headed document format, including fields such as document number, issuing entity, effective date, and clause hierarchy (article, paragraph, sub-paragraph). The length of a single document ranges from hundreds to tens of thousands of words. There is no unified fixed document length unit, and segments follow the natural content divisions of the file.

## What constraints do these characteristics impose on deployment and upgrade?
The characteristics of this category of policy data impose multiple constraints on deployment and upgrade.
Long-text and clearly hierarchical document structures require adapting long-context processing configurations during deployment. This prevents truncation of core policy clauses and preserves Q&A response completeness.
Non-fixed update cycles require supporting incremental synchronization mechanisms during the upgrade process. This reduces downtime from full data re-import.
Metadata fields including effective date and applicable scope require configuring corresponding metadata extraction rules. This ensures accurate matching of policy application scenarios during Q&A.
Wide variation in document length requires adjusting chunking logic to fit policy documents of different lengths. This avoids semantic fragmentation that harms answer accuracy.

## Three Common Misconfigurations
- Phenomenon: After updating the version, the length of policy Q&A responses becomes significantly shorter, and cannot fully cover policy clause content. Cause: Long-context adaptation configuration was not synchronized for the update, causing the model inference length to be restricted by default settings.
- Phenomenon: When configuring the MCP service, the parameter type is fixed as string and cannot be modified to other types. Cause: The corresponding version of the MCP service configuration template was not loaded correctly during deployment, causing the parameter verification logic to be locked.
- Phenomenon: After uploading a large policy compilation file, the parsing process times out and the request fails. Cause: The file processing timeout configuration was not adjusted, and the parsing time of a single long document exceeded the default threshold.

## How to Confirm Configurations Are Correctly Set
- Perform an intranet storage node connectivity test to confirm that the address configured for `STORAGE_S3_ENDPOINT` can read and write policy documents normally.
- Initiate multiple high-frequency policy Q&A requests to check whether cache hit rate and response time meet business expectations.
- Upload policy documents of different lengths to verify that the parsing and chunking processes have no abnormal interruptions.
- Verify that the parameter configuration of the MCP service can be modified normally, with no type locking issues.
