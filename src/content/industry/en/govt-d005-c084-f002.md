# Policy and Regulatory Basis Q&A for Commodity Trading: Vector Models and Indexes

## What the Data for This Category Looks Like
Commodity trading policy and regulatory basis data is primarily sourced from public announcements, normative documents, and industry standards issued by national development and reform commissions, commerce departments, general administration of customs, and local industry regulatory authorities. Data updates are triggered by policy release timelines, with no fixed cycle. New policies are added to the knowledge base upon implementation. Individual documents typically include fields such as document number, issuing authority, effective date, clause details, and applicable trade category scope. Some files include quantitative indicators such as tariff rates, storage billing units, and transaction margin ratios.

## Constraints Imposed on Vector Models and Index Workflows
The characteristics of commodity trading policy data impose multiple constraints on vector model and index workflows.
First, documents include structured fields such as effective date and applicable category. Indexes must support filtering recall results by time range and trade category to avoid misapplication of policy clauses across scenarios.
Second, individual policy document clauses are highly interconnected. When splitting documents, retain clause integrity to prevent semantic fragmentation that reduces retrieval accuracy.
Third, data is available in multiple formats including PDF, DOCX, and web pages. The system must adapt to multi-format parsing to fully extract text content.
Finally, updates have no fixed cycle. Indexes must support incremental synchronization to avoid resource consumption and time delays caused by full reconstruction.

## Configuration Settings
This configuration is based on the interface and functional logic of FastGPT v4.17.0.

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value Range | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Chunk Length | 800–1200 characters | Commodity policy document clauses are mostly coherent paragraphs. This range preserves semantic integrity for single clauses or related clauses |
| Overlap Length | 100–150 characters | Prevents contextual fragmentation of policy clauses after splitting, ensuring semantic coherence during retrieval |
| `maxToken` | 4096 token | Adapts to the context window limits of most open-source embedding models, while covering the core content of split policy document chunks |
| `defaultToken` | 512 token | Serves as the default context fill length during retrieval, adapting to the input requirements of most embedding models |
| Similarity Threshold | 0.75–0.85 | Policy clauses have high strictness requirements. Filter low-correlation retrieval results to avoid misapplication of unrelated policies |
| Single Search Limit | Top 8 results | The number of commodity trading policies is relatively concentrated. A small number of results covers the policy basis scope required by users |

## Three Common Misconfigurations
- Symptom: After creating a new knowledge base, it remains stuck in "Index Building" status with no progress over time. Cause: Failed to adapt to multi-format parsing of commodity policy documents, or set the chunk length too small, creating an excessive number of chunks that exceed index construction resource limits.
- Symptom: After switching the embedding model, retrieval tests return an error indicating semantic matching failure. Cause: Did not adjust the similarity threshold for quantitative fields in commodity policies such as tariff rates and storage fees, leading to high-correlation results being incorrectly filtered.
- Symptom: Retrieval results only return vector data, and do not include structured fields from the original policy documents such as document number and effective date. Cause: Did not enable the index's metadata synchronization configuration, only storing vector features without associating structured fields from the original documents.

## How to Confirm Proper Configuration
- Upload a single commodity policy document, view the chunk preview results, and confirm that splitting does not damage the integrity of core clauses.
- Submit a retrieval request related to policies, verify that returned result metadata includes fields such as document number and effective date, and confirm that the `defaultToken` setting matches the input requirements of the current embedding model.
- Adjust the similarity threshold, observe changes in retrieval result relevance, and confirm that the threshold meets business strictness requirements.
- Add a test policy document to trigger incremental indexing, and confirm that index progress updates normally.

The values provided are common starting points and should be measured against the reader's own samples.
