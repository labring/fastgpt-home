# Policy and Regulatory Basis Q&A for Oil and Gas Exploration and Development: HTTP Interfaces and External Systems

## What this category of data looks like
Policy and regulatory basis data for oil and gas exploration and development comes from public regulatory documents issued by the Ministry of Natural Resources, the State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission of the State Council, and oil and gas industry authorities. Update timing aligns with the release of new policies, with no fixed cycle. Document structures are mostly clause-based, and include fields such as policy document number, issuing authority, effective date, applicable exploration blocks, and core compliance clauses. Field formats follow specific rules: document numbers use a combination of Chinese characters and Arabic numerals, dates use the YYYY-MM-DD format, and blocks are marked with longitude-latitude ranges or administrative region names. The length of a single policy document is mostly 800 to 1500 characters, while some industry-specific policies can reach 3000 characters.

## What constraints these characteristics impose on the "HTTP Interfaces and External Systems" link
The non-fixed update cycle of policy data requires interface cache duration to match policy effective cycles, to avoid returning expired content. The structured multi-field feature requires passing screening parameters such as exploration blocks and policy types via `variables` in HTTP requests, to ensure the interface returns compliance policies for the corresponding region. The long document pagination feature requires configuring the number of items returned per single request, limiting the scale of content returned per request to avoid interface timeouts. The specific field format requirements require format verification after receiving `responseData`, to ensure extracted policy content meets compliance requirements for oil and gas exploration and development.

## Three common errors
- Phenomenon: A `Failed to connect to jyfkk:1433` error is returned when calling the external policy database. Cause: Dedicated connection parameters for the oil and gas exploration and development policy database are not configured, and the correct data source type and access permissions are not specified.
- Phenomenon: The reply contains both AI-generated content and HTTP interface return results. Cause: The FastGPT built-in generation switch is not turned off, and `responseData` is not directly mapped as the final reply content.
- Phenomenon: The policy analysis results returned when calling other models do not meet expectations. Cause: Dedicated screening parameters for oil and gas exploration policies are not passed in `variables`, and the model analysis scope is not limited to public compliance documents.

## How to confirm the configuration is complete
- Send a test request, check if the returned `responseData` includes required fields such as policy_number and effective_date, with no missing or incorrectly formatted fields.
- Check if the `Authorization` header carries a valid authorization token, and the interface returns a status code of 200, with no 401 or 403 permission errors.
- Verify that the exploration block parameters passed in the custom `variables` are correctly recognized by the interface, and the returned policy content only includes compliance clauses for the corresponding region.
- Test after turning off the built-in generation switch, send a user question, and confirm that the reply only includes the policy content returned by the HTTP interface, with no additional AI-generated text.
