# Policy and Regulatory Basis Q&A for Oil and Gas Exploration and Development: Workflow Orchestration

Reference only; verify against the target FastGPT release and deployment documentation.

## What This Category’s Data Looks Like
Oil and gas exploration and development policy and regulatory basis data comes from official regulatory documents released by the Ministry of Natural Resources and the National Energy Administration, plus internal compliance manuals from central oil enterprises. Official policies are revised quarterly. Internal policies are updated irregularly alongside business adjustments. Document structure includes issuing document number, effective date, applicable exploration and development stage, clause number, and original compliance requirement text. Fields include compliance clause ID, applicable block scope, and penalty threshold. Units are clause character count and applicable area in square kilometers.

## Constraints on Workflow Orchestration
Oil and gas exploration and development policy data is sourced from multiple channels. These include official regulatory documents and internal compliance manuals from central oil enterprises. Update cadences vary significantly across different data sources. Documents are organized into hierarchical clauses grouped by exploration and development stage and block scope.
These traits require the workflow to support incremental synchronization configuration for multiple data sources. It must filter and match policy clauses based on a project’s affiliated block. It must adjust chunking logic for hierarchical documents to avoid invalid recall of cross-stage clauses. It must also distinguish call priorities between official policies and internal compliance materials.

## Configuration Settings
| Configuration Item | Recommended Setting | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chunk Length | 800–1200 characters | Oil and gas exploration policy clauses are mostly paragraph-style. Splitting at this length preserves clause integrity and avoids cross-clause recall |
| Overlap Length | 100–200 characters | Policy clauses have associated logic. Overlap preserves contextual connections and improves recall accuracy |
| Data Source Sync Cycle | Daily incremental sync | Official policies are updated quarterly, internal policies are adjusted irregularly. Daily incremental sync balances timeliness and synchronization efficiency |
| Node Timeout | 600 seconds | Policy document retrieval pulls data across multiple sources. A longer timeout prevents mid-process request interruptions |
| `variables` Binding | Bind exploration project block ID and business stage fields | Filter and match policy clauses based on a project’s affiliated block to accurately recall relevant content |
| Node ID | Name by business stage (e.g., "Block Policy Matching") | Allows quick identification of abnormal nodes during workflow debugging and improves troubleshooting efficiency |

## Three Common Configuration Mistakes
- Symptom: Workflow node times out and reports an error. Logs show the request did not complete. Cause: No reasonable timeout configured for multi-data source synchronization. Only generic short timeout thresholds are used, which cannot cover the pull process across official and internal data sources.
- Symptom: Recalled policy clauses do not match the current exploration project’s block. Cause: No `variables` binding for the project block ID. Only global keyword searches are used, leading to recall of policies from unrelated blocks.
- Symptom: Global workflow history cannot be updated. Cause: No storage and update node for historical records configured in `variables`. This prevents session context synchronization.

## How to Confirm Proper Configuration
- Manually trigger the workflow, enter a compliance inquiry for a specific exploration block, and check if the recalled policy clauses include exclusive requirements for that block.
- View data source synchronization logs to confirm daily incremental sync tasks run normally with no failed records.
- Modify the project block ID in `variables`, and verify that the workflow’s recalled policy clauses automatically update to match the corresponding block’s content.
- Check workflow node timeout logs to confirm no unfinished requests exceed 600 seconds.
