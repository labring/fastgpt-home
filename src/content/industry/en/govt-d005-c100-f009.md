# Policy and Regulatory Basis Q&A for Oilfield Engineering Services: Model Access and Configuration

## What Data Looks Like for This Category
Oilfield engineering services policy and regulatory basis data comes primarily from the National Energy Administration, local energy regulatory departments, and internal compliance documents of state-owned enterprises responsible for the services. Updates trigger when official policies are released or revised, with no fixed schedule. Most documents are structured original policy texts, including fields like policy number, issuing authority, applicable operational links, compliance clauses, and effective date. They also use common oilfield engineering metrology standards such as meters, megapascals, person-times, and ten thousand yuan.

## Constraints Imposed on Model Access and Configuration
The structured nature of policy documents requires model access to preserve original field associations, to avoid losing binding relationships between policy numbers and applicable scenarios. Non-fixed update cycle data sources require configurations to support a manual data refresh entry, to adapt to temporary policy revisions. Content involving engineering measurement units requires models to accurately match operational parameters with policy-specified units during retrieval, to prevent compliance judgment deviations from unit conversion errors. Additionally, policy content mostly covers compliance clauses, so model outputs must restrict redundant information, retaining only policy basis directly relevant to user queries.

## Three Common Misconfigurations
- When testing a workflow, the response always ends with the phrase "FastGPT is a large language model (LLM)-based knowledge base Q&A system". This occurs because the default system prompt is not disabled in the model configuration; this prompt is built-in platform initialization content.
- A 403 permission error occurs when calling the model. This is caused by an incorrect `AIPROXY_API_TOKEN` configuration or failure to bind the corresponding service permission, resulting in failure to pass proxy verification.
- After connecting to the user system, historical conversations from different users are displayed across accounts. This occurs because independent user identity verification parameters are not configured, causing conversation data to not be bound to corresponding user identifiers.

## How to Verify Successful Configuration
- Submit a query regarding compliance for a specific oilfield operation, verify that the response only contains content related to the original policy texts, with no redundant platform identification phrases.
- Check the model call logs, confirm that call requests for `AIPROXY_API_ENDPOINT` and `AIPROXY_API_TOKEN` have been sent successfully, with no connection timeouts or permission errors.
- Adjust the `temperature` parameter, submit the same query, verify that changes in response content consistency align with expectations, to confirm the parameter takes effect.
- Upload the newly revised policy document, trigger data refresh, then submit the corresponding query, confirm that retrieved content has been updated synchronously.
