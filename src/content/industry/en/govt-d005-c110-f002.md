# Policy and Regulatory Basis Q&A for Mineral Mining and Processing: Vector Models and Indexes

## What the data for this category looks like
Policy data for the mineral mining and processing sector originates primarily from public regulatory documents issued by the Ministry of Natural Resources, the Ministry of Emergency Management, and local mine supervision authorities. It also includes internal corporate compliance records, operating procedures, and tailings pond management rules. Update frequency follows two patterns: national-level policies are updated quarterly to annually, while internal corporate documents are updated irregularly to align with adjusted operating standards or changing regulatory requirements. Document structure includes fields such as policy document number, issuing authority, effective date, applicable mining area scope, and corresponding operating link. Units used include square kilometers, cubic meters, tons, and other standard units.

## How These Characteristics Create Constraints for Vector Models and Indexing
Policy documents include clear metadata for applicable mining areas and operating links. These fields must be retained during indexing to enable precise filtering later, preventing return of policy content that does not fit the current mine scenario. Documents follow a strict clause-based structure, so chunks must not cross clauses. Crossing clauses breaks the complete semantic meaning of the policy. Update frequencies include both regular and irregular cycles, so support for incremental index updates based on release date and effective date is required to reduce redundant calculations. Multiple document fields are present, so retrieval must combine metadata filtering and vector similarity scoring for dual filtering, improving the accuracy of results.

## Configuration Settings
| Configuration Item | Recommended Setting | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chunk Length | 800–1200 characters | The content length of single clauses in mineral mining and processing policy documents falls within this range, preventing semantic fragmentation after splitting |
| Overlap Length | 100–150 characters | Retains contextual connections between adjacent chunks, ensuring logical coherence of policy clauses |
| `maxToken` | 8192 | Adapts to the maximum token limit of mainstream open-source embedding models, avoiding truncation of policy document content |
| Similarity Threshold | 0.75–0.85 | Filters policy entries that do not match the query, ensuring returned content aligns with specific mineral mining and processing scenarios |
| Single Search Limit | Top 10 entries | The number of associated policy entries related to mineral mining and processing is limited; too many entries will exceed the context window |
| Reranking | Enabled | Further filters policy clauses with higher semantic relevance, improving retrieval accuracy |

## Three Common Misconfigurations
- Issue: Local deployment of a vector model results in startup errors. The root cause is incorrect mapping of the model port in the FastGPT v4.17.0 service configuration, which prevents normal service communication.
- Issue: Configuring the Baidu embedding-v1 interface returns a 404 error. The root cause is an incorrectly entered interface address, with the official specified service endpoint path not used.
- Issue: Policy entries returned after incremental index updates do not match the submitted query. The root cause is crossing policy clauses during chunking, which breaks semantic connections and causes deviations in retrieval results.

## How to Verify Proper Configuration
- Review the vector model's runtime logs to confirm no connection timeout or permission error messages are present.
- Manually upload a mineral mining and processing policy document, and confirm that chunked content retains complete clause logic.
- Submit a specific policy query, and verify that the similarity scores of returned results fall within the preset threshold range.
- Test an incremental index update, confirming that only newly added policy files are added to the index, with no repeated processing of existing data.
