# Document and Material Drafting for State-owned Capital Operation Companies: Multi-turn Dialogue and Prompt Engineering

## What this type of data looks like
State-owned capital operation company document data mainly comes from internal stock documents and external state-owned assets supervision documents. Internal documents include investment and financing agreements, internal control manuals, compensation plans, performance appraisal reports, and similar materials. External documents are normative documents issued by state-owned assets supervision departments. The update rhythm follows the frequency of adjustments to state-owned assets supervision policies and the progress of the company's annual operating nodes, with no fixed cycle. Document structure includes document header, effective date, applicable subjects, core clauses, and attachment list. Fields include document number, applicable level, execution period, and associated asset identifier. Units uniformly adopt measurement standards standardized by state-owned asset supervision, such as ten thousand yuan and person-time.

## What constraints these characteristics impose on the multi-turn dialogue and prompt engineering link
The dispersion of internal and external data sources requires multi-turn dialogue to support cross-source context recall, to avoid generated content omitting regulatory requirements or internal business rules. The fixed structure of documents requires prompt engineering to clearly specify field extraction and format matching rules, to prevent generated content from deviating from compliant templates. The unified measurement standards require checking the unit matching of input parameters during multi-turn dialogue, to ensure generated content complies with state-owned asset supervision measurement specifications. The uncertain update rhythm requires prompt engineering to include trigger logic for real-time policy synchronization, to ensure generated content always matches current regulatory requirements.

| Session timeout period | 240–360 seconds | Long document drafting requires sufficient time for generation and multi-round verification, adapting to complex compliance logic |
| `chatId` management rules | Divide by company business units | Isolate document drafting sessions of different business lines to avoid context confusion |
| `messages` context truncation strategy | Retain latest interactions and core history | Prioritize retaining key information of current drafting tasks, filter redundant past conversations |

## Three common configuration mistakes
- Receiving a 422 status code when calling the dialogue interface: The `messages` parameter was not formatted correctly, or `variables` were not injected with compliant business fields as required.
- Expired policy content appearing in session context: The real-time update trigger logic for `variables` was not configured, causing the prompt to use outdated state-owned assets supervision policies.
- Generated content does not comply with measurement unit specifications: Input verification rules were not enabled, and the unified measurement standards of state-owned asset supervision were not matched.

## How to confirm correct configuration
- Initiate a test dialogue containing long document fragments, check whether the system fully recalls associated internal documents and regulatory file content.
- Verify when calling the interface that `chatId` is divided by business units, and session contexts of different business lines do not interfere with each other.
- After injecting `variables`, check whether the generated content includes the specified regulatory policy version and applicable subject information.
- Simulate a timeout scenario for long document drafting, confirm that the session completes the response within the set duration or triggers a reasonable timeout prompt.
