# Document and Material Drafting for Urban Public Transit: Knowledge Base Retrieval and Recall

## What the Data for This Category Looks Like
Data is primarily sourced from internal standardized operation manuals, safety management regulations, ticketing service specifications, emergency response plans, and document template libraries of urban public transit enterprises, as well as public transit operation policy documents released by local transportation authorities.
Most documents follow a chapter-based structured format, including fields such as applicable scenarios, execution processes, and responsible entities. They involve specialized units including shifts, passenger flow, and maintenance cycles.
Update frequency aligns with policy adjustments or internal revisions. Core documents are reviewed on a quarterly basis.

## What Constraints Do These Characteristics Impose on Knowledge Base Retrieval and Recall
The document characteristics of this category impose multiple constraints on retrieval and recall.
Structured chapter-based documents require retrieval to match chapter content for specific target scenarios, to avoid retrieving irrelevant operation records.
The multi-field design requires support for precise matching via fields such as responsible entities and applicable scenarios, to improve the targeting of document drafting.
The presence of specialized units such as shifts, passenger flow, and maintenance cycles requires retrieval to support semantic matching of terms, to avoid errors caused by unit confusion.
The non-fixed update frequency requires recall logic to prioritize filtering policy and template documents updated in the most recent quarter, to avoid using expired content.
Additionally, the unified document template format requires retrieved content to retain its original structure, to ensure direct reusability.

## Three Common Mistakes
- Phenomenon: Retrieval results return a large amount of non-document content such as vehicle maintenance and driver attendance, with unsatisfactory relevance. Cause: The exclusive dataset collection is not restricted, and operation data irrelevant to document drafting is retrieved.
- Phenomenon: After entering "bus spring festival travel emergency plan", only a small number of literal matching results are returned, without covering relevant scenarios such as passenger flow control and station scheduling. Cause: The question optimization function is not enabled, and semantic related keywords of the query are not automatically expanded, so hidden associated content cannot be matched.
- Phenomenon: The latest public transit fare policy document uploaded cannot be retrieved. Cause: The file is not classified into the specified policy document collection, or the original chapter structure of the document is not retained during upload, causing key clauses to fail to be correctly retrieved.
