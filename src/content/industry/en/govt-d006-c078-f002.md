# Document and Material Drafting for Urban Water Utilities: Vector Models and Indexes

## What Data for This Category Looks Like
Documents and materials for urban water utilities primarily include water supply dispatch logs, water quality test reports, pipe network operation and maintenance records, water fee overdue notices, emergency response plans, and similar items. Data sources include structured CSV files exported from internal business systems, PDF inspection reports uploaded by inspection personnel, and Word-format operation and maintenance plan documents. Update cadences are categorized as daily real-time updated dispatch logs, weekly or monthly updated water quality reports, and on-demand generated emergency plans and notice documents. Structured data includes fields such as inspection time, pipe network location, and water quality indicators, with attached units including pH value, turbidity, and pipe diameter. Unstructured documents have fixed report headers and coherent business logic paragraphs.

## How These Characteristics Create Constraints for Vector Models and Indexes
The multi-structured nature of urban water utility data creates multiple constraints for vector processing: 100,000-level structured CSV batches require efficient batch vectorization workflows to avoid processing timeouts. Unstructured long texts need strict chunking rules to prevent splitting that breaks business logic—for example, do not separate test items and conclusions of water quality reports into different chunks. Varying update frequencies require indexes to support flexible switching between incremental and full updates. Fields with units must retain semantic connections to prevent loss of critical business information during vector generation. Additionally, empty fields and duplicate records in batch datasets increase filtering and deduplication costs for the vector engine.

## Three Common Misconfigurations
- After uploading 100,000 CSV entries to the knowledge base, the total data volume is thousands of entries fewer after vectorization. Empty fields or duplicate records in the CSV were not filtered, causing some data to be automatically skipped by the vector engine.
- The collection is shown as created successfully, but the index never builds on the page. The vector task timed out without completing, and no automatic retry mechanism was configured, causing the indexing process to get stuck.
- The vector index runs normally locally, but vector scores show abnormal fluctuations after packaging the Docker image. The local model cache directory was not mounted inside the Docker container, causing the model to be re-downloaded on each call and local cached vector results cannot be reused.

## How to Verify Proper Configuration
- Upload a single 1000-character water utility operation and maintenance document, confirm vector generation time falls within the preset timeout range.
- Import 100,000 entries of structured CSV data, confirm the final number of indexed entries matches the count of source data entries.
- Search for keywords in water quality test reports, confirm the similarity of returned results falls within the preset threshold range.
- Restart the Docker container and execute the vector task again, confirm vector results do not exhibit abnormal fluctuations.

The values provided are common starting points and should be evaluated using local test samples.
