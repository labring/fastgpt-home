# Document and Material Drafting for Urban Water Utilities: Multi-turn Dialogue and Prompt Engineering

## What this category of data looks like
Data related to document and material drafting for urban water utilities comes primarily from pipeline operation and maintenance logs in water utility operation systems, water quality test reports, drainage permit application archives, and flood control emergency response records. Data update rhythms include daily sync for daily inspection data, weekly updates for water quality test reports, and batch updates for administrative licensing materials as applications are submitted.

Document structures mostly combine structured fields and free text descriptions. Structured fields include pipe diameter (unit: millimeters), pipeline pressure (unit: MPa), water turbidity (unit: NTU), and average daily water supply (unit: cubic meters). Free text sections include inspection abnormality descriptions, emergency response procedures, and similar content.

## What constraints these characteristics impose on multi-turn dialogue and prompt engineering
The presence of structured fields and clear units requires that prompts for multi-turn dialogue explicitly specify the unit of each parameter, to avoid mismatches between numerical values and units.
Frequently updated daily operation and maintenance data requires that dialogue context be limited to valid data from the past 7 days, to prevent use of outdated information that reduces document drafting accuracy.
The mixed structured and free text document structure requires that multi-turn dialogue be guided in stages: first confirm required structured parameters, then supplement free text scene descriptions.
Water utility documents are often associated with administrative regions and compliance requirements. Multi-turn dialogue must first confirm the application or filing region, then match the filling rules for corresponding fields.

## Configuration Settings
| Configuration Item | Recommended Approach | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `variables` | Inject pipe diameter, pressure thresholds, regional compliance fields | Deliver structured business data exclusive to urban water utilities, avoid hardcoding parameters in prompts |
| `messages` | Retain 8000–12000 token of conversation history | Cover the multi-turn interaction context required for water utility document drafting, connect field confirmation and content generation |
| `chatId` | Bind exclusive water utility project session identifiers | Distinguish business sessions for different pipelines or regions, prevent cross-project data confusion |
| Multi-turn interaction timeout | 600 seconds | Allow sufficient time to supplement structured fields, avoid premature session termination |
| Template field validation switch | Enable required field validation | Ensure generated documents include mandatory parameters for water utility documents such as pipe diameter and water supply volume |

This configuration applies to FastGPT v4.17.0.

## Three Common Misconfigurations
- Phenomenon: Form input fields are not displayed in the dialogue interface, and interaction content is blank. Cause: Form variables are not bound to the `variables` parameter, causing the session to fail to read structured data entered by users.
- Phenomenon: Generated documents lack water utility-specific fields such as pipe diameter and turbidity parameters. Cause: The prompt does not inject exclusive business data via `variables`, and only uses a general template to generate content.
- Phenomenon: Outdated pipeline data appears in the conversation history, causing document content to not match current operation status. Cause: The context range of `messages` is not limited, and historical data older than 7 days is retained.

## How to Verify the Configuration
- Launch a test dialogue, input the pipeline parameters of the water utility project, and check whether the entered field content is correctly displayed in the dialogue interface.
- View the business data bound to the `variables` parameter, confirm that it includes the exclusive fields and units required for the current project.
- Generate a test document, check whether it includes mandatory water utility business parameters and that units comply with industry requirements.
- Switch to different test project sessions, confirm that contexts do not interfere with each other.
