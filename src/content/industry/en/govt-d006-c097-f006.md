# Document and Material Drafting for Nuclear Power Operations: Citation Sourcing and Traceability
Reference only; verify against the target FastGPT release and deployment documentation.

## What this category of data looks like
Documents and materials for nuclear power operations originate from four data source categories: nuclear safety regulatory guidelines, power station operation procedures, incident handling reports, and periodic test records. Operation procedures are updated annually or after major equipment changes. Incident reports are entered and archived in real time. Periodic test records are compiled and archived quarterly. Individual documents have a fixed structure that includes chapter numbers, professional operation steps, safety limits, equipment numbers, and radiation dose units. These documents strictly follow the standardized format of the nuclear power industry, with no redundant unnecessary content.

## What constraints do these characteristics impose on the citation sourcing and traceability link?
The standardized fields and unit requirements for nuclear power documents mean original identifiers must be retained during citation. They cannot be modified or simplified arbitrarily, or traceability verification by regulators will fail. Document update frequency is high, so the knowledge base synchronization rhythm must match the data source update cycle. Citing outdated documents will not meet current regulatory requirements. Long documents have closely linked chapters, so sufficient contextual overlap must be retained when chunking. This avoids losing the correspondence between chapter numbers and operation steps after splitting. Strict traceability requirements mean every citation must correspond to a specific document number and chapter. Vague source descriptions cannot be used.

## Three Common Mistakes
- Phenomenon: API call returns results without associated context, or the large model output does not cite knowledge base content. Cause: When `quoteMaxToken` is set above 3000, it exceeds the system's default total context length limit, resulting in truncation that prevents complete transmission to the large model.
- Phenomenon: Cited content appears as garbled text or professional units are lost. Cause: The native `responseData` return configuration is not enabled, causing the document's encoding format to be converted and losing nuclear power professional characters and field information.
- Phenomenon: The cited chapter number does not match the actual document. Cause: The chunk overlap length is set too low, causing complete chapter identifiers to be split during chunking, making it impossible to associate with the correct document location.
