# Business Process Guidance for Advanced Chemical Materials: Vector Models and Indexes

## What This Category of Data Looks Like
The business process data for advanced chemical materials is primarily sourced from internal corporate compliance archives, product qualification filing systems, and production license application process forms. Data updates are triggered along business nodes, with synchronous updates occurring during new product filing or qualification changes. Documents are a mix of structured forms and non-compliance explanatory materials, including fields such as the applicant’s unified social credit code, product grade, technical parameters (with units including MPa, ℃, tons), approval process nodes, and required submission material lists. The length of individual documents varies widely, ranging from several hundred characters of process prompts to thousands of characters of compliance regulations.

## Constraints Imposed on Vector Models and Indexing
The mixed structure and variable length of the business process data for advanced chemical materials require vector indexes to support mixed chunking logic for both structured forms and unstructured explanatory documents. Technical parameter fields with specific physical units must retain unit associations during indexing, to avoid losing parameter precision during semantic recall. The requirement for updates to trigger along business nodes means indexes must support on-demand batch reindexing, to avoid resource waste from full repeated indexing. The wide variance in individual document length requires limiting chunk boundary ranges, to prevent key process node information from being split or redundantly concatenated.

## How to Configure Settings
| Configuration Item | Recommended Setting | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chunk Length | 800–1200 characters | Adapts to the length variance of advanced chemical material documents, from process prompts to compliance regulations, and avoids splitting key information |
| Overlap Length | 100–150 characters | Retains cross-chunk process node context, and prevents core content such as approval steps from being split |
| `maxToken` | 8192 | Adapts to the embedding model calling logic of FastGPT v4.17.0, matches the input upper limit of most commercial embedding models, and supports chunk processing for long compliance documents |
| Similarity Threshold | 0.72–0.80 | Filters low-match irrelevant process prompts, and accurately recalls business process steps |
| Single Search Limit | Top 6 entries | Controls the number of recall results, and avoids overloading business process guidance information |
| Reranking | Enabled | Optimizes the sorting logic of recall results, and prioritizes matching process node keywords |

## Three Common Misconfigurations
- Phenomenon: After replacing the embedding model and performing batch re-embedding, the recall rate for multilingual business documents fails to meet standards. Cause: The chunk length and overlap length parameters were not adjusted synchronously, resulting in a mismatch between the original document chunking logic and the semantic encoding of the new model.
- Phenomenon: The default index generated for manually inserted business process documents automatically disappears after several hours. Cause: The persistent storage configuration for the index was not enabled, or the original index’s associated records were overwritten during batch index updates.
- Phenomenon: After connecting to qwen3-embedding-8b, the file status consistently displays "indexing" with no progress. Cause: The currently configured `maxToken` does not adapt to the input length requirements of this model, resulting in embedding tasks timing out without completion.

## How to Verify Proper Configuration
- Upload a typical advanced chemical material business process document, and confirm that chunked text retains technical parameters and units with no key information split.
- Trigger a small-batch document indexing task, confirm indexing is complete, then use test queries to recall corresponding process steps, and verify that recall results match expectations.
- Check the persistent storage configuration for the index, manually refresh the page, and confirm that index records are not lost.
- After replacing an embedding model, compare recall results between the old and new models, confirm that semantic matching meets business expectations, and verify that `defaultToken` matches the input upper limit of the current embedding model.
