# Business Process Guidance for Commodity Trading: Vector Models and Indexes

## What This Category of Data Looks Like
Business processing data for commodity trading primarily originates from internal purchase and sales contracts, standard warehouse receipts, logistics waybills, and business approval process documents. Data updates trigger when a single business node completes, or sync to the knowledge base in daily batches. Each document typically includes structured fields and unstructured descriptions. Structured fields cover commodity category, specification model, transaction quantity, delivery location, and approval process nodes, using industry-standard measurement units such as tons, yuan per ton, cubic meters, and others. Unstructured content mostly includes business rules and special clause explanations.

## What Constraints Do These Characteristics Impose on Vector Models and Indexes
Commodity trading data mixes structured fields and unstructured descriptions, and individual documents are lengthy. Indexing must balance precise matching of structured information and semantic association of unstructured content. Business data updates alongside transaction nodes, so indexing must support incremental synchronization to avoid excessive delays from full reconstruction. Document fields vary widely across business scenarios. For example, process documents for spot trading and futures hedging use different fields. Independent indexing rules must be configured for each business scenario. Unit mapping for measurement fields must also be unified to prevent vector matching deviations caused by inconsistent units.

## How to Set the Configuration
`defaultToken` configures the default token limit for a single vector block, while `maxToken` sets the maximum total token count for a single call. The following configurations apply to FastGPT v4.17.0:

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value Range | Rationale |
| ---- | ---- | ---- |
| Chunk Length | 800–1200 characters | Adapts to long paragraphs and structured fields in commodity business documents, balancing semantic completeness and indexing efficiency |
| Overlap Length | 100–200 characters | Prevents semantic breaks in cross-chunk process steps, ensuring continuity of business guidance |
| Similarity Threshold | 0.72–0.85 | Filters low-match irrelevant results, meeting the precision requirements for business processing |
| Single Search Limit | Top 10 results | Controls the volume of returned results, avoiding redundant information interfering with business judgment |
| Maximum Context | 4000 tokens | Covers the conventional total length of commodity business documents, ensuring complete information retrieval |
| Reranking | Enabled, sorted by match degree | Prioritizes content most relevant to business processing workflows, improving guidance accuracy |

## Three Common Misconfigurations
- Index progress stalls for a long time with no clear error. This occurs when reasonable chunk length is not configured for large-volume warehouse receipt documents of commodity trading, causing single block content to exceed the model's processing limit and triggering background queue blocking.
- Search returns results unrelated to the business question, including incorrect process nodes. This happens when unit mapping for measurement fields is not unified, causing vector matching to misclassify transaction data with different units as relevant content.
- The knowledge base fails to load the latest business approval process content. This is caused by not enabling incremental synchronization configuration. Full synchronization times out due to excessive business data volume, resulting in incomplete index updates.

## How to Confirm the Configuration Is Correct
- Check the running logs of indexing tasks to confirm that the number of chunked documents matches the number of originally imported business documents, with no abnormal errors.
- Submit test questions containing business terminology, verify that the returned field units are consistent with the measurement standards in the business documents.
- Simulate a single business node change to trigger an incremental synchronization task, confirm that the index update time meets expectations.
- Adjust the similarity threshold, verify that the number of returned results changes in line with the requirements of the business scenario.

The values provided here are common starting points, and should be evaluated against samples from the actual deployment.
