# Business Process Guidance for Oilfield Engineering Services: Document Parsing and Chunking

## What the data for this category looks like
Oilfield engineering service business documents mainly originate from internal materials such as project construction plans, equipment operation and maintenance ledgers, acceptance approval forms, and bills of quantities. Update rhythm is adjusted alongside project progress: updated according to construction phases during a single project cycle, and synced to stock documents quarterly during non-project periods. Most documents have fixed structures, including dedicated fields such as well number, construction date, material model, and operating parameters. Units involve engineering-specific units such as cubic meters, tons, and hours. Some documents contain complex nested tables and approval documents with digital signatures.

## What constraints these characteristics impose on the document parsing and chunking link
The dedicated fields, complex document structures and format characteristics of oilfield engineering services impose multiple constraints on the parsing and chunking process. Dedicated engineering fields and units require that field relevance be preserved during parsing, avoiding splitting content across business nodes. Approval PDFs with digital signatures obscure underlying content, so standard parsing engines cannot fully read the original text. Complex nested bills of quantities tables are easily truncated by conventional chunking logic, leading to broken business logic. Large-capacity single files and long documents increase parsing time, exceeding default timeout limits. Documents updated by project phase need to adapt to incremental parsing logic to avoid repeated parsing of stock content.

## How to set the configurations
Reference only; verify against the target FastGPT release and deployment documentation.

| Configuration Item | Recommended Setting | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `chunkSize` | 800–1200 characters | Oilfield engineering documents contain long paragraphs of construction steps and table content. This range preserves the integrity of a single business logic unit |
| `chunkSplitter` | Split by document structure | Matches the fixed chapter structure of construction plans and acceptance reports, avoiding splitting process content across nodes |
| `customPdfParse` | Enabled | Supports recognition of PDF documents with digital signatures, bypassing the signature layer to read complete original content |
| `UPLOAD_FILE_MAX_SIZE` | 1000 MB | Oilfield engineering documents contain a large number of drawing attachments and raw data, adapting to the demand for large single-file capacity |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | 600 seconds | Large engineering documents take longer to parse, avoiding interrupting the parsing process due to timeout |
| `chunkSettingMode` | Manual configuration | Adjust chunking parameters for different types of engineering documents such as construction plans and equipment ledgers, to adapt to dedicated field structures |

## Three common misconfigurations
- Symptom: After uploading an oilfield engineering PDF with a digital signature, the parsing result has no valid content. Cause: The `customPdfParse` configuration is not enabled, and the default parsing engine cannot bypass the signature layer to read the underlying document content.
- Symptom: When calling the Doc2x tool to process engineering documents, a file read error is returned, and the error message includes "Only support .txt". Cause: The uploaded file format does not match the range supported by `customPdfParse`, or the file size exceeds the `UPLOAD_FILE_MAX_SIZE` limit.
- Symptom: After uploading a document containing a complex bill of quantities, parsing and chunking results in table rows being split across chunks and field loss. Cause: `chunkSplitter` is set to split by line breaks, not by document structure, leading to truncated table logic.

## How to confirm proper configuration
- Upload one oilfield engineering PDF document with a digital signature, and check whether the parsing result includes all original content with no missing fields.
- Upload a single engineering document of the expected capacity, confirm that the parsing process does not trigger a timeout prompt, and the number of generated chunks matches the document's chapter structure after completion.
- Select an engineering document containing complex nested tables, and check whether the parsed chunks retain the complete table structure with no table rows split across chunks.
- Call the Doc2x tool to process a test engineering file, confirm that no file read errors occur, and the returned chunk content can normally associate with business process nodes.
