# Party Building Learning Q&A for Medical Insurance Fund Supervision and Audit: Citation Sources and Traceability

## What the Data for This Category Looks Like
This category of data covers all materials related to the end-to-end medical insurance fund supervision process. Daily documents include audit notices, inspection work drafts, suspicious point lists, interview records, handling decision letters, and rectification reports.
Data sources include medical insurance intelligent monitoring systems, fund supervision rule bases, medical insurance settlement data, drug and consumable traceability codes, and complaint clues. The update frequency is monthly.
Data fields include supervision suspicious point identifiers, basis for identifying unauthorized expenses, information of supervised entities, and additional relevant fields. Statistical dimensions primarily use count, number of documents, and person-times.

## Constraints Imposed on the Citation Sources and Traceability Link
Monthly updated data requires regular knowledge base synchronization. Referencing expired supervision rules or settlement data will reduce the accuracy of compliance references during Party Building learning.
Single documents such as inspection work drafts and handling decision letters are lengthy. Limit cited passages reasonably to avoid information truncation caused by exceeding context thresholds.
Data sources are dispersed. Clearly link traceability records to original systems or clue types to avoid confusion between supervision data from different channels.
Audit work has strict requirements for data reliability. Citation traceability must include document types and update times. This ensures the compliance and traceability of referenced content for Party Building learning.

## Three Common Misconfigurations
- Phenomenon: Generated Party Building Q&A results do not show source information for cited documents. Cause: The `responseData` configuration item is not enabled, and the traceability information return logic is not activated.
- Phenomenon: Results exceed token limits even for simple Q&A queries. Cause: The single search citation limit is set too high, failing to adapt to the passage length characteristics of medical insurance audit documents.
- Phenomenon: Cited supervision rules or settlement data include expired content. Cause: The knowledge base synchronization cycle does not match the monthly update frequency of this data category, leading to delayed data refreshes.
