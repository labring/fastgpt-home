# Party Building Learning Q&A for Environmental Law Enforcement and Monitoring Regulation: Document Parsing and Chunking

## What this category of data looks like
Party building learning Q&A for environmental law enforcement and monitoring regulation scenarios relies on daily documents and business data from entities such as law enforcement detachments and monitoring stations. This includes on-site inspection records, sampling records, monitoring reports, order for rectification decisions, and administrative penalty decisions. Data sources include online monitoring platforms, automatic pollution source monitoring data, environmental law enforcement systems, and third-party test reports. The update frequency is daily.

Document structures vary significantly: on-site inspection records contain fields such as inspection time, law enforcement officer ID, and location name. Sampling records note sampling locations and pollutant concentration units. Monitoring reports include professional content such as test items and judgment basis. Law enforcement documents have fixed document numbers and rectification requirement clauses.

## What constraints do these characteristics impose on the document parsing and chunking link
Party building learning Q&A in this scenario requires generating accurate Q&A content based on real business documents. Multi-source data and diverse document structures require the parsing process to adapt to both structured system data and unstructured office documents. This prevents splitting of key business information that would reduce Q&A accuracy.

The daily update rhythm creates demand for high-frequency, batch parsing. Reasonable timeout and size limits must be configured to support updates of daily learning materials. Professional test terminology and law enforcement-specific fields require the parsing engine to accurately identify content. This avoids generating incorrect business basis for party building Q&A.

Document lengths vary widely: on-site records are as short as tens of characters, while law enforcement documents can span thousands of words. Chunking rules must be flexibly adjusted to retain contextual connections. This ensures that party building Q&A can link complete business scenarios and policy requirements.

## How to set the configurations
These configurations are based on FastGPT v4.17.0.

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value Range | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `chunkSize` | 800–1200 characters | Adapts to the typical paragraph length of law enforcement documents and monitoring reports, avoids splitting key content containing test data or law enforcement clauses |
| `chunkSplitter` | Split by paragraph + title | Matches the title hierarchy of daily documents, retains contextual information such as inspection locations and test items |
| `customPdfParse` | Enabled | Accurately extracts tables and professionally formatted content from PDF-format monitoring reports and law enforcement documents, prevents content loss |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | 300–600 seconds | Adapts to the parsing duration of large individual monitoring reports, prevents parsing failures due to timeout |
| `UPLOAD_FILE_MAX_SIZE` | 100–200 MB | Accommodates packaged archive files of batch sampling records and law enforcement documents, meets daily file upload requirements |
| `chunkSettingMode` | Manual configuration | Adjusts chunking parameters for different document types, adapts to the differentiated needs of short-paragraph on-site records and long-clause law enforcement documents |

## Three Common Misconfigurations
- Symptom: Content missing or garbled after parsing PDF-format law enforcement documents used for party building learning. Cause: The `customPdfParse` configuration is not enabled, and the default parsing engine cannot accurately extract tables and professionally formatted content from PDFs.
- Symptom: Timeout errors returned when calling the document parsing tool in a workflow. Cause: The `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` parameter is not adjusted, and the default duration is insufficient to parse large monitoring reports containing multiple sets of test data.
- Symptom: Uploaded on-site inspection records lose the association between law enforcement officer IDs and inspection locations after being chunked. Cause: The `chunkSplitter` is not configured to split by title, and the default character-based splitting breaks key information pairs.

## How to Confirm Configurations Are Correct
- Upload a standard-format monitoring report PDF, check if the parsed text contains complete test items, concentration data, and judgment conclusions.
- Upload a single law enforcement document file of approximately 100 MB, confirm that the parsing task completes within the duration set by `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`.
- After adjusting the `chunkSize` parameter, check that the chunking results do not split complete law enforcement document paragraphs containing document numbers and rectification requirements.
- Check if the `UPLOAD_FILE_MAX_SIZE` in the configuration file covers the maximum file size of daily uploaded file types.

The values provided are common starting points, and should be evaluated against the user's own samples.
