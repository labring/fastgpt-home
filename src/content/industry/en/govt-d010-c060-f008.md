# Party Building Learning Q&A for Warehousing and Logistics: Document Parsing and Chunking

## What the data for this category looks like
Data for Party Building learning Q&A in the warehousing and logistics industry originates from internal enterprise Party Building ledgers, study documents issued by superior Party organizations, internal Party course materials, and Party member study records. Update schedules are adjusted per superior Party building tasks, with no fixed cycle. The volume of newly added documents varies widely per batch.
Supported document formats include PDF, Word, Excel, and scanned documents. Some documents contain tabular Party member check-in data and study duration statistics. Fields include document issuing unit, study theme, associated warehousing and logistics business description, and more. Common units are hours, person-times, and batches.

## What constraints do these characteristics impose on document parsing and chunking
The mixed-format documents, tabular business association data, and fluctuating volume of newly added documents for warehousing and logistics Party Building learning create multiple constraints for document parsing and chunking.
Scanned Party course materials require OCR recognition to extract valid text. Tabular Party member check-in and study duration data must retain cell association links to avoid losing business field connections after chunking.
Fluctuating document volumes require support for parsing timeout limits across different file sizes, while preventing semantic fragmentation caused by overly large single-parsing content.

## Configuration guidelines
Reference only; verify against the target FastGPT release and deployment documentation.

| Configuration Item | Recommended Value | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `customPdfParse` | Enabled | Supports OCR text extraction for the large number of scanned Party course materials in warehousing and logistics Party Building documents |
| `chunkSize` | `800–1200 characters` | Warehousing and logistics Party Building documents often include business association descriptions, and this length preserves full context |
| `chunkSplitter` | By paragraph + semantic boundary | Documents contain mixed table and paragraph structures, avoiding rigid splitting that breaks semantic connections |
| `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` | `300–600 seconds` | Large scanned Party course materials take extended time to parse, requiring sufficient processing time |
| `UPLOAD_FILE_MAX_SIZE` | `1000 MB` | Supports uploads of multi-page scanned document collections for warehousing and logistics Party Building documents |

## Three common configuration errors
- Symptom: Parsing result is empty or only extracts a small amount of meaningless characters after uploading a scanned PDF. Cause: The `customPdfParse` configuration is not enabled, so the OCR recognition process is not triggered.
- Symptom: Vectorization index results are disorganized and business field associations are lost after uploading an Excel Party member study check-in form. Cause: Chunking logic is not adjusted for table structures, and rigid splitting breaks cell data associations.
- Symptom: Knowledge base chunked content is incomplete after uploading large Party Building documents via API. Cause: The `chunkSize` or `chunkSplitter` parameters are not configured correctly, and chunking rules do not match document structure characteristics.

## How to verify correct configuration
- Upload a single scanned Party course material, check if the parsing result includes complete text content, and confirm the `customPdfParse` configuration is active.
- Upload an Excel Party member study form, verify that chunked data retains cell association relationships, and confirm chunking rules are compatible with table structures.
- Call the API to upload a test file, check if returned chunked data matches expected length, and confirm `chunkSize` and `chunkSplitter` configurations are correct.
- Upload a large document, check if the parsing process triggers a timeout, and confirm the `PARSE_FILE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` setting is appropriate.

The values given are common starting points and should be measured against the reader's own samples.
