# Internal Office and Employee Services for Urban Public Transit: Vector Models and Indexes

## What Data for This Category Looks Like
Data for internal office and employee services for urban public transit comes primarily from internal scheduling systems, station operation logs, employee job manuals, and ticket settlement systems. Scheduling data updates weekly. Operation logs are generated in real time. Employee manuals are static documents. Ticket data syncs daily.

Document structures fall into three categories: structured tables such as scheduling tables that include route numbers, shift times, and driver employee IDs, semi-structured logs such as fault records that include station IDs, fault types, and processing durations, and plain text specifications such as emergency response procedures. Fields include operating mileage, shift frequency, and employee IDs. Most units used are kilometers, hours, and shifts.

## What Constraints These Characteristics Impose on Vector Models and Indexing
High proportions of structured data with tightly linked fields require retaining field-level metadata during indexing to avoid breaking business associations when splitting text. Real-time generated operation logs need incremental indexing logic configured to reduce resource consumption from full synchronization.

Document length varies widely: fault records are just tens of characters, while emergency procedures span thousands of words. This demands a splitting strategy that adapts to different text lengths. Ticket settlement and scheduling data have significantly different update frequencies. Trigger timings for full and incremental indexing must be differentiated to avoid wasting resources.

Some fields use fixed units. Marking these via metadata during indexing can assist vector matching and improve retrieval accuracy.

## How to Configure Settings
| Configuration Item | Recommended Setting | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Chunk Length | 800–1200 characters | Adapts to the wide range of public transit document lengths, balances reasonable splitting for short fault logs and long emergency procedures |
| Overlap Length | 100–150 characters | Prevents cross-chunk business logic from being split, maintains contextual integrity for scheduling and operation workflows |
| Similarity Threshold | 0.72–0.85, calibrated via actual testing | Matches the semantic matching accuracy requirements of public transit business fields, differentiates valid retrievals from irrelevant results |
| Single Search Limit | Top 8 results | Balances retrieval efficiency and recall coverage, fits multi-scenario retrieval needs for internal office work |
| `defaultToken` | Bind the dedicated vector channel token | Avoids third-party token permission errors, aligns with the configuration logic of FastGPT v4.17.0 |
| `maxToken` | 4096 token | Adapts to the input limit of most vector models, ensures effective encoding of long text chunks |

## Three Common Configuration Mistakes
- Symptom: The interface displays "This token does not have permission to use the model" or no available vector channels. Cause: Failed to bind the `defaultToken` for the dedicated vector channel, and still relies on the default third-party token configuration, leading to permission verification failure.
- Symptom: Knowledge base search takes longer than reasonable, with delayed returned retrieval results. Cause: Failed to configure incremental indexing based on the update frequency of public transit data, and still uses full indexing logic, causing each retrieval to scan all data.
- Symptom: Retrieval results cannot match complete business processes, such as missing cross-chunk shift information in scheduling tables. Cause: Chunk length set too small or overlap length insufficient, leading to long text being split and breaking contextual associations.

## How to Verify Correct Configuration
- Check the vector model binding status, confirm that the channel corresponding to `defaultToken` has completed authorization verification.
- Randomly select an internal document, run a retrieval test, and verify that the chunk length of the returned results matches the configured value.
- Adjust the similarity threshold, observe changes in the number of retrieved results, and confirm that the threshold meets business matching requirements.
- Check system logs, confirm that incremental indexing only triggers during the update cycles of scheduling and operation logs, and does not occupy excessive system resources.
