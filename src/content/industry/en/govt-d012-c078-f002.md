# Internal Office and Employee Services for Urban Water Utilities: Vector Models and Indexes

## What the Data for This Category Looks Like
Data sources for internal office and employee services in urban water utilities include pipeline inspection logs, water quality test reports, internal approval process documents, employee job training courseware, maintenance work order records, and more. Data update rhythms differ:
- Inspection logs are uploaded per shift and updated daily
- Water quality reports are aggregated monthly and updated monthly
- Training courseware is updated quarterly
- Work order records are generated in real time

Document structures include structured fields such as pipeline ID, detection point, pressure unit MPa, turbidity unit NTU, and inspector employee ID, alongside unstructured text such as on-site inspection descriptions and approval opinions. Some documents include table or image data.

## What Constraints These Characteristics Impose on the Vector Models and Indexes Workflow
Structured data carries clear units and business identifiers. This requires vector models to support text encoding with field metadata, to avoid losing semantics of units and specialized vocabulary.
Data update frequencies vary widely. Real-time work orders and periodic reports need separate adaptation for incremental index and full index update strategies, to prevent delayed index updates or resource waste.
Some inspection reports and training courseware are lengthy. This requires support for long text chunking and semantic stitching, to avoid cutting off critical business information.
Queries in internal office scenarios mostly revolve around water utility specialized terms. The index must adapt to the recall accuracy of domain vocabulary, to ensure retrieval results meet business needs.
Multi-source heterogeneous data including logs, reports, and documents requires a unified parsing and vectorization process, to avoid index missing caused by format incompatibility.

## How to Set Configurations
| Configuration Item | Recommended Setting | Rationale |
| ---- | ---- | ---- |
| `maxToken` | `8192` | Meets the encoding needs of long water utility domain texts such as annual water quality reports and complete inspection records, to avoid cutting off critical business information |
| Chunk Length | 800–1200 characters | Balances semantic completeness of single-chunk text and index retrieval efficiency, adapts to the average length of water utility documents |
| Overlap Length | 100–150 characters | Reduces semantic breaks caused by chunking, ensures specialized vocabulary across chunks such as pipeline diameter and water quality standards are fully encoded |
| Similarity Threshold | 0.72–0.80 | Filters low-relevance retrieval results, adapts to the business accuracy requirements of internal office scenarios |
| Single Search Limit | Top 8 entries | Controls the volume of returned results, prevents employees from being distracted by excessive redundant information |
| `defaultToken` | 4096 | Adapts to the context length of daily employee inquiries, such as historical records of maintenance work orders and core content of job training |

This page’s configurations are based on FastGPT v4.17.0.

## Three Common Misconfigurations
- Phenomenon: Embedding generation results do not match expected performance from public documentation. Cause: The text preprocessing logic is not adjusted for water utility specialized terms, and general tokenization rules are directly applied to exclusive vocabulary such as pipeline and turbidity.
- Phenomenon: The vector database cannot be connected after container startup. Cause: Only the vector database image is started, MongoDB and Sandbox containers are not started synchronously, and necessary dependent services are missing.
- Phenomenon: The same query triggers vector database retrieval multiple times, and response latency is high. Cause: Cache optimization configuration is not enabled, and results for repeated queries are not reused.

## How to Confirm the Configuration Is Correct
- Upload a water utility inspection report containing specialized vocabulary, check if the chunked text length falls within the configured 800–1200 character range.
- Submit the same business query twice, confirm that the second response latency is significantly lower than the first, to verify that the cache optimization mechanism is active.
- View the index management interface of the vector database, confirm that vector index entries corresponding to water utility business documents have been generated.
- Test text encoding containing specialized vocabulary such as pipeline and turbidity, confirm that the generated embedding results fall within the set similarity threshold range.

The values provided are common starting points and should be measured against the reader's own samples.
