# Internal Office and Employee Services for Basic Chemical Industry: Forms and Interactions

## What this category's data looks like
Basic chemical industry internal office data mainly comes from production logs, equipment inspection records, safety compliance documents, and employee attendance scheduling systems. Data update rhythms vary widely. Production logs update in real time with shift operations. Inspection records update daily or per shift. Compliance documents are revised quarterly or annually. Document structure includes structured fields and unstructured attachments. Structured fields cover equipment number, inspection time, hidden danger level, material usage, and similar items. Unstructured attachments include inspection photos, test reports, and similar items. Most field units are physical measurement units: pressure uses MPa, temperature uses ℃, material usage uses tons.

## What constraints do these characteristics impose on the forms and interactions link
The multi-type data characteristics of the basic chemical industry impose multiple constraints on the forms and interactions link. Structured physical quantity fields require forms to support input validation with units to prevent format errors. Data sources with different update rhythms require forms to have configurable flexible pull cycles to match real-time and periodic business requirements. Unstructured large-volume attachments require the interaction link to support large-capacity uploads and multi-format compatibility. Security-related approval processes require interaction components to filter users by department and position to ensure permission compliance.

## How to set the configuration
Reference only; verify against the target FastGPT release and deployment documentation.

| Configuration Item | Recommended Setting | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `formInput` | Enable unit validation, preset common units such as MPa, ℃, tons | Most basic chemical industry forms involve physical measurement input, need to standardize formats to avoid errors |
| `userSelect` | Filter by department grouping, default display direct supervisor and safety specialist | Chemical internal office work requires binding approval roles related to safety and production, to improve selection efficiency |
| Attachment upload limit | Single file maximum 1000 MB, supports PDF, JPEG, PNG formats | Business attachments such as test reports and inspection photos have large volumes, covering common upload scenarios |
| Interaction timeout setting | 600 seconds | Complex form submission needs to load multiple sets of production log data, reserve sufficient response time |
| Data pull cycle | Update per shift (8 hours) | Production log data has high real-time requirements, matches the 8-hour shift operation cycle |

## Three common configuration mistakes
- The preset hidden danger rectification process does not trigger after form submission. This occurs when conditional branch logic for `formInput` is not configured, and the hidden danger level output by GPT is not bound to subsequent approval nodes.
- Target users cannot be quickly located in the approval node. This occurs when the department grouping filtering rule is not configured through `userSelect`, and all users are displayed, leading to low filtering efficiency.
- Attachment upload fails. This occurs when the attachment upload limit is not adjusted, and the default limit cannot accommodate large-volume chemical test report files.

## How to confirm the configuration is complete
- Submit input content using common chemical industry units, verify whether the validation logic of `formInput` matches the preset unit specifications.
- Select the corresponding business department, verify whether the `userSelect` component only displays available users within that department.
- Upload attachment files that meet the business format, verify whether the upload limit adapts to business needs.
- Trigger form loading across multiple data sources, verify whether the timeout threshold reserves sufficient response time.

The values listed above are common starting points, and should be evaluated against the reader's own samples.
