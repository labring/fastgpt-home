# Security and Compliance Material Review for Content Audit Rule Operations: Permission and Data Isolation

## What This Category’s Data Entails
Core data for this category originates from the content audit backend, user reporting channels, regulatory policy documents, and audit sample libraries. Four types of documents are routinely produced: audit rules, disposal guidelines, appeal records, and quality inspection reports. Audit rules include fields for rule ID, trigger conditions, and effective time period. Appeal records include fields for appeal subject, appeal content, and processing results. Data is updated either on a daily basis or immediately following policy changes. All data is bound to its affiliated operations team, with no default cross-team sharing permissions.

## Constraints Imposed by These Characteristics on Permission and Data Isolation
The category’s data sources are scattered and involve compliance-sensitive content, so cross-team access must be strictly restricted. Data is updated frequently alongside policy changes, so distinct operation permissions must be set for actions such as editing, viewing, and approval to prevent accidental modification of active audit rules. Appeal records and quality inspection reports contain user privacy information, so separate desensitization isolation is required. If permission configurations have oversights, it may lead to leakage of compliance rules, spread of sensitive content, and trigger regulatory rectification. Therefore, permission and data isolation must align with the team ownership, role hierarchy, and sensitivity level of the data, and cannot be configured generically.

## How to Configure the Settings
The following configuration items apply to FastGPT v4.17.0:

| Configuration Item | Recommended Approach | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `teamId` | Divide by business operations teams, each independent operations group corresponds to a unique `teamId` | Aligns with the business logic of storing audit rules by team ownership, prevents cross-team rule leakage |
| `tmbId` | Bind to audit roles, operations, quality inspection, and appeal specialists each correspond to an independent `tmbId` | Differentiates operation permissions for different positions, limits access scope for sensitive documents |
| Data isolation scope | Restricted to the current team’s audit rules, appeal records, and quality inspection reports | Aligns with the business characteristic that category data only circulates within the team |
| Sensitive data masking | Mask user phone numbers and ID number fields in appeal records, retain the first 3 and last 4 digits | Meets data desensitization requirements for compliance material reviews |
| Operation log retention period | 180 days | Meets regulatory compliance log retention standards |
| Permission approval workflow | Enable two-level approval, audit rule modifications require confirmation from a superior operations role | Mitigates the risk of accidental modification of active compliance rules |

## Three Common Misconfigurations
- An interface prompt stating "No permission to operate this conversation record" occurs because the current operating role is not bound to the corresponding `teamId` or `tmbId`, preventing access to team-specific audit rule data.
- A permission error is reported during testing after configuring a locally deployed model, because the model call permission was not assigned to the role group corresponding to the current `tmbId`, resulting in inability to call the configured model.
- The open-source version cannot create teams or share deployment programs, because the permission isolation configuration for team management was not enabled, and `teamId` was not bound to implement team permission division.

## How to Verify Successful Configuration
- Switch to role accounts corresponding to different `tmbId`s, verify that only the current team’s audit rules, appeal records, and quality inspection reports can be accessed, and that related data from other teams cannot be viewed.
- Submit an audit rule modification request, verify that the system triggers a two-level approval workflow, and the modification can only take effect after confirmation by a superior operations role.
- View the operation log list, confirm that the log retention period meets the 180-day configuration requirement, and all operations record the corresponding `teamId` and `tmbId`.
- Open an appeal record, verify that user phone number and ID number fields have been masked to the format "first 3 digits + **** + last 4 digits", with no complete sensitive information displayed.

The values provided are common starting points and should be measured against the reader's own samples.
