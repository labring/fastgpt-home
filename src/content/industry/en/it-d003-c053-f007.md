# Security and Compliance Material Review for Customer Requirement Pool Management: Conversation Logs and Auditing

## What this category of data looks like
Data for customer requirement pool management typically originates from ticketing systems, customer interviews, CRM platforms, and product roadmaps.
Update frequency follows a weekly or per-release cycle.
Daily produced documents include requirement pool lists, requirement assessment forms, customer feedback records, and version planning descriptions.
Common data fields include requirement ID, customer identifier, submission time, processing status, priority, associated version, and more.
There are no unified fixed unit requirements. Only record specifications matching the business scenario need to be followed.
Typical users include product operations, customer success, and product managers.
Data flows through the full lifecycle of customer-submitted requirements, from initial submission to final implementation. A complete business chain covers this entire process.

## How these characteristics impose constraints on conversation logs and auditing
The characteristics of customer requirement pool management data impose multiple constraints on the conversation logs and auditing process.
Multi-source data sources require conversation logs to bind unique identifiers such as requirement IDs and customer IDs. This prevents mixing log content from different requirements.
Weekly or per-release update cycles require auditing periods to match the business iteration rhythm. Auditing periods should not be too short or exceed the business review cycle.
Differences in fields across multiple document types require logs to fully record all operational details related to requirements. This includes field changes and communication content, to facilitate subsequent traceability.
Different user roles require logs to divide permissions by role. This ensures that sensitive requirement conversation content is only visible to authorized personnel.
Additionally, the flow characteristics of requirement pool data require logs to associate conversations with specific requirements. This supports full-chain auditing for requirement changes.

## How to set configurations
| Configuration Item | Recommended Approach | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `chatId` | Combine requirement ID and customer ID as the bound value | Match the requirement identifiers associated with multi-source data to avoid log confusion |
| `responseChatItemId` | Generate a unique value incremented by conversation turn | Adapt to multi-turn conversation traceability for requirement communication, ensuring turn identification |
| `detail` | Store full conversation content and requirement field change records | Cover all operational details of requirement pool documents to meet auditing requirements |
| Conversation log retention period | 7–14 days | Align with weekly or per-release update cycles, matching standard auditing periods |
| Role permission configuration | Divide operational visibility by job role | Match the job division of product operations, customer success, and product managers |
| Log export format | CSV format aligned with requirement pool list fields | Facilitate synchronization with existing requirement management tools, reducing data integration costs |

## Three common misconfigurations
- Symptom: The frontend session list shows no historical records in guest login scenarios, but complete conversation content exists in the `detail` field. Cause: No `chatId` mapping rule for guest login scenarios is configured, causing the frontend list to fail to associate corresponding log data.
- Symptom: A `cannot read properties of undefined` error pops up when viewing conversation log details. Cause: The correspondence between `responseChatItemId` and conversation turns is not correctly bound, causing the log parsing process to fail to read necessary fields.
- Symptom: Exported conversation log fields do not match existing requirement pool documents. Cause: The log export format is not aligned with the standard field structure of the requirement pool list, causing data to not be directly reused in existing requirement management workflows.

## How to verify correct configuration
- A requirement communication conversation is initiated in a guest login scenario. The frontend session list is checked for the corresponding record, and the `detail` field is verified to contain complete conversation content.
- The conversation log details page is accessed. No `cannot read properties of undefined` error is confirmed to appear, and `responseChatItemId` values for all conversation turns are verified to generate normally.
- A log file is exported, and fields are checked to match the standard fields of the requirement pool list.
- Accounts with different job roles are switched. Only authorized roles are confirmed to view the conversation logs of corresponding requirements.
