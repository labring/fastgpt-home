# Customer Technical Support and Pre-sales Q&A for Personal Information Protection Compliance: Sharing and Embedding

## What the data for this category looks like
This category’s data primarily comes from business systems, App permission lists, privacy policies, and user request records, and is maintained by privacy compliance teams, security consultants, or legal departments. Daily processed documents include personal information lists, processing rules, impact assessment reports, and user authorization texts. Teams update this data quarterly or whenever the product version changes. The core of the data revolves around information that can identify a natural person. Teams must complete processes including scenario sorting, confirmation of legal basis, and organization of impact assessment materials. Improper handling may lead to complaints, administrative penalties, or removal and rectification. Unlike the data security classification and grading category, this category focuses on full-process compliance handling of information that can identify natural persons.

## What constraints these characteristics impose on the "Sharing and Embedding" link
This category’s data contains sensitive information that can identify natural persons, and the update frequency is not real-time. Therefore, the sharing and embedding link must strictly restrict access scope to prevent unauthorized data leaks. Since the data source involves multiple types of business systems and compliance documents, data source synchronization after embedding must be implemented separately by external systems, matching the update rhythm of quarterly or product changes. At the same time, the document structure includes structured lists and unstructured reports. When embedding, teams must ensure that the recalled content only corresponds to the specified compliance document set, avoiding information confusion across data sources. In addition, the consequences of errors are severe. Complete interaction logs must be retained to meet compliance audit requirements, and front-end display must comply with the principle of minimal storage to avoid excessive local storage of personal information.

## Three common mistakes
- Sharing entries related to personal information protection compliance fail to open. First check the complete system-generated link and its validity period, then compare browser request status; do not replace `shareId` on one’s own.
- The visible scope of personal information protection compliance data does not match expectations. Check third-party identity verification results, workflow request parameters, and external system data permissions respectively; the sharing entry identifier itself does not provide business authorization.
- The same personal information protection compliance Q&A cannot be seen on another device. For anonymous scenarios, check device, browser, and local identity status first; record retention cannot be guaranteed across devices or after cache clearing, and service abnormalities cannot be judged solely based on this phenomenon.

## How to confirm configurations are complete
- Parse the share link, confirm that `shareId` is the system-generated sharing entry identifier.
- Initiate an interactive operation on the embedded page, check whether the backend log generates corresponding records, and there are no locally stored interactive traces on the front end.
- Access the embedded page using a non-whitelist domain name, verify that content cannot be loaded normally, and the whitelist domain name can display content normally.
- Test the transfer of externally passed parameters, confirm that only configured non-identifiable parameters are received, and sensitive fields have been desensitized according to external rules.

The values provided are common starting points and should be measured against the reader's own samples.
