# Online Troubleshooting and Retrospective for Supplier and Equipment Delivery Management: Chat Logs and Auditing

## What data for this category looks like
The core data for this category typically comes from ERP systems, supplier shipping documents, logistics information, and equipment manufacturer backends. Commonly associated documents include purchase orders, delivery acceptance forms, serial number lists, and invoice materials. Data updates are grouped by single batch purchases, with synchronization completed per order or weekly. Core fields include purchasing entity, equipment model, serial number, acceptance status, and logistics tracking number. This supports the full equipment delivery process for integration projects. Compared to general enterprise IT procurement data, it focuses more on delivery link details for individual projects.

## How these characteristics impose constraints on chat logs and auditing
Multiple data sources require chat logs to associate unique identifiers across multiple types of business documents. This ensures the full link from purchase application to equipment warehousing can be traced during audits. The per-order or weekly update rhythm requires audit logs to be archived by project batch. This makes it easier to retroactively identify fault links for individual projects. The specificity of core fields requires logs to retain key information such as equipment model and serial number. This prevents missing information during audits. Participation from multiple business entities requires logs to distinguish sessions from different operating personnel. This clarifies responsibility boundaries. Severe error consequences require logs to have non-tamperable properties. This ensures the credibility of retrospective results.

## Configuration guidelines
This configuration is based on FastGPT v4.17.0, and adapts to the business characteristics of supplier and equipment delivery management.

| Configuration Item | Recommended Setting | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `chatId` | Bind to the unique number of the purchase order | Ensure that session logs for each purchasing project can be accurately retrieved and archived, matching the per-order management business logic of this category |
| `responseChatItemId` | Associate with the unique identifier of the delivery acceptance form | Bind conversation nodes to specific acceptance links, making it easier to locate fault points in time-consuming links such as model configuration verification and serial number registration |
| `detail` | Enable full field recording, including serial number, logistics tracking number, and invoice number | Cover key fields of core category documents, meeting the complete information requirements for audit traceability |
| User chat record differentiation | Assign independent session identifiers by business entity | Distinguish operation records from project purchasers, integrator business personnel, and warehouse administrators, preventing order mixing and responsibility confusion |
| Log retention period | 7 days to 30 days | Match the per-order or weekly update frequency of category data, adapting to the time cycle of conventional project retrospectives |

## Three common misconfigurations
- Symptom: Conversation history contains redundant interaction content from designated reply plugins, which cannot be filtered via standard settings. Cause: No filtering rules are configured for the `detail` parameter, and non-business conversation fragments triggered by plugins are not excluded when generating logs.
- Symptom: An error "cannot fetch internal url" is displayed when creating an associated knowledge base using a URL, and background logs report an error. Cause: No internal resource access whitelist is configured for session logs, causing the audit link to fail to pull associated content from internal documents such as supplier shipping lists and ERP data.
- Symptom: Chat records from multiple business users show mixed orders, making it impossible to distinguish sessions from different operating personnel. Cause: `chatId` is not correctly bound to a unique business entity identifier, causing sessions from different users to be grouped under the same link.

## How to verify correct configuration
- Randomly select a completed purchase order, retrieve the session log corresponding to the `chatId`, and confirm that the log contains core fields such as purchase order number, equipment serial number, and acceptance status.
- View the session list for multiple business entities, confirm that each entity's chat records are independently archived, with no cross-entity order mixing.
- Check the log retention period configuration, confirm that it falls within the preset time range, and adjust the threshold according to actual business retrospective requirements.
- Simulate an interaction operation with a designated reply plugin, confirm that non-business plugin prompt content is not written to the audit log if filtering rules have been configured.
