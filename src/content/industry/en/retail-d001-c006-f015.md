# General export declarations: product questions with retrieval

## What the documents look like

For product questions in General export declarations, this page examines FastGPT retrieval. Compare the material received with the sources used in the answer. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the retrieval step

A new export invoice test can state, “Display-rack components, delivered disassembled.” This supports a product designation and delivery form. The rack noun alone does not establish an assembled finished item. Inspect candidates for components and disassembled, then compare the three search modes using the complete document-form question. Keep both qualifications attached to the product. This reading test does not assign a classification code or make a customs-declaration decision from retrieval.

## Three common errors

A proposed packing list separately lists an accessory bag. Inspect the level described by each document: the invoice states the overall form, while the list identifies a packed sub-item. These statements need not contradict each other and do not supply a complete assembly list. Retain original wording and file identity, checking whether a returned passage is a product description or packaging entry. Record any token-limit omission that leaves only the rack name without its disassembled condition.

## How to verify the setup

Component dimensions and manufacturing details were not supplied. Neither the rack nor accessory-bag name provides those parameters. Until corresponding technical material is obtained, preserve the missing sources without expanding them into established specifications. Repeat the question after supplementation and trace the added originals and citations. Optimization should retain components and disassembled. Check relevance under its applicable conditions, without replacing examination of document scope with a score or snippet count.
