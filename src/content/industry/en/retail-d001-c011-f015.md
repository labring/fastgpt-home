# Refund-only disputes: product questions with retrieval

## What the documents look like

For product questions in Refund-only disputes, this page examines FastGPT retrieval. Compare the material received with the sources used in the answer. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the retrieval step

A new refund-only application test says, “Missing accessory; specific model still required.” This is the applicant’s issue wording, without an identified accessory. Search the literal issue and a question about what product the available material establishes, preserving the missing-model qualification. An answer borrowing a product name from another application confuses the retrieval target. Compare the original application, cited record and question rather than treating related topics as the same item.

## Three common errors

A proposed shipment record lists a main item and accessory bag, while an appeal requests a replacement accessory. Turning the request into an established shipment event misuses the source role. Trace the three pieces of text to their actual purposes. Token limits should preserve request and application-reason contexts, beyond isolated accessory nouns. Correspondence between records still requires transaction linkage; appearing in the same candidate set does not establish that relationship.

## How to verify the setup

The model, contents list and transaction-linking evidence remain absent. They cannot be derived from main item or accessory bag. For a follow-up about which replacement to use, inspect whether optimization turns an unresolved object into a specified product, recording the source of additions. After obtaining corresponding material, repeat the question and trace new citations, separating candidate changes from settings changes. Check minimum relevance only under its applicable conditions; several hits do not establish product identity or resolve the dispute.
