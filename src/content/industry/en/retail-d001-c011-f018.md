# Refund-only disputes: product questions with hybrid retrieval

## What the documents look like

For product questions in Refund-only disputes, this page examines FastGPT hybrid retrieval. Compare the material received with the sources used in the answer. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the hybrid retrieval step

The proposed refund-only application names product A and gives “description mismatch” as the reason. Product identity and the stated reason are different information. A product question should reach the name field; a question about the complaint needs the reason’s original wording in candidates. Preserve that distinction in the full reranking question, so a nearby appeal summary does not turn an applicant’s statement into an established defect. This example contains self-drafted application text, without a dispute decision. Preserve the source role of an application reason in the wording, instead of abbreviating it into an established fact. Reading a product name is also separate from a later refund or appeal decision; neither follows from candidate order alone.

## Three common errors

Product B in the application and product C in the appeal require checking whether both documents concern the same matter. Candidates and citations need to retain their separate names. Similarity to “product dispute” does not make both passages one object. Inspect recall completeness first, then whether reranking conceals the other name. Without an order or other linking evidence, leave the identity unresolved rather than deciding which material to accept from its rank.

## How to verify the setup

A name without model or specification information leaves the selection question without sufficient evidence. Inspect whether the final description borrows a model from another case or expands a refund reason into specific performance. Once corresponding specifications are supplied, repeat the question and trace new citations, recording candidate coverage separately from ordering changes. Check the conditions for minimum relevance; a highly scored application passage does not establish complete product-selection evidence.
