# Warehouse picking and packing: product questions with retrieval

## What the documents look like

For product questions in Warehouse picking and packing, this page examines FastGPT retrieval. One paper alias covers different batches and neck forms, requiring item-by-item specification identity checks. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the retrieval step

This warehouse retrieval exercise proposes four ordinary paper texts: a picking list says blue jar, Spring batch, short neck; a specification says blue glass jar, short-neck version, applicable to Spring batch; an outer-case label says blue jar, Autumn batch; and another specification describes a long-neck blue glass jar for Autumn batch. These are invented paper descriptions, not FastGPT parameters. A question about the Spring short-neck item needs batch and neck form checked alongside its alias. The shared blue jar label cannot join the two specifications into one product. Inspect literal-term candidates, then citations for the complete batch-and-form question.

## Configuration and implementation checks

| Identity check | Proposed paper wording | Acceptance action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alias candidates | Blue jar appears on labels for both batches | Retain each source identity. |
| Complete subject | Spring batch, short neck, blue glass jar | Check all conditions in the specification. |
| Search comparison | Literal terms and batch-qualified question | Read actual candidates without promising exact matches. |
| Optimized reference | This jar remains the current short-neck item | Inspect dropped batch or added long neck. |
| Citation qualification | Specification applies to Spring or Autumn batch | Keep batch and neck form after trimming. |

## Three common errors

Full-text searches can use the paper terms blue jar and short neck to observe actual returns, while a semantic question should retain Spring batch and the requested specification relationship. Both require reading the originals, without a guarantee of exact matching. One failure returns only the Autumn label but answers using Spring specifications. Another expands a follow-up about this jar into the long-neck version during question optimization, changing the picking subject. Compare original question, optimized wording, candidate identity and actual citation to locate a dropped batch or an unsupported neck form. Similar naming provides a verification lead, not proof of shared batch or compatibility. A third error retrieves the correct Spring short-neck specification but answers only blue jar, allowing the result to be reused for Autumn goods. Compare final wording with citation applicability, retaining batch and neck form. Correct candidate identity does not replace inspection of the answer scope.

## How to verify the setup

Acceptance can separate alias candidates, the specification applicable to the complete Spring short-neck conditions and whether the original citation remains applicable after changing the batch to Autumn. The answer needs the specification sentence carrying batch and neck form; a short label alone is insufficient. Citation trimming must retain Spring or Autumn applicability rather than glass jar alone. If a material cross-reference is added, check its paper subjects and version before comparing new candidates for the same questions. Record source updates separately from settings changes. Check minimum relevance only under applicable semantic or reranking conditions; relevance cannot replace identity verification.
