## Constraints at the hybrid retrieval step

“Second shelf” and “promotional label attached” in the proposed store-visit form record display information, without describing product performance. Use product A and both recorded details to mark required candidates, then inspect whether reranking can support separate answers about location and label status. Retain the product association. Another item’s display standard cannot become an event recorded in this visit. This is a hypothetical text test, without verification of an actual store display or promotion.

## Three common errors

Ten units in the visit report and twelve in the display standard describe a recorded value and a prescribed value. Preserve those roles instead of selecting one quantity as the unified answer. Inspect candidates for both product-and-quantity statements, then check the applicable standard version and scope. The two numbers identify a review point; whether there is an operational issue requires the corresponding business confirmation, not a conclusion supplied by reranking. Preserve the roles of observed visit wording and prescribed display wording with their citations. A standard value must not become a measured store value. Whether rectification is needed depends on actual applicable requirements, beyond comparing two numbers in candidates.

## How to verify the setup

The existing report does not explicitly identify the item as new. Shelf location and a promotional label cannot supply that conclusion. Use the new-product question to inspect whether the answer infers launch status from promotion wording. After adding an explicit designation or corresponding product material, inspect its path through candidates, reranking and citations. Check minimum relevance under the actual semantic or reranking conditions, without treating the score as evidence of new-product status.
