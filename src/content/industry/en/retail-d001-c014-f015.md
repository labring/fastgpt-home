# Convenience fresh-food orders: product questions with retrieval

## What the documents look like

For product questions in Convenience fresh-food orders, this page examines FastGPT retrieval. Compare the material received with the sources used in the answer. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the retrieval step

A new fresh-food order test says, “Individually packed sandwich, recorded for the day’s order batch.” This supports reading unit packaging and record scope, without stating units per transit carton. Inspect candidates for individually packed and the day’s batch, then compare modes with the complete sales-packaging question. This ordinary-text test provides no real storage or consumption advice, and a packaging label cannot be expanded into an unsupplied quantity specification.

## Three common errors

A proposed near-expiry sheet says only “carton batch awaiting review.” Its level differs from individual packaging. An answer using carton wording to redefine the sale unit mixes batch handling with a product attribute; trace both sources. Inspect token-limited citations for omitted day-specific or awaiting-review conditions, avoiding conversion of a limited record into a confirmed state. The statements can coexist, without a retrieval ranking deciding which packaging level represents the actual product. A third error turns a batch awaiting review into a decided whole-carton disposal arrangement. Seek the actual handling decision for that batch. A pending check and an execution decision are different evidence objects; record granularity alone cannot establish the action.

## How to verify the setup

The units-per-carton count and corresponding packing specification are absent. Identify that missing material rather than calculating a count from individually packed. For a carton-count follow-up, inspect whether optimization preserves the same product and batch without inventing a number. After obtaining the actual specification, repeat the question and trace added candidates and citations, recording sources separately from settings. Minimum relevance applies to semantic search or reranking; one highly scored result cannot replace packing details.
