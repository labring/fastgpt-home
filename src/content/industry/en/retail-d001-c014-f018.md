## Constraints at the hybrid retrieval step

The fresh-food example supplies a suggested storage range of zero to four degrees Celsius for one sandwich in a proposed order attachment. Mark the product, range and the word “suggested” as the reading target before inspecting candidate completeness. Do not transfer another item’s temperature wording to this sandwich. ## Three common errors

The beverage order gives a suggested range of zero to four, while the temperature record states zero to six. These are different source roles. Inspect both candidates, establish product and period correspondence, then check whether reranking encourages a recorded range to be described as the recommended standard. Examine token trimming for lost distinctions between a suggestion and a record. Actual conformity needs the applicable evidence and complete records, beyond the ranking result. Inspect the situation described by each passage as well as its range. The available wording supplies no complete temperature history, so output must not invent continuous conformity or continuous deviation. Preserve the limited evidence scope alongside the two source statements.

## How to verify the setup

No allergen information has been supplied for the dessert. Its name or ordering temperature cannot replace ingredient evidence. Inspect whether high-ranked candidates concern another dessert or the answer makes unsupported claims about the presence or absence of an ingredient. After obtaining the actual product material, repeat the question and trace the new citation, checking filtering and ordering separately. Reranking cannot generate absent allergen evidence, and relevance does not confirm a food attribute.
