# Letter-of-credit documents: product questions with retrieval

## What the documents look like

For product questions in Letter-of-credit documents, this page examines FastGPT retrieval. Compare the material received with the sources used in the answer. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the retrieval step

A new documentary product test can use an invoice statement: “Maintenance spare-parts kit; test tools excluded.” Retrieve the spare-parts subject and exclusion together, without turning a tool-term match into inclusion. Inspect literal spare-part and tool candidates, then compare the three modes for the full contents question. This reads document wording without deciding bank acceptance, rejection or documentary conformity.

## Three common errors

A proposed insurance record broadly describes maintenance equipment, while a letter-of-credit note refers to a product-details list. An answer treating the broad equipment description as an itemized list exceeds its source scope; retain all three originals beside the actual citation. Token trimming may also remove the invoice exclusion. Trace the complete sentence to distinguish incomplete candidate coverage from a restriction lost during citation. Similar broad terms do not establish that the sources specify identical contents.

## How to verify the setup

The details list and technical parameters were not supplied. Register the two gaps separately instead of deriving a model from maintenance use. For a follow-up about tools, inspect whether optimization changes an exclusion into a request assuming tools are present, preserving both question versions. After obtaining the corresponding sources, repeat the original question and trace new citations. Check minimum relevance under semantic or reranking conditions; scores and candidate counts cannot provide details absent from the documents.
