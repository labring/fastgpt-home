# Franchise fee settlement: product questions with hybrid retrieval

## What the documents look like

For product questions in Franchise fee settlement, this page examines FastGPT hybrid retrieval. Compare the material received with the sources used in the answer. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the hybrid retrieval step

The franchise-fee example’s invoice attachment states that an invoice was issued. That written status is the reading target, without establishing actual payment or receipt of funds. Mark the original status wording, check candidates against the correct fee and rerank using the complete question. Invoices, ledgers and payment plans serve different purposes; a nearby settlement passage need not support invoicing. Preserve source identity and the scope of what the passage actually supports. Retain separate sources for invoicing wording and payment evidence, so a final summary does not compress “invoice issued” into “funds received.” Reranking checks retrieval and order; the action verb used in the answer still needs verification against the corresponding supporting record.

## Three common errors

Party A and party B are named as payer in the proposed invoice and contract payment plan respectively. Retain both identities. Inspect candidate completeness before checking whether the documents concern the same fee and applicable versions. A longer or higher-ranked contract passage cannot rewrite the invoice party. The difference can remain a review item; establishing the actual payer needs corresponding business evidence, beyond a reranking decision.

## How to verify the setup

The available invoice attachment supplies no receiving-account information. The model should not fill it from party names or the title of a deposit ledger. Inspect candidates and final wording for an account borrowed from an unrelated source. Once the corresponding authorized material is obtained, repeat the question and trace the new citation. Candidate order does not establish an account’s authenticity, ownership or completed settlement; preserve those distinctions in the review record.
