# Certificates of origin: product questions with document chunking

## What the documents look like

For product questions in Certificates of origin, this page examines FastGPT document chunking. Keep title scope, production statements and transport side notes distinct in origin-product text. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the document chunking step

A proposed origin-product page is titled stand housing, states coating at Pinebridge Works and adds a side note that the complete device ships from Eastbank Warehouse. The question asks whether the whole device is produced at Pinebridge. Parsing needs housing scope and coating retained, without joining whole device from the side note to the production statement. Dispatch location explains no manufacturing process. Other-component records are absent. No actual origin rule is introduced; the exercise checks layout scope, including a short title that must not lose its subject restriction during chunking.

## Three common errors

First, the housing title may detach from the coating sentence; compare heading hierarchy, parsed paragraphs and block for the statement’s actual subject. Second, whole-device dispatch may merge into production; inspect side-note origin and action words rather than deriving manufacture from shipment. Third, another page’s complete-device assembly may serve the similarly named block; compare product, component and version. These concern heading, sidebar and source identity. Related excerpts appearing together cannot complete a production chain or broaden a component-level statement beyond what its own original text actually asserts.

## How to verify the setup

Ask which object the page covers, which process it describes and what the whole-device note records, retaining layout-to-text-to-reference correspondence. Repeat housing, coating and natural whole-production questions for supported scope without promising exact matching. Added component records need item and version checked before processing and reference adoption. Missing production-chain evidence remains pending rather than proving a false statement. This test decides no origin eligibility and submits no application. It establishes no verified real factory or transport event beyond the proposed statements and their preserved document relationships.
