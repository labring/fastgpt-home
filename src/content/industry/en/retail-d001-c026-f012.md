# Brand-owned store operations: product questions with document chunking

## What the documents look like

For product questions in Brand-owned store operations, this page examines FastGPT document chunking. Preserve shared headings, optional markings and role examples in company-store supplies text. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the document chunking step

A proposed company-store catalogue shares an employee-clothing heading across a shirt with cuff buttons and an apron with optional pockets. An adjacent person is labelled a manager illustration. The question asks whether the apron has buttons and is manager-only. Parsing must preserve item boundaries. The person supplies no exclusive-use scope, and a shared heading does not share every attribute. Detaching cuff buttons from the shirt could invent apron contents. A role caption also records no actual appointment or staffing decision for anyone shown in the hypothetical material.

## Three common errors

First, shirt cuff attributes may transfer to the apron; compare catalogue item, parsed row and block for the correct subject. Second, optional pockets may summarize into included pockets; inspect the selection marker with its item rather than filling state from an illustration. Third, a manager caption may become exclusive apron use; read caption purpose and actual product-scope sources. These concern item identity, selection state and illustrative role. A common heading or nearby figure establishes no shared attribute or staff eligibility for every product under that heading in the proposed catalogue.

## How to verify the setup

Ask what each garment states and what the figure explains, retaining hierarchy, parsed text and citations. Repeat shirt-button, apron-pocket and natural role questions after chunk edits for correctly labelled returns without promising precision. Updated options require processing, original-data and actual-reference checks rather than upload presence. Missing exclusive-use evidence remains unresolved, without proving universal use. This exercise manufactures no uniform, changes no role and contains no real staff data. It establishes no actual garment receipt or adoption beyond the hypothetical catalogue wording and the source relationships preserved for its reading.
