# Customer-service knowledge: citation tracing for product questions

## What the documents look like

This page covers knowledge-base maintenance for product data and selection questions, within e-commerce customer service and after-sales work. The documents handled day to day are FAQ entries, reply templates, product parameter tables and escalation tickets. Product operations supplies parameter tables, knowledge-base operations rewrites the standard replies, and the customer-service supervisor performs quality spot checks. Material updates run weekly or monthly, and synchronizing new-product parameters and consolidating high-frequency questions take up much of the time. This task does not approve refunds; it is upstream question-and-answer material preparation. Failures show up as answers that miss the question, escalated complaints, or failed customer-service training.

Citation tracing requires that an answer can be returned to the specific source text. Product material commonly contains fields such as model, applicable scenario and parameter range, and selection questions need to separate what the material states from what it does not state. When the material does not provide a field, that field is unknown and cannot be filled in by inference from other entries.

Two features of this material shape the task. First, parameter tables and selection questions revolve around model identifiers and numeric ranges, so a citation check has to compare the same subject and the same field rather than accept a fluent answer. Second, the same product family may have several models with overlapping fields, so a sentence that is correct for one model can be wrong when attached to another. Weekly or monthly updates also mean that a cited version may already be superseded, and the source version has to be confirmed alongside the wording.

## Constraints at the citation tracing step

The knowledge-base search node can be associated with one or more knowledge bases that use the same vector model, and it outputs cited content as an array whose length can be zero. Even when nothing is found, that output path still runs. The citation limit controls, by tokens, the maximum amount of content cited in a single search. The minimum relevance value takes effect only in semantic search or when result reranking is used. Hybrid search merges multiple result paths through RRF, and a rerank model produces a score from 0 to 1. These mechanisms affect recall and ordering; they do not constitute permission or entity isolation, and retrieved results still need manual source checking.

Hypothetical paper exercise (paper material, not a real record): the source text of a customer-service knowledge-base entry reads in three sentences: "The A-type water purifier filter replacement interval is 6 to 12 months, subject to the water quality test result. The B-type water purifier applicable water pressure range is 0.1 to 0.4 MPa. The C-type water purifier included accessories contain one pre-filter." A separate draft sentence awaiting judgment reads: "The B-type water purifier filter replacement interval is 6 months, with an applicable water pressure of 0.1 to 0.4 MPa." The handover request states: "Please confirm this draft sentence can be used directly in selection question answering." That request is in a pending-review state. Pending review does not demonstrate that operating permission is absent, and it does not constitute approval.

Several conditions follow from this. The draft sentence mixes a field that the source states for one model with a field the source never states for that model. Retrieval returning the entry does not establish that every field in the draft is supported. The pending-review status is a separate fact from approval, and an unsigned or unapproved handover is not a completed authorization. A relevance threshold is a filtering value and does not exclude unrelated businesses or unauthorized records.

## Three common errors

1. Treating the B-type filter replacement interval in the draft sentence as a stated fact. The sample source states a replacement interval only for the A type, and for the B type states only the water pressure range. The input is the draft sentence and the three source sentences; compare the subject of each field. The B-type replacement interval is not stated in the source and is unsupported.
2. Treating the pending-review handover request as approved. The request text is "Please confirm this draft sentence can be used directly in selection question answering," and its status is pending review. Compare the request status against the existence of an approval record. With only a pending-review status, the matter is unverified; a signed or approved record would require a separate check that its scope covers this draft sentence.
3. Using minimum relevance to filter unrelated businesses or unauthorized records. Minimum relevance is a 0-to-1 filtering threshold and does not perform permission or entity isolation. The input is the threshold setting and the returned candidates; compare whether the candidates come from the associated base. An unauthorized-record problem needs a separate permission implementation.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the three source sentences and the draft sentence from the hypothetical sample, and compare each field of the draft against the same subject and same field in the source. The A-type replacement interval matches the source; the B-type water pressure range matches the source; the B-type replacement interval is not stated in the source. When an unstated field is written into the answer, judge the source as unsupported.
2. Input the pending-review handover request, and compare the request status against whether an approval record exists. With only a pending-review status, judge the matter as unverified. If a signed or approved record appears, separately check whether its approval scope covers this draft sentence.
3. Input an explicit paper citation array of `[]`, and compare the design of the downstream answering step. If the downstream step runs through the empty array and outputs that no content was retrieved, this matches the documented output path whose length may be zero. If the downstream step treats the empty array as retrieved content, judge the handoff as incorrect. Whether retrieval actually returns an empty array depends on the real data and parameters; the paper input does not mean that a fictional rule's retrieval necessarily returns `[]`.
