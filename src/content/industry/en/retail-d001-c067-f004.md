# Group-buy leader operations: vector indexes for product questions

## What the documents look like

Group-buy leader operations revolve around community nodes and commission settlement. A leader agreement defines the cooperating parties and the commission basis. A commission table records accrual rates per group round. A pickup list names arrived goods and quantities. A community announcement carries the day's group opening and after-sales notes. These materials update daily and per group, and the same pickup list corresponds to different batches on different dates. Product data and selection questions work on exactly this kind of text: whether a product is sellable in a given group opening, what its specification is, and which commission tier applies.

Two consequences follow. First, the same product name may appear under different wordings in the agreement, the commission table, and the announcement, so retrieval must return to the specific document to check the subject and the validity period. Second, the authorization state in a leader agreement and the accrual record in a commission table are different facts; a retrieval hit on a passage shows that the passage exists, not that authorization has taken effect.

## Constraints at the vector indexes step

A knowledge base is made of library, collection, and data. The smallest search unit is the library; a collection only classifies data and does not affect search results. Knowledge bases linked for retrieval must use the same vector model, so the actual model and input type are checked first. Libraries on different vector models cannot be linked together; verify the identifier and exclude those that do not match.

In the MongoDB `dataset.datas` table, an `indexes` field records the corresponding vector IDs as an array, so one data item can map to multiple vectors. One source data block can associate with several indexes, and indexes are checked separately from the source text. Build indexes from complete and focused information, and weigh retrieval coverage against index length using samples.

Image retrieval also depends on whether image descriptions or image vectors were generated at ingestion time, and cannot be judged from the query side alone. Semantic retrieval computes similarity through vector distance, full-text retrieval depends on keyword hits, hybrid retrieval fuses the two paths through RRF, and Rerank performs a second ordering of candidate text. RRF is a fusion process for mixed results, not a standalone switch. Image retrieval requires the corresponding image input and model capability, and reranking requires the model and its configured enablement. Optional paths shown in the overall flow diagram do not mean every request executes all of them.

## Configuration and implementation checks

The following is a hypothetical drill sample. Source text: A community group-buy platform signs a leader agreement with Leader A. The agreement states a cooperation period from March 1, 2025 to August 31, 2025, with commission accrued at 8% for fresh produce. A pickup list dated April 10, 2025 lists 120 portions of a batch of vegetables. Draft sentence to be judged: Leader A may sell that batch of vegetables in April 2025 and accrue commission at 8%.

## Three common errors

1. Treating a retrieval hit as effective authorization. The draft sentence says Leader A may sell that batch of vegetables in April 2025. A retrieval hit on the agreement text only shows the agreement states a cooperation period. Signing, reaching cooperation, and contract effectiveness are three different facts; when the text does not state them, or only shows a related stage, the result is unsupported or pending verification.
2. Linking libraries on different vector models. The input is two libraries with different model identifiers. The comparison item is the model identifier, and the observed result is that the identifiers do not match. Exclude the non-matching library and record the actual observation; do not run cross-model retrieval to compare missed recalls.
3. Treating minimum relevance as an entity or permission filter. The input is a similarity threshold setting. The comparison item is that setting against the business ownership of returned records. The observed result is that the threshold only affects similarity ordering; semantic recall and relevance do not handle permission or entity isolation.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the agreement text from the hypothetical sample and the draft sentence. Compare the sellable period stated in the draft sentence with the cooperation period stated in the agreement, March 1, 2025 to August 31, 2025. Observe whether the stated April 2025 falls inside that interval. Falling inside satisfies the period condition only; the commission rate still requires separate checking against the commission table.
2. Input the model identifiers of the libraries to be linked. Compare whether the identifiers are consistent. Observe that consistent identifiers permit linking together. When identifiers differ, record and exclude them, and do not infer retrieval results from that observation.
3. Input an explicit paper empty-result array. Compare how the downstream node handles a zero-length reference. Observe whether that output path runs through. Do not claim that a fictional rule makes retrieval always return an empty array.

After a model or index change, compare cited source text using the same set of questions, and do not promise results from vector scores alone. A pending review state does not show a lack of operating permission, and a quotation validity period does not indicate a supply period.
