# Cross-border product labels: document chunking for product questions

## What the documents look like

Cross-border product compliance and labelling material sits around product safety and label evidence, and it is used before listing and before import. The main sources are platform policy centres, testing laboratory reports, destination-country regulation libraries and supplier material. The documents handled day to day are product test reports, English labels, MSDS files and platform prohibited-item lists. Errors at this stage lead to platform delisting, customs detention or consumer claims. Updates are irregular, and each new product launch can bring a new version of a document.

The structures differ widely. A test report carries a sample name, test items, a standard number and a conclusion. An English label carries ingredients, warnings and compliance statements. An MSDS is organised into fixed sections. A prohibited-item list presents entries with the platforms they apply to. Chunking has to preserve these structures and the constraints they place on each other, so that a condition, its explanation and the content it governs are not separated.

## Constraints at the document chunking step

Chunking follows the structure of the specific document. One set of source data can map to several indexes, and the source data must be distinguished from the retrieval indexes built from it. For retrieval, semantic search suits natural-language questions, while full-text search suits numbers, model designations and proper nouns. Hybrid search merges multiple result paths through RRF. Reranking requires a model and is enabled according to configuration. Image retrieval requires image input and the corresponding model capability. The paths shown in the overall workflow diagram do not mean that every request executes all of them.

Similarity and relevance affect ordering and filtering only. They do not perform permission isolation or entity isolation, and retrieval does not constitute authorisation. Libraries built on different vector models cannot be associated together; identifiers are checked and non-matching libraries are excluded, with the actual observation recorded.

Hypothetical paper exercise sample

Material text for checking (hypothetical): A test report for a batch of portable power banks states "Sample name: portable power bank; test items: battery capacity and overcharge protection; standard applied: IEC 62133-2; conclusion: the tested items meet the requirements." The English label draft states "Model: PB-100; Capacity: 10000mAh; Warning: Do not expose to high temperature." A platform prohibited-item list entry states "Products containing lithium batteries must provide a UN38.3 test summary."

Draft sentence to be assessed (hypothetical): This product has completed all compliance testing and can be listed and sold on the destination platform.

## Configuration and implementation checks

## Three common errors

1. Expanding "the tested items meet the requirements" in the test report into "all compliance testing completed". The sample text covers battery capacity and overcharge protection only, and the word "all" in the draft sentence has no supporting evidence.
2. Treating the platform prohibited-item list entry and the test report as the same piece of evidence. In the sample, the UN38.3 test summary and the IEC 62133-2 report are two separate objects and cannot substitute for each other.
3. Treating a retrieval result or a form submission as an authorised action. Retrieval does not constitute authorisation; a pending review does not demonstrate that operating permission is absent; a field not stated in the document remains unknown.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the sample material text and the draft sentence, then compare the scope of "all compliance testing" against "the tested items" in the original. When the draft sentence widens the scope beyond what the original states, the finding is unsupported and the original must be checked again.
2. Input "UN38.3 test summary" and "IEC 62133-2" from the sample, then compare whether the two refer to the same test object. When they are merged into one piece of evidence, the finding is object confusion and each must be recorded separately.
3. Input the sample material text and compare whether the chunking result keeps "standard applied: IEC 62133-2" adjacent to "conclusion". When the condition and the conclusion are separated, the finding is incomplete chunking and the split must be redone. An empty result can be checked against a downstream design using an explicit paper input of []; this does not assert that a fictional rule search will return [].
