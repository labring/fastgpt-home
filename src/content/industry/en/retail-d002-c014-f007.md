# Convenience fresh-food orders: conversation logs for after-sales questions

## What the documents look like

Convenience-store fresh food moves on short shelf lives and high-frequency store-level ordering. The store manager places orders within a shift, and the working records are the fresh-food order form, the damage write-off form, the near-expiry handling sheet and the cold-chain temperature log. These sit alongside POS sales, the headquarters ordering system and store inventory as the main data sources. The conversation logs and audit step handles the correspondence between those documents and after-sales questions, so the order form and write-off form carry a date range plus a store and shift identifier that become the basic unit of reconciliation. Records update by day or by shift. The existence of a log shows that session content was recorded; reconciliation, retention periods and compliance judgments require a separate, complete business arrangement.

For this task, three industry facts shape the work. First, fresh food is ordered by the store on a short shelf life rather than handled through a joint-operation revenue split, so the store and shift identity on the order matters more than a counter identity. Second, the daily and per-shift update cadence means an order form and its after-sales conversation must be matched within the same period and the same field before any conclusion is drawn. Third, an error here can surface as expired sales, stockout loss or a food-safety complaint, which is why the audit trail has to be checked against the actual returned fields rather than assumed complete.

## Constraints at the conversation logs step

Conversation identity is distinguished by chatId. Different entry points under the same app produce different chatIds, so when checking business records, compare chatId against the target conversation first and avoid merging sessions from different entry points into one calculation. When run detail is needed, set detail according to the official contract and read module responses inside responseData. Fields that are not returned cannot be treated as fully retained. A similarity value returned by a retrieval module expresses text relevance only and does not act as a store, region or permission filter. Retrieval is not authorization.

Hypothetical paper exercise. A convenience-store manager submits a fresh-food order form on the morning shift of 10 June 2025. The order number is XD-20250610-07, the objects are sandwiches and rice balls, and the status is submitted and awaiting headquarters confirmation. In the same day's after-sales conversation record, a draft reply reads: "This order has been confirmed and has entered delivery." The draft sentence lacks evidence of headquarters confirmation, the order status remains awaiting confirmation, and the draft wording does not match the source material.

## Three common errors

1. Merging sessions with different chatIds into one calculation. In the exercise, the after-sales session is HY-20250610-A. Mixing it with a session from another entry point misattributes the correspondence of order XD-20250610-07. Check: input two chatIds, compare the dataId lists each returns, and observe whether any cross-over appears.
2. Treating a similarity value as a permission filter. In the exercise, the returned score reflects text relevance only and cannot establish that the record belongs to that store. Check: input the same query, compare the dataset_id of returned records against the target library identifier, and observe whether records from other libraries appear.
3. Writing an awaiting-confirmation status as confirmed. In the exercise, the draft sentence "This order has been confirmed and has entered delivery" conflicts with the form status "submitted and awaiting headquarters confirmation". Check: input the draft sentence and the source form text, compare the status field values, and observe whether mutually exclusive values appear.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input sample chatId HY-20250610-A and order number XD-20250610-07, then compare the chatId returned by getHistories against the target value. The condition is met when that chatId appears uniquely in the returned list. When other chatIds are mixed in, record it as not matching.
2. Input the sample draft sentence and the source form text, then compare the two status expressions "confirmed" and "submitted and awaiting headquarters confirmation". The condition is met when the draft sentence does not present a confirmed conclusion. When a confirmed conclusion appears, record it as not matching.
3. Input a request with detail=true, then compare whether the returned structure contains responseData. The condition is met when module-level responses are present. When only choices are returned without responseData, record it as unsupported and keep it for further verification.
