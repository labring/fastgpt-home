# Tmall flagship operations: HTTP integrations for after-sales questions

## What the documents look like

Tmall flagship store operations for order fulfillment and after-sales questions draw on four material types: product publishing pages, brand authorization letters, delivery commitment records, and after-sales tickets. The brand authorization letter determines flagship store identity and category qualifications. Delivery commitment records carry the 48-hour delivery promise. After-sales tickets record return, exchange, and compensation communication. These materials update daily and per order. That update rhythm does not change the functional boundary of this page.

The HTTP integration step sends fields from these materials to external services or retrieves status from them. Request addresses and methods follow the external interface documentation; addresses may reference supplied variables. Params and Headers are checked separately, and authentication credentials are injected through security configuration rather than written into public examples. Body applies only under the corresponding request type, and the JSON structure is checked against the external interface contract. Variables come from global variables, system variables, and upstream node outputs, and actual passed values are checked item by item. chatId and responseChatItemId do not exist in test mode, so a debug empty value does not equal a business fault.

Two industry facts shape this task. First, Tmall flagship stores place weight on brand authorization and flagship identity, so authorization materials and category qualifications are part of the operating record. Second, after-sales tickets and delivery commitment records carry the 48-hour delivery promise and compensation communication, which means the HTTP step may carry order and ticket fields outward. Neither fact turns retrieval or form collection into an authorization action.

## Constraints at the HTTP integrations step

Hypothetical paper exercise. The following materials are for paper checking only and point to no real store or real identity.

Material one (brand authorization letter excerpt): "Authorizing party A Brand Management Co., Ltd. authorizes authorized party B E-Commerce Co., Ltd. to operate the A Brand flagship store on the Tmall platform. Authorization validity runs from January 1, 2025 to December 31, 2025. Authorized category is home appliances."

Material two (delivery commitment record excerpt): "Order number T20250301001, payment time March 1, 2025, 10:00, committed delivery time March 3, 2025, 10:00, current status: shipped, logistics tracking number not returned."

Draft sentence to be judged: "The flagship store authorization has expired, so the after-sales ticket may be handled as unauthorized."

Judgment point: Material one's authorization validity covers all of 2025. The draft sentence states the authorization has expired, which conflicts with the validity period value in the material text. Material two records only delivery status and does not state after-sales handling authority. The draft sentence treats delivery status as an authorization basis, which is unsupported.

The constraint is that the HTTP module only sends requests per the external interface contract and parses return values. Retrieval or forms do not constitute authorization execution. Business verification, approval, database writes, and external synchronization each require their own stated downstream implementation. A similarity score is not a category, entity, or permission filter, and retrieval results do not carry authorization decisions.

## Three common errors

1. Treating a retrieval hit as an authorization basis. Input: a retrieved after-sales ticket record. Comparison: whether that record states the authorizing subject, object, and validity period. Observable result: when the record states no authorization field, authorization status remains unknown.

2. Treating an empty chatId in test mode as a business fault. Input: a request under test mode. Comparison: whether chatId and responseChatItemId exist in that mode. Observable result: both are absent in test mode, which is a documented condition and does not constitute a business exception.

3. Treating a similarity score as a permission filter. Input: a retrieval result carrying score. Comparison: whether that result carries a category or permission field. Observable result: the score reflects similarity only and does not exclude unrelated businesses or unauthorized records.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input: material one's text and the draft sentence "The flagship store authorization has expired." Comparison: material one's authorization validity of January 1, 2025 to December 31, 2025 against the expiry claimed in the draft sentence. Observable result: the two take different values for the same validity field, and the draft sentence is unsupported by the material.

2. Input: material two's text and the draft sentence "The after-sales ticket may be handled as unauthorized." Comparison: whether material two states after-sales handling authority. Observable result: material two records only delivery status and whether the logistics tracking number was returned, states no after-sales authority, and the conclusion is unsupported, with the evidence gap retained.

3. Input: an HTTP request to be sent, with the authentication credential in Headers injected through security configuration. Comparison: whether that credential appears in public examples or the body text. Observable result: the credential does not appear in public examples and the configuration meets the secure injection condition. Whether the request is accepted by the external system is a separate fact that requires an actual call to observe, and this page makes no assertion about it.
