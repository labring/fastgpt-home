# Customer-service knowledge: citation tracing for after-sales questions

## What the documents look like

Customer-service knowledge-base maintenance sits in e-commerce customer service and after-sales work. It is preparation of question-and-answer material that happens before customer contact, and it does not directly approve refunds. The everyday documents are FAQ entries, reply-script templates, product parameter tables and escalation tickets. Their sources include the customer-service system, product detail pages, after-sales question statistics and quality-check sampling. Material is refreshed on a weekly or monthly rhythm, and the time-consuming steps are collecting high-frequency questions, rewriting standard answers and synchronising new product parameters.

For citation tracing, this means the original text, version and effective scope behind an answer must remain traceable to a specific document. A checking chain has to survive between the moment material is organised and the moment retrieval returns output. When knowledge-base operators, customer-service supervisors and product operations hand work over, they should be able to state which version of which material a given answer relies on. Two industry facts shape this page directly. First, the material set is mixed: FAQ entries, scripts, parameter tables and escalation tickets carry different structures and different owners, so a single retrieved passage rarely carries its own version label. Second, updates run on a weekly or monthly cadence, which means a retrieved passage and the current effective version can diverge without any retrieval error occurring.

## Constraints at the citation tracing step

The knowledge-base search node can be linked to one or more knowledge bases that share the same vector model, and it outputs cited content as an array. That array length may be zero, which means the output path still runs even when nothing is found. Whether the cited content is adopted by the answering step, and whether the original text is displayed, belongs to downstream implementation and manual checking. The retrieval step performs recall and fusion only. It does not decide whether a piece of material has been authorised or whether it is the current effective version.

Hypothetical paper exercise sample: in March, a knowledge-base operator is organising a batch of order-fulfilment and after-sales question-and-answer material. One FAQ entry reads in full: "A return may be requested within seven days after the order is signed for, and the goods must remain in good condition. This rule applies to ordinary goods; customised goods are excluded." A separate escalation ticket records: "Ticket number H-0312, status pending review. The applicant proposes changing 'seven days' in the above FAQ to 'fifteen days'. No confirmation has been signed." The draft sentence to be judged is: "Customised goods may have a return requested within fifteen days after signing." Judging whether this draft sentence is supported by the original text requires comparison of the same subject, the same period and the same field.

## Three common errors

1. Treating retrieval output as an authorisation conclusion. Finding a piece of material means only that the content was recalled; it does not mean the material has been approved or is the effective version. In the sample, ticket H-0312 has the status pending review. Pending review does not demonstrate that operating permission is absent, and it does not constitute approval of the draft sentence.
2. Mixing evidence from different fields. The original text states "seven days" and limits itself to ordinary goods; the draft sentence states "fifteen days" and points at customised goods. Both the time field and the applicable object differ, so the original text cannot be used to conclude that it supports or rejects the draft sentence. A field that is not stated, or evidence that covers only a related stage, is recorded as unsupported or pending verification.
3. Treating a similarity threshold as an entity or permission filter. Minimum relevance is a filtering threshold. It does not exclude unrelated businesses or unauthorised records, and it does not ensure that the first hit is correct or that all matching records are recalled. Knowledge bases built on different vector models cannot be linked together; check the identifier, exclude those that do not match, and do not run a cross-model search to compare missed recalls.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the sample FAQ original text and the draft sentence, then compare whether the two agree on subject, applicable object and time field. The original text limits itself to ordinary goods with a time of seven days; the draft sentence points at customised goods with a time of fifteen days. The fields do not agree, so the draft sentence is not supported by the original text and is recorded as pending verification.
2. Input the pending-review record of ticket H-0312 and compare its status field against the facts of signature and effectiveness. Pending review is not approval, and an unsigned change is not a completed modification. Record this as an evidence gap, and do not supply an authorisation or effectiveness clause to close it.
3. Input the paper citation array [] and compare whether the downstream answering step is designed to accept an empty result. Observe whether the output path runs. Make no claim that a fictional-rule search returns [], and make no claim that unrelated queries are always empty.
