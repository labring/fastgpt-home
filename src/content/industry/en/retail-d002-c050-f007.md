# Complaints and official replies: conversation logs for after-sales questions

## What the documents look like
Complaints and official replies are directed to a platform or a regulatory authority, so they require formal evidence and rectification wording. This differs from routine after-sales repair or reshipment handling. Data sources include the 12315 platform, platform complaint centers, customer-service chat, and order systems. Everyday documents include complaint tickets, regulatory inquiry letters, order evidence, and rectification reports. This page addresses only conversation logs and auditing for order fulfilment and after-sales question handling within this category. The presence of a stored log does not mean a compliance audit has been completed. The supplied material gives an update frequency of irregular, per case. This page does not derive batch synchronization, real-time processing, or transaction-triggered mechanisms from that frequency.

## Constraints at the conversation logs step
Conversation identity needs to be checked against chatId together with the target conversation and business record. Sessions from different entry points are not counted together. Where run details are needed, set detail according to the official contract and inspect which fields responseData actually returns. Business data that is not returned cannot be presumed to have been retained in full. Retrieval and similarity carry recall and ranking only; they do not carry permission or entity isolation, and a retrieval result does not constitute authorization. Reconciliation, retention, and compliance requirements are implemented separately by the complete business solution. An interactive node response returns interactive containing only type and params; internal runtime-state fields are not returned. Continuing the run requires keeping chatId consistent, and form input is passed as an object serialized to a string in the messages content.

Hypothetical drill sample: a shop customer-service supervisor receives a regulatory inquiry letter, with a hypothetical reference XF-2024-001, concerning a hypothetical order DD-2024-001. The complainant states that customer service once promised a reshipment. The supplied material text reads: "The shop's customer service stated in the conversation on 1 March 2024 that a reshipment was possible, and that the dispatch time would be subject to later notice." The proposed finding sentence reads: "The conversation completed the reshipment and notified the complainant." That sentence contains two facts, completion of the reshipment and notification. The material records only a statement that a reshipment was possible; it does not record whether the reshipment was dispatched or whether notification occurred.

## Three common errors
1. Treating a retrieval hit as authorization. The input is a historical conversation with a high similarity score; the comparison object is the current complainant's identity and order ownership; the observable result is whether a record belonging to another entity appears. Similarity does not exclude unrelated business or unauthorized records.
2. Writing a statement that a reshipment was possible as a completed reshipment. The input is the material text above; the comparison object is "completed the reshipment" in the proposed finding sentence; the observable result is whether the material records dispatch and notification. Where the material does not record them, the claim is unsupported.
3. Treating a pending review as approved. The input is a "pending review" status in a handover request; the comparison object is evidence of a completed approval; the observable result is whether an approval record exists. A pending review does not show that operating permission is absent, and it is not an approval.

## How to verify the setup
1. Input the hypothetical material text and the proposed finding sentence; compare "a reshipment was possible" with "completed the reshipment and notified the complainant"; observe whether the proposed sentence goes beyond the source text. If it does, the condition is not met.
2. Input the hypothetical chatId value XF-CHAT-001; compare it with the conversation identifier in the customer-service chat record; observe whether both point to the same conversation. Only a match satisfies the same-entity check.
3. Input a hypothetical responseData of an empty array []; compare it with the downstream design's handling condition for empty references; observe whether the downstream treats it as an empty result. This does not assert that a rule-based retrieval will return an empty result.
