# E-commerce warehouse receiving: after-sales questions with conversation logs

## What the documents look like

For after-sales questions in E-commerce warehouse receiving, this page examines FastGPT conversation logs. “Found it” in warehouse service history may refer to a task or to physical goods. Review follows the referent and actual location evidence. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the conversation logs step

A proposed buyer asks where the goods are. Staff say they found the shelving task, and the assistant replies that the goods occupy the planned shelf. Link the purchase arrival and conversation identifier, then inspect surrounding passages under the actual detail response. Finding a task is not finding stock. The task supplies a planned location without proving current position. An omitted warehouse receipt also does not establish that the warehouse was queried.

## Configuration and implementation checks

| Inbound-location answer | Proposed passage | Distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What was found | Shelving task | Not physical goods |
| Position source | Task’s planned shelf | Separate actual location |
| Arrival identity | Conversation linked to purchase arrival | Exclude another record |
| Response contents | Warehouse receipt absent | No claim of querying stock |
| Business state | Task allocation visible | Not usable inventory |

## Three common errors

Misread referent: Finding the task summarized as finding goods needs the actual object in surrounding messages. Planned position promoted to current location needs shelving or count evidence for this item. A different purchase arrival used here requires conversation-linkage and material-origin checks. These inspect sentence meaning, location status and event identity rather than answer completeness.

## How to verify the setup

Retain the question, task passage and assistant location answer. Add this item’s actual position evidence and inspect whether the follow-up uses it. A newly assigned task still cannot establish shelved goods. Stock correction, discrepancy investigation and external commitments require subsequent business records. Querying history locates the faulty inference without changing locations or proving that procurement goods entered usable inventory.
