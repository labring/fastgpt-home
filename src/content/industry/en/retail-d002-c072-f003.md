# Overseas stock replenishment: after-sales questions with shared access

## What the documents look like

For after-sales questions in Overseas stock replenishment, this page examines FastGPT shared access. Separate warehouse appointments and delivery records; entry access confirms no receipt. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the shared access step

Suppose replenishment awaits supplier confirmation, an inbound appointment is merely received, a stock-age sheet is an earlier snapshot, and a last-mile note concerns another order. A shared support entry needs a defined audience and material scope. Appointment receipt proves no current-batch arrival. Invented samples include no actual stock, waybill or arrival date; a public entry grants no whole-warehouse inventory access.

## Three common errors

- If the publisher reads the stock-age sheet but a visitor cannot, inspect the same resource, intended identity and citation address. Administrator results cannot replace visitor tests.

- If appointment receipt becomes completed inbound delivery, inspect warehouse acknowledgments and planning stages. Entry availability proves no logistics outcome.

- If another order’s delivery note becomes this batch’s receipt, compare order and recipient. A shared warehouse name does not merge orders.

## How to verify the setup

Test approved examples through the actual shared or embedded entry, inspecting answers, originals and attachments. Review internal collaboration and external support audiences separately. Embedding grants no broader inventory publication. Verify access after closure. Real replenishment and logistics queries need separate interfaces and authority; this exercise performs neither.
