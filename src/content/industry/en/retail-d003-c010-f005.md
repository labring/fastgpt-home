# JD POP seller operations: copy drafting with multi-turn prompts

## What the documents look like

For copy drafting in JD POP seller operations, this page examines FastGPT multi-turn prompts. Preserve source conditions during copy revisions so another product’s benefits or service wording do not enter the current draft. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the multi-turn prompts step

A writing exercise starts with an accessory’s interface and packaging descriptions, then asks to emphasize that earlier advantage. Preserve the original statement behind the advantage instead of expanding an interface into speed, compatibility or performance promises. Compare question, historical material and output when such claims appear, identifying the unverifiable support. Sources can introduce the product without supporting every promotional expression. Repeated polishing does not add original facts.

## Three common errors

- If interface wording becomes speed, compatibility or performance promises, trace originals, history and added benefits. The interface statement supplies no such conditions; repeated polishing adds no facts.

- If writing another item the same way retains earlier packaging or service, compare both originals and optimized question. Separate overwrite of delivered evidence from missing evidence.

- If registered repair becomes completed service or a broader warranty, compare record and draft, obtaining actual conditions from the owner. Historical promotional phrasing creates no new commitment.

## How to verify the setup

Compare empty-history, same-product and changed-product writing tasks, tracing every added claim to an original. A registered repair cannot become completed service or a wider warranty promise in copy; obtain actual conditions from the responsible owner. For structured handoff to an editor, inspect fields and source correspondence, then actual writing. Generated text updates neither a product-information table nor a shop page. Preserve draft identity so classroom material and unconfirmed conditions never appear as formal commitments.
