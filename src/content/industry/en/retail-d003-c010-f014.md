## Constraints at the model connections step

The proposed product attachment states plastic as its material, providing one explicit fact for copy input but no recyclability or environmental-result evidence. Inspect delivery of that sentence before reviewing which draft statements it supports. If the original never arrives but output names a material, investigate history or generation as the source. Connectivity does not establish factual support. The task can restate a supplied attribute; additional benefits need their corresponding product evidence rather than inference from a material name.

## Three common errors

- If material wording never arrives but the answer specifies it, retain input and statements to inspect history or generated extension. Connectivity establishes no support for the fact.

- If plastic and metal arrive but only the earlier attachment is used, inspect product and version, then compare the response for overwrite. Model choice does not decide actual material.

- If plastic becomes recyclability or an environmental outcome, trace applicable evidence and cited conditions. Keep missing support unresolved; repeat the original question after adding the description.

## How to verify the setup

A recyclability question needs the missing source identified instead of a forced yes-or-no answer. Inspect whether the draft expands plastic into recyclable and whether its citation contains that condition. Keep it unresolved without support. After obtaining the applicable description, repeat the original question and settings, retaining input and the passage adopted. If the actual file includes a label image or diagram, inspect that capability separately. The current text result does not validate every material form.
