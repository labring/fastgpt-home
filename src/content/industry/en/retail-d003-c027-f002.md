# Dealer ordering events: copy drafting with context limits

## What the documents look like

For copy drafting in Dealer ordering events, this page examines FastGPT context limits. An ordering-event invitation can describe samples and interest registration without confirming supply. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the context limits step

A proposed dealer-event exhibit list offers samples for viewing. A registration page collects preferred styles, with bulk supply pending separate confirmation. A short invitation may describe viewing and registration without converting every displayed sample into available stock merely because the occasion is an ordering event. Proposed headings for arrangements and pending matters are ordinary copy organisation, not business-state fields. Check that the real generation request includes the note; heading names themselves cannot establish availability, acceptance of an order or any other commercial result.

## Three common errors

First, copy may present display samples as stock; trace the identity term in the exhibit list and inspect added inventory conclusions. Second, compression may change pending supply into confirmation; compare note and output for stage and give the condition room. Third, style registration may become purchase; inspect the dealer audience and preference record in the request rather than equating a recorded preference with an order. These concern sample nature, supply state and actor behaviour. Larger context cannot replace the confirmation material missing from this proposed set.

## How to verify the setup

Ask what visitors can do, whether supply is confirmed and what registration establishes, comparing actual input with the draft. Historical ordering records need the correct event round and style before use; they cannot create orders for displayed samples. Check reply budget separately from reference volume and verify the actual model context capability. This exercise sends no invitation, gathers no real contact data and creates no order. Copy can describe proposed arrangements, while viewing samples and collecting interest remain insufficient evidence of an actual supply commitment to a dealer.
