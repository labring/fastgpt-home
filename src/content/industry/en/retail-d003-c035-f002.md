# Trade returns and claims: copy drafting with context limits

## What the documents look like

For copy drafting in Trade returns and claims, this page examines FastGPT context limits. Return-shipment copy must distinguish a proposed return, carrier handover and receipt by the original warehouse. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the context limits step

A proposed return contact note plans a return to the original warehouse. Carrier handover text says the item was passed to the carrier, while the original warehouse’s receipt is not provided. Progress copy can describe that transport handover without stating the warehouse has received this item. Actual input needs destination and the actor for each stage. A phrase such as handed over cannot complete the entire return. Historical warehouse receipt for another item also needs its own identity, rather than filling the present receiving-evidence gap.

## Three common errors

First, proposed return may become warehouse receipt; inspect destination wording and this item’s receipt from that warehouse, leaving completion unasserted without evidence. Second, carrier handover may become warehouse acceptance; compare the actor and original action to locate a changed recipient. Third, a different item’s historical receipt may enter this account; inspect item identity with empty-history comparison. These concern intended destination state, participating actor and item ownership. Larger context or reply allowance cannot make the three records interchangeable or establish that a warehouse performed an action recorded only for a carrier.

## How to verify the setup

Mark who handed what to whom, the supported progress stage and the missing original-warehouse evidence, comparing actual request and draft. Inspect retained references and history separately and distinguish actual model context capacity from reply allowance. This exercise initiates no return or claim and decides no real responsibility, refund or closure. A later receipt must match this item and warehouse before revision. Failure to retrieve a receipt does not establish that the warehouse has not actually received the item; it leaves a receiving-evidence gap in the present supplied material.
