# Trade returns and claims: citation tracing for copy drafting

## What the documents look like

Trade returns and claims work handles reverse logistics and liability determination after goods have been exported. The materials involved do not arrive together and do not share a version. A return agreement, a claim letter, a quality report and a return customs declaration may each carry a different date and a different revision, and each case updates on its own schedule rather than on a fixed cycle.

For copy drafting in this category, the practical consequence is that any statement about quantity, liability or agreement status has to trace back to one specific document version. A draft sentence that describes the reason for a return, the progress of handling or a compensation arrangement is only as reliable as the material behind it. A field the material does not state stays unknown, and the draft cannot fill it in.

The sources are scattered across customer email, quality inspection reports, customs systems and insurance claim systems. A single case may draw a quantity from one source and a liability position from another, and those two sources may have been produced weeks apart. Citation tracing at this step means matching each claim in the draft to the exact sentence and version that supports it, and marking everything else as unsupported or pending verification.

## Constraints at the citation tracing step

The knowledge base search node selects one or more knowledge bases that share the same vector model, and it outputs citations as an array whose length may be zero. A zero-length result still lets the downstream chain proceed; an empty array is not the same as a workflow error or termination. Whether the retrieved citations are then handed to the answering step, and whether each answer sentence is checked against the cited original text, depends on how the downstream chain is built.

Semantic retrieval and the minimum relevance value are similarity filters. They do not isolate records by permission, entity or business category, and a retrieval result does not by itself constitute authorization. Hybrid retrieval merges multiple retrieval paths through RRF, and reranking requires a model and must be enabled in settings. Image retrieval requires image input and a model with the corresponding capability. These optional paths in the overall retrieval flow do not all execute on every request.

Knowledge bases built on different vector models cannot be associated together. The correct handling is to check the vector model identifier of each selected base, exclude the ones that do not match, and record the observation. No conclusion follows about what a cross-model association would return.

Hypothetical paper exercise sample. The following materials are invented for this page and are not real records.

Material one, return agreement, reference HYP-RMA-001, signature page dated 10 March 2025, states: "The buyer and seller have reached a return arrangement for batch HYP-LOT-07, return quantity 120 pieces, and the signature page has been signed by both parties."

Material two, quality report, reference HYP-QC-014, dated 5 March 2025, states: "30 pieces sampled, of which 6 pieces failed appearance inspection."

Material three, claim letter, reference HYP-CLM-003, dated 12 March 2025, states: "A claim is raised for the above batch, and the amount is pending confirmation by both parties."

Draft sentence under review: "All 120 pieces of batch HYP-LOT-07 have completed the return, and the claim amount has been confirmed and compensated."

Tracing result: the return quantity and the signing fact are supported by material one. The phrase "have completed the return" goes beyond the signing status that material one records, so it is unsupported. The phrase "has been confirmed and compensated" conflicts with material three, which records the amount as pending confirmation by both parties, so it is unsupported. A separate handover request, "please arrange the subsequent refund according to the effective return agreement," cannot be traced because no material states an effectiveness clause for the agreement; that item remains pending verification.

## Three common errors

1. Treating the signing fact in material one as an effective agreement or an executed refund. Input: the draft sentence and the original text of material one. Comparison point: whether signing and effectiveness are the same field. Observable result: the draft sentence contains an effectiveness or execution phrase that the material does not state. Check: compare the verb in each draft sentence against the status word in the material, and record any expression that is not supported.

2. Expanding material two's "30 pieces sampled, of which 6 failed" into a claim that all 120 pieces in the batch failed. Input: the draft sentence and the original text of material two. Comparison point: the sampling scope against the batch scope. Observable result: the quantity scope in the draft is wider than the scope the material records. Check: compare the sampled piece count and the batch piece count stated in the material, and mark the scope difference.

3. Writing material three's "amount pending confirmation by both parties" as a confirmed amount. Input: the draft sentence and the original text of material three. Comparison point: the value of the same field. Observable result: mutually exclusive values for one field, "pending confirmation" and "confirmed," appear together. Check: list the material value and the draft value for the same field, and mark the contradiction.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the original text of material one together with the draft sentence "120 pieces have completed the return." Comparison point: the return quantity and status recorded in the material against the status word in the draft. The condition is met when the draft keeps only the quantity and contains no completion-of-return phrase; if a completion phrase appears, the item is judged unsupported.

2. Input the original text of material three together with the draft sentence "the claim amount has been confirmed and compensated." Comparison point: the value of the amount field. The condition is met when the draft is rewritten to state that the amount is pending confirmation; if "confirmed" or "compensated" appears, the item is judged inconsistent with the material.

3. Input the paper retrieval output `[]` together with the downstream answering step design. Comparison point: whether the downstream step can proceed under an empty citation array. The condition is met when the downstream step does not error out and does not produce a conclusion without a source; if the downstream step treats the empty result as an error and stops, the handover design does not meet the condition.

All three items are paper checks. No test, deployment, approval, refund or other business action has been carried out, and no result is claimed.
