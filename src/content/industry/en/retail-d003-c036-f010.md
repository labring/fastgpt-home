# Multi-warehouse inventory: citation tracing for copy drafting

## What the documents look like

Multi-warehouse allocation happens at the order routing stage and decides which warehouse ships an order. The working materials come from WMS, OMS, ERP inventory and platform inventory interfaces. For copy drafting, the relevant daily documents are allocation rules, inventory synchronization tables, transfer orders and stockout warnings.

Citation tracing for this copy-drafting task asks a narrow question: does a proposed sentence about sellable quantity, shipping scope or timing have support in the source text, and which warehouse, version and time window does that source text belong to? A sentence can look precise and still lack a matching field in the material. The drafting step must compare each claim against the source rather than treat fluent phrasing as evidence.

Two category facts shape this task. Allocation sits at order routing, so a statement about one warehouse's sellable stock does not by itself describe another warehouse's ability to ship. The materials also carry explicit status and date fields, so a transfer order marked as created, or a warning with a start date and no release date, remains a partial record rather than a completed business fact.

## Constraints at the citation tracing step

A hypothetical paper exercise is set out below. It is a desk comparison, not an executed search or deployment.

Source text one, inventory synchronization table excerpt: item A at the East China warehouse, recorded at 10:00 on 10 March 2026, has a sellable inventory field value of 120 units. The data source is marked as a WMS interface, and the synchronization frequency is marked as hourly.

Source text two, transfer order excerpt: transfer order number TR-2026-0310-07, source warehouse East China, destination warehouse South China, item A, planned transfer quantity 80 units, status field "created", creation date 10 March 2026.

Source text three, stockout warning excerpt: warning number SK-2026-0310-02, covering item A at the East China warehouse, warning start date 10 March 2026, warning reason field "sellable inventory below safety threshold", with no release date recorded.

The draft sentence under review: "Item A at the East China warehouse currently has 120 sellable units, and the South China warehouse can ship in sync." This sentence contains two assertions. The East China sellable quantity matches source text one in subject, field and time point. The claim that the South China warehouse can ship in sync does not appear in any source text; the transfer order status "created" does not mean the destination warehouse has acquired sellable inventory.

Citation tracing requires a field-by-field comparison of subject, object, field, status and validity range. Content that is not recorded stays recorded as unsupported. Authorization or effective clauses must not be supplied to make an answer look complete.

## Three common errors

1. Reading the transfer order status "created" as the destination warehouse being sellable. Input: source text two. Comparison object: the draft sentence "the South China warehouse can ship in sync". Observable result: the status field in source text two is "created", no sellable inventory field for the destination warehouse is recorded, and the assertion remains unsupported.
2. Treating the single-time-point value in the inventory synchronization table as continuously valid. Input: source text one. Comparison object: "currently has 120 sellable units" in the draft sentence. Observable result: source text one records the field value at 10:00 on 10 March 2026 and an hourly synchronization frequency, but does not record that the value is still valid at the drafting time, so an evidence gap must be recorded.
3. Treating the warning start date as a confirmed stockout period. Input: source text three. Comparison object: any draft sentence claiming item A at the East China warehouse is out of stock after 10 March. Observable result: source text three records no release date and gives the warning reason field "sellable inventory below safety threshold", so the stockout period is not confirmed.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input source text one, source text two, source text three and the draft sentence "Item A at the East China warehouse currently has 120 sellable units, and the South China warehouse can ship in sync." Compare each assertion in the draft sentence against the subject, field, time point and status in the source texts. Observable result: the first assertion finds a matching subject, field and time point in source text one; the second assertion has no corresponding field in any source text and is recorded as unsupported.
2. Input transfer order number TR-2026-0310-07 and the status field "created" from source text two. Compare whether the draft sentence presents "created" as destination-warehouse sellability or a completed transfer. Observable result: source text two records no sellable inventory field for the destination warehouse, so any draft sentence reading "created" as sellable does not satisfy the citation condition.
3. Input warning number SK-2026-0310-02 and the unrecorded release date from source text three. Compare whether the draft sentence claims the stockout has been released or continues to a specific date. Observable result: source text three records no release date, so any definite stockout-period statement does not satisfy the citation condition. These are paper comparisons; no search or deployment has been executed.
