# Peak-sale fulfillment: copy drafting with context limits

## What the documents look like

For copy drafting in Peak-sale fulfillment, this page examines FastGPT context limits. Peak-period copy can describe proposed staffing without asserting attendance or capacity. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the context limits step

A proposed peak-period staffing sheet plans a packing role for the night shift. Its note leaves names for the supervisor’s confirmation, while a historical report records attendance on the previous day shift. Current coordination copy needs proposed roles, pending names and observed attendance as separate evidence. A request containing night-shift arrangement alone may invite already staffed wording; old day-shift experience does not establish current execution. This exercise supplies no headcount or actual efficiency figure and instead tests whether the writing input distinguishes plans from observations for the relevant shift.

## Three common errors

First, planned staffing may become attendance; compare sheet and draft tense and seek a current-shift record before reporting execution. Second, short copy may omit pending names; trace the note through references, input and output, retaining who must confirm. Third, historical day attendance may enter this night shift; inspect shift and date using empty-history comparison. These concern stage, responsible actor and record period. Increasing reply allowance cannot establish actual staff presence, create evidence that confirmation occurred or demonstrate the warehouse’s operational capacity during the proposed peak period.

## How to verify the setup

Require the notice to state the proposed role, name-list status and confirming person, tracing each statement to actual request material. Record references, history and reply length separately and verify actual model context capacity. Restore conditions missing from input first. A current-shift record is needed before a time-matched update; another shift cannot establish this one’s attendance. This exercise schedules no real worker, notifies nobody and tests no practical competence or processing effect. A complete draft proves neither staff readiness nor completion of work at the warehouse.
