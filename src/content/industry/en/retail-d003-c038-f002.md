# Fresh-food concession counters: copy drafting with context limits

## What the documents look like

For copy drafting in Fresh-food concession counters, this page examines FastGPT context limits. Fresh-food counter copy can report packaging observations without deciding food suitability. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the context limits step

A proposed fresh-food counter note observes moisture on an outer bag. A handover card places the item in a pending-check area and says the contents have not been inspected; another page describes counter displays. Current handover copy concerns the bag, leaving contents unobserved. Promotional material cannot establish normal food condition. Actual requests need the pending location and observation scope together. Moisture alone may invite overinterpretation, while pending alone loses the written reason for holding the item. The exercise examines the supplied wording rather than establishing a real product condition.

## Three common errors

First, an outer-bag observation may become a conclusion about contents; compare note subject and draft attribute and remove unsupported food judgments. Second, shortened copy may change pending placement into completed handling; inspect card stage and adopted wording and seek a separate disposition record. Third, counter promotion may enter the item’s handover; inspect object scope using current references and empty history instead of applying a general description to this item. These concern observed subject, handling stage and source type. Extra words supply no physical inspection or suitability evidence for the food.

## How to verify the setup

Require the notice to identify what was observed where, current placement and the unresolved check, comparing actual input and output. References need room for the observation limit; inspect reply length separately from model context capacity. This exercise inspects no food, decides no suitability or regulatory matter and performs no disposition. Actual staff need their own applicable process. A later observation of contents must match the same item before revision. Missing material establishes neither normality nor a definite problem, so the copy must preserve the precise evidence gap.
