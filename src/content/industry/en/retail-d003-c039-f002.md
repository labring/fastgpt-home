# Tmall flagship operations: copy drafting with context limits

## What the documents look like

For copy drafting in Tmall flagship operations, this page examines FastGPT context limits. Flagship brand copy must preserve the object of logo-display permission and the scope of text review. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the context limits step

A proposed brand correspondence permits logo use on this display page, while a note leaves body copy for brand review. No manufacturer-identity description is supplied. Current page copy cannot expand the object of logo permission into approval of all text or infer that the store manufactures products from its branding. Actual context needs the display-page object and pending-body note together. Historical approval concerns another draft; a shared brand does not make that record evidence of review for the current page version being written in this exercise.

## Three common errors

First, display permission may become whole-copy approval; compare the correspondence’s object and draft conclusion, seeking comments on this body version. Second, input trimming may remove pending review; trace original page, references and actual request to locate the missing condition. Third, a logo may become manufacturer identity; inspect entity-description sources and leave the role unspecified without evidence. These concern permission object, input restriction and entity identity. Larger context or a longer reply cannot obtain permission or establish an actual business role absent from the supplied material.

## How to verify the setup

Ask what may be displayed, the current body-review stage and whether manufacturer identity has a source, comparing request and draft. Record reference, history and reply allowances separately and verify actual model context capacity. Later brand comments need page and draft-version checks before revision; old approval cannot carry itself forward. This exercise decides no actual legal validity, publishes no page and contacts no brand. Text generation, publication, approval and entity status require separate evidence. Missing identity material must not be filled from the store name or the appearance of its logo.
