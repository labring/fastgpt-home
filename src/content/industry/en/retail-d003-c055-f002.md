# Franchise filing management: context limits for copy drafting

## What the documents look like

Franchise filing management works around legal disclosure and the commerce department system, and it does not cover the engineering of a specific store location. The materials handled in this category are the franchise contract, the operations manual, the filing materials and the information disclosure document. These materials are revised on an irregular schedule, roughly annual or on demand, so the version state of each source has to be confirmed before any drafting begins.

Copy drafting for goods and marketing in this setting means checking proposed sentences against wording that already exists in those four document types. A promotional sentence that mentions store counts, business resources or the scope of authorization has to have a matching record in the filing materials, the contract or the disclosure document. When no such record exists, the sentence is not supported by the available materials, and the gap is recorded rather than filled by assumption.

This category differs from head-office site selection because the drafting task never touches lease terms, floor plans or construction schedules. The relevant evidence is the recorded authorization boundary: who the franchisor is, what registered mark or business resource is licensed, which territory and which period the contract covers, and which conditions the disclosure document states.

## Constraints at the context limits step

The length of content a model can reference in a single request is determined by the actual model capability. The reply limit controls the length of one answer and cannot be treated as the full context window. The knowledge base citation limit is controlled by tokens, so a long source passage may be truncated, and the returned citation content has to be checked against the question sample to see whether it is complete. Prior conversation input affects retrieval results, so the same question should be observed separately under empty history and under history-bearing conditions. When a fixed reply format is used, field completeness and successful parsing both need verification.

Hypothetical paper exercise sample: the source text reads, "Party A is the franchisor and holds registered trademark No. 1234567, authorizing Party B to open a franchise store in XX Province, XX City; the contract term runs from January 1, 2025 to December 31, 2027." The proposed draft sentence reads, "The franchisee may use the brand to open stores nationwide." The check compares the authorized territory in the proposed sentence against "XX Province, XX City" in the source text, and confirms that the source text does not state a nationwide authorization. This sample is a paper exercise and does not involve a real entity.

Two further constraints follow from the same source. The contract term in the sample is a defined period with a start and an end date, so a draft sentence that describes an open-ended or permanent authorization would not be supported by that passage. The registered mark number is a specific identifier, so a draft sentence that refers to an unnamed brand asset would also lack a matching record. Both comparisons use the same source passage and the same proposed sentence, and both keep the missing evidence missing rather than inferring a broader grant.

## Three common errors

1. Treating the reply limit as the full context window. The input is the reply limit value in the model configuration, the comparison object is the actual maximum context of the model, and the observable result is whether the two values are used interchangeably.
2. A proposed sentence exceeding the authorization scope in the source. The input is the proposed sentence together with the source text, the comparison covers authorized territory and term, and the observable result is whether the proposed sentence introduces a nationwide authorization that the source does not state.
3. A citation limit set too low, cutting the key sentence from the source. The input is the citation limit value and the length of the source passage, the comparison is between the two, and the observable result is whether the returned citation content omits the authorization scope sentence.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the source text and the proposed sentence from the hypothetical sample, compare the authorized territory in the proposed sentence against "XX Province, XX City" in the source, and the condition is met when the proposed sentence is flagged as exceeding the authorized scope. The expected outcome is a flag, and no actual test has been run.
2. Input the same question under empty history and under history-bearing conditions, compare the returned citation content, and the condition is met when the two citation contents match and no history interference appears. If one condition returns nothing, record the gap and do not infer that retrieval rules must return an empty result.
3. Input the citation limit value and the source passage, compare whether the returned content includes the authorization scope sentence, and the condition is met when the key sentence appears complete in the citation. If the return is empty, record the gap rather than asserting a deterministic retrieval rule.
