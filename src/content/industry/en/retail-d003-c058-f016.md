# Fresh-food supplier claims: interactive forms for copy drafting

## What the documents look like

Fresh-food supplier claims pursue responsibility for the quality of goods at arrival. They do not address natural loss inside a store. That distinction shapes the drafting work: the material set is built from quality exception sheets, photo evidence, deduction notices and claim reconciliation statements, and the source systems named in the brief are the quality inspection system, WMS receiving, supplier contracts and financial reconciliation. Two update rhythms coexist. Quality exception sheets arise with each batch, while claim reconciliation statements are assembled by month. The information collected at the interactive forms step therefore falls into two groups: facts tied to a single batch, and the scope of a monthly aggregation.

The main actors are fresh-food purchasing, quality inspection and supplier-facing staff. The consequences of getting the drafting wrong are supplier disputes, bad-goods cost absorbed internally, and interrupted supply. The three most time-consuming steps are fixing the evidence, calculating the deduction, and negotiating replacement goods or compensation. The brief marks its confidence in these general observations as broadly accurate rather than certain.

## Constraints at the interactive forms step

The form input node is a user-interaction node. When it is triggered, the conversation enters an interactive state, the workflow state is recorded, and execution waits until the user completes the interaction. After the user fills in the required information and submits, the node collects the submitted form values and passes them to later nodes. The user selection node is also a user-interaction node with the same interactive-state behaviour, and it evaluates the user's choice to run the corresponding branch. Neither node carries a business database. Field validation, business approval, write-back and batch operations are implemented as needed in external systems or in later nodes. The documents do not describe any control that sets a submission count, performs automatic judgement, or guarantees a snapshot.

Hypothetical paper exercise sample. A supplier contract is said to provide that a deduction follows the exception sheet when a received batch shows a quality exception. The quality inspection system record states that batch A was judged "arrival rot above limit" at receiving on 12 March. The WMS receiving record for the same batch states a quantity of 120 boxes, of which 18 boxes were judged abnormal. The proposed copy sentence is: "The rot rate of this batch at arrival is about 15%, deducted per contract." The percentage in that sentence is derived on paper by dividing 18 by 120. The contract clause text is not provided in the material, so the basis for the deduction remains to be verified. The form is to collect batch number, exception sheet number, abnormal quantity, total received quantity and the proposed copy sentence, and to ask the submitter to choose whether that sentence already has contract clause support.

## Configuration and implementation checks

| Configuration item | Value or check content | Relation to this page's documents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form input node | Enabled; batch number, exception sheet number, abnormal quantity, total received quantity and proposed copy sentence set as required | Collects the fields the sample on this page needs |
| User selection node | Two branches for "does the proposed copy sentence have contract clause support" | Runs the later branch according to the choice |
| Interactive state | Workflow state recorded after the node is triggered, waiting for submission | Execution does not continue before submission |
| Required-field gap check | Test the behaviour when one field is missing, one field at a time | Confirms the submission condition |
| Division of validation work | Percentage calculation and contract clause comparison implemented in later nodes or an external system | The nodes themselves do not carry that function |
| Hypothetical trial starting point | Abnormal quantity 18 and total received quantity 120 as the paper sample input | Used to check how the percentage is written; this is not an official default |

The starting values 18 and 120 are chosen only because they are the two figures stated in the sample material, and the comparison is the paper division of abnormal quantity by total received quantity. No document field is supplied for any other number, model, rule or identifier, so those remain unknown.

## Three common errors

1. Writing the quality inspection judgement "arrival rot above limit" directly as the contract deduction basis. The inspection judgement and the contract clause are two separate materials. The comparison objects differ, and the judgement being present does not mean the deduction clause has been checked.
2. Treating the percentage obtained by dividing 18 by 120 as a confirmed fact in the copy. The percentage comes from a paper calculation, and the material does not state whether the contract uses this basis of calculation. It remains to be verified.
3. Placing a supplier directory or contract list inside the user selection node. That node runs branches according to a choice and does not act as a business data selector. Supplier identity checking is done in an external system.

## How to verify the setup

1. Enter the five required fields from the hypothetical sample and submit. Compare the field values passed to later nodes after submission. The condition is met when all five appear in full. If the exception sheet number is missing, the submission behaviour is recorded as not completed.
2. At the user selection node, choose "no contract clause support" and compare the branch taken afterwards. The condition is met when the branch for matters to be verified is taken. When "has contract clause support" is chosen, the contract clause text must be supplied separately; if it is not supplied, record the evidence gap.
3. Compare the proposed copy sentence word by word against the material text, checking the two expressions "arrival rot above limit" and "rot rate about 15%". When the percentage in the proposed sentence has no corresponding text in the material, record it as unsupported. When the contract clause text is absent, keep it absent and do not infer that the deduction is established.
