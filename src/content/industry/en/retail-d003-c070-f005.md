# Cross-border product labels: multi-turn prompts for copy drafting

## What the documents look like

This page covers copy drafting for product and marketing text in cross-border product compliance and labeling. The materials available for checking are product test reports, English labels, MSDS files, and platform prohibited-item lists. These materials form before listing and import, and their core function is product safety and label evidence. A promotional sentence in drafted copy needs corresponding material support; where evidence is absent, the gap stays open.

Materials update irregularly, and each new product listing triggers a fresh check. In a multi-turn conversation, a user may first supply a report and then ask about a specific sentence. What the history carries into context determines which evidence the model can see.

Compared with cross-border payment risk control, product compliance work happens before listing and import. The typical roles are cross-border sellers, product managers, and compliance specialists. Errors lead to platform delisting, customs seizure, or consumer claims. The three most time-consuming steps are judging destination-country requirements, arranging additional testing, and revising packaging labels.

## Constraints at the multi-turn prompts step

Multi-turn dialogue requires a clear statement of material scope, the answering task, and how to handle missing evidence. History is carried in according to the memory-round setting; a larger value makes earlier content easier to reference and also increases consumption. Question optimization is used for reference resolution in continuous knowledge-base dialogue, and short questions do not necessarily qualify. Model context and reply limits determine whether a long report combined with follow-up questions can be processed in one pass. Reply format is chosen according to downstream handling; selecting json_schema requires filling in the corresponding format requirement.

Hypothetical paper exercise sample: the source text of a product test report reads, "Sample name: portable power bank; test item: battery capacity; test result: conforms to the enterprise standard provided by the commissioning party; report number: HYP-2024-0317; issue date: March 20, 2024; this report is responsible only for the sample submitted." A sentence awaiting judgment reads, "The battery capacity of this product has been tested by an authoritative body, conforms to the mandatory safety requirements of the destination country, and can be listed for sale with confidence." Checkpoints: the report states conformity only with an enterprise standard and does not mention destination-country mandatory requirements; the issue date falls within the sample scope; "authoritative" and "with confidence" have no material source. This sentence is unsupported on destination-country requirements and on authoritativeness.

## Three common errors

1. Writing "conforms to the mandatory requirements of the destination country" when the report says "conforms to the enterprise standard." The input is the sample source text above; the comparison item is the report's test basis against the destination-country requirement claimed in the drafted sentence; the two are not the same, and the claim is unsupported.
2. Treating "authoritative testing" and "listing with confidence" as already stated in the material. The input is the sample source text; the comparison item is the wording in the source against the wording in the drafted sentence; the source has no corresponding expression, so this is an addition without source.
3. Treating a pending review or pending additional testing as already completed. The input is a sentence awaiting confirmation by a compliance specialist; the comparison item is that status against an "already passed" statement; a pending review does not constitute completion.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the sample source text above and the drafted sentence, and compare each claim in the drafted sentence against the corresponding field in the source. The expected observation is that "conforms to the mandatory requirements of the destination country," "authoritative," and "with confidence" are marked as unsupported, and the evidence gap is preserved.
2. Input the report and the follow-up under the same chatId, and compare the actual number of history rounds carried in against the memory-round setting. The expected observation is that it is possible to see which earlier content entered the context; if the earlier report is not carried in, record this as insufficient context scope.
3. Input a follow-up containing an image link, and compare the model's multimodal capability against the image recognition switch. The expected observation is that unsupported types are not sent to the model; if the dialog shows no support, switch to a model supporting the corresponding input, and do not conclude from this that retrieval results are empty.
