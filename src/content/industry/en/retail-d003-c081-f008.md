# Chain-store closure accounts: copy drafting with workflow routing

## What the documents look like

For copy drafting in Chain-store closure accounts, this page examines FastGPT workflow routing. Closure-notice drafting separates approval, inventory counting and lease termination. Closure approval does not establish completed stock or asset handling. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the workflow routing step

Proposed material includes a closure approval, an inventory-clearance sheet awaiting review and a draft lease-termination document. Collect the store and each status. A person may obtain stock evidence or inspect notice wording. Approval establishes a decision, not actual shutdown, lease termination or asset transfer. Another store’s asset count cannot fill this store’s gap. Supply no promised dates or amounts, preserving unverified matters downstream.

## Configuration and implementation checks

| Closure-notice drafting | Proposed state | Corresponding evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Closure approval | Decision made | Not actual shutdown |
| Stock clearance | Review pending | Actual handling record |
| Lease termination | Draft | Not an effective outcome |
| Asset count | Exclude another store | Source subject |
| Draft boundary | Gaps retain their status | No inferred final closure |

## Three common errors

Approval promoted to execution needs actual clearance records. A draft promoted to effective termination needs genuine confirmation. Another store’s asset count applied here requires source-subject and received-text checks. These inspect decision versus execution, file status and subject without merging stores under one brand.

## How to verify the setup

Compare store subjects on stock and notice paths. Added clearance evidence changes that state alone while draft lease handling remains unresolved. A public notice cannot infer ended operations from missing execution material. Shutdown, asset transfer and contract handling need separate authority and receipts. A draft establishes neither an already closed store nor legal conclusions about debt discharge or lease effects.
