# Amazon FBM fulfillment: document comparison with access controls

## What the documents look like

For document comparison in Amazon FBM fulfillment, this page examines FastGPT access controls. Inspect seller-fulfilled sources by service, warehouse and carrier task, preserving boundaries around addresses and tracking information. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the access controls step

A comparison exercise uses a picking note, shipping confirmation and return-address description. Warehouse work needs product and parcel details, service needs the relevant order, and carrier collaboration needs necessary delivery data. Inspect actual account resources and operations. A carrier identity receiving the complete dispute record requires response retention and grant investigation. A tracking number authorizes no universal order access. Carrier classification organizes files without granting external personnel resource rights.

## Configuration and implementation checks

| Permission check | Seller-fulfilled collaboration sources | Acceptance evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Task needs | Warehouse parcel, service order, carrier delivery | Actual accounts, resources and operations |
| Address scope | Separate return recipient and customer address | Full responses with restricted messages or addresses |
| Personnel mapping | Seller and carrier labels create no members | Owner-confirmed accounts |
| External sources | Seller backend, carrier and ERP | Identity and authentication for each |
| Events and execution | Confirmation, collection and delivery differ | Business approval and actual outcomes |

### Documented conditions

| Documented item / implementation check | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| `Team roles` | Inspect roles for the actual member identity. |
| `Resource permissions` | Inspect reading, editing and management by resource. |
| `Document classification` | Classification and tags do not replace authorization. |
| `External-system permissions` | Verify external-interface authentication separately. |
| `Per-account validation` | Test the same source with authorized and unauthorized accounts. |

## Three common errors

- If seller, carrier or return-recipient labels become members, inspect accounts and operations. Business roles configure no resource rights; the owner confirms correspondence.

- If carrier collaboration exposes full disputes or other addresses, retain full response and grant scope. Tracking or carrier classification authorizes no universal reading.

- If carrier or ERP tracking becomes internal authorization or delivery proof, inspect external identity, resource access and event evidence separately. Interface success supplies no missing source or receipt.

## How to verify the setup

Ask warehouse, service, carrier and unrelated-order identities about the same shipment, recording originals, denials and operations. Inspect full answers for paraphrased restricted addresses or messages; an unlisted attachment may still expose text. Correct grants with another order’s tracking need subject and history checks, not permission changes alone. Retain resource gaps separately from unresolved content. Address amendments, shipping confirmations and claim handling still require business approval and actual system outcomes rather than inference from readable records.
