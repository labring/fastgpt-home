# Retail checkout and loyalty: document chunking for document comparison

## What the documents look like

Retail checkout and loyalty work involves comparing contract and transaction materials. The materials include promotional price tags, membership point rules, daily checkout reports and abnormal refund slips. Data arrives from POS systems, membership CRM platforms, payment platforms and price tag systems, and it changes daily or per shift. Checkout promotion work concerns price and member benefits. It does not handle short-shelf-life inventory. Errors in this area lead to price complaints, cash discrepancies and disputes over member benefits.

For document parsing and chunking, each material must be split into passages that can be retrieved and traced back to the source file. A promotional price tag carries a validity period, applicable stores and member tiers. Membership point rules carry effective conditions and exceptions. A daily checkout report carries shift and amount fields. An abnormal refund slip carries an approval status. Within one field, values may be mutually exclusive. After parsing, one group of source data may map to several index entries, and source data must be kept distinct from its indexes.

## Constraints at the document chunking step

Chunking must preserve the original structure and version identifier so each passage can point back to its source file. Conditions, explanations and the content they govern cannot be separated. If they are split apart, the validity scope of a promotional price tag, the exception clauses of point rules and the approval status of a refund slip lose their context. Source data and retrieval indexes are managed separately, and one group of source data may correspond to multiple indexes. Retrieval checks should use terminology, identifiers and natural-language queries separately, then observe how the segmentation affects recall for actual questions. After a material changes, a successful upload does not mean retrieval has updated. The actual training and index state must be checked. Hybrid retrieval merges multiple result paths through RRF. ReRank performs a second ordering of candidate text. The minimum relevance score takes effect only during semantic retrieval or result reranking. These mechanisms do not constitute an authorization decision, and retrieval results cannot replace business approval.

Hypothetical paper exercise sample: the source text of a promotional price tag reads, "Store A applies a 10% discount to member-priced product X from 1 March 2025 to 7 March 2025. Non-members are not eligible. This price tag was signed and confirmed by the checkout supervisor." A proposed sentence reads, "Store A applies a 10% discount to product X for all customers from 1 March 2025 to 7 March 2025." The question is whether the proposed sentence matches the source text. The source text contains the subject, the object, the status and the validity scope. The proposed sentence omits the member restriction.

## Three common errors

1. Treating a successful upload as an updated retrieval state. Input a revised membership point rule. Compare the upload time with the training and index state, and observe whether retrieval still returns the old clause. If the old clause is returned, the update has not taken effect.

2. Treating the minimum relevance score as a permission or entity filter. Input a query for Store A's promotional price tag. Compare whether returned content includes Store B records, and observe whether relevance filtering excludes unrelated stores. If it does not, relevance does not perform entity isolation.

3. Treating "all customers" in the proposed sentence as equivalent to "member-priced product" in the source text. Input the proposed sentence and the source text. Compare the subject and applicable-object fields, and observe whether "non-members are not eligible" is omitted. If it is omitted, chunking or comparison has not preserved the restriction.

## How to verify the setup

- Verification one: input the promotional price tag source text and the proposed sentence. Compare the two fields "member-priced product" and "all customers". Observe whether the proposed sentence is marked as inconsistent with the source text. The condition is met when the subject scope of the proposed sentence does not match the source restriction.

- Verification two: input the natural-language question "Who is eligible for the discount on product X at Store A during the first week of March". Compare whether the returned data chunk contains "member-priced product" and "non-members are not eligible". Observe whether chunking preserves the complete condition. The condition is met when the returned content contains the validity period, the member restriction and the signed confirmation together.

- Verification three: input a revised membership point rule. Compare retrieval results before and after the revision. Observe whether the old clause is still recalled. The condition is met when the old clause no longer appears in the returned content and the training and index state has updated. If the old clause is still returned, record it as an update that has not taken effect, without inferring automatic synchronization.

Empty-result handling: use the paper input `[]` to check the downstream design. Observe whether the downstream handles it as an empty reference. Do not claim that a fabricated rule retrieval necessarily returns an empty array. Cross-model association is not possible. First check the vector model identifier and exclude those that do not match. Record the actual observation. Do not run cross-model retrieval to compare missed recall.
