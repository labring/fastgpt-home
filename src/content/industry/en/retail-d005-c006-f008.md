# General export declarations: procurement with workflow routing

## What the documents look like

For procurement in General export declarations, this page examines FastGPT workflow routing. Compare the material received with the sources used in the answer. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the workflow routing step

An export procurement workflow can collect the contract’s supply wording, invoice item passage and matters awaiting supplier confirmation. Proposed inputs lack a response for this procurement instance, so contractual terms do not become current preparation results. Each passage needs its identity and scope downstream. Packing material, even when supplied, requires correspondence to the present subject; complete document titles do not establish transaction relationships or actual supply confirmation.

## Three common errors

Test operator choices to obtain the current supply response or organize the contract, inspecting each connected yes branch. If requesting a response still produces supplier-confirmed wording, trace received text and the judgment’s origin, preserving the unanswered state. Another failure passes packing counts as contractual demand. Inspect units and objects before retesting; apparently different figures alone establish neither under-shipment nor breach. If a party named in the invoice is automatically treated as the contract supplier, inspect the roles and transaction correspondence in both sources. Appearing in one document set does not establish the same role; retain unresolved identity without that relationship.

## How to verify the setup

Trace the procurement question, submitted passages, recipient and final explanation. After adding the supplier response, repeat the topic and inspect its actual use. Waiting and continuing arrange source handling without creating procurement records. External inquiries, amendments or ordering require separate interfaces and authorization, with real returns and operation states. Workflow completion establishes neither an amended contract, prepared goods nor accepted declaration.
