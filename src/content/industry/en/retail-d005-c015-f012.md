# Letter-of-credit documents: document chunking for procurement

## What the documents look like

Letter-of-credit examination and presentation serves bank payment conditions. The working focus is document consistency rather than tax matching. The documents handled in this category are the letter of credit, commercial invoice, bill of lading, insurance policy, and draft. Data sources include issuing-bank notices, UCP600 clauses, freight-forwarder bills of lading, and bank presentation records. Typical roles are trade documentation staff, bank international settlement teams, and exporters. Errors lead to refusal of payment, deductions, or delayed collection of foreign exchange. Material updates at a per-transaction frequency.

The procurement and supplier task sits inside this category. Purchase contracts, supplier lists, goods-receipt notes, and reconciliation statements are commonly cross-checked against the documents above. Parsing and chunking must preserve document numbers, dates, amounts, and party names so that later retrieval can point back to a specific document. When a condition, an explanation, and the content it constrains are split apart, the retrieved fragment no longer supports a reliable comparison.

## Constraints at the document chunking step

Each transaction has a different document structure, so segmentation is checked against the actual structure of the material at hand. One set of source data can map to multiple indexes, and the source data is distinguished from the retrieval index. Retrieval precision is affected by vector model quality, data completeness, and the quality of the search terms. Semantic retrieval depends on vector similarity, full-text retrieval depends on keyword hits, and hybrid retrieval merges the two through RRF. Retrieval does not carry authorization duties, and a similarity score does not constitute a permission or entity filter.

Hypothetical drill sample: on 10 March 2025, a trade documentation staff member at a foreign-trade company receives an issuing-bank notice for letter of credit LC-HYP-0310, requiring presentation of a commercial invoice, a bill of lading, and an insurance policy. The supplier materials include a purchase contract numbered PO-HYP-0301, which provides for delivery in two batches. Draft sentence under review: "This purchase contract already covers all documents required by the letter of credit and can be presented directly." The sentence does not state whether the insurance policy has been obtained, and it does not state the batch scope of the bill of lading. This sentence is unsupported.

## Three common errors

1. Treating a successful upload as an updated index. Input: in the drill sample, purchase contract PO-HYP-0301 is uploaded without confirming training status. Comparison: the upload record against the actual index state. Observable result: if training is incomplete, a search for that contract number may return nothing, and the gap must be recorded.
2. Treating a similarity score as a permission filter. Input: a goods-receipt note from a different supplier in the drill sample. Comparison: the returned content against the authorized scope of the current presentation task. Observable result: a high or low similarity score does not exclude records from unrelated suppliers, so the party must be checked manually.
3. Treating a draft sentence under review as a confirmed conclusion. Input: the draft sentence above. Comparison: the coverage claimed by the draft against the document list stated in the source text. Observable result: the insurance policy and the bill-of-lading batch are not stated, so the sentence is judged unsupported.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input letter-of-credit number LC-HYP-0310 from the drill sample, using hybrid retrieval. Compare whether the returned content includes the clause corresponding to that number. Observable result: if the first item does not match, record the actual return and check whether segmentation split the number from the clause.
2. Input the draft sentence "This purchase contract already covers all documents required by the letter of credit," with rerank enabled. Compare the rerank score against the minimum relevance of 0.4. Observable result: if the score falls below the threshold, the sentence is not referenced; if it rises above the threshold, the insurance-policy evidence still requires manual checking.
3. Input an empty-result scenario: use the paper input [] to check the downstream design. Compare the system's output behavior when nothing is recalled. Observable result: no claim can be made that a fictional rule necessarily returns an empty array; the actually observed state must be recorded.

All three checks address document parsing and chunking and draft-sentence review. They do not involve authorization execution or business actions. A retrieval result does not constitute presentation authorization; presentation requires bank examination.
