# Brand concession operations: scheduled workflows for procurement

## What the documents look like

Brand concession operations in shopping malls accept unified mall cashiering and deduction-based settlement. That single condition shapes how procurement and supplier records appear in documents. Sales receipts and monthly reconciliation statements come from the mall side; brand ERP and finance systems sit on the brand side; the joint-operation deduction contract and mall activity letters form the written basis between the two sides. The purchased quantity of a given batch of goods, supplier details and deduction rate are often spread across the contract, the activity letter and the reconciliation statement, with daily and monthly update rhythms existing side by side.

In this task, scheduled workflows and automation connect external interface reads, request sending and result checking into reviewable steps. They do not replace contract judgment or authorization confirmation. Whether mall POS data and brand ERP data are connected depends on whether an interface has been configured; a scheduled trigger by itself does not establish a business system connection.

## Constraints at the scheduled workflows step

Trigger responsibility needs to be settled first: whether execution is triggered by the application's scheduling capability or by an external scheduler, with the available entry point checked against the actual version. External data is read through a configured interface; a scheduled trigger cannot be assumed to establish a business system connection on its own. The HTTP request method, headers, body and variable inputs are checked item by item against the external interface documentation. Deduplication, idempotency and business writes need explicit design in the interface or a downstream flow. Execution records should be checked against trigger time, real input, returned result and business status; the existence of a task does not equal successful execution.

Hypothetical drill sample. The material text supplied for checking reads: "Party A, the mall, and Party B, the brand, signed a joint-operation deduction contract, with the contract number assumed to be HYP-2024-031 and a validity period ending on the assumed date of 31 December 2024. The attached mall activity letter states that during the assumed period from 1 June 2024 to 30 June 2024, the concession participates in the mall's unified promotion, with the deduction applied at an assumed 18%. The monthly reconciliation statement shows assumed June 2024 sales receipts totalling an assumed 120,000 yuan." One draft sentence awaiting determination reads: "The concession's June 2024 deduction fee has been settled at 18%, and inventory procurement may be booked on that basis." This sentence touches three distinct facts: signing, settlement and booking. The material records only the period and rate in the contract and activity letter; it does not record completed settlement or booking authorization.

The starting points above are chosen for this hypothetical trial because the sample contains three material types—contract, activity letter and reconciliation statement—which makes item-by-item comparison practical. They are compared against the field names given in the interface documentation and the sample material text, and are not claimed as any official default.

## Three common errors

1. Treating a scheduled trigger as an established business system connection. The input is the fact that a scheduled trigger has been configured; the comparison object is whether an external interface has been configured; the observable result is that no response returns when the interface is not configured. Missing interface configuration remains missing, and the existence of a trigger cannot be used to infer the existence of a connection.
2. Treating the draft sentence's "has been settled at 18%" as a verified fact. The input is the period and rate recorded in the activity letter; the comparison object is whether the monthly reconciliation statement records completed settlement; the observable result is that the material does not record completed settlement or booking authorization. Not recorded means pending verification, not negation or conflict.
3. Treating return value extraction configuration as permission filtering. The input is the key extracted by JSON path; the comparison object is the actual field returned by the interface; the observable result falls into two cases: extracted and not extracted. Relevance does not handle permission or entity isolation, and retrieval does not constitute authorization.

## How to verify the setup

1. For the hypothetical sample, the input is contract number HYP-2024-031 with a validity period ending on the assumed date of 31 December 2024; the comparison object is the period from 1 June 2024 to 30 June 2024 recorded in the activity letter; the observable result is whether the activity period falls within the contract validity period. The condition is met when the two periods are consistent; if the activity letter period exceeds the contract validity period, record it as pending verification.
2. For the draft sentence, the input is "has been settled at 18%"; the comparison object is whether the monthly reconciliation statement records completed settlement; the observable result is that the material does not record that status. The condition is met when the reconciliation statement explicitly records completed settlement; under the current material, that sentence is unsupported.
3. For the execution record, the input is one hypothetical interface request and its returned result; the comparison object is whether trigger time, real input and returned result correspond; the observable result is whether the three can be matched. The condition is met when the three are consistent; if the return is empty, carry the empty result forward to check the downstream design, without claiming that a fabricated rule retrieval must return empty.
