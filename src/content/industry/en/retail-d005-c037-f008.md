# Peak-sale fulfillment: workflow routing for procurement

## What the documents look like

Peak-sale warehouse and delivery scheduling centers on short-term peak capacity and the arrangement of people, vehicles, and dock space. This differs from routine multi-warehouse inventory synchronization, which deals with steady-state scheduling. The main data sources for this category are historical peak-sale orders, platform campaign registrations, WMS capacity, and courier pickup capability. The typical documents are peak-sale forecast tables, staff rosters, courier support plans, and exception daily reports. These materials update daily, with some updating per campaign.

In workflow routing for procurement and supplier records, these documents act as inputs. The forecast table supplies stock-up quantities and arrival times. The roster supplies the presence status of temporary staff. The courier support plan supplies vehicle counts and pickup windows. The exception daily report supplies records of the day's deviations. A form input node collects required information and passes it to later nodes after submission. A user selection node executes the corresponding "yes" branch after the user makes a choice, and each branch connection needs item-by-item verification. Interaction state recording is behavior of the interaction nodes; no separate global state-recording switch is documented. Output handoff must pass collected information explicitly to later retrieval, judgment, or interface nodes, comparing output against input item by item. Order writes, document backfill, and external status synchronization require a separate business interface or a later process. Retrieval or form collection does not constitute authorized execution.

## Constraints at the workflow routing step

The form input node triggers an interaction state, collects required fields, and passes the information to later nodes after submission. Order writes, document backfill, and external status synchronization require a separate business interface or later process. Retrieval or form collection does not constitute authorized execution.

Hypothetical drill sample: the source material reads, "Peak-sale forecast table (November 1 to November 11, 2025) states: East China warehouse Category A goods forecast outbound volume 12,000 units; Supplier A commits to arrive at warehouse 8,000 units before November 5. Staff roster states: November 1 to November 3, 40 temporary sorters on duty. Courier support plan states: November 1 to November 11, 6 pickup vehicles per day." The draft sentence to be judged is: "Supplier A has confirmed all 12,000 units will arrive at the warehouse before November 5, and the procurement process can be closed." This draft sentence equates forecast outbound volume with the supplier's arrival commitment quantity, and equates an arrival commitment with completed arrival.

## Three common errors

1. Treating forecast outbound volume directly as the supplier's arrival commitment quantity. In the hypothetical sample, the forecast table states 12,000 units, while the supplier commits 8,000 units. These are different fields with different values and cannot be merged into one fact.
2. Treating the supplier's arrival commitment as completed arrival. In the sample, the supplier commits to arrive at the warehouse 8,000 units before November 5. The material does not state an actual arrival record, so this status is unsupported.
3. Treating form submission or a retrieval hit as the authorization basis for closing the procurement process. Form-collected values do not constitute approval, and retrieval does not constitute authorization. Closing procurement requires a separate business interface.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the hypothetical sample source material and the draft sentence. Compare the "12,000 units" in the draft sentence against the 12,000 units stated in the forecast table and the supplier's committed 8,000 units. If the draft sentence equates the two, judge it as inconsistent with the source material.
2. Input the hypothetical sample source material and the draft sentence. Compare "has confirmed all units arrived at warehouse" in the draft sentence against the material's only statement, "commits to arrive at warehouse 8,000 units before November 5." If the draft sentence claims completed arrival while the material has no arrival record, judge it as unsupported.
3. Input the hypothetical sample source material and the draft sentence. Compare "the procurement process can be closed" in the draft sentence against whether the material contains any approval or authorization record. If the material has no approval record, judge it as an authorization gap and record it as pending verification.
