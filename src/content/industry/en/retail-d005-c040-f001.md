# Customer-service knowledge: procurement with HTTP integrations

## What the documents look like

For procurement in Customer-service knowledge, this page examines FastGPT HTTP integrations. FAQs, product guidance and escalation records provide different procurement leads. Reading them does not establish complete manufacturer evidence or resolution of a supply question. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the HTTP integrations step

When service material supports procurement, separate FAQ advice, product guidance and actual escalation outcomes. Proposed input contains scripts and parameter-source wording without current manufacturer supply confirmation. Inspect the real contract’s item, version and relationships without assuming connected service and procurement systems. An entry supports its covered question, not complete performance, supplier identity or processing evidence from its title.

## Configuration and implementation checks

| Procurement-reading check | Proposed material | Actual supporting evidence |

| Script status | Advice separate from manufacturer commitment | Inspect actual product passage |

| Handling stage | Escalation without closure result | No resolved-problem conclusion |

## Three common errors

FAQ advice described as a definite manufacturer commitment needs product wording and scope. Another model’s parameters require model and request-origin checks. Escalation described as resolved needs this handling outcome. These concern statement status, item identity and business stage rather than an entry’s existence.

## How to verify the setup

Retain question, source version, actual request and return coverage. Repeat after manufacturer or handling evidence arrives and inspect actual use. Check parameters, headers and any appropriate body under the external contract with protected credentials. Procurement creation, FAQ changes, work-order actions and writes require permission and real receipts. Reading establishes neither confirmed supply, published content nor supplier performance.
