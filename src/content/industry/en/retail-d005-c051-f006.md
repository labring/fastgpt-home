# Douyin storefront operations: scheduled workflows for procurement

## What the documents look like

Douyin storefront operations rely on product cards and the store experience score rather than the rhythm of a single livestream. The daily documents tied to inventory procurement and supplier records are product qualification pages, experience score details, shipping violation appeals, and store announcements. The public brief gives two update frequencies, daily and per order; this page does not derive batch synchronization or real-time triggering from them. Product qualification pages determine whether a product card can keep displaying, experience score details record fulfillment performance, and shipping violation appeals reflect how out-of-stock and no-stock cases were handled. In a scheduled workflow these materials are inputs. The workflow itself does not create a connection to a business system.

A supplier record is a document that states a supplier name, a legal entity, a supply scope, a submission date, and an authorization status. A handover request is a separate document that asks for an action. The two are not the same kind of evidence, and the status written in the supplier record governs what the handover request may do.

## Constraints at the scheduled workflows step

The trigger responsibility has to be stated: whether the application's scheduled capability or an external scheduler starts the run, checked against the actual version's available entry points. External data is read through an interface that has already been configured; a scheduled trigger does not by itself establish a connection to a business system. An HTTP request is checked against the external interface document for method, headers, body, and variable inputs. De-duplication of repeated triggers, idempotency, and business writes need explicit design in the interface or in a later step. Execution records are checked for trigger time, actual input, returned result, and business status; the existence of a task does not stand in for a successful run. Values collected by retrieval or by a form still need their own downstream implementation for business verification, approval, database writes, and external synchronization.

Hypothetical paper exercise sample. A Douyin merchant holds a supplier record whose text reads: "Supplier A, entity is XX Trading Co., Ltd., supply scope is summer household goods, submission date is 10 May 2024, authorization status is pending review." A separate proposed handover request reads: "Please add Supplier A to the purchasable list and arrange daily automatic replenishment." This request touches the authorization boundary: a pending-review status does not constitute approval, and both "add to the purchasable list" and "automatic replenishment" in the draft sentence exceed the current authorization scope. The sample is a paper construction for checking wording and status; it is not a real customer, real company, or real identity number.

## Three common errors

1. Treating a pending-review status as approved. The sample record states "authorization status is pending review," while the draft sentence asks to "add to the purchasable list." The input is the record text and the draft sentence; the comparison is how each describes the authorization status; the observable result is that the draft sentence exceeds the record's authorization scope.

2. Treating a scheduled trigger as an established business system connection. The sample does not state that an interface has been configured. If automatic replenishment is assumed executable, the input is the sample and the trigger responsibility setting; the comparison is whether a configured interface record exists; the observable result is that interface configuration evidence is missing.

3. Treating a retrieval or form-collected value as authorized execution. The proposed handover request in the sample is a form-style request. The input is the request text and the authorization status; the comparison is the requested action against the pending-review status; the observable result is that the requested action is not authorized.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the sample record text and the draft sentence "Please add Supplier A to the purchasable list and arrange daily automatic replenishment." Compare the record's authorization status "pending review" with the actions in the draft sentence. The condition is met when the draft sentence is identified as exceeding the authorization scope and the pending-review status is recorded as not constituting approval.

2. Input the sample record text and the trigger responsibility setting. Compare whether a configured interface record exists. The condition is met when a missing interface configuration is recorded as a gap and automatic replenishment is not judged executable.

3. Input the sample record text and the execution record design. Compare whether the record includes trigger time, actual input, returned result, and business status. The condition is met when the record fields are complete and the existence of a task is not used in place of a successful run.

If the paper input is an empty array, check whether the downstream design handles an empty result; do not claim that a fictional rule retrieval necessarily returns empty. Libraries using different vector models cannot be associated together; check identifiers first, exclude those that do not match, and record the actual observation. A relevance score is not a category, entity, or permission filter, and retrieval is not authorization.
