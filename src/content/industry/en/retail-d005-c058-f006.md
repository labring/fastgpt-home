# Fresh-food supplier claims: scheduled workflows for procurement

## What the documents look like

Fresh-food supplier claims pursue quality responsibility for goods as delivered. They do not handle ordinary in-store shrinkage, and that distinction shapes the records. The working material is organised around batch-level quality exceptions rather than around shelf-life markdowns.

The main sources are the quality inspection system, WMS receiving records, supplier contracts, and finance reconciliation. The daily documents are quality exception forms, photo evidence, deduction notices, and claim reconciliation statements. The three most time-consuming steps are fixing the evidence, calculating the deduction, and negotiating replacement or compensation. These steps normally sit between the arrival of a batch and the monthly reconciliation.

For handover, whether these materials carry a checkable authorization state matters more than how many documents exist. A quality exception form, a receiving record, and a contract clause may all be present while the authority to raise a deduction remains unstated.

## Constraints at the scheduled workflows step

A scheduled trigger can only send a request to an externally configured address. External data is read through an interface that has been configured; a scheduled trigger does not by itself establish a connection to a business system. The HTTP method, headers, body, and variable inputs must be checked against the external interface documentation. De-duplication, idempotency, and business writes require explicit design in the interface or in a later flow. Execution records must be checked against the trigger time, the actual input, the returned result, and the business state. A task existing does not mean it executed successfully.

Hypothetical drill sample: a supplier contract states that an unqualified sampling inspection of a delivered batch may give rise to a claim. The quality inspection record states that batch A failed sampling inspection on 10 March 2025. The WMS receiving record states that batch A was put into storage on 10 March 2025. The draft sentence under review is: "This batch already meets the claim conditions and a deduction notice can be generated automatically." The sentence involves a subject, an object, a date, and a state, but the sample does not state whether the contract provides for automatic deduction or how the deduction amount is calculated. Those points remain to be verified.

## Three common errors

1. Treating a scheduled trigger as an automatic database write. Check whether the input comes from a configured interface, compare whether a business write appears, and observe that the result is only a request return.
2. Treating a pending review as a missing operating permission. Check whether the sample states an authorization state, compare the same subject, the same period, and the same field, and observe whether only evidence from a related stage exists.
3. Treating a similarity score as a permission or entity filter. Check the retrieval scope identifier, compare the vector model identifier, and observe whether non-matching records are excluded.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the sample text and the draft sentence, compare whether the contract states automatic deduction, and observe whether the only available conclusion is that the point remains to be verified. The sample does not contain the deduction calculation rule, so that gap stays open.
2. Input an empty result `[]`, compare whether the downstream design accepts an empty value, and observe whether an assertion appears that a fictional rule retrieval must return `[]`. The empty input is a paper input for checking the downstream design, not a claim about what retrieval returns.
3. Input two library identifiers that use different vector models, compare whether the identifiers match, and observe whether only the exclusion result is recorded. Do not run a cross-model retrieval to compare missed recalls; check the identifiers first and exclude non-matching libraries.
