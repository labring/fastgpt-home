# Cross-border product labels: document chunking for procurement

## What the documents look like

Cross-border product compliance work happens before listing and before import. The central evidence is product safety and label material. In inventory procurement and supplier material handling, the documents that pass through parsing and chunking include product test reports, English labels, MSDS files and platform prohibited-item lists. Sources include platform policy centers, testing laboratory reports, destination-country regulation libraries and supplier submissions. Updates arrive irregularly and each new product launch can trigger a new round.

These materials differ in structure. A test report carries a sample name, test items, a conclusion and a validity period. An English label carries ingredients, warnings and destination-country marks. An MSDS carries chemical identity and transport classification. A prohibited-item list carries categories and restriction conditions. Parsing and chunking decide whether later retrieval can point back to the original text. Two layers exist here: original data and index. One group of original data can map to several indexes, and after retrieval the vector ID leads back to MongoDB for merging. Semantic recall and relevance affect content ordering; they do not carry authorization or entity isolation.

A hypothetical paper sample for this page: Supplier A submits an English label draft. The source text reads: "Product: Children's Raincoat, Model RC-2024. Warning: Keep away from open flame. Not suitable for children under 3 years." A handover request is drafted as: "This label already meets the destination-country listing requirements, so procurement and warehouse entry can be arranged." That request exceeds what the material states. The source text contains product identity and warnings only. It does not state the destination country, a test conclusion or an authorization status.

## Constraints at the document chunking step

The first constraint is structural difference across material types. A chunking rule that suits a test report paragraph will not suit a prohibited-item list row. The second constraint is that conditions and warnings must stay attached to what they govern. A warning separated from its product identity loses meaning during retrieval.

The third constraint is the separation of original data from index. A single original data group can hold several vectors through the indexes field. Retrieval recalls vectors, then uses the vector ID to find the original content in MongoDB, merging entries that map to the same original data and taking the highest vector score. Chunking choices therefore shape both what is recalled and what is merged back.

The fourth constraint is that search mode selection interacts with material type. Semantic retrieval depends on vector distance and handles natural language and cross-language matching, but its precision is unstable and affected by keyword and sentence completeness. Full-text retrieval depends on keyword hits and suits numbers, models and proper nouns. Hybrid retrieval uses both and merges results through RRF. Rerank performs a second ordering and produces a 0-1 score.

The fifth constraint is that relevance filtering operates only in semantic retrieval or when result reranking is used. It filters low-relevance results. It does not exclude unauthorized records or unrelated businesses. Retrieval is not authorization.

## Three common errors

1. Treating a successful upload as an updated index. After material changes, the actual training and index state must be checked. Upload completion and retrieval availability are separate facts.
2. Treating minimum relevance as a permission filter. This value filters low-relevance results only. It does not exclude unauthorized records, and it does not separate business entities.
3. Treating a pending review request as approved. In the hypothetical sample, the drafted sentence claims the label meets destination-country listing requirements. The source text does not state a destination country, a test conclusion or an authorization status. The claim remains unsupported.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the hypothetical sample source text and the drafted sentence. Compare whether "already meets the destination-country listing requirements" has support in the source text. The source text does not state a destination country, so record an evidence gap. The comparison target is the source wording and its stated scope, not the drafted conclusion.
2. Run a full-text search for "RC-2024" and observe whether the label paragraph is returned. If it is not returned, inspect the chunking and index state. The observable result is a hit or a miss on that specific model number. A miss does not by itself establish why the index is incomplete.
3. Use an empty reference input `[]` to check the downstream design and observe what the system returns. Do not assume a fabricated rule must return empty. The observable result is the actual returned content under that input, and any behavior beyond the documented conditions remains unknown.

These checks describe what to compare and what to observe. They do not claim that any test, deployment, approval or procurement action has taken place. Where the source material does not state a field, that field remains unknown.
