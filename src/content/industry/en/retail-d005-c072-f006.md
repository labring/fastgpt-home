# Overseas stock replenishment: procurement with scheduled workflows

## What the documents look like

For procurement in Overseas stock replenishment, this page examines FastGPT scheduled workflows. Overseas replenishment review separates plans, inbound appointments and available stock coverage. An appointment does not establish received inventory. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the scheduled workflows step

A proposed replenishment plan lists cartons, but an inbound appointment covers only part of them and an age report concerns earlier stock. Periodic review can expose these differences without expanding appointment scope or inferring arrival from old inventory. Establish the deployment’s available application call or external trigger, then inspect each input’s warehouse and item. Last-mile material that does not cover this inbound event cannot supply overseas receiving evidence.

## Configuration and implementation checks

## Three common errors

Appointment promoted to receipt needs this warehouse’s receiving record. Old stock promoted to new arrival requires source-batch checks. Another warehouse’s material applied here needs input-subject and actual-return checks. These inspect logistics stage, stock origin and warehouse identity rather than match item names alone.

## How to verify the setup

Retain the plan, appointment scope and read result. Repeat after adding this receiving record and inspect changes limited to the corresponding warehouse and cartons. Keep unappointed quantities and earlier stock distinct. Purchasing, cross-border transport and inventory writes need separate authority and receipts. Deduplication follows actual system design. Scheduled output establishes neither executed replenishment nor inventory available for sale.
