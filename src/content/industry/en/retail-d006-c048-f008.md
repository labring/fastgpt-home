# Parcel labels and handoffs: workflow routing for staff training

## What the documents look like

Parcel handoff happens at the point where a package leaves the warehouse. The responsibility core at this step is carrier scanning, which differs from in-warehouse packing. For store service and internal training, the relevant materials are electronic waybills, handoff lists, pickup scan records, and exception item registers. Data sources include the courier system, WMS shipment records, GB/T courier service standards, and carrier bills. Updates occur per batch and daily.

Training and store service staff use these documents to connect filling, submission, and checking into a traceable flow. Workflow routing is responsible for collecting information at interaction nodes and deciding subsequent branches. The Form Input node and the User Selection node are both user-interaction nodes. Both can be added repeatedly and trigger execution. When either node is triggered, the conversation enters an interaction state, the workflow state is recorded, and the flow continues after the user completes the interaction. Form Input collects required information and passes it to later nodes. User Selection judges the user's choice and executes the "yes" branch. Collected information must be explicitly passed to later retrieval, judgment, or interface nodes, and output must be compared item by item against input. Order writing, document backfill, and external status synchronization require a separate business interface or later process. Form submission alone does not constitute authorization. Retrieval and similarity scores do not carry permission or entity isolation duties.

## Constraints at the workflow routing step

The Form Input node enters an interaction state when triggered, records the workflow state, and passes required user information to subsequent nodes after submission. The User Selection node executes the corresponding "yes" branch after the user makes a choice. Both nodes are user-interaction, repeatably addable, and trigger execution. The constraints at this step come from the need to keep collected fields consistent with what downstream nodes receive, and from the boundary between collecting values and performing business actions.

A hypothetical paper exercise sample is provided here for training and checking purposes. The source material reads: "On 10 March 2025, Store A submitted handoff list J-0310 to Carrier B, covering 40 items, with status pending pickup; the pickup scan record shows 32 of these items scanned and 8 not scanned." A draft sentence to be judged reads: "All 40 items in this batch have completed pickup handoff." The judgment condition is same subject, same period, same field. The handoff list records 40 items pending pickup. The scan record records 32 items scanned. These give mutually exclusive values for the field "number of scanned items," so the draft sentence is not supported. The subsequent status of the 8 unscanned items is not stated in the material and remains an evidence gap.

This sample does not establish that any test, deployment, approval, refund, or other business action occurred. It is a paper exercise for training and checking.

## Three common errors

1. Treating form submission as completed authorization. In the sample, submitting the handoff list means information has been collected. The pickup scan record reflects scan progress. These two documents cannot substitute for each other. The workflow routing step collects values; it does not grant permission or execute business actions.

2. Treating a retrieval result as a permission filter. A relevance score is not a category, entity, or permission filter. A retrieval hit does not mean the record belongs to this store or this carrier. Similarity settings do not exclude unrelated businesses or unauthorized records. Retrieval is not authorization.

3. Writing a pending-pickup status as completed handoff. In the sample, 8 items are not scanned. The draft sentence states that all items are complete. Under the same field with mutually exclusive values, this sentence is not supported. The status of the 8 items remains an evidence gap, not a confirmed contradiction or a confirmed completion.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the sample source text and the draft sentence. Compare the field "number of scanned items." The handoff list records 40 items pending pickup. The scan record records 32 items scanned. The draft sentence states that all 40 items are complete. The expected result is that the draft sentence is not supported, and the status of the 8 items is recorded as an evidence gap. This is a paper comparison, not an actual test.

2. Input the fields collected by the Form Input node and the fields received by the downstream judgment node. Compare field names and values item by item. The expected result is that the two sets match item by item. Any missing field is recorded as not passed. If a field is absent from the downstream input, record it as an unknown rather than assuming a default.

3. Input an explicit empty result [] and check the downstream design. The expected result is that the downstream node has a defined receiving path for empty input. Do not claim that a fictional rule retrieval will return empty. Do not claim that the first hit is guaranteed, that all relevant records are recalled, or that an unrelated query must return empty. Observe the actual output and record the failure branch when the downstream node has no defined path for empty input.

These checks use the hypothetical sample given above. They describe what to compare and what remains unknown. They do not claim that any test, deployment, approval, or business action has occurred.
