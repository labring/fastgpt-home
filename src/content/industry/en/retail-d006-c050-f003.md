# Complaints and official replies: shared access for staff training

## What the documents look like

Complaints and official replies are directed at platforms or regulators, so the material needs formal evidence and rectification wording. Sources include the 12315 platform, platform complaint centres, customer service chats and the order system. Updates are irregular and handled case by case. The everyday documents are complaint tickets, regulatory inquiry letters, order evidence and rectification reports. The three most time-consuming steps are reconstructing the transaction, preparing the evidence pack and submitting the rectification reply.

In a store service and internal training setting, the shared access question is narrower than the full complaint workflow. It concerns what authorisation state a store colleague or training participant holds when a material or a dialogue entry point is handed to them, which actions are checking steps, and which actions require a separate system to complete. A share link identifier locates an entry point. It does not carry a business authorisation. The no-login window generates a localId per user to identify that user and pull conversation records from the cloud, but this only holds on the same device and the same browser; switching device or clearing browser cache loses those records, and only the most recent 30 days and 20 records can be pulled. Identity verification is limited to the commercial edition scope. After a root address is configured, initialisation, conversation start and conversation end each send a request to a specific interface at that address, and the server returns success and uid, where uid is a string that excludes certain characters and does not exceed 255 bytes. The conversation result reporting interface has no prescribed return value and can carry its own logic. These mechanisms place the share entry, the identity check and the result report at three different layers. Retrieval or a form does not constitute authorisation execution.

## Constraints at the shared access step

The share link identifier only locates the entry. Treating it as proof of authorisation is a category error, because the link stays usable regardless of what the underlying complaint material states. The no-login window relies on a per-user localId and cloud-pulled conversation records, and this arrangement is bounded to the same device and same browser, with a pull limit of the most recent 30 days and 20 records. Crossing devices or clearing cache therefore falls outside the documented guarantee.

Identity verification carries its own boundaries. It is available within the commercial edition scope, it is configured as a root address rather than a full request path, and it fires at initialisation, conversation start and conversation end. The returned success flag decides whether the user may continue, and the returned uid must satisfy the documented format conditions. A successful identity check confirms the identity layer only. It does not establish that a complaint has been verified, that rectification is complete, or that submission to a regulator has been approved.

The result reporting interface sits at a third layer. It has no prescribed return value and its logic is left to the implementation. This means the reporting step is not itself an approval step, and it does not write business approval into the complaint record unless a separate downstream implementation does so.

Hypothetical drill sample: a store submits a training handover request. The attached material reads: "Complaint ticket number A-2024-0417, complaint subject is a store order, complaint content is delayed shipment, current status is awaiting verification of evidence by the store manager." A draft sentence reads: "This ticket has been verified and rectification is complete, and it may be submitted to the regulator." To be judged: the material records only a pending-verification status, and it does not record a verification conclusion, completed rectification or submission authorisation. The draft sentence exceeds the material scope.

| Points cap | Check the usage limit of the share entry and the cost attribution | Compare against the assumed usage for this trial; some parameters are edition-restricted |
| Identity verification root address | Configure the root address within the commercial edition scope, without the full path | Observe the three request points at initialisation, start and finish, and the returned success and uid |
| Conversation record boundary | Understood as same device, same browser, most recent 30 days, 20 records | After crossing devices or clearing cache, check whether records are lost |

## Three common errors

1. Treating the share link identifier as business authorisation. Input is the link identifier; the comparison object is the authorisation state recorded in the material; the observable result is that the link is usable while the material records no submission authorisation.
2. Writing a pending-verification status as completed rectification. Input is the ticket text "awaiting verification of evidence by the store manager"; the comparison object is the draft sentence "verified and rectification is complete"; the observable result is that the two do not agree and the draft sentence has no support in the material.
3. Treating a successful identity verification response as business approval. Input is the interface return of success and uid; the comparison object is the approval or submission authorisation in the material; the observable result is that identity verification passed while the approval state remains unknown.

## How to verify the setup

1. Against the sample material text, check whether the four items of ticket number, complaint subject, complaint content and current status are all present. If the draft sentence claims verification and completed rectification while the original text records only pending verification, judge that the draft sentence is unsupported and keep the evidence gap open.
2. Against the draft sentence "may be submitted to the regulator", check whether the material records submission authorisation, the submission recipient and the effective scope. If none is recorded, log it as pending verification and do not infer that submission has been authorised or completed.
3. Against the identity verification configuration, check the root address entry and the three request trigger conditions, and observe the returned success and the uid format. If success returns false, the corresponding error message is shown; identity verification passing is not equivalent to business approval or a completed database write.
