# Franchise filing management: shared access for staff training

## What the documents look like

Franchise filing management works with legal disclosure and the commerce department system. It sits apart from head-office site selection and store engineering. The working materials are franchise contracts, operations manuals, filing submissions and information disclosure documents. These come from the commercial franchise information management system, enterprise licences, store lists and contract ledgers. In store service and internal training, the same materials are shared with franchisees or internal staff, and the act of sharing carries no authorisation effect on its own. Filing submissions and disclosure documents update on an irregular basis or by year; this page does not depend on that frequency.

Two facts shape the sharing step. First, a no-login window is created from the publishing channel and produces a temporary address that any internet user can open, with costs charged to the owning team. Second, identity verification is limited to the commercial edition, and the interface returns only whether verification succeeded plus a user identifier. Neither fact turns a shared link into a filing submission, a signed contract or a training authorisation.

## Constraints at the shared access step

A shared link, a completed form and a retrieval result each fail to constitute a filing submission, a contract signature or a training authorisation. The link identifier is not a business authorisation credential. Identity verification is judged by the interface response: the system reads whether `success` is `true` and reads the returned `uid`, which must be a string of no more than 255 bytes and must not contain the characters "|", "/" or "\". A `uid` that breaks these conditions returns an `Invalid UID` error. That `uid` is used to pull and save conversation records; it does not carry filing or contract authorisation decisions.

Conversation records rely on a local identifier tied to the same device and the same browser. Switching devices or clearing browser cache loses those records. The system permits pulling only the most recent 30 days and 20 records. Cross-device records require separate identity and session management, which this page does not construct.

The expiration time and QPM are set within the actual usage scope and checked against the real version. QPM means the maximum number of visits per user per minute. The points cap is checked against the usage limit of the sharing entry and the cost ownership. Several parameters are supported only in the commercial edition.

Hypothetical paper exercise sample: a material submitted for checking, titled Store Service and Internal Training, reads: "This manual is used for internal training of franchise store employees, and training records are kept by the store. The shared link is used only to view the operations manual and does not replace the submission of filing materials." A handover request awaiting judgement reads: "Please treat this shared link as a completed internal training authorisation." The material text carries a subject (franchise store employees), an object (the operations manual) and a state (used only for viewing, does not replace filing submission). The request awaiting judgement attempts to treat the sharing act as a completed authorisation, which does not match the state in the material text.

The identity verification address is a `POST` root address. Once filled in, initialisation, conversation start and conversation end each send a request to a specific interface under that address. Only the root address is configured, not the full request path. The sharing link carries an extra `authToken` parameter, and the system sends `token=[authToken]` in the request `body`. The conversation result reporting interface has no specified return value and receives `responseData`, where `totalPoints` and `token` are the fields to watch.

## Three common errors

1. Treating the shared link identifier as a filing submission or training authorisation credential. The shared link is used only for access and produces no authorisation effect. The check: input the hypothetical material text and the handover request, compare "used only to view the operations manual and does not replace the submission of filing materials" with "treated as a completed internal training authorisation". When the two states do not match, the request is not supported and the evidence gap is recorded.

2. Treating the `uid` returned by identity verification as a business permission. The `uid` is used to pull and save conversation records and does not carry filing or contract authorisation judgement. The check: input the sharing link with its `authToken`, compare the returned `success` and `uid` against the documented format. When `success` is not `true`, or the `uid` contains a forbidden character or exceeds 255 bytes, an error is prompted and the next step is not entered.

3. Treating a request awaiting review as a completed authorisation. A pending request does not show an absence of operating permission and does not equal a completed authorisation. The check: input the handover request on its own, compare its stated state against the material text state. When the request claims completion while the material states viewing only, the claim is not supported.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the hypothetical material text and the handover request given above. Compare "used only to view the operations manual and does not replace the submission of filing materials" with "treated as a completed internal training authorisation". When the two states do not match, the request is not supported, and the evidence gap is recorded. No test has been run; this states the expected comparison only.

2. Input the sharing link with its `authToken`. Compare the interface return against the documented conditions: `success` must be `true`, and `uid` must be a string of no more than 255 bytes without "|", "/" or "\". When `success` is not `true`, or `uid` breaks the format, an error is prompted and the next step is not entered. The document states this behaviour; this exercise does not claim it was observed.

3. Input a cross-device access scenario. Compare whether the local identifier can pull the same conversation records. The document states that the local identifier works only on the same device and the same browser, and that switching devices or clearing cache loses records. When records are not visible after switching devices, that observation is recorded, and no claim is made about automatic synchronisation or authorisation taking effect.

Across these checks, the retrieval library and the relevance score do not carry permission or entity isolation. A similarity setting does not exclude unrelated businesses or unauthorised records. Retrieval is not authorisation. Forms collect values; business verification, approval, database writes and external synchronisation need their stated downstream implementation. Where the document gives no error code or no observed result, the comparison is described and the outcome remains unknown.
