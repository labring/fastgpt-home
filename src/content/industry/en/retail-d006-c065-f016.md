# Livestream violation appeals: interactive forms for staff training

## What the documents look like

Livestream violation appeals address penalties that have already been issued. The working materials are the violation notice, the livestream recording, the product qualification file, and the appeal statement. In store service and internal training, operations staff assemble these materials into a handover record, then decide who supplements evidence and who drafts the appeal reasoning. The brief describes the update frequency for this category as irregular, once per violation. This page does not derive batch synchronization or real-time triggering from that description.

For forms and interaction, the relevant documented facts are narrow. A form input node collects the information a user fills in and passes it to later nodes. It does not include a business database function. A user selection node executes a branch according to the user's choice. It is not a personnel directory or a business data selector. When an interaction node is triggered, the conversation enters an interaction state, the workflow state is recorded, and execution continues after the user completes the interaction. Field validation, business approval, write-back, and batch operations require an external system or a later node.

## Constraints at the interactive forms step

Forms and interaction can collect information and select a branch. They do not verify business facts, grant approval, write to a database, or synchronize with an external system. Retrieval and form submission do not constitute authorization. During handover, operations staff must distinguish whether a material is submitted, pending verification, or authorized.

Hypothetical paper exercise sample. A store receives a platform violation notice whose text reads: "This notice concerns account A and a qualification display issue for product link B during the livestream on 12 March 2025. An appeal statement must be submitted by 20 March 2025." The same batch of materials contains livestream recording segment C and product qualification file D. The proposed handover request reads: "The store fills in the appeal statement in the form and submits it; headquarters reviews and then decides whether to appeal." In this request, "submits" and "reviews and then decides" are two different states. Form submission does not mean the appeal has been approved.

Hypothetical starting point for this exercise: the form has four required fields, because the sample materials correspond to four document types. The comparison object is the submission behavior when one field is missing. This starting point is a paper setting and is not an official default value.

## Three common errors

1. Treating form submission as an approved appeal. The proposed handover request in the sample says "submits it; headquarters reviews and then decides." Reading that as an approved appeal turns a pending-review state into an approved state. The form node collects information; approval requires a later node or an external system.
2. Treating the user selection node as a personnel directory. "Headquarters reviews" in the sample is a process branch. Listing named reviewers inside the user selection node misuses a branch selector as a business data selector.
3. Treating retrieval results as permission isolation. Recording segment C and qualification file D belong to the same account in the sample. Using a similarity setting to exclude other stores' records treats relevance as a permission filter. Retrieval does not perform authorization judgment.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the sample text "This notice concerns account A and a qualification display issue for product link B during the livestream on 12 March 2025. An appeal statement must be submitted by 20 March 2025." Check the four required fields, account, date, product link, and deadline, one by one. The expected observable result is that the form can be submitted when all four are present, and the interaction state remains waiting when any one is missing, with no automatic submission.
2. Input the proposed handover request "The store fills in the appeal statement in the form and submits it; headquarters reviews and then decides whether to appeal." Compare the form submission action with the "reviews and then decides" state. The expected observable result is that the record shows pending review after submission and does not show approved. A record showing approved indicates that downstream approval was written into the form node.
3. Input the sample materials "livestream recording segment C and product qualification file D." In the user selection node, check whether the branch options are only "submit for review" and "supplement materials." The expected observable result is that choosing "supplement materials" enters the supplement branch and choosing "submit for review" enters the review branch. A node that lists reviewer names directly indicates the branch selector has been misused. These are paper checks; no actual test is claimed.
