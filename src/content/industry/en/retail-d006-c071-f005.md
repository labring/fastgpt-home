# Cross-border settlements: staff training with multi-turn prompts

## What the documents look like

For staff training in Cross-border settlements, this page examines FastGPT multi-turn prompts. Inspect the periods needed for a trend question and prevent other product records entering the lesson. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the multi-turn prompts step

The training exercise asks for a sales trend for Product M, but sales quantity and amount alone do not establish a trend comparison. First inspect whether comparable periods and their records were supplied. Limit the lesson to reading the available data or identify the comparison material still needed. In a follow-up, check that an earlier Product G copywriting task does not enter the Product M training question. Match the cited settlement statement with the actual input instead of treating another task’s summary as this lesson’s source.

## Three common errors

- If M’s quantity and sales value become a rising or falling trend, inspect comparable periods and sources. Historical G summaries cannot supply the missing comparison.

- If N’s two-record follow-up becomes H or loses one source, retain questions and input. Restore identities, period, currency and definitions without teaching automatic priority for one record.

- If O’s product training borrows historical features, trace an actual manual or description. Settlement supplies sales information only; continued questions cannot obtain a missing manual. Add it and retest.

## How to verify the setup

Product O’s features require a training manual or product description; its settlement statement contains sales information. Repeated questions do not make the absent manual part of the input. Inspect whether the answer distinguishes sales records from product-training evidence and whether it transfers a feature discussed earlier to Product O. After adding the manual, repeat the original lesson question and a follow-up, retaining the new source, actual history and answers separately to identify the evidence change.
