# Creator commission settlement: shared access for staff training

## What the documents look like

In creator commission settlement, the commission event occurs after a sale closes. The review focus sits on valid promotion orders and post-sale commission deductions. Store service and internal training work with four recurring document types: commission setting records, settlement statements, refund deduction details, and invoices. These are updated weekly or monthly.

A shared entry point hands these materials to store staff or trainees for viewing, form entry, and handover. The entry point and the authorization state must be recorded separately. A link identifier grants access; it does not grant business authorization. Retrieval relevance and similarity scores do not perform permission or entity isolation.

## Constraints at the shared access step

A no-login window is created under the publish channel and the system generates a complete link that can be copied. The link identifier serves access only. Expiry time, QPM, and points cap belong to the usage scope settings of the share entry, and some parameters are restricted by version and must be checked against the actual version. Identity verification is limited to the commercial edition. Access is decided by the official interface's success state and uid. The uid is a string that excludes "|", "/", and "\" characters and is at most 255 bytes in length. The anonymous local identifier depends on the same device and same browser; cross-device records require separate identity and session design.

Hypothetical drill sample: a store training lead plans to place creator commission settlement materials into a no-login share entry for trainees to view. The source material reads: "Commission setting record: March 1 to March 31, 2025, creator A's commission rate is 8%. Settlement statement: for the same period, creator A's valid order commission totals 1,200 yuan. Refund deduction detail: order R-01 refunded on March 12, commission of 40 yuan deducted back." The proposed sentence is: "Creator A's commission for March 2025 has been fully settled." The pending request is: "Please give this share link to trainees and allow them to view all settlement statements."

## Three common errors

1. Treating the system-generated share link as business authorization and giving it directly to trainees to view all settlement statements. Input: the pending request above and the link. Comparison: whether the link carries identity verification and scope limits for that person. Observable result: the request is allowed or remains in a pending authorization state.

2. Treating the proposed sentence "Creator A's commission for March 2025 has been fully settled" as a verified conclusion. Input: the source material above and the proposed sentence. Comparison: same subject, same period, same field. The material gives only the commission rate, the valid order commission total, and one refund deduction. It does not state a settlement status. Observable result: the sentence is unsupported.

3. Treating retrieval or similarity results as permission filtering. Input: a retrieval request on the share entry. Comparison: whether returned records pass through identity verification and authorization scope. Observable result: the similarity value does not change the allow condition.

## How to verify the setup

1. Identity verification check: input the share link carrying authToken and the auth interface response. Compare whether success is true and whether uid meets the format. The observable result is allow or an identity error prompt; not tested.

2. Proposed sentence check: input the source material above and the proposed sentence. Compare whether subject, period, and field match. The material does not state a settlement status. The observable result is that the sentence remains pending verification, not judged as contradictory.

3. Empty-result handling check: use the paper input [] as a retrieval return and check whether the downstream of this share entry is designed for empty results. The observable result is whether an unhandled branch appears downstream. No claim is made that a fictional rule retrieval necessarily returns [].
