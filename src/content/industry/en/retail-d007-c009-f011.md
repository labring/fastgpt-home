# Amazon FBM fulfillment: database operations for operating reviews

## What the documents look like

Amazon FBM fulfillment is operated by the seller, who arranges delivery and sets the return address rather than relying on marketplace warehouse inspection. The working data comes from Amazon Seller Central, carrier systems, ERP orders and customer service email, and the recurring documents are the order picking list, the shipment confirmation record, the return address setting and the logistics tracking sheet.

For operating reports and exception review, these four documents carry different fields, and the same order may hold unsynchronized values across them. The database and operations step separates the storage of original business data from the vector retrieval backend, and each deployed service is checked against the actual deployment plan. A retrieval result does not constitute authorization. A form collects values. Business verification, approval, database writes and external synchronization each require their own downstream implementation. The supplied material gives a per-order and daily update frequency; this page does not infer batch synchronization, full synchronization, real-time or transaction-triggered mechanisms from it. After a configuration change, the same business sample is rechecked, because process liveness alone does not confirm correct behavior.

Hypothetical paper exercise sample. The following material text is constructed for this paper comparison and does not refer to any real customer or company.

Logistics tracking sheet, sentence one: "Order A-001, shipment confirmation record status is confirmed, carrier tracking number CN0001, return address setting is address R1."

Shipment confirmation record, sentence two: "Order A-001, confirmation time 2024-06-01, operator account U-07, return address field is empty."

Customer service email, sentence three: "The customer for order A-001 reports not receiving the goods and requests a check of the tracking number."

One draft sentence to be assessed: "Order A-001 has completed the return address setting, and the exception review can be closed."

## Constraints at the database operations step

The four documents hold distinct fields, and the status values for one order need not be synchronized across them. In the sample, the logistics tracking sheet states that the return address setting is address R1, while the shipment confirmation record states that the return address field is empty. These are two different documents describing the same order and the same field, and the values are mutually exclusive. The comparison result is that the draft sentence is not supported by the material, and the item remains pending verification. The material does not state which document governs, so the gap stays open rather than being resolved in either direction.

The tracking number CN0001 appears in the logistics tracking sheet. That sheet does not record a signed-receipt status, and the customer service email only states that the customer reports not receiving the goods. A tracking number is not delivery evidence, and the absence of a signed-receipt field means the delivery claim is unsupported rather than disproved.

Closing an exception review is a business action. The material does not name an authorization subject, an effective condition or a completion record for closure. A pending request is not an approval, and a draft sentence is not an executed closure. The evidence gap remains.

Retrieval and similarity settings do not perform permission or entity filtering. Libraries built on different vector models cannot be associated together; identifiers are checked first and non-matching entries are excluded, with actual observations recorded. Missing evidence stays missing.

## Three common errors

1. Treating the logistics tracking sheet value "return address setting is address R1" as a completed return address field on the shipment confirmation record. The error is taking a field across documents: the shipment confirmation record explicitly states that the return address field is empty. An executable check inputs both document texts, compares the value of the same field for the same order, and records the field as set only when the two values agree.

2. Treating tracking number CN0001 as evidence of delivery. The logistics tracking sheet records only a tracking number and states no signed-receipt status, while the customer service email states only that the customer reports not receiving the goods. An executable check inputs the tracking sheet and the email text, compares whether a signed-receipt field exists, and records the delivery claim as unsupported when the field is missing.

3. Treating the draft sentence "has completed the return address setting, and the exception review can be closed" as an established conclusion. The sentence conflicts with the empty field in the shipment confirmation record, and closure is a business action that does not appear in the source text. An executable check compares the draft sentence with the three source texts item by item for subject, object and field value, marks the parts without source support, and preserves the evidence gap.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the three hypothetical source sentences and the draft sentence. Compare "has completed the return address setting" in the draft sentence with "return address field is empty" in the shipment confirmation record. When the comparison shows that the two disagree on the value of the same field for the same order, the draft sentence is judged unsupported and recorded as pending verification, not written as a denial.

2. Input the hypothetical source text and the request "the exception review can be closed." Compare whether the source text states an authorization subject and an effective condition for closure. When the source text states no authorization boundary, record an evidence gap and do not infer that approval or execution occurred.
