# Convenience fresh-food orders: database operations for operating reviews

## What the documents look like

Convenience-store fresh food is ordered at short shelf life and high frequency, with each store placing orders for the next day rather than operating under a revenue-sharing arrangement. The working documents are the fresh-food order form, the spoilage report, the near-expiry handling sheet and the temperature record. The order form carries the store's estimate of next-day sales alongside the quantity actually ordered. The spoilage report carries quantities and reasons for expired or damaged goods. The near-expiry handling sheet carries discount or removal actions. The temperature record reflects cold-chain equipment status. These four document types update daily or per shift, and they are the main inputs to operating reports and exception reviews.

Because the store itself carries the ordering and spoilage responsibility, reports need to aggregate ordered quantity, spoilage quantity and near-expiry handling quantity by store, by category and by date. Exception review asks whether the same store, the same date and the same field agree across documents. The order form's received quantity can be compared with the inventory record, for example, and the spoilage report's quantity can be compared with the removal quantity on the near-expiry handling sheet. Data sources include POS sales, the headquarters ordering system, store inventory and cold-chain temperature records. These sources can differ in update cadence and field definitions, so reconciliation begins by confirming that subject, date and field are aligned.

## Constraints at the database operations step

The database and operations step must keep storage, retrieval and recovery available for the documents above. Business source data and the vector retrieval backend are separate storage responsibilities; connection settings are checked against the actual deployment rather than inferred from a variable name. Connection pool, cache and log settings are adjusted against observed connection counts, error rates and durations under the operator's own load. Untested concurrency thresholds are not copied from elsewhere.

Backup and recovery are verified separately. A successful backup generation does not mean the backup is restorable, and a checkable sample plus a record of the check are retained. After a database or service configuration change, the same business sample is re-examined; process liveness alone does not show that reads and writes behave correctly. The association requirement between a retrieval index and a vector model applies only to knowledge-base retrieval under the same model, and it does not extend to external systems needing the same model. A relevance score does not carry permission or entity isolation duties, and a retrieval result is not an authorization basis. A pending request is not approval, an offered option is not execution, and an unsigned termination is not a completed termination.

## Three common errors

1. Treating a retrieval relevance score as a permission filter. The document states that the relevance score is used for ordering and does not carry entity or permission isolation. If a report query relies on the score to exclude records outside the operator's authority, that exclusion does not take effect, and a separate permission check implementation is needed.

2. Checking only process liveness after a configuration change. The document states that the same business sample is re-examined after a change. Confirming that a service started does not show whether data reads and writes behave correctly, so the same query is re-run and results are compared.

3. Treating backup generation as recovery availability. The document states that backup and recovery are verified separately. A successful backup task does not mean the backup can be restored, so a restore is performed with the backup sample and data completeness is checked.

## How to verify the setup

Hypothetical drill sample: a convenience store's order form dated 10 June 2025 states "rice ball order 30 units, received 30 units." The same day's inventory record states "rice ball inbound 30 units." The spoilage report states "rice ball spoilage 2 units, reason expired." The draft sentence to be assessed is "the store's rice ball order, receipt and inbound quantities agree for that day, and the 2-unit spoilage is registered."

- Verification one: compare the received and inbound quantities on the order form and the inventory record. Input the two source texts above and compare whether "received 30 units" and "inbound 30 units" share the same subject, the same date and the same field. Both are 30 units, so the quantity-agreement condition is met. The spoilage quantity is outside this verification's scope.

- Verification two: compare the spoilage report with the near-expiry handling sheet. Input the spoilage report text "rice ball spoilage 2 units, reason expired" and compare whether the near-expiry handling sheet has a removal record for the same day and the same category. If the near-expiry handling sheet is not provided, the correspondence between spoilage quantity and removal quantity is unsupported and is recorded as pending confirmation, not judged as a contradiction.

- Verification three: check the "agree" wording in the draft sentence. Input the draft sentence and the materials above, and compare whether "order, receipt and inbound quantities agree" is supported by all three materials. The order form and the inventory record support agreement between receipt and inbound, and the ordered quantity of 30 units agrees with the received quantity of 30 units. The registered 2-unit spoilage is supported by the spoilage report. If the near-expiry handling sheet is absent, the draft sentence does not mention near-expiry handling, so no contradiction arises. If the draft sentence claims "no expired sales," the materials provide no sales record, and that claim is unsupported.
