# Franchisee onboarding: database operations for operating reviews

## What the documents look like

Franchisee training and opening coaching produces four recurring materials: training sign-in sheets, opening checklists, SOP manuals, and assessment scores. These materials record the transfer of operating capability from headquarters to a franchise location. That purpose differs from franchise filing management, which is a legal disclosure activity. For this page, the working task is operating reporting and anomaly review, and the feature under discussion is database and operations.

Training sign-in sheets show which participants attended and in what state. Opening checklists record whether materials and process steps were checked. SOP manuals carry headquarters standard actions. Assessment scores show the level of mastery. Operating reporting and anomaly review aligns these materials by store, period, and field so that a reviewer can judge whether a coaching session met expectations. The materials update per store and per month. This update rhythm does not by itself create batch synchronization, full synchronization, real-time processing, or transaction-triggered behavior at the database operations layer.

Database and operations work here covers storage and comparison conditions. It does not replace business judgment. Business source data storage and vector retrieval backends are two separate responsibilities, and each is checked against the actual deployment plan. Vector model identifiers must match before records can be associated together. Cross-model association is not performed as a comparison.

## Constraints at the database operations step

Materials arrive per store and per month. Connection and load behavior is observed from the deployment itself. Concurrency thresholds from other systems are not copied in without testing. Backup generation and actual restore are verified separately, and a checkable sample is retained for each. After a configuration change, the same business sample is rechecked. A running process does not by itself show that business results are correct.

Hypothetical drill sample: the source text available for comparison reads, "In March 2025, franchise owner Li of Store A attended cashier training, and the sign-in sheet records attendance; the opening checklist records that cashier counter materials were checked; the assessment score is recorded as pass." The draft sentence to be judged reads, "Li has completed all opening coaching, and the store may open." The source text records only cashier training attendance, material checking, and a passing assessment. It does not record completion of all coaching, and it does not record opening authorization. The draft sentence is unsupported and requires the remaining coaching records and authorization documents before any judgment can be made.

Retrieval is not authorization. A similarity score is not a category, entity, or permission filter, and it does not exclude unrelated businesses or unauthorized records. Forms collect values; business verification, approval, database writes, and external synchronization require their own downstream implementation.

## Three common errors

1. Treating a retrieval result as an authorization basis. The input is the sample sign-in sheet and checklist. The comparison is whether an opening authorization field is recorded. A retrieval hit does not change the authorization state. If the field is not recorded, the matter remains pending verification.

2. Treating a pending review state as missing permission. The input is the sample assessment score record. The comparison is its status field. A pending review does not show that operating permission is absent; it shows only that the field is not complete.

3. Treating related-stage evidence as a contradiction. The input is the sample sign-in sheet and checklist. The comparison is the same subject, the same period, and the same field. The two sheets do not record mutually exclusive values, so no contradiction exists. The matter is unsupported.

## How to verify the setup

1. Take the sample source text, "In March 2025, franchise owner Li of Store A attended cashier training, and the sign-in sheet records attendance." Enter it into the retrieval scope. Compare whether the returned record's subject, period, and field match the source text. If the returned record contains the same subject and period and the field is readable, the comparison condition is met. If the result is empty, record the gap. Do not infer that a rule must be empty.

2. Take the draft sentence, "Li has completed all opening coaching, and the store may open." Enter it for field-by-field comparison against the sample source text. The source text does not record completion of all coaching or opening authorization, so the sentence is unsupported. The remaining coaching records and authorization documents are required before a further judgment can be made.

3. Take the sample opening checklist record. Enter it for comparison against the sign-in sheet by the same store and the same period. The two sheets have no mutually exclusive field values, so no contradiction exists. If a field is not recorded in either material, record it as pending verification. Do not judge it as a denial.
