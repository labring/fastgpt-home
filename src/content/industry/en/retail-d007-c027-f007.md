# Dealer ordering events: conversation logs for operating reviews

## What the documents look like

Dealer ordering events run by season or by year. The working materials for a single event include the ordering handbook, the ordering contract, the sample list, and the credit approval record. Operating reports and exception reviews compare these materials against each dealer's reserved quantity, contract status, and credit status for a defined ordering period.

The conversation log and audit step adds a second layer: each conversation carries a chatId, and the review must match that chatId to the correct business record. Ordering materials update on a seasonal or annual cycle, while conversation logs are produced per request. A conversation log that exists does not by itself complete a compliance audit. Reconciliation, retention, and compliance requirements are handled by a fuller business process. Values collected through retrieval or through a form do not constitute an authorization to execute.

Two or three industry facts shape this step directly. First, the ordering event is an offline dealer reservation activity, so the conversation log is not managing a platform storefront; the review ties a conversation to a dealer, an event, and a reservation record. Second, the primary data sources are the ordering system, historical sales, dealer inventory, and financial credit, so a conversation log alone cannot confirm a credit decision. Third, the costly failure modes are under-ordering, inventory-pressure disputes, and collection risk, which means the review must keep approval states and execution states separate.

## Constraints at the conversation logs step

The review must distinguish four things: behavior a node already performs, fields that are actually configurable, manual reconciliation actions, and business actions that require a separate system implementation. A log entry is not an audit conclusion. A retrieval result is not an authorization. A form value is not an approval.

Hypothetical paper exercise. The source material reads: "East China regional ordering event, spring 2025 session. Dealer A submitted a letter of ordering intent. The sample list contains three items in category X. Credit approval status is submitted and pending review." The draft sentence under judgment reads: "Dealer A's credit line is approved and this order may be executed." The source material shows only that credit approval status is submitted and pending review. It does not state an approval outcome. The draft sentence converts a pending state into an approved state, which is unsupported.

The same exercise covers a handover request: a form collected Dealer A's ordering intent, and the request treats that collection as permission to execute the order. The form value contains no approval conclusion, so the request is unsupported. A third variation uses two chatIds, one for the spring session and one for the autumn session; merging the two conversations into one total produces a chatId list that does not match the session identifiers in the business records.

## Three common errors

1. Writing a pending state as approved. In the hypothetical sample, credit approval status is submitted and pending review. If the draft sentence says approved, the input is the credit approval status field, the comparison object is the source wording "submitted and pending review," and the observable result is that the draft sentence and the source wording do not match.
2. Treating a retrieved or form-collected value as an authorization to execute. In the hypothetical sample, the form collected Dealer A's ordering intent. If the request concludes that credit is approved, the input is the form value, the comparison object is the credit approval status field, and the observable result is that the form value contains no approval conclusion.
3. Merging conversations that carry different chatIds. In the hypothetical sample, the spring and autumn sessions use different chatIds. If the two conversations are combined into one total, the input is the chatId list, the comparison object is the session identifiers in the business records, and the observable result is that the chatIds and the session identifiers do not match.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the hypothetical source wording and the draft sentence, then compare the credit status in the draft sentence against the source wording "submitted and pending review." If the draft sentence says approved, the result is unsupported. If it says pending review, the result matches the source wording.
2. Input the hypothetical form-collected value and the credit approval status field, then compare whether either contains an approval conclusion. If the form value contains no approval conclusion, the result is that the order cannot be executed on that basis.
3. Input the hypothetical chatId list and the session identifiers in the business records, then compare whether they correspond one to one. If a mismatch exists, the result is that conversation ownership needs to be rechecked.

In each case, the expected outcome is stated for the paper exercise; no test, deployment, approval, or refund is claimed to have occurred. Where the source material does not state a field, the field remains unknown. A pending request is not an approval. An offered option is not an execution. An unsigned termination is not a completed termination.
