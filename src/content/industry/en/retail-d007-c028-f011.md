# Brand livestream operations: database operations for operating reviews

## What the documents look like
Brand livestream operations place scriptwriting, inventory, and after-sales responsibility on the brand itself, so the operating record differs from a creator-hosted session that runs mainly on commission. The main data sources named in the brief are the Douyin Shop Compass, Juliang Qianchuan, livestream comments, and transaction details. Four documents are handled daily: the livestream script, the product explanation card, the shift schedule, and the livestream review. Of these, the shift schedule carries the personnel authorization boundary, and the product explanation card carries the explanation content and price wording. The three most time-consuming steps are arranging products and prices, controlling explanation pacing, and reviewing traffic conversion.

For this page the working task is operating reports and anomaly review at the database operations step. The update frequency given in the brief is per session and hourly, which means the report and the review need to be organized by session and by hour. That granularity is what makes the four documents joinable: script, explanation card, shift schedule, and review record need to be associated under the same session identifier. The brief does not state any batch sync, full sync, real-time, or transaction-triggered mechanism, so none is assumed here.

## Constraints at the database operations step
Database connection settings need to distinguish the business source data store from the vector retrieval backend, checked against the actual deployment. A configuration value is not inferred from a variable name. Connection pool sizing, error counts, and latency need to be observed against the deployment's own load; a concurrency threshold that was not tested for this deployment is not copied over. Backup generation and actual recovery are verified separately, with a sample and a record that can be checked. After a database or service configuration change, the same business sample is read again; a running process alone does not show that the change is correct.

Hypothetical drill sample. A brand livestream team ran a session on 1 June 2025 from 20:00 to 23:00. The shift schedule records host A and floor manager B on duty. The product explanation card records the explanation price of product X as 199 yuan. The livestream review draft states: "Product X conversion this session fell below expectation; recommend delisting." A draft sentence is pending judgment: "According to this session's review, product X has been delisted because conversion did not meet target." The draft sentence concerns subject product X, session date 1 June 2025, and the field product status. The shift schedule does not state who holds delisting authority. The review draft is in a pending-review state, with no signature or effective record. Authorization boundary: floor manager B may adjust explanation pacing; product delisting requires approval by the operations lead, and no approval record has been provided.

## Three common errors
1. Treating a pending-review review draft as an effective delisting decision. Input: the draft text "recommend delisting." Compare against: the approval record. Observable result: when the approval record is absent, the product cannot be judged as delisted.
2. Treating an on-duty entry in the shift schedule as delisting authorization. Input: the shift schedule showing host A and floor manager B on duty. Compare against: the operations lead's approval record. Observable result: when the approval record is not provided, the authorization status remains to be verified.
3. Treating a retrieval relevance score as a permission filter. Input: a retrieval request containing product X. Compare against: the session scope the current account may access. Observable result: the score level does not change the accessible scope.

## How to verify the setup
1. Input the review draft original text "Product X conversion this session fell below expectation; recommend delisting" and the draft sentence "product X has been delisted because conversion did not meet target." Compare the draft status field with the approval record field. Condition to satisfy: when the draft status is pending review and the approval record is absent, the draft sentence is judged unsupported.
2. Input the shift schedule original text "host A, floor manager B on duty" and a product delisting request. Compare the shift schedule authorization field with the operations lead's approval record. Condition to satisfy: when the shift schedule does not state delisting authority and the approval record is not provided, the request is recorded as pending verification and delisting is not executed.
3. Input the backup sample for the 1 June 2025 session, containing shift schedule, explanation card, and review draft. Compare the recovered sample against the original on session identifier, product X price field, and draft status field. Condition to satisfy: when all three fields match item by item, recovery verification passes; when any field is missing or differs in value, record the difference and retain the gap without inferring a cause.

A retrieval relevance score is not a category, entity, or permission filter, and retrieval is not authorization. Forms collect values; business verification, approval, database writes, and external synchronization each need their own stated downstream implementation. No error code or actual observation is supplied for this drill, so only the comparison and the remaining unknown are described.
