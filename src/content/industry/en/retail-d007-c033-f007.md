# Trade and FX administration: conversation logs for operating reviews

## What the documents look like

Foreign-exchange receipt and settlement work centers on the authenticity of FX funds and the underlying trade background. It does not review a full set of letter-of-credit documents. The everyday materials are receipt vouchers, trade contracts, invoices and FX business registration records. Reconciling the receiving entity, completing settlement and explaining the trade background are the time-consuming steps.

Operating reports and exception reviews align conversation records with business documents at two frequencies: per transaction and monthly. The chatId, dataId and run details of a conversation log become traceable reconciliation clues at that point. The existence of a log shows only that a record was kept. Reconciliation, retention and compliance audit are delivered by a complete business solution elsewhere.

## Constraints at the conversation logs step

Conversation identity is checked against the target conversation and business records through chatId. Sessions from different entry points are not merged into one count. When run details are needed, detail is set according to the official contract, and module status and complete results inside responseData are inspected. The log is checked against the fields the interface actually returns; business data that is not returned cannot be assumed to be fully retained. Identity fields in a test mode may differ from a formal call, so input, history and returned content are each checked separately. Retrieval and forms collect and recall values; they do not constitute authorization.

Hypothetical drill sample: an export-trade finance officer processes one export receipt in March 2025. The source material reads: "The receipt voucher shows the payer as Company A, amount USD 12,000, dated 5 March 2025; the trade contract seller is Company B; the invoice issuer is Company B; the FX business registration record lists the receiving entity as Company B." The handover request to be judged is: "Please confirm the receiving entity for this receipt is consistent and can be credited directly." The phrase "can be credited directly" is a business execution action; the paper materials support only an entity comparison, and the authorization boundary for crediting is not stated.

## Three common errors

1. Treating a retrieval hit as authorization. The input is the handover request above. When a high similarity score is taken as grounds for crediting, the comparison object should be the receiving entity in the source material against the contract and invoice parties. The observed result is whether the three entities are consistent. Consistency supports an entity comparison only; the crediting authorization remains to be verified.

2. Treating the presence of a log as a completed compliance audit. The input is the session record for the hypothetical chatId. The comparison object is the reconciliation and retention requirements set by the business solution. The observed result is whether the log fields cover those requirements. Requirements that are not stated are recorded as gaps.

3. Merging sessions from different entry points. The input is the session list with source=api. The comparison object is the target chatId against each chatId in the list. The observed result is whether the target session appears uniquely. When several sources appear, each is checked separately and not combined into one count.

## How to verify the setup

1. Entity consistency check. Input the three source sentences and the handover request. Compare the receipt-voucher payer, the contract and invoice issuer, and the receiving entity in the registration record. The observed result is whether the entities are the same or different. Sameness does not yield a conclusion that crediting is allowed; difference is recorded as pending verification.

2. Run-detail field check. Input the hypothetical appId, chatId and dataId with detail=true. Compare the module name, module type and running time inside the returned responseData. The observed result is whether the target module appears. Fields that do not appear are recorded as not returned.

3. Session source check. Input source=api and pageSize=20. Compare each chatId in the returned list against the target chatId. The observed result is whether the target session is in the list with a consistent source. When this cannot be confirmed, the state is retained as unsupported and no claim of exclusion is made.
