# Multi-warehouse inventory: operating reviews with citation tracing

## What the documents look like

For operating reviews in Multi-warehouse inventory, this page examines FastGPT citation tracing. Trace allocation rules, read snapshots and stock alerts as different warehouse evidence. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the citation tracing step

A proposed multi-warehouse review discusses candidate locations, completes reading a snapshot with its data time still to verify and raises an alert requiring on-site review. The draft declares goods allocated and shortage confirmed. Rules propose work, a successful read supplies a table and an alert remains unverified. Retain warehouse, time and evidence type. A rule does not establish that goods were moved. Reading an older snapshot does not establish the current state. No quantities or synchronization effects are supplied, and no actual interface connection is assumed by this proposed source-tracing exercise.

## Configuration and implementation checks

| Material check | Conditions in this test | Implementation evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rule status | Candidate discussion | Do not make performed allocation. |
| Time distinction | Read time and data time | Trace separately. |
| Alert status | Signal needing review | Do not make proven shortage. |

## Three common errors

First, candidate allocation may become performed assignment; obtain corresponding operation records because rules do not establish that goods were moved. Second, completed reading may become fresh data; inspect original data time and current origin instead of replacing source timing with read timing. Third, an alert may become proven shortage; inspect on-site or applicable stock evidence because an alert alone does not establish a shortage or its cause. These concern execution, freshness and inference. A nonempty citation array cannot establish reconciled warehouse states without the relevant actual evidence for the report’s individual claims.

## How to verify the setup

Ask what the rule proposes, when the snapshot applies and what the alert requires, expanding actual references. A table alone supports visible data and definitions, not a physical finding. An empty reference array does not establish that inventory is zero or determine the actual balance. Added review needs warehouse and item matched, updating supported states without borrowing another site. This exercise allocates no stock, synchronizes nothing and closes no alert. It guarantees no cross-warehouse consistency. Formal reporting requires corresponding records and scope rather than treating successful reading as proof of completed business execution or current actual goods conditions.
