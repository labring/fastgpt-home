# Customer-service knowledge: database operations for operating reviews

## What the documents look like

Customer-service knowledge base maintenance works on FAQ entries, reply templates, product parameter tables and escalation work orders. It prepares question-and-answer material in advance and does not approve refunds or returns. The material arrives from the customer-service system, product detail pages, after-sales issue statistics and quality spot checks. The people who normally handle it are customer-service supervisors, knowledge base operators and product operators. When this work goes wrong, the visible outcomes are answers that miss the question, escalated complaints and failed agent training.

Operating reviews and exception reviews need the database and operations layer to keep checkable records of this material, so that weekly or monthly update results can be traced. This layer stores, retrieves and verifies configuration. It does not produce business approval conclusions.

The update rhythm is weekly or monthly. That rhythm matters here only because review periods group the same FAQ entries, templates, parameter tables and escalation work orders. It does not create any batch synchronization, full synchronization, real-time or transaction-triggered mechanism.

## Constraints at the database operations step

Business source data storage and the vector retrieval backend have different responsibilities and must be checked separately against the actual deployment. Connection settings and load behaviour are judged from direct observation; concurrency thresholds that have not been tested in this environment are not copied from elsewhere. Backup and recovery are verified as two separate facts: that a backup was produced, and that a restore actually works. Both need a checkable sample and a record. After any change to a database or service configuration, the same business sample is checked again; a running process is not evidence that the change is correct.

Retrieval and similarity scoring do not perform permission filtering or entity isolation. Libraries that use different vector models cannot be associated together; the model identifier is checked and non-matching libraries are excluded. An empty result can be used to check downstream design against a clearly stated paper input of `[]`. It is not a claim that a fictional rule always returns `[]`. Image retrieval requires an image input and model capability. Reranking requires a model and must be enabled by configuration. Optional paths in a flow diagram do not mean every request executes all of them.

Hypothetical drill sample: the source text reads, "Knowledge base operator A submitted a revision of the FAQ entry 'Return shipping cost responsibility' on 10 March 2026; the status is pending review, and customer-service supervisor B has not signed." The draft sentence to be judged reads, "This FAQ entry revision has taken effect and can be synchronized to the customer-service reply template." The source text shows only a pending review and an unsigned record. The draft sentence's claim of effect is not supported, and the synchronization cannot be treated as completed. The gap is recorded as awaiting verification.

## Three common errors

1. Treating a pending review as already effective. In the sample, the FAQ entry status is pending review and supervisor B has not signed. If the report counts this entry as effective, the input is the status field and the signature field, the comparison is between "pending review / unsigned" and "effective", and the observable result is that the report's effective count disagrees with the source status.

2. Treating a retrieval result as an authorization basis. A similarity score reflects textual closeness only and does not perform permission or entity filtering. The input is a retrieval request from a customer-service account, the comparison is between that account's visible scope and the ownership of the returned entries, and the observable result is a returned entry outside that scope.

3. Associating libraries that use different vector models. The input is the model identifier of the two libraries, the comparison is whether the identifiers match, and the observable result is a joint retrieval executed despite different identifiers. The correct action is to record the identifiers and exclude the non-matching library, without inferring missed recall or redundancy.

## How to verify the setup

- Verification one: use the source text "Knowledge base operator A submitted a revision of the FAQ entry 'Return shipping cost responsibility' on 10 March 2026; the status is pending review, and customer-service supervisor B has not signed." The input is the status field and the signature field. The comparison is between pending review and unsigned, on one side, and the draft sentence's claim of effect, on the other. The condition is met when the report does not count the entry as effective and the gap is recorded as awaiting verification.

- Verification two: use the draft sentence "This FAQ entry revision has taken effect and can be synchronized to the customer-service reply template." The input is the draft sentence and the source text. The comparison is between "has taken effect" and the fact of effect, which the source text does not state. The condition is met when the draft sentence is marked as unsupported and no conclusion of completed synchronization is produced.

- Verification three: use the paper input `[]` to check the downstream design. The input is an empty result. The comparison is against the downstream flow's stated handling of an empty result. The condition is met when the downstream flow follows its empty-result branch, without claiming that a fictional rule always returns `[]`, and without guaranteeing a first hit or full recall. All three verifications describe expectations only and do not indicate that testing has been performed.
