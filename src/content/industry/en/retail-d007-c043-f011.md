# Multi-store management: database operations for operating reviews

## What the documents look like

Multi-store matrix operations draw on platform merchant backends, ERP channel pricing, brand anti-counterfeit systems and dealer reports. The working documents for this page are the store authorization list, the price control table, the diversion complaint form and the monthly sales report. Operating reviews and anomaly retrospectives here centre on cross-store authorization status and price order; single-store traffic efficiency belongs to a different layer. The brief gives daily and monthly update frequencies, and this page does not derive batch synchronization, full synchronization, real-time or transaction-triggered mechanisms from them.

The database and operations step inherits constraints from the documents themselves. Authorization lists, price control tables, complaint forms and monthly sales reports need storage and retrieval that can be checked against the source. Authorization status, price validity periods and complaint handling stages are business facts. A retrieval hit does not constitute authorization, and a form that collects a value does not perform verification, approval, database writes or external synchronization; those require their own stated downstream implementation.

## Constraints at the database operations step

Business source data and the vector retrieval backend have different responsibilities and must be checked separately against the actual deployment. Connection settings, error counts and latency should be observed under your own load rather than copied from untested concurrency thresholds. Backup generation and actual restore are two separate things and need separate verification with checkable samples kept. After a database or service configuration change, the same business sample should be re-checked; a live process does not mean the re-check is complete. A relevance score is not a category, entity or permission filter, and retrieval is not authorization.

Hypothetical paper exercise sample. The material text supplied for checking is: "Store authorization list, row 12: subject is a brand e-commerce department, object is a flagship store on a given platform, authorization status is pending review, valid range starts April 2025, no end date stated." "Price control table, row 7: same store, same product, control price is 199, valid until 30 June 2025." The draft sentence to be judged is: "This flagship store is authorized and may execute at 199." In the supplied material the authorization status is pending review, and the price control table does not state whether this store falls within the authorized scope. The authorization and price execution conclusions in the draft sentence are not supported.

## Three common errors

1. Treating a retrieval hit as confirmed authorization. Input is the pending-review authorization list row above and the draft sentence; compare the retrieval return against the authorization status field. The observable result is that the return does not change the "pending review" field value. Writing "authorized" from that return is the error.
2. Treating a quoted price or control-price validity period as a supply or execution deadline. Input is the validity field in price control table row 7 (valid until 30 June 2025) and the draft sentence; compare the validity field against the execution conclusion. The observable result is that the validity period does not state that this store is authorized, and does not state that the price may be executed.
3. Treating a live process as a completed post-change re-check. Input is a database or service configuration change record and the same business sample before and after; compare the two sample reads. The observable result is that consistency or difference in the samples is the basis, and a live process is not evidence of the re-check.

## How to verify the setup

1. For the pending-review authorization list row and the draft sentence, input the material text, the draft sentence and the authorization status field; compare the draft conclusion against the field value. The condition is met when the draft no longer claims authorization, or clearly marks it as pending verification. If not met, record the evidence gap.
2. For price control table row 7 and the draft sentence, input the validity field and the execution conclusion; compare whether they are the same fact. The condition is met when the execution conclusion is not supported by the validity period, or is clearly marked as not indicating a supply deadline. If not met, keep the source text unchanged.
3. For empty-result handling, input the explicit paper retrieval result `[]` and check whether the downstream design distinguishes an empty result from "no authorized stores". The condition is met when the downstream does not equate an empty result with a denial of authorization. If not met, record it as pending verification. All of the above are paper checks; no actual retrieval or deployment was executed, and retrieval does not carry permission or entity-isolation duties.
