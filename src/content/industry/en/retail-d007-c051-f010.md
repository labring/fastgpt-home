# Douyin storefront operations: citation tracing for operating reviews

## What the documents look like

Douyin storefront shelf operations center on product cards and the store experience score. The working materials for operating reviews and exception retrospectives are product qualification pages, experience score breakdowns, shipping violation appeals, and store announcements. An operating review has to keep each of these materials as a traceable citation source, so that every conclusion can be checked against the original wording rather than against a summary. The materials update daily and per order, so the effective version of a document directly affects whether a citation still holds.

The practical difference from livestream operations is the unit of attention. Shelf operations do not depend on the rhythm of a single live session; they depend on the product card and the store experience score, which change as orders and after-sales events accumulate. A retrospective that cites a qualification page or an experience score breakdown therefore has to record which version of that page or breakdown was in force, and which period and which field the cited value belongs to.

## Constraints at the citation tracing step

Retrieval begins by checking the selected knowledge bases and the version of the material. Only knowledge bases that share the same vector model can be associated together. The dataset search node outputs cited content as an array, and the array length may be zero; an empty array does not mean the workflow has errored or terminated. The retrieval output then has to be passed to the answering step, where each answer statement is checked against the cited original text.

A similarity score is a relevance number. It does not perform permission filtering or entity isolation, and retrieval by itself is not authorization. Whether a reviewer may read a given record is a separate question from whether that record scored highly.

Hypothetical paper exercise sample: the source material consists of three sentences. Sentence one: "This store's product qualification page was updated on 10 March 2025; the entity is a certain daily goods store, covering the disposable paper cup category." Sentence two: "The March 2025 experience score breakdown shows that the shipping violation deduction record dated 12 March is in appeal-pending status." Sentence three: "The store announcement states that shipping to some regions is suspended from 15 March to 20 March." The draft sentence to be judged is: "The store's March shipping violation appeal has been approved, and the qualification page now covers all categories." This sample is a paper exercise only. The entity, object, status, and effective scope are limited to the source text and do not refer to any real store.

## Three common errors

1. Treating a similarity score as a permission or entity filter. Input: one retrieval request and its score list. Comparison: the scores against the records actually accessible to the reviewer. Observable result: score level and record ownership do not correspond, so authorization must be checked separately.

2. Writing an appeal-pending status as an approved appeal. Input: the original text of the experience score breakdown. Comparison: the field values "appeal pending" and "appeal approved." Observable result: the two values are mutually exclusive, so the draft sentence is not supported by the source text.

3. Associating knowledge bases that use different vector models. Input: the vector model identifiers of two knowledge bases. Comparison: whether the identifiers match. Observable result: differing identifiers mean the bases cannot be associated; exclude the non-matching base and record the observation.

## How to verify the setup

- Verification one: input the source sentences above and the draft sentence, compare "appeal has been approved" in the draft against "appeal pending" in the source. When the two field values are mutually exclusive, the draft sentence is judged unsupported and must be returned for confirmation.

- Verification two: input "the qualification page now covers all categories" from the draft and "covering the disposable paper cup category" from the source, compare the coverage scope field. When the source states only a single category, "all categories" has no basis; keep the evidence gap open.

- Verification three: input a paper-based empty citation array, compare the downstream answering step's handling design for that array. The condition is met when the design can continue without error. This array is a hypothetical input and does not assert that a fictional retrieval necessarily returns an empty result.

Verification four: input the March 2025 experience score breakdown and the store announcement covering 15 March to 20 March, compare the announcement's effective period against the period cited in the draft. When the cited period falls outside the announcement's stated range, the citation is not supported for that period and the reviewer records the gap rather than treating the announcement as covering the whole month.

The optional paths in the overall retrieval flow do not all execute on every request. Image retrieval requires a corresponding image input and model capability; reranking requires a model and must be enabled in settings. Knowledge bases with different vector models cannot be associated, so the check is limited to comparing identifiers, excluding the non-matching base, and recording what was observed.
