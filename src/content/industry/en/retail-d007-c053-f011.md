# Channel receivables: database operations for operating reviews

## What the documents look like

Channel receivables collection begins after credit sales. The core records come from the financial system, bank receipts, distributor orders, and sales contracts. Day-to-day documents include the receivables aging table, collection letters, credit agreements, and shipment freeze notices. Operating reviews and exception reviews align these materials by the same distributor, the same aging period, and the same field before any payment status can be judged. Data updates on a weekly or monthly cycle. When report definitions drift away from the underlying documents, bad-debt judgments and freeze decisions lose their basis.

The database and operations layer must keep the aging table, bank receipts, and credit records traceable in storage and retrieval. Any connection configuration or index change needs to be rechecked against the same batch of business samples. The aging amount, credit limit, and freeze status come from different sources. The database stores each field value; it does not perform business verification on its own. Retrieval similarity affects recall ordering only. It does not constitute authorization or entity isolation. Libraries sharing the same vector model can be associated together; libraries on different vector models must first have their identifiers checked and non-matching items excluded, without running cross-model retrieval to compare missed recalls. An empty result can be checked against downstream design using the explicit paper input []. A fabricated rule cannot be claimed to return [] on retrieval.

## Constraints at the database operations step

Consider a hypothetical drill sample. Source text one: "In distributor A's receivables aging table as of March 31, 2025, the amount overdue more than 90 days is RMB 120,000. The credit agreement sets this distributor's credit limit at RMB 100,000." Source text two: "A bank receipt dated April 2, 2025 shows distributor A paid RMB 30,000; the purpose field does not note a corresponding invoice number." Draft sentence to be judged: "Distributor A has settled all overdue amounts and shipment can resume."

Check point: the receipt amount of RMB 30,000 is less than the overdue amount of RMB 120,000, and the purpose field does not point to a specific invoice. The draft sentence's claim of full settlement is not supported. The relationship between the credit limit of RMB 100,000 and the overdue amount of RMB 120,000 requires separate checking; no freeze can be inferred as executed from this alone.

The database stores the aging amount, credit limit, and freeze status as separate field values. It does not reconcile them into a settlement conclusion. Report queries must read from the main business store, not the log store. Index creation and cleanup at startup are governed by `SYNC_INDEX`; when it is off, indexes are maintained manually. Retrieval returns candidate records for review; it does not grant access to records outside the account's authorized scope.

## Three common errors

1. Treating retrieval similarity as a permission filter. Input is a vector retrieval request by distributor name. The comparison object is the returned records against the current account's accessible scope. Treating a high similarity score as direct viewing permission confuses recall with authorization. Executable check: record the distributor identifiers returned by retrieval, then compare them item by item against the manually confirmed authorized scope, recording only actual observations.

2. Writing a pending-review status as already approved. Input is a field value of "pending finance review" in the credit agreement. The comparison object is the agreement source text against the draft sentence "the limit is in effect." The two states differ, so the draft is not supported. Executable check: quote the field's source text from the agreement, compare it word by word against the draft sentence, and mark it as pending verification.

3. Offsetting a receipt amount directly against a named invoice. Input is the RMB 30,000 receipt and the RMB 120,000 aging amount. The comparison object is the receipt purpose field against the invoice number field. When the purpose is not noted, no correspondence can be made. Executable check: list the receipt amount, the aging amount, and the purpose field source text, confirm whether mutually exclusive values exist in the same field, and record the gap when missing.

## How to verify the setup

- Verification one: take hypothetical sample source text one and the draft sentence "Distributor A has settled all overdue amounts and shipment can resume." Input is the RMB 120,000 aging amount and the RMB 30,000 receipt. Compare the two amounts and the purpose field. The condition is met when the draft sentence's "settled" does not match the source amounts, and the judgment is not supported.

- Verification two: take hypothetical sample source text two. Input is the receipt purpose field "does not note a corresponding invoice number." The comparison object is the reference to "all overdue amounts" in the draft sentence. The condition is met when the purpose field cannot confirm the corresponding invoice, and the judgment is pending verification.

- Verification three: take the MONGODB_URI and MONGODB_LOG_URI items from the configuration table. Input is the value filled in per actual deployment. The comparison object is where aging records actually land versus where logs are written. The condition is met when the report query connection points to the main business store and does not misread the log store; if the connection target is unclear, record it as not supported. All of the above are paper comparisons. No test has been run, and no business action has been executed.
