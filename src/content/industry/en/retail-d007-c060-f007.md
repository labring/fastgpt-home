# Fruit-store procurement: conversation logs for operating reviews

## What the documents look like

Procurement work in a fresh fruit store produces four recurring record types: purchase orders, acceptance records, pesticide-residue test reports, and loss records. The underlying data comes from wholesale market pricing, supplier quotations, store sales, and test reports, and it updates daily or per batch. Compared with convenience-store prepared-food ordering, fruit procurement relies more on wholesale market pricing and grade-based acceptance, and it lacks a head-office standard-meal reference. The practical consequence for operating reports and exception reviews is that source documents differ substantially from batch to batch, grade decisions depend on on-site acceptance, and conversation logs and audit work must match the session identity of each inquiry, acceptance, or loss entry against the corresponding business document.

The conversation interface identifies a session by chatId, a single exchange within that session by dataId, and the application by appId. During an audit reconciliation, the target conversation's chatId is checked first against the business record, and sessions from different entry points are not combined. When run details are needed, detail is set according to the official contract and, in a non-streaming response, responseData is inspected; module status and the complete result are held in that field, while streaming mode distinguishes them through event. The fields actually returned by the log and interface are the basis for reconciliation, and business data that is not returned cannot be treated as fully retained. Identity fields in a test mode and in a formal call may differ, so input, history, and returned content are checked separately. The existence of a log does not mean a compliance audit is complete; reconciliation, retention, and compliance requirements are handled by the full business solution.

Assume a paper exercise sample. The source text has three sentences. First, a store fresh-produce supervisor submitted a purchase order on 12 March 2025 for a batch of Red Fuji apples, quantity 200 boxes, acceptance standard grade-one fruit. Second, an acceptance record of the same day states 198 boxes were actually received, grade judged as grade-one fruit, with a note that two boxes were crushed. Third, a pesticide-residue test report states the batch passed testing, report date 11 March 2025. The pending handover request is: "Please archive the purchase, acceptance, and test documents for the Red Fuji apples of 12 March together, and confirm that this batch has completed procurement settlement." The request covers two matters, archiving and settlement, and no settlement-completion record appears in the materials.

## Constraints at the conversation logs step

Session identity is the first constraint. A chatId locates one session under one application, and a dataId locates one exchange under that session. Reconciliation starts by confirming that the target conversation's chatId matches the business record. Sessions created through different entry points are counted separately; the conversation-list interface can restrict results by source, and source=api returns sessions created through the API rather than sessions on the page.

Run details are the second constraint. When intermediate values are required, detail is set to true. In a non-streaming response, module status and the complete result are held in responseData; in streaming mode, events distinguish them, with flowNodeStatus, answer, and flowResponses among the documented event values. The fields actually returned are the evidence. Business fields that are absent from the return remain unknown and are not treated as retained.

The third constraint concerns the boundary between collection and execution. An interactive node returns a display configuration containing type and params; entryNodeIds, memoryEdges, nodeOutputs, and nodeResponseId are internal runtime fields and are not returned. A form collects values. Business verification, approval, database writes, and external synchronization require their own downstream implementation and are not performed by the form or by retrieval. Retrieval is not authorization, and a similarity score is not a category, entity, or permission filter.

The fourth constraint concerns debugging and formal calls. Identity fields may differ between a test mode and a formal call, so input, history, and returned content are checked separately. The existence of a log does not by itself complete a compliance audit.

## Three common errors

1. Treating the existence of a log as a completed audit. The error is inferring that reconciliation and compliance are in place because conversation records were retained. Check: input the three sample documents and the request to "confirm that this batch has completed procurement settlement"; compare whether a settlement record exists in the materials; the observable result is that the materials do not state settlement, so the request remains pending verification.

2. Treating retrieval or a form as authorized execution. The error is reading the display configuration returned by an interactive node as business authorization. Check: input the sample handover request; compare the fact that interactive contains only type and params; the observable result is that the form collects values only, and business verification and writes require separate implementation.

3. Combining identities across sessions. The error is merging conversations under different chatIds into one business record. Check: input the session lists returned for two different chatIds; compare each chatId against the target record; the observable result is that entries that cannot be matched are excluded and the gap is recorded.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the three sample source sentences and the pending request. Compare whether the request's two items, archiving and settlement, each have corresponding evidence. The observable result is that archiving can proceed on the materials, settlement is not supported, and the evidence gap is retained.

2. Input one query request with detail=true. Compare whether the non-streaming response's responseData contains module status and the complete result. The observable result is that the presence of that field satisfies the reconciliation condition; business fields that are not returned are recorded as unknown.

3. Input the conversation-list request for the sample chatId. Compare the returned dataId against the target exchange. The observable result is that a match locates a single record; if the result is empty, the downstream design is checked against a clearly stated paper input, and no rule is assumed to return empty.
