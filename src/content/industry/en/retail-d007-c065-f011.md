# Livestream violation appeals: database operations for operating reviews

## What the documents look like

Livestream violation appeals address penalties that have already been issued. The working materials are grouped around four document types: the violation notice, the livestream recording, the product qualification file, and the appeal statement. Operating reports and exception reviews collect these documents by violation event so that a checkable evidence chain exists. The database operations role keeps those records traceable through storage, retrieval, backup, and recovery, without altering the penalty facts themselves.

The violation notice carries the penalized subject, the violation type, and the effective time. The livestream recording maps to a specific time segment. The product qualification file has a validity period. The appeal statement is written by hand. These four materials update irregularly, and each violation produces its own batch. Reports therefore aggregate by event, and exception review compares subject, time, and field values within the same violation event. Merging across events discards the basis for a determination.

## Constraints at the database operations step

The materials fall into two storage classes. Business source records enter the main business database; recordings and qualification files enter object storage. The two are linked by a record identifier. A vector retrieval backend only performs semantic recall. It does not perform permission filtering and does not perform entity isolation. A retrieved recording segment does not mean that segment has been authorized for this appeal.

Hypothetical drill sample: on 12 March 2025 a shop receives a platform violation notice stating that the penalized subject is "the livestream room of Shop A," the violation type is "missing product qualification," and the effective time is "13 March 2025, 00:00." On the same day a customer service record states: "Received the merchant's supplemental quality inspection report scan, pending compliance review." The recording file is labelled with the time range "12 March 2025, 20:00 to 21:00." The draft sentence to be judged is: "This appeal has submitted the quality inspection report and completed compliance review." Compared with the customer service record, the review status remains pending review; the draft sentence writes pending review as completed, so it is unsupported.

## Three common errors

1. Treating a retrieval hit as authorization. A query on the recording time range in the sample returns several segments, and those segments are then treated as approved for the appeal. Authorization status must be checked separately; retrieval returns candidates only.
2. Writing pending review as completed. The draft sentence "has submitted the quality inspection report and completed compliance review" compared with the customer service record "pending compliance review" shows mutually exclusive values in the same field. This is unsupported and cannot be written into the report as a completed fact.
3. Jointly associating libraries under different vector models. Knowledge bases under different vector models cannot be jointly associated. Check the model identifier first, exclude non-matching ones, and record the actual observation. Do not run cross-model retrieval to compare missed recall.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input: the violation notice text, the customer service record text, and the draft sentence from the hypothetical sample. Compare: the review status in the draft sentence against the status field in the customer service record. Observable result: when the two values agree, the sentence may be retained; when the values are mutually exclusive, mark the sentence unsupported and record the evidence gap.
2. Input: a time-range query on the sample recording, plus one clearly unrelated query. Compare: the identifiers of returned segments against the sample event identifier. Observable result: record truthfully whether the relevant segment appears and whether the unrelated query is empty; do not guarantee a first hit or full recall.
3. Input: a paper empty result `[]`, and check the downstream report's display design under an empty result. Compare: whether the downstream distinguishes "no record" from "not retrieved." Observable result: if the downstream presents the empty result directly as "no violation," that design needs correction; actual runtime results depend on observation.

The checks above are paper-based reasoning. They were not executed in a real environment, and no deployment, approval, or recovery action occurred.
