# Chain-store site selection: database operations for operating reviews

## What the documents look like

Site selection and store opening work happens before a store trades. The core materials are lease and engineering delivery documents rather than daily sales records. For this category the working set is the site evaluation form, the letter of intent for the lease, the renovation budget, and the opening schedule. These materials update irregularly, per store, not on a daily or per-order cycle. Operating reports and exception reviews need to connect fields from those documents to the store record: a rent-to-sales ratio estimate for a given store and period, a renovation budget amount, an opening schedule milestone.

Document states differ widely and must be read as document states, not as business authorization. A site evaluation form may sit in draft or submitted. A letter of intent may sit at intent, pending review, or signed. A renovation budget may sit at submitted for review or confirmed. The database operations step has to keep these fields readable, comparable, and traceable, while treating a document state as a document state and not as an approval outcome.

## Constraints at the database operations step

Database connection settings are checked against the chosen deployment plan. Connection addresses and credentials are read from the actual deployment, not inferred from variable names. The business record store and the vector retrieval backend are checked separately; a vector store connection address and a business database connection address belong to different services. Connection pool limits, error rates, and latency are observed against the actual workload, and untested concurrency thresholds are not copied from elsewhere. Backup and recovery are verified separately: a successful backup does not mean a usable restore, so a sample and a record that can be compared are retained. After a configuration change, the same business sample is read again; a running process does not replace a data check.

Hypothetical paper exercise sample. A chain headquarters development team submits a Site Evaluation Form. The subject is a third-floor unit in a new-district shopping centre. The object is a planned directly operated store. The status is submitted, pending review. The date range is 1 March 2026 to 31 March 2026. The rent-to-sales ratio estimate is 18%. The same batch contains a Letter of Intent for the Lease with status intent and no stated signing date. One draft sentence under review reads: "The lease terms for this unit have been signed, and the renovation schedule proceeds at an 18% rent-to-sales ratio." The source text being compared reads: "Site Evaluation Form status is submitted, pending review; rent-to-sales ratio estimate: 18%. Letter of Intent for the Lease status: intent, no stated signing date." The draft sentence treats the intent state as signed and treats an estimate as a confirmed condition for proceeding. Both statements lack support in the source text.

## Three common errors

1. Treating the intent status of the Letter of Intent for the Lease as signed. The input is the letter-of-intent status field. The comparison object is the value signed. The observable result is that the status field remains intent, and the signed wording in the draft sentence lacks support.
2. Treating the 18% rent-to-sales ratio estimate as a confirmed condition for proceeding. The input is the estimate field on the site evaluation form. The comparison object is the value confirmed or approved. The observable result is that the form status is submitted, pending review, and the estimate is only a number; the condition for proceeding lacks support.
3. Using vector retrieval similarity in place of a business database field check. The input is similar text returned by vector retrieval. The comparison object is the field value on that unit record in the business database. The observable result is that when subject or period does not match, the record cannot be used to decide lease status or the estimate.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the source text of the Site Evaluation Form from the hypothetical sample and the draft sentence. Compare the signed wording in the draft sentence with the intent status in the source. The observable result is a mismatch, and the draft sentence lacks support. Support would require the source status field to read signed.
2. Input the rent-to-sales ratio field and the status field of the same Site Evaluation Form. Compare 18% with submitted, pending review. The observable result is that the estimate exists while the condition for proceeding lacks support. Support would require the status field to read confirmed.
3. Input the similar text returned by vector retrieval and the business database record. Compare subject, object, and period for consistency. The observable result is that a record with mismatched identifiers is excluded, and no cross-model association comparison is performed. A business database record that can be read with matching fields meets the check condition.
