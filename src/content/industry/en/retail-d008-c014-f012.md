# Convenience fresh-food orders: document chunking for multilingual work

## What the documents look like

Convenience-store fresh food is ordered by the store at high frequency against short shelf lives, and it is handled outside any revenue-sharing arrangement. The working materials are fresh-food order forms, damage write-off forms, near-expiry handling sheets, and cold-chain temperature records. These materials carry a store, a shift, a date, and a product object, so parsing and chunking need to preserve a document identity and version that can point back to the source text.

In cross-border material and multilingual retrieval, the same batch of order forms and write-off forms may appear in different languages. Semantic retrieval carries cross-language understanding, so a Chinese question can match an English knowledge point. If chunking separates a shelf-life condition from the product line it constrains, a retrieved fragment will lack its limiting scope. The brief records a daily and per-shift update rhythm; after source material changes, the actual training and index state needs checking, because a successful upload does not mean retrieval has updated.

## Constraints at the document chunking step

Segmentation is checked against the structure of each specific material: a condition, its explanation, and the content it constrains stay in one chunk. One set of original data can map to multiple vectors, and the `indexes` field records the corresponding vector IDs; a data set is returned if any one of its vectors is recalled. Original data and retrieval indexes are distinct, and adding indexes does not change which original data a record belongs to.

Hypothetical drill sample: on 10 March 2025 a convenience-store regional supervisor receives a Chinese order form reading "Store A ordered on 11 March 2025: 40 sandwiches, shelf life to 13 March 2025; 60 rice balls, shelf life to 12 March 2025." The same day an English write-off form is received reading "Store A, March 10, 2025: 5 sandwiches discarded, expired March 9, 2025." The handover request awaiting a determination is: "Using the two materials above, confirm whether Store A's 11 March 2025 order includes rice balls, and state whether the write-off has been approved." Whether the write-off has been approved is not stated in the materials and remains to be verified; retrieval or a form does not constitute authorized execution.

Full-text retrieval suits exact queries such as numbers, models, and proper nouns; semantic retrieval suits natural-language phrasing. Hybrid retrieval combines multiple result paths through `RRF`, and `Rerank` re-sorts candidate text. The minimum relevance value takes effect only in semantic retrieval or with result re-ranking; it is a filter threshold and does not perform permission or entity isolation.

## Three common errors

1. Treating a successful upload as retrieval already updated. Input is the English write-off form uploaded on 10 March 2025; compare the upload time against the training and index state record and observe whether retrieval returns that write-off content. If the state has not updated, the retrieved result lacks that content and the matter remains to be verified.
2. Using minimum relevance to exclude unrelated businesses or unauthorized records. Input is the sample retrieval above; compare returned content against the permission scope and observe whether cross-store or unauthorized material appears. Relevance is a filter threshold and does not perform entity or permission isolation.
3. Asserting that the write-off has been approved. Input is the handover request awaiting determination; compare the approval wording in the request against the source text and observe whether the source states an approving party and status. The source does not state approval, so the point is unsupported and cannot be judged approved or rejected.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the order-form text "Store A ordered on 11 March 2025: 40 sandwiches, shelf life to 13 March 2025; 60 rice balls, shelf life to 12 March 2025." Compare whether the retrieved fragment contains the store, date, product, and shelf life together. If the returned fragment lacks the shelf life or the product object, chunking does not meet the condition.
2. Input the English write-off text "Store A, March 10, 2025: 5 sandwiches discarded, expired March 9, 2025." Compare whether retrieval hits that write-off content and check whether the returned fragment keeps the store, date, quantity, and status. If it does not hit, record the matter as to be verified and do not infer a cause.
3. Input the handover request "Using the two materials above, confirm whether Store A's 11 March 2025 order includes rice balls, and state whether the write-off has been approved." Compare the approval wording in the request against the source text and observe whether the source states approval. The source does not state it, so the determination is unsupported; a form and retrieval do not constitute approved execution.
