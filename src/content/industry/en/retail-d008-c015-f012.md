# Letter-of-credit documents: document chunking for multilingual work

## What the documents look like

Letter-of-credit examination and presentation serves the payment conditions set by a bank, and the working standard is consistency across documents rather than tax matching. The main materials are issuing-bank notices, UCP600 clauses, freight forwarder bills of lading, and bank presentation records. Daily handling covers the letter of credit, commercial invoice, bill of lading, insurance policy, and draft. These materials update per shipment. Document examiners, bank international settlement staff, and exporters all work with them.

The same batch of documents may carry Chinese, English, and the language of the importing country. Retrieval therefore has to work across languages, and semantic retrieval supports cross-language matching, so a Chinese question can match an English knowledge point. That capability depends on how well the vector model was trained. Full-text retrieval suits exact queries such as numbers, models, and proper nouns, while hybrid retrieval merges multiple result paths through RRF. Material length varies widely, from a draft number to a full letter-of-credit clause, and the way content is split directly affects retrieval quality. Errors in this work lead to refusal of payment, deductions, and delayed collection.

## Constraints at the document chunking step

Cross-border materials and multilingual retrieval require parsing followed by chunking into semantically complete units. Letter-of-credit clauses, invoice line items, and bill-of-lading remarks each have different structures. A condition and the content it constrains must not be separated. One group of source data can map to multiple indexes, and source data must be stored separately from retrieval indexes.

The minimum relevance value is a filter threshold that takes effect only in semantic retrieval or when result reranking is used. It is a filtering threshold, not a final ranking rule. The reference limit controls how many tokens of content a single search may cite at most. Retrieval does not perform permission or entity isolation, and a similarity score does not constitute an authorization judgment. Hybrid retrieval results cannot be filtered directly by similarity, so reranking is usually needed to reorder and then filter by the rerank score. RRF is a fusion process for combining result paths, not a switch that is turned on.

Hypothetical paper exercise sample: a letter of credit states "port of loading Shanghai, port of discharge Hamburg, latest shipment date 30 June 2025, partial shipment allowed." The commercial invoice shows "port of loading: Shanghai; port of discharge: Hamburg; shipment date: 28 June 2025; partial shipment: allowed." The draft sentence to be assessed is "this batch was shipped from Shanghai to Hamburg on 28 June 2025 and meets the letter-of-credit shipment requirements." The question is whether this draft sentence is consistent with the letter-of-credit and invoice originals.

## Three common errors

1. Treating a retrieval score as an authorization judgment. Input the draft sentence, retrieve the letter-of-credit clause, compare the scores, and observe whether a high score is taken to mean the draft sentence has passed examination. Retrieval returns related content only; it does not judge whether documents are consistent, and the examination conclusion requires manual comparison against the originals.
2. Treating a successful upload as an updated index. Input the revised invoice original, compare the completed upload action against what retrieval actually returns, and observe whether the result is still the old version. The documentation states that data training status must be confirmed; a successful upload cannot be taken directly as an updated retrieval index.
3. Treating minimum relevance as an entity filter. Input the draft sentence with minimum relevance set, compare returned results under different thresholds, and observe whether raising the threshold excludes other letters of credit or unrelated business records. Minimum relevance is a filter threshold and does not perform permission or entity isolation.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the letter-of-credit original and the draft sentence from the hypothetical sample, and compare whether the draft sentence "shipped from Shanghai to Hamburg on 28 June 2025" matches the letter of credit on the same subject, same period, and same field: latest shipment date 30 June 2025, port of loading Shanghai, port of discharge Hamburg. The expectation is that retrieval returns the letter-of-credit shipment clause and the invoice shipment date. If the returned content lacks the shipment date or port fields, record that the needed evidence was not recalled.
2. Input the revised invoice original and compare whether the retrieved content matches the revised text. The expectation is that retrieval hits the new shipment date. If the old date is still returned, record that the index was not updated and check the training status.
3. Input an empty-result scenario using the paper input `[]` to check the downstream design. The expectation is that downstream treats an empty reference as no content and does not automatically produce an examination conclusion. If downstream interprets the empty result as "no discrepancies," record that this branch needs manual intervention.

Libraries across different vector models cannot be jointly associated. Check identifiers first and exclude non-matching ones; do not run cross-model retrieval to compare missed recalls. Image retrieval requires corresponding image input and model capability, and reranking requires a model and must be enabled as configured. A pending review does not demonstrate the absence of operating permission. Signing, reaching cooperation, and contract effectiveness are three distinct facts. Fields not stated remain unknown, and authorization or effectiveness clauses must not be supplied.
