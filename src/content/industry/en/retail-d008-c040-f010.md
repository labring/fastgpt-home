# Customer-service knowledge: citation tracing for multilingual work

## What the documents look like

Knowledge-base maintenance for customer-service inquiries sits at the stage of preparing question-and-answer material. It does not approve refunds or returns. The working materials are FAQ entries, reply templates, product specification tables and escalation tickets. They come from the customer-service system, product detail pages, after-sales issue statistics and quality-sampling checks.

In cross-border and multilingual retrieval, these materials appear as Chinese and English FAQ entries held in one knowledge base, product specification tables split by market version, and escalation tickets that record multilingual conversation summaries. Citation tracing requires that each answer can be traced back to a specific document entry and its version. Material updates follow a weekly or monthly rhythm, and retrieved results need to correspond to the currently effective version.

Multilingual retrieval relies on the cross-language understanding of semantic search. The documentation states that a Chinese question can match an English knowledge point. That ability depends on how well the vector model was trained, and its precision is not stable. This matters for citation tracing because a match across languages still has to be checked against the original wording rather than accepted on the strength of the similarity score alone.

## Constraints at the citation tracing step

The knowledge-base search node can associate one or more knowledge bases that share the same vector model. Knowledge bases built on different vector models cannot be associated together. The output is an array of cited content whose length may be zero, and an empty array does not mean the workflow has errored out or stopped.

Tracing a citation means comparing the answer with the cited original text entry by entry, confirming which knowledge base, which data record and which version the citation came from. Retrieval by itself does not constitute authorization and does not perform permission or entity isolation. The minimum relevance value is a filtering threshold; it is not used to exclude unauthorized records. Hybrid retrieval merges multiple result paths through RRF, and the rerank model produces a 0-1 score used for filtering. These mechanisms affect ordering; they do not change source attribution. Full-text retrieval suits precise queries such as numbers, model codes and proper nouns, while semantic retrieval suits natural-language questions. In cross-border scenarios both query types can appear at once.

Hypothetical drill sample: one English FAQ entry in the knowledge base reads: "Standard shipping to Germany takes 7-12 business days after dispatch. Delivery estimates exclude customs clearance time." A second entry in the same knowledge base, a Chinese reply template, reads: "Standard logistics to Germany is delivered within 7 to 12 business days after dispatch, and customs clearance time is not counted in the above timeframe." (English rendering) A draft sentence awaiting judgment reads: "German orders are delivered within 7 days after dispatch, and customs clearance delays are borne by the replying party." (English rendering) The materials available for checking contain only the two original texts above. They do not state who bears customs-clearance responsibility, and they do not contain the wording "within 7 days."

## Three common errors

1. Treating a retrieval hit as a passed authorization check. Retrieval only returns cited content; it does not verify whether the asker may view a given record, and it does not exclude unauthorized records. Check: submit a question that spans knowledge bases, compare the source-base identifiers in the returned citations against the list of bases the asker is authorized to access, and observe whether any citation comes from an unauthorized base.

2. Treating minimum relevance as an entity or permission filter. The value is a 0-1 filtering threshold; it does not distinguish business categories and does not identify record ownership. Check: run the same question under different relevance starting values, compare the source identifiers of the returned entries, and observe whether unrelated business records appear, confirming that the filter carries no isolation function.

3. Judging an empty citation array as a workflow failure. The documentation states that the citation length may be zero and the output path still continues. Check: carry the downstream node with the paper input [] and compare whether the downstream step continues to execute, observing whether the empty array interrupts the flow.

## How to verify the setup

1. For the hypothetical sample's English FAQ original text, enter the Chinese question "How long does delivery to Germany take after dispatch" (English rendering). Compare the original sentence in the returned citation against the English text above, and observe whether the citation contains "7-12 business days" and the statement that customs clearance time is excluded. If the returned content contains "within 7 days" or a statement that customs clearance is borne by the seller, the citation does not match the original.

2. For the hypothetical sample's Chinese reply-template original text, enter the same question and compare the Chinese original in the returned citation, observing whether it agrees with "7 to 12 business days, customs clearance time not counted" (English rendering). If the returned content introduces a responsibility statement, that content is not supported by the original text.

3. For the draft sentence awaiting judgment, "German orders are delivered within 7 days after dispatch, and customs clearance delays are borne by the replying party" (English rendering), compare field by field against the two original texts. The source gives a range of 7-12 business days, while the draft promises delivery within 7 days without stating the calendar. The supplied wording does not support that tighter guarantee; these statements alone do not establish mutually exclusive times. The responsibility field is not stated in the original; the draft assigns responsibility, which is unsupported. The observed conclusion is that the time guarantee is unsupported by the supplied wording, the responsibility statement awaits verification, and an effective version stating customs-clearance responsibility must be added before a judgment can be made.
