# Warehouse picking and packing: upgrades for operations handover

## What the documents look like

Warehouse picking, checking, and packing handles outbound orders. It differs from inbound receiving and shelving, which handle supplier arrivals. The main data sources are the WMS, the order system, the electronic waybill platform, and weighing equipment. Daily documents are wave picking lists, checking records, waybills, and packaging consumable sheets. Update frequency is per order and hourly. The consequences of error are wrong or missing shipments, damaged parcels, and after-sales compensation.

Operations permissions and handover management concerns the correspondence among three things: handover materials, operation requests, and authorization status. At the deployment and upgrade step, a version change may affect field meaning or process branches. Whether a material is in a handover-ready state depends on its current status marker in the system, not on a retrieval hit or a completed form. Retrieval and similarity do not perform authorization decisions. Forms collect values. Business verification, approval, database writes, and external synchronization each require their own downstream implementation.

## Constraints at the upgrades step

The V4.17.2 upgrade notes list two manually triggered migration scripts: historical chunk training task migration (`20261008_migrate_chunk_training`) and knowledge base rebuild status migration (`20261009_migrate_dataset_rebuild_status`). Both are manually triggered, do not block service startup, and require that all old-version FastGPT App and Pro instances have fully stopped and old task leases have expired before execution. The second depends on the first having completed. These are deployment-order constraints. They do not map directly to authorization decisions in warehouse handover, and they cannot be used to infer the status of handover materials.

Hypothetical paper exercise sample: a warehouse fulfillment operator, during an upgrade rehearsal, prepares to check the handover status of one wave picking list. Source text (hypothetical): "Wave picking list WB-2026-0315-07. The picker has completed picking. The checker has not signed. The checking record is empty. The waybill has not been printed. The packaging consumable sheet has not been generated." Proposed judgment sentence (hypothetical): "This wave has completed checking handover and may proceed to packing." The fields needed for the judgment are the checking record signature status and the waybill print status. The source text shows an empty checking record and an unprinted waybill. The proposed sentence's claim of completed checking handover has no supporting value in those fields. This sample is used only to demonstrate a paper comparison method. It does not represent any real document or any executed business action.

## Three common errors

1. Reading an empty checking record as completed checking. Input: the sample source text "the checking record is empty." Comparison object: the proposed sentence "completed checking handover." Observable result: the field has no signature value, and the proposed sentence is unsupported. This error treats missing evidence as positive evidence.

2. Combining an unprinted waybill and an ungenerated packaging consumable sheet into an inference that packing may proceed. Input: the two statuses in the sample source text. Comparison object: "may proceed to packing" in the proposed sentence. Observable result: neither prerequisite status is met, and the proposed sentence is unsupported. This error is a cross-field combined inference.

3. Treating completion of an upgrade script as equivalent to handover authorization taking effect. Input: the execution status of the manually triggered scripts in the upgrade notes. Comparison object: the authorization conclusion in the sample proposed sentence. Observable result: script status and handover authorization are different facts, and the former is not evidence for the latter. This error substitutes one fact type for another.
