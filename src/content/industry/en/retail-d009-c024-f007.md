# Brand concession operations: conversation logs for operations handover

## What the documents look like

A brand concession inside a shopping mall differs from a brand-operated street store in one structural way: the mall handles unified cashiering and settles on a concession-rate basis. Sales receipts originate from the mall POS, while the brand ERP and finance system keep separate records, and the monthly settlement statement has to be checked line by line against mall data. Everyday documents in this setting include the joint-operation concession-rate contract, mall activity letters, sales receipts, and the monthly settlement statement. Data updates daily and monthly.

For conversation logs and audit, these traits matter because session identity, run detail, and quotation records must line up with those business materials to support operations permission and handover checks. The presence of a log does not by itself show that reconciliation closed or that a compliance audit finished. Reconciliation, retention, and compliance duties belong to a complete business plan implemented elsewhere; this page only addresses what the conversation-log step can and cannot support.

## Constraints at the conversation logs step

Conversation identity has to be checked against the target conversation and the business record using chatId. Sessions from different entry points are not added together. When run detail from the conversation interface is needed, set detail according to the official contract and inspect responseData; with detail=false, intermediate values are not returned. When comparing logs with fields the interface actually returns, business data that was not returned cannot be assumed to be fully retained. Identity fields in test mode may differ from a production call, so input, history, and returned content are checked separately. A value collected through retrieval or a form does not constitute authorization; business verification, approval, database writes, and external synchronization each need their own downstream implementation.

Hypothetical paper exercise sample: during a handover at a brand concession, the store manager provides a paper record stating: "From March 1 to March 31, 2025, this concession had 120 sales receipts at the mall POS; the brand ERP recorded 118 in the same period, leaving 2 differences pending check; the joint-operation concession-rate contract sets the rate at 18%; the monthly settlement statement has not yet been received." A draft sentence is also submitted for review: "The handover is complete, all sales data is consistent, and concession fees are settled." The comparison object is the wording of that draft sentence against the source material above. The material shows only 2 differences pending check and a monthly settlement statement not yet received, so the claims "all consistent" and "settled" are not supported by the material.

## Configuration and implementation checks

| Configuration item | Assumed starting point or check item for this exercise | Reason and comparison method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| chatId use | Each conversation entry in the handover sample uses its own chatId | Check whether sessions from different entries are combined; compare whether each entry's chatId is unique |
| detail setting | Set to true when run detail is needed | Compare against the detail=false return to confirm whether responseData appears |
| Log field check | Check moduleName, tokens, quoteList and other fields the interface actually returns | Compare field by field against business materials; record missing fields as gaps |
| Test versus production identity | Check input, history, and return separately for test mode and production calls | Compare whether identity fields match; when they differ, record each separately |
| Handover authorization state | Check the difference count and settlement statement status in the material | Pending check and not received do not constitute authorized execution; record as pending verification |
| Retrieval scope | Check whether the target retrieval scope contains the needed material | When material is missing, record the gap; do not assume it was fully retained |

## Three common errors

1. Treating the existence of a log as a completed compliance audit. A log records conversation content; reconciliation, retention, and compliance requirements are implemented through a complete business plan, and the two do not substitute for each other.
2. Treating a retrieved or form-collected value as authorization. Retrieval results and form inputs only supply information; business verification, approval, and database writes each need their own downstream implementation.
3. Writing a pending-check state as completed. In the sample, 2 differences are pending check and the monthly settlement statement has not been received, while the draft sentence says "all consistent" and "settled," which is an unsupported judgment.

## How to verify the setup

1. Input the source material above and the draft sentence, compare "all consistent" in the draft against "2 differences pending check" in the material, and observe whether an unsupported judgment appears. The expectation is unsupported; no actual test is claimed.
2. Input conversation records with two separate entry chatIds, compare each record's chatId against its corresponding business material, and observe whether the sessions are combined. The expectation is that they are recorded separately; sessions from different entries are not merged into one count.
3. Input the returns from a detail=true request and a detail=false request, compare whether responseData appears, and observe the field difference. The expectation is that responseData appears with detail=true and does not appear with detail=false; specific fields follow the actual return.
