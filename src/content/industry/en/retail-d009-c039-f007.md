# Tmall flagship operations: conversation logs for operations handover

## What the documents look like

Tmall flagship store daily operations revolve around product publishing pages, brand authorization letters, delivery commitment records, and after-sales work orders. Brand authorization and flagship store identity carry particular weight on this platform. The conversation logs and audit step draws on the Tmall merchant backend, the Tmall rules center, ERP order tables, and customer service chat records. Brand e-commerce departments, Tmall store managers, and agency operators frequently hand operations between one another, and during a handover the authorization state of each material, action, and request needs item-by-item checking. Customer service chat records are produced per order, and a daily update rhythm causes log volume to accumulate continuously, so checking work has to locate entries by conversation identity rather than pooling sessions from different entry points into one count.

For this task, conversation logs serve to reconstruct which chatId carried a given round of dialogue, which dataId identifies it, and which appId made the call. The existence of a log does not amount to a completed compliance audit; reconciliation, retention, and compliance requirements are handled separately by a complete business solution. Retrieval results and form-collected values require their own downstream implementation for business verification, approval, database writes, and external synchronization. A similarity score reflects retrieval relevance and does not perform business entity or permission filtering.

Two or three industry facts genuinely affect this task. First, brand authorization and flagship store identity are emphasized on Tmall, so a handover review has to distinguish the authorization subject, the authorized scope, and the validity period rather than treating a storefront name as proof of authority. Second, logs arrive per order on a daily cadence, so checks must be anchored to a specific chatId and dataId instead of an aggregate. Third, multiple parties—brand e-commerce department, store manager, agency operator—may share operational access, so an entry point difference between API-created sessions and page sessions matters when locating records.

## Constraints at the conversation logs step

During an operations handover, the log is used to establish which chatId carried a round of dialogue, which dataId marks it, and which appId invoked it. A log record existing is not the same as a completed compliance audit, and reconciliation, retention, and compliance obligations are implemented by a separate full business solution. Values collected through retrieval or forms need their own downstream implementation for business verification, approval, database writes, and external synchronization. A similarity score is a retrieval relevance measure and does not act as a business entity or permission filter.

Hypothetical drill sample, clearly labelled: an agency handover for a Tmall flagship store. The source material reads: "The brand authorization letter states that the authorizing party is A, the authorized party is B, the authorized scope is limited to maintenance of the Tmall flagship store product publishing page, and the validity period runs to 31 March 2025." It also reads: "The delivery commitment record states that the 48-hour delivery commitment is performed by B." The sentence to be assessed is: "From April 2025, B continues to maintain the product publishing page in the name of the authorizing party." In assessment, the period asserted in the draft sentence exceeds the validity period stated in the authorization letter; the authorized scope covers only product publishing page maintenance; and no renewal or new authorization after April is stated. The claim of use for that period is not supported.

This is a paper assessment only. No test, deployment, approval, refund, or other business action has taken place. Missing evidence remains missing: the authorization letter states no renewal clause, and the record states no post-March arrangement.

## Configuration and implementation checks

The table below is a hypothetical starting point for this exercise. The selection reasons and comparison objects are given alongside; these are not official default values.

| Configuration item | Hypothetical starting point for this exercise | Reason for selection and comparison method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| appId | The application designated by the handover sample | Compare with the request body.appId and the key-associated value, and take the highest-priority one |
| chatId | The conversation identifier in the handover sample | Compare with the chatId in the log record to confirm the same session |
| dataId | The conversation identifier to be checked | Compare with the dataId in the conversation list to locate a single conversation |
| detail | true | Set when run details are needed; check whether responseData is returned |
| loadCustomFeedbacks | true | Set when custom feedback is read; check whether the returned field includes it |
| source | api | Set when retrieving API-created sessions, to distinguish them from page sessions |

A chatId is a session identifier under one application; a dataId identifies one conversation under a session. The request-side appId priority is body.appId, then `<apiKey>-<appId>`, then the appId associated with the apikey under the older adaptation. The `model` and `temperature` fields passed in the request are ineffective because orchestration determines them. Actual token consumption is not returned; where needed, set `detail=true` and calculate the `tokens` value in `responseData` manually. Clearing application sessions only clears sessions created through the API key and does not clear sessions from online use, share links, or other sources. When a workflow contains an interaction node, the interface returns the deepest user-facing interaction node, and the returned `interactive` is a display configuration containing only `type` and `params`; internal runtime fields such as `entryNodeIds`, `memoryEdges`, `nodeOutputs`, and `nodeResponseId` are not returned.

## Three common errors

1. Treating an identical chatId as identical business identity. Input: two session records from different entry points. Compare: the chatId against the business subject field. Observable result: whether the two records point to the same authorized party. A chatId is a session identifier under one application and does not by itself establish the business entity.

2. Treating the responseData returned under detail=true as complete business retention. Input: one call response. Compare: the returned fields against the list of fields the business requires. Observable result: whether any missing item is marked as unknown rather than presumed retained.

3. Treating a similarity score as a permission filter result. Input: the score returned by retrieval. Compare: that score against the authorized scope. Observable result: whether the value reflects relevance only, with no bearing on whether a record is within the authorization boundary.

## How to verify the setup

1. Take the hypothetical sample source material and the draft sentence. Input the draft sentence together with the authorization letter's validity period field. Compare whether the asserted period falls within the authorization validity period. The condition is satisfied when the draft sentence is not supported because the period exceeds the stated validity, and no renewal or new authorization is stated for the later period.

2. Take one call request. Input the appId and chatId. Compare the chatId recorded in the log against the requested chatId. The condition is satisfied when the two match and the corresponding dataId can be located. Where the chatId is absent, the request does not use the FastGPT context function and builds context entirely from the supplied messages.

3. Take a response with detail=true. Input responseData. Compare the module fields within it against the list of fields the business requires. The condition is satisfied when fields that were not returned remain marked as unknown, with no presumption that they were retained. Where `detail=true` is set and the response is in stream mode, intermediate values are distinguished by `event`; in non-stream mode they are held in `responseData`.
