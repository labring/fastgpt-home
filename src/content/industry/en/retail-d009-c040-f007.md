# Customer-service knowledge: conversation logs for operations handover

## What the documents look like

Knowledge-base maintenance for customer-service enquiries is the preparation of question-and-answer material that sits ahead of the conversation. It does not approve refunds. The recurring documents are FAQ entries, reply templates, product parameter tables and escalation tickets, drawn from the customer-service system, product detail pages, after-sales problem statistics and quality spot checks. The supplied brief gives a weekly or monthly update rhythm. That rhythm does not change how this page's feature behaves, so no synchronisation mechanism is described here.

The distinguishing point of the conversation-logs and audit step is that knowledge-base work produces the wording of an answer, while a log records how that wording was actually reached in a live session. The public interface document shows that a session is located by three identifiers in layers: appId, chatId and dataId. Records can be read through the endpoints for listing sessions, listing the dialogues inside a session, and retrieving the run detail of a single dialogue. During an operations handover, the material, the action and the authorisation state of the request all need to be stated together. A retrieval action and a form entry are not authorisation to execute anything.

## Constraints at the conversation logs step

Conversation identity has to be checked against the target dialogue and the business record with chatId. Sessions arriving through different entry points are not added together. When run detail is needed, detail is set according to the official contract and responseData is inspected; business data that was not returned cannot be treated as fully retained. A test-mode call and a production call may carry different identity fields, so the input, the history and the returned content are each checked separately. Reconciliation, retention and compliance requirements belong to a complete business plan implemented elsewhere. The existence of a log does not mean a compliance audit has been completed.

Hypothetical paper exercise. A knowledge-base operator writes in a handover note: "This week the 'seven-day no-reason return' FAQ entry was updated to the latest wording; the colleague taking over should answer according to it." The source material supplied for checking reads: "The current version of the FAQ entry 'seven-day no-reason return' was modified by the customer-service supervisor this Wednesday, and its status is published; the handover note is dated this Thursday." The sentence to be assessed is the handover note above. In this exercise the modifying party, the object, the status and the date range are all stated, so the note can be compared with the material. No real customer or identity number is involved.

## Three common errors

1. Treating a retrieval action or a form entry as authorisation to execute. In the sample the handover note describes an update action and does not state who approved the handover. Reading "the colleague taking over should answer according to it" as an approved handover turns a pending instruction into an authorisation result. Check: the input is the handover note text; compare whether an authorising party and an authorisation scope appear in it; when they do not appear, record the item as pending verification.

2. Mixing the identity fields of a test-mode call with those of a production call. The sample does not give a call mode. Filling a production record directly with appId and chatId returned by a test call mixes identity fields. Check: the input is the returned fields of the two calls; compare whether appId and chatId agree; when they differ, record them separately and do not merge them.

3. Using a similarity or relevance value in place of a permission judgement. No score or similarity value appears in the sample. Inferring from a retrieval score that a record is accessible to the receiving colleague goes beyond the evidence. Check: the input is the score field returned by retrieval; compare whether any permission field exists; when no permission field exists, keep the question unknown and do not judge accessibility from it.

## How to verify the setup

1. Check the handover note against the FAQ entry. The input is the handover note text and the FAQ entry text from the hypothetical exercise. Compare the modifying party, the object, the status and the date. When all four correspond, record them as consistent; when any one is missing, record a gap and do not supply an authorisation clause to close it.

2. Check the fields returned in the run detail. The input is a single-dialogue run-detail request with detail set to true. Compare moduleName, moduleType, runningTime and similar fields in the returned responseData. When a field is present, record its actual value; when a field is absent, record that it was not returned and do not assert that retention is complete.

3. Check the read range of the dialogue records. The input is offset and pageSize in the dialogue-list request. Compare the returned list with total. When the relation between the number of list entries and total is clear, record it; when total is greater than the number of list entries, record a partial read and do not infer the content of the remaining records.

All three checks use the hypothetical sample as input and describe expected results as comparison conditions. They were not actually executed. Retrieval and relevance do not carry permission or entity isolation. Libraries built on different vector models cannot be associated together; the identifiers are checked first and non-matching libraries are excluded. Empty-result handling can be checked against a paper input of an explicit empty list to examine the downstream design, without claiming that a fictional rule search necessarily returns an empty list.
