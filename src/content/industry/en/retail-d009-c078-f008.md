# Chain-store site selection: operations handover with workflow routing

## What the documents look like

For operations handover in Chain-store site selection, this page examines FastGPT workflow routing. Handover site plans, measurement permission and custodians without treating document transfer as premises delivery. The examples are hypothetical.

## Constraints at the workflow routing step

This page invents a site-document handover. A candidate has an older floor plan, a survey note says the back area needs redrawing, the landlord permits measurement only, and a custodian retains the newer original. Collect location, plan version and each author. A successor chooses to request the plan or review measurement permission. The handover concerns tasks and source leads, not leased premises. Permission to measure establishes no tenancy or renovation consent. An available contact establishes no actual original-file access. No real address, area or rent is supplied.

## Three common errors

- If an older plan establishes usable space despite the back-area note, compare plan version, the affected area and actual citations to locate outdated coverage.

- If measurement permission becomes lease or renovation consent, inspect the permitted action and subject. Entry to measure expands no later-use authority.

- If naming a custodian becomes delivery of the new plan, inspect request records, file receipt and original access. A name identifies a contact only.

## How to verify the setup

Follow plan-request and permission routes separately, retaining plan identity, area restrictions and pending actions actually received downstream. A new plan replaces only corresponding drawings; measurement permission retains its original scope. Missing access remains an open task without pretending the drawing was read. Responsible staff retain lease, construction and resource-authorization reviews. The workflow grants no account and transfers no premises. Text descriptions of drawings may test handoff without establishing image recognition or completed field measurement.
