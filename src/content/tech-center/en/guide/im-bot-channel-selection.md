# Connecting a Bot to Chat Apps: WeCom, Feishu, DingTalk, Official Accounts and Personal WeChat

## When this decision has to be made
You must make this decision when your enterprise needs to deliver FastGPT AI capabilities via instant messaging channels to internal employees or external customers. Specific trigger scenarios include building an internal intelligent customer service team, creating group chat collaboration assistants, or providing AI interaction services for WeChat Official Account or personal WeChat users. You can start selection once your deployed FastGPT version meets the minimum requirements for the target channel and you have clarified core use cases.

Early execution risks include redundant configuration from unclear scenarios, repeated adjustments to permissions and links, and wasted operational and configuration time. Late execution risks include breaking existing user habits if you have already built a non-standard temporary AI service entry, requiring additional training costs, and increasing the complexity of migrating accumulated conversation data. This decision is mandatory when your enterprise needs to unify AI service entries, meet compliance requirements, or expand the coverage of AI services.

## Why each criterion matters
### Minimum FastGPT Version Requirement
This criterion determines if your current FastGPT deployment supports connecting to a target channel, and is the foundational prerequisite for selection. Different channels have clear minimum version requirements from official documentation. For example, Feishu bots require 4.8.10+ commercial editions. If your deployment version is lower than the required threshold, you cannot complete integration directly and must first upgrade FastGPT. Upgrading involves service restarts, configuration validation, and other operations that consume operational resources. Confirming version requirements in advance avoids invalid configuration attempts and lets you plan upgrades ahead.

### Applicable Deployment Type
This criterion matches your enterprise’s current FastGPT deployment solution, including commercial and cloud service editions. For example, WeCom integration on cloud service editions requires first configuring a custom domain, while commercial editions can use enterprise-owned domains. If your selected channel does not match your deployment type, you will encounter unresolvable errors during configuration. For example, cloud service edition users cannot access WeCom bots without a custom domain. Clarifying this criterion avoids conflicts between your selection and existing deployment environments, reducing the chance of configuration rework.

### Required Configuration Credentials
Different channels have different configuration credential types and acquisition processes, which directly affect configuration complexity and cross-departmental coordination costs. For example, WeCom bots require Token and Encoding-AESKey, which must be generated by a WeCom super admin in the management backend. WeChat Official Accounts require four types of credentials and IP whitelist configuration. If credential acquisition requires cross-departmental authorization, it will extend the configuration cycle. Incorrect credential configuration will prevent the bot from receiving or forwarding messages normally. Clarifying required credentials lets you plan permission application processes in advance and ensure smooth configuration progress.

### Callback Address Requirements
The callback address is the core communication link between the bot and FastGPT. Different channels have clear requirements for the callback address’s protocol and domain. For example, WeCom cloud service editions must use a custom domain, while Feishu recommends configuring Encrypt Key if using the HTTP protocol. Incorrect callback address configuration will prevent messages from being forwarded to FastGPT, leaving the bot unresponsive. Clarifying this criterion lets you complete domain configuration, network policy adjustments, and other preparatory work in advance, avoiding repeated troubleshooting during configuration due to link issues.

### Permission Configuration Complexity
This criterion affects the human and time costs required for configuration. For example, Feishu bots require adding message receiving events and enabling multiple permissions, with more configuration steps. DingTalk bots only require adding robot capabilities and publishing the application, with relatively simple steps. Missing permission configurations will prevent the bot from receiving or sending messages normally. For example, Feishu bots cannot reply to user messages if the "send messages as the application" permission is not enabled. Clarifying this criterion lets you arrange personnel with corresponding platform permissions to participate in configuration and reasonably distribute work tasks.

### Supported Message Scenarios
This criterion matches your enterprise’s actual use needs and determines the coverage of the bot’s services. For example, personal WeChat only supports one-on-one chats with IOS system WeChat friends and cannot meet group chat collaboration needs. Feishu bots support one-on-one chats, group chats @, and topic group conversations, covering more collaboration scenarios. Choosing a channel that does not meet your core use cases will prevent your AI service from being implemented. Clarifying supported message scenarios ensures your selection aligns with business requirements.

### Reset Chat Method
Different channels have different ways to reset chat records, which affect user experience and operational guidance costs. For example, personal WeChat supports two reset commands, while the reset method for WeCom bots is not stated in official documentation. If your user base is unfamiliar with command operations, you will need to invest additional resources in guidance. Clarifying reset methods lets you plan user guidance content in advance and lower user access thresholds.

## The cost of switching later
Switching from your selected instant messaging channel to another solution incurs multi-dimensional costs. First, there is conversation data migration cost: chat record storage formats and session ID rules vary across channels. For example, Feishu bot chat records use chatId distinguished by three sources: one-on-one chats, individual topics in topic groups, and group ID plus personal ID. Official documentation does not specify cross-channel session record migration methods, so you must test based on your deployment environment.

Second, there is configuration reconstruction cost: each channel has different credentials, callback addresses, and permission configurations. When switching, you must reapply for permissions from the target platform, configure callback links, and adjust permission settings. For example, switching from WeCom to Feishu requires creating a new Feishu open platform application, obtaining credentials, and configuring events and callbacks.

There is also downtime window risk: during the switching period, the bot cannot receive or send messages normally. You must plan downtime in advance and notify users, which affects service continuity. Verification workload is another cost: after switching, you must conduct multiple rounds of testing for all scenarios including one-on-one chats, group chats @, message sending, and chat reset to ensure the bot works normally. You must also verify if existing user habits adapt to the new channel, increasing testing and training costs. Finally, you must adjust internal operational monitoring configurations to replace monitoring metrics for the target channel, further increasing operational workload.

## When this decision can wait
You can delay this decision in several scenarios. First, if your enterprise has not deployed a FastGPT commercial or cloud service edition, you do not need to consider this selection. The FastGPT community edition does not support third-party application publishing and cannot connect to instant messaging channels.

Second, if your AI application is only used for internal testing, you can use the FastGPT built-in debug interface to complete function verification without accessing formal instant messaging channels.

Third, if your core requirements are unclear—for example, you have not determined if your AI service will be used for internal customer service or external customer acquisition—delaying the decision avoids redundant configuration and repeated adjustments caused by premature selection.

Fourth, if you cannot obtain administrator permissions for the target channels—for example, you cannot get WeCom super-administrator access or permission to create a Feishu open platform app—you can delay the decision until you obtain sufficient permissions.

Finally, if your enterprise plans to use a temporary AI solution, such as only sharing AI applications via web links, you can temporarily avoid connecting to instant messaging channels and proceed with selection only when officially launching the service.
